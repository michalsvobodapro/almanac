- body: 'The bidirectional relationship between oral and general health moved from
    plausible association toward mechanistic certainty this week. An 8-year cohort
    study found complete tooth loss associated with a fourfold increase in type-2
    diabetes risk — cohort-level evidence that edentulism is a metabolic risk marker
    worth flagging in the dental record. Simultaneously, a lab study showed P. gingivalis
    hijacking immune checkpoints to drive oral cancer progression, and a mouse model
    linked periodontitis to fatty liver disease via gut microbiota disruption. Neither
    preclinical finding changes Monday''s clinic, but the RA-periodontitis mechanistic
    review published alongside them does: the case for screening every RA patient
    at recall is now strong enough to act on. A phenomenological study of Dutch RA
    patients confirmed the intervention window is being missed — by patients and rheumatologists
    alike. The practical upshot is simple: document systemic conditions, ask about
    joint symptoms, and refer bidirectionally.'
  heading: The Oral-Systemic Axis Grew Teeth
- body: 'AI stories this week split cleanly into promise and caution. A DeepSDF neural
    network reconstructed 3D pulp chamber geometry from crown surface data alone —
    no CBCT required at prediction — which, if it survives clinical validation, would
    genuinely reshape access-cavity planning. A CNN estimated dental age from panoramics
    at 85% accuracy, outperforming YOLOv8, though single-population training data
    makes external validation non-negotiable before deployment. A systematic review
    found AI orthodontic planning matches expert clinicians in controlled studies
    but lacks prospective clinical validation — expert-level accuracy in a lab is
    not the same as expert-level judgment in a consultation. Most sobering: 40% of
    RCT abstracts on digital implant surgery contained spin, with misleading language
    overstating benefit where none was demonstrated. Studies reporting exact p-values
    were significantly less likely to spin. The lesson for a student reading the literature
    is structural: go past the abstract, check the primary outcome, and ask whether
    the comparison condition was clinically realistic.'

- body: 'Three periodontology papers this week added up to more than the sum of their
    parts. A PRISMA-grade meta-analysis on gingival enlargement is now the reference

    conversation as a potential non-invasive alternative to probing; larger trials
    will determine whether it earns a place beside the probe.'
