- body: 'The PROSPERO-registered systematic review confirming that periodontitis and
    edentulism independently raise metabolic syndrome risk is not another speculative
    association study — it is a quantified, confounder-adjusted signal strong enough
    to change how you frame a perio appointment to a patient. Pair that with the molecular
    mechanism now identified for P. gingivalis in esophageal cancer: the bacterium
    activates the ATR DNA-damage pathway, polarises tumour-associated macrophages
    toward the immunosuppressive M2 phenotype, and accelerates cancer progression.
    That is a concrete biological chain, not an epidemiological correlation. Together,
    these two papers mean that when you are scaling and root planing, you are also
    intervening in a patient''s cardiometabolic and oncological risk profile. The
    clinical implication is immediate: periodontal status deserves a line in the medical
    history review, not just the dental chart.'
  heading: Periodontology Has Earned Its Place at the Metabolic Table
- body: 'Nearly 800,000 NHS root canal treatments followed for 15 years is the kind
    of dataset that resets priors. The 77% survival rate (83% when measured by extraction
    alone) is lower than many clinicians quote to patients, and the modifying factors
    — patient age, geographic region, and socioeconomic status as proxied by charge-paying
    status — are not ones you can adjust with a better file system. Mandibular and
    posterior teeth outperform anterior and maxillary counterparts, which aligns with
    clinical intuition but is now backed by population-level numbers. For a student
    learning to consent patients, this dataset is the honest baseline. The separate
    finding that external cervical resorption prognosis depends heavily on treatment
    strategy — and that the two dominant classification systems (Heithersay and Patel)
    do not reliably translate grade into prognosis — adds a further layer: knowing
    the class is not enough; knowing what to do with it is the harder skill.'
  heading: Endodontic Survival at Scale — and the Modifiers That Actually Matter
- body: 'Two papers this week pointed toward the same problem from different directions:
    how do you navigate a canal system you cannot see? A DeepSDF neural network demonstrated
    it can reconstruct the 3D pulp chamber from the external crown surface alone —
    no CBCT required at the prediction stage. This is still a validation-stage finding,
    not a clinical protocol, but the concept is sound and the accuracy data are promising.
    Separately, an electrochemical paper laid out the rationale for impedance mapping
    to localise pulp chambers and canal orifices in calcified cases, including a conceptual
    model for reading impedance signatures at the dentine–gutta-percha interface in
    retreatment. Neither paper changes what you do tomorrow, but both signal the direction
    of travel: the access cavity of the future will be planned before the bur touches
    enamel. Worth watching alongside these: the formal critique of the EFCD-ESE-ORCA
    deep caries guideline, which questions how the evidence was graded — a reminder
    that the protocols you are taught are only as strong as the methodology behind
    them.'

- body: 'A 32-study meta-analysis settled what had been a running clinical debate:

    aligners as the biologically safer orthodontic choice. The practical implication

    selection, not follow it.'
