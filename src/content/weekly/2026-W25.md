- body: 'Three implant stories this week deserve to be read as a single argument.
    A high-grade RCT meta-analysis confirmed that concave or narrowed abutment profiles
    reduce marginal bone loss and improve soft-tissue esthetics — a finding strong
    enough to update your mental default at the planning stage, not just note for
    later. A moderate-grade cohort confirmed immediate placement is safe at five years
    when protocol is followed. And a separate cohort found that nearly one in three
    implant patients has undiagnosed bruxism before surgery — a screening gap that
    undermines every other protocol decision. The practical sequence: screen for bruxism
    first, plan abutment profile second, place with confidence third. Short implants
    under single crowns also earned a high-grade meta-analysis endorsement as non-inferior
    to conventional implants, which means augmentation should now be the second conversation,
    not the first, when bone height is limited.'
  heading: Implantology Got a Tighter Protocol, Not Just More Data
- body: 'The only thread to advance with multiple stories this week was salivary-antimicrobial-peptides-caries-biomarkers,
    and the pairing matters. A cross-sectional cohort showed all five measured AMPs
    are higher in low-caries young adults — a directional signal, but causality-free.
    The same week, a meta-analysis elevated histatin-5 depletion and hBD-2 elevation
    to the most evidence-backed AMP biomarkers for severe caries risk. Neither study
    justifies a chairside test today: the meta-analysis is moderate-grade and the
    cross-sectional design cannot prove direction of effect. But the convergence of
    a population cohort and a systematic review on the same peptides in the same week
    is the kind of replication that moves a biomarker from ''interesting'' to ''watch
    closely.'' When a point-of-care salivary AMP test eventually arrives, you will
    want to have understood the biology already.'
  heading: Salivary Antimicrobial Peptides Are the Caries Biomarker Story Worth Watching
- body: 'Two moderate-grade cohorts added to the systemic case for aggressive periodontal
    treatment: one showed successful therapy restores olfactory function (probing
    depth reduction was the key driver), another showed it reduces kidney inflammation
    markers in CKD patients. Neither proves causality, but both are biologically plausible
    and clinically motivating — the kind of data that changes the conversation with
    a hesitant patient more than it changes the protocol. What did change protocol
    this week was the high-grade systematic review confirming CAF alone drifts within
    five years in thin-phenotype patients; CTG is the long-term anchor. That finding
    is from a high-grade SR and is actionable now. Separately, a high-grade RCT confirmed
    that adding an infrazygomatic block to AMSA reduces intraoperative pain and surgical
    time for maxillary SRP — a small but immediately implementable technique upgrade.'
  heading: Periodontal Therapy's Systemic Reach Kept Expanding — With Caveats
- body: 'Four conservative stories this week converged on the same message: the right
    technique depends on the specific situation, and the evidence now tells you which
    situation calls for what. A high-grade umbrella review confirmed no single caries
    removal technique wins across all outcomes — selective removal is best for deep
    lesions (supported by a separate high-grade RCT showing it cuts post-op pain),
    but the choice must be lesion-specific. A high-grade SR established that rubber
    dam is non-negotiable for adhesive restorations — no decontamination method fully
    rescues bond strength after blood contamination, full stop. For root caries, a
    high-grade network meta-analysis placed quarterly 5% NaF varnish as first-line
    arrest, with SDF reserved for high-risk or access-limited patients. And for radiation
    caries, a high-grade RCT showed fluoride varnish plus saliva substitute outperforms
    either alone. These are not nuanced findings requiring further study — they are
    clean, high-grade directives ready for clinical use.'
