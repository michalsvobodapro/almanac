- body: 'Two independent findings this week establish AI as a functional clinical
    adjunct while simultaneously marking its ceiling. A moderate-evidence cohort showed
    AI annotation measurably improves dental student caries detection on panoramic
    radiographs — not by replacing judgment, but by scaffolding it. Separately, a
    benchmark of five frontier LLMs on pediatric caries risk assessment found ChatGPT-5
    and Claude 4.5 leading but all models showing meaningful accuracy gaps; and GPT-5.1
    and peers matched expert periodontists on scientific accuracy for patient Q&A
    while lagging on empathy. The pattern is consistent: AI is factually competitive
    at population-level reasoning, brittle at edge cases, and blind to the patient
    in the chair. The practical posture is to use these tools as a second reader and
    a knowledge retrieval layer — never as a decision endpoint. The student who learns
    AI''s failure modes now will be ahead of the colleague who learns them from a
    missed diagnosis.'
  heading: AI Is Now a Tool, Not a Trend — Learn Its Failure Modes
- body: 'Three stories converged to reframe caries as a downstream readout of systemic
    and social conditions. A moderate-evidence case-control study confirmed that GERD
    severity tracks directly with caries increment and erosive wear, with salivary
    pH as the mediating variable — meaning every PPI prescription in your patient''s
    file is a caries-risk flag. A moderate-evidence cohort of nearly 8,000 Chinese
    schoolchildren demonstrated that socioeconomic deprivation drives caries increment
    independently of dietary behaviour, which should kill the reflex of handing a
    leaflet and calling it prevention. Most actionably, a high-evidence RCT — the
    strongest grade this week — showed 38% silver diamine fluoride halves new carious
    lesions in head-and-neck radiotherapy patients at six months, regardless of hyposalivation
    severity. That RCT is practice-changing: SDF applied before and at one and three
    months post-radiotherapy is now the defensible standard for this cohort.'
  heading: Caries Risk Is Systemic — Stop Treating It as a Tooth Problem
- body: 'Implant placement is increasingly an act of medical triage. A moderate-evidence
    cohort found carotid artery calcification on OPGs in nearly one in four implant
    patients, the majority unaware — making systematic carotid-region review before
    implant surgery a straightforward standard of care with real referral consequences.
    A dose-response cohort confirmed that moderate smoking doubles and heavy smoking
    triples peri-implantitis risk, giving you a quantified threshold (≥10 cigarettes/day)
    to anchor cessation counselling. A separate cohort showed that mucosal tunnel
    depth >5 mm elevates TNF-α and shifts microbiota even in clinically healthy tissue,
    suggesting documentation at placement is not bureaucratic but prognostic. On the
    reconstructive side, a high-evidence RCT settled the soft-tissue graft question
    for peri-implant augmentation: FGG delivers superior dimensional stability; XCM
    is a reasonable morbidity-sparing second choice. Taken together, the implant appointment
    now carries obligations that extend well beyond the surgical site.'
  heading: Implantology's Expanding Medical Obligation
- body: 'Two moderate-evidence cohort studies this week exposed a gap between clinical

    in gingival crevicular fluid after clinically successful non-surgical periodontal
    therapy — a finding with a direct procedural implication: wait at least three
    months before regenerative procedures, because tissue that looks healed is not
    yet molecularly resolved. A separate cohort confirmed that metabolic syndrome

    methodological framework for future trials. The week''s high-evidence scoping
    review on periodontitis and chronic disease in older adults delivered a sobering
    counterweight: the systemic link is biologically plausible but the age-specific
    evidence base is nearly empty.'
