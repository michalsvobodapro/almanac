summaryDeep: 'Access to dental care is one of the most socially stratified health
  services in Europe, yet comparative cross-national data on how public expenditure
  shapes utilisation have been sparse. This study analyses dental visit rates and
  public spending data across European countries and Japan, finding that the expenditure-utilisation
  relationship is non-linear and heavily context-dependent. Countries with similar
  per-capita public dental spending show markedly different visit rates depending
  on how benefits are structured, what co-payments apply, and how services are delivered.
  Older adults are particularly sensitive to system design, with means-tested or complex
  entitlement systems suppressing utilisation even where nominal public funding exists.
  For a Czech dental student, this is relevant context: the Czech system''s mix of
  public insurance coverage and private top-up payments sits within a European landscape
  where the evidence increasingly favours universal, low-barrier access models for
  achieving population oral health gains.'
