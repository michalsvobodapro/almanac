  public health burdens. Oral microbiota plays a key role in caries development. Although
  several studies have compared the oral microbiota before and after caries treatment,
  few have investigated the dynamic changes in oral microbial communities beyond 3

summaryDeep: 'Children with ECC were assessed at baseline, immediately post-treatment,
  and at 1, 3, and 6 months. Oral hygiene improved significantly after treatment (p<0.001)
  and microbial alpha-diversity (Chao1) was higher at T2–T4 than at baseline. However,
  salivary pH, which rose immediately post-treatment, returned to pre-treatment levels
  during follow-up — suggesting that the buffering environment reverted despite improved
  hygiene. Relative abundances of Corynebacterium and Corynebacterium matruchotii
  were significantly higher at 3 and 6 months compared to immediately post-treatment,
  while Saccharibacteria correlated positively with dmfs scores. The findings underscore
  that caries management in young children cannot be a one-time intervention: the
  microbial and environmental conditions that drive caries re-establish themselves
  within months. For pediatric dental practice, this supports intensive, repeated
  preventive follow-up rather than discharge after restorative treatment.'
