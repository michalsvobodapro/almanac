  compatible root surface that facilitates the attachment, migration, and proliferation

summary: An in-vitro SEM study compared the ultrastructural effects of several root

summaryDeep: 'Scaling and root planing leaves a smear layer that may impede new attachment,
  and biomodification agents — citric acid, EDTA, tetracycline, and others — are used
  to remove it and expose collagen fibrils. This SEM study systematically compared
  the root surface alterations induced by multiple agents, evaluating smear-layer
  removal efficiency, surface morphology, and collagen fibre exposure at high magnification.
  The in-vitro design is the primary constraint: SEM captures surface topography but
  not biological response (cell adhesion, fibroblast migration), and extracted teeth
  lack the vascular and cellular environment of a living periodontium. The study does
  not report clinical outcomes. Its value is as a reference for understanding the
  mechanistic rationale behind agent selection in regenerative periodontal surgery
  — useful background for a student preparing for perio rotations, but not a protocol-changing
  finding on its own.'
