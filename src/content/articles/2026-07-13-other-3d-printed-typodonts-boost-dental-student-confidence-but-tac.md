summaryDeep: 'Additive manufacturing has transformed dental education logistics, but
  the question of whether printed typodonts actually feel like teeth has been answered
  inconsistently across the literature. This systematic review (PRISMA 2020, INPLASY-registered,
  7 databases, 29 studies) is the first to synthesise tactile perception data specifically.
  The educational benefits are real and consistent: students report greater engagement
  and improved technical confidence, and objective skill assessments generally favour
  3D-printed practice over no practice. But the tactile gap is equally consistent
  — cutting resistance, surface texture, and dentine-enamel junction feel are all
  rated inferior to natural teeth or traditional resin models by both students and
  educators. Material science is the bottleneck: no current photopolymer replicates
  the anisotropic hardness of enamel. For a Prague dental student, this is directly
  relevant — if your faculty is transitioning to printed typodonts, expect the operative
  feel to be softer and more uniform than the real thing, and compensate by seeking
  natural tooth practice where available.'
