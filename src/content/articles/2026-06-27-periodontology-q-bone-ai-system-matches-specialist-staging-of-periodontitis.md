  for anatomically driven assessment of alveolar bone loss and assistance in the diagnosis
  of periodontitis across multiple clinical centers and imaging devices. This study
  included 1,273 periodontitis cases from four clinical centers using diverse imaging
  devices. A multitask deep learning model, Deep Gradient Network (DGNet), was employed

summaryDeep: 'Q-Bone integrates tooth segmentation (S-measure 0.929) with anatomical
  keypoint localization (PRCK@0.5 = 0.994) and a curvature-based algorithm that computes
  the alveolar bone resorption ratio (ABRR) directly from periapical or panoramic
  radiographs. In a multicenter external validation cohort of 174 patients, tooth-level
  ABRR showed ICC 0.973 against specialist reference measurements — a bias of just
  −0.238%. Patient-level agreement for the 2018 AAP/EFP classification reached κ 0.9351
  (Stage), 0.9367 (Grade), and 0.9770 (Extent), with linear weighted κ above 0.95
  for the ordinal outcomes. The system works across diverse imaging devices, which
  is the usual Achilles heel of radiographic AI. What makes Q-Bone clinically meaningful
  is not just accuracy but the anatomically driven design: bone loss is referenced
  to the cemento-enamel junction rather than a fixed threshold, mirroring how a periodontist
  actually reads the film. If this holds in prospective deployment, it could standardize
  staging in general practice where specialist oversight is absent.'
