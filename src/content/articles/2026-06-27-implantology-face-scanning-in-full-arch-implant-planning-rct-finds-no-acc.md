excerpt: The effect of integrating facial scans into digital planning on the accuracy
  of placement of dental implants and the preplanned interim prosthesis in maxillary
  complete arch implant rehabilitations has not been clearly established. The purpose
  of this study was to assess the impact of using face scanners in maxillary complete
  arch implant supported rehabilitations on the trueness of placement of the implants
  and the preplanned interim prosthesis. Sixteen completely edentulous participants
  were selected for maxillary complete arch guided implant placement with immediately
  loaded interim prosthesis. Participants were randomized into 2 groups; prosthetically
  driven (PD) and facially driven (FD). In both groups the dual scan protocol was
  performed. The FD group received additional face scans. All data files were superimposed
  and the implants' sites, surgical guide, and immediate interim prosthesis were planned.
  The surgery was carried out and participants were recalled after implant insertion
  for another cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) scan. For implants placement trueness,
  linear deviation at the apex and the tip as well as angular deviations were measured.
  Regarding trueness of the interim prostheses, the overall deviation and the deviations
  at the midline, the cusp tips of the canines, and the mesiobuccal cusp of the first
  molar were measured. Data were non parametric and the Mann Whitney U test was used.
  Two-step cluster analysis was performed regarding implant deviation

summary: A parallel RCT randomized 16 edentulous patients to prosthetically driven
  (PD) versus facially driven (FD) guided implant placement for maxillary complete-arch
  rehabilitation. Angular and linear implant deviations, and interim prosthesis trueness,
  did not differ significantly between groups. Adding a face scan to the digital workflow
  does not improve placement accuracy — at least at this sample size.
summaryDeep: 'Both groups underwent the dual-scan protocol; the FD group additionally
  received facial scans integrated into the planning software. Implant placement trueness
  was assessed by superimposing post-surgical CBCT onto the planned positions, measuring
  apex and tip linear deviations plus angular deviation. Interim prosthesis trueness
  was measured at the midline, canine cusp tips, and first molar mesiobuccal cusps.
  Data were non-parametric (Mann-Whitney U). No statistically significant differences
  emerged between PD and FD groups on any primary outcome. The study is underpowered
  — 16 patients is a pilot, and the authors acknowledge this — but it is the first
  RCT to directly address this question. The clinical implication is not that face
  scanning is useless: it clearly adds value for esthetic planning and communication.
  But the claim that it improves surgical accuracy of implant placement is not yet
  supported by controlled evidence.'
