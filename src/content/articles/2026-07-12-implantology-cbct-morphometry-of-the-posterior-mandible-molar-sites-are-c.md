summaryDeep: 'Posterior mandibular implant placement is complicated by progressive
  ridge resorption, proximity to the mandibular canal, and lingual cortical concavities
  that risk perforation. This single-centre retrospective CBCT study measured residual
  bone height, bone width at three levels, lingual concavity angle, and horizontal
  concavity offset across 252 sites in 45 patients, using linear mixed-effects models
  to account for within-patient clustering. First and second molar sites showed the
  lowest residual bone height and the most pronounced lingual concavities — findings
  that translate directly into higher augmentation rates and greater perforation risk
  at those positions. Premolar sites were substantially more favourable. For implant
  planning in the clinic, the study reinforces that a standardised multiplanar CBCT
  workflow is not optional in the posterior mandible: visual inspection of a panoramic
  alone will miss lingual concavity morphology. The retrospective design and single-centre
  sample limit generalisability, but the anatomical gradients reported are consistent
  with existing literature.'
