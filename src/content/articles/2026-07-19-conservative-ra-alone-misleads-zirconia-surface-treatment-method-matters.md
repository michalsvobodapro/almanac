summaryDeep: 'Clinicians and lab technicians routinely specify zirconia surface roughness
  by Ra, but this study of 96 zirconia discs (12 treatment groups, 1.2 million loading
  cycles) demonstrates that Ra is an insufficient descriptor. Airborne-particle abrasion,
  diamond burs, and silicon-carbide papers applied before or after sintering produced
  statistically different values for Rp, Rz, Rc, and Rt despite comparable Ra targets
  (0.3–0.8 µm). The antagonist materials — polymethacrylate resin and lithium disilicate
  ceramic — showed wear patterns that did not map simply onto agonist Ra. The finding
  has direct relevance for intraoral adjustments: grinding sintered zirconia with
  a bur and polishing to a given Ra does not guarantee the same tribological outcome
  as a pre-sintering treatment at the same Ra. Until clinical wear studies confirm
  which surface parameter best predicts in vivo antagonist wear, the safest protocol
  remains manufacturer-recommended polishing sequences rather than Ra-target-only
  specifications.'
