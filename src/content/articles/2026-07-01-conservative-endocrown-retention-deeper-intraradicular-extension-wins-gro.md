summaryDeep: 'Endocrowns have gained traction as a conservative alternative to post-and-core
  restorations for endodontically treated teeth, but optimal preparation design remains
  debated. This study tested two variables — buccal groove addition and intraradicular
  extension depth (3 mm vs. 5 mm) — in a factorial design using Vita Enamic CAD/CAM
  blocks on 40 mandibular premolars. Retention was measured on a universal testing
  machine; microleakage was assessed via methylene blue dye penetration. The headline
  finding: 5 mm intraradicular extension delivered significantly greater retention
  than 3 mm regardless of groove presence, while the buccal groove modification added
  nothing statistically meaningful to either retention or microleakage. Failure mode
  analysis showed predominantly adhesive failures, suggesting the cement interface
  remains the weak link. The practical takeaway is straightforward — when you have
  the root canal length to work with, go deeper rather than adding surface complexity.
  Limitations are the usual in-vitro caveats: no fatigue cycling, no thermal cycling,
  and mandibular premolars only.'
