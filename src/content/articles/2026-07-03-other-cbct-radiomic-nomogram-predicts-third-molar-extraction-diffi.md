summaryDeep: Six hundred patients undergoing mandibular third molar extraction were
  split into training and validation sets. LASSO regression selected 11 robust CBCT
  radiomic features to construct a Rad-score; multivariable logistic regression identified
  independent clinical predictors; and the final nomogram combined both. The model
  demonstrated strong discrimination (AUC reported as significant), good calibration
  on Hosmer-Lemeshow testing, and positive net benefit on decision curve analysis
  across a range of threshold probabilities. Compared to conventional difficulty indices
  (Pell-Gregory, Winter's classification), the radiomic nomogram captures three-dimensional
  bone density and root morphology information invisible on two-dimensional OPG. Limitations
  include the retrospective single-centre design and the need for CBCT — not always
  justified for routine third molar assessment under ALARA principles. For cases where
  CBCT is already indicated (proximity to IAN, complex root morphology), the nomogram
  adds meaningful preoperative stratification. Relevant for students approaching oral
  surgery rotations.
