summaryDeep: Endocrowns have gained traction as a conservative post-endodontic restoration,
  but margin design recommendations have been largely biomechanical and in-vitro.
  This multicenter RCT provides 6-year clinical follow-up data comparing two margin
  geometries within the same ZLS system — a meaningful methodological strength, since
  cross-material comparisons confound interpretation. Both the 90° shoulder and butt-joint
  designs demonstrated high survival rates, suggesting that for ZLS endocrowns, margin
  geometry is not the dominant failure driver. The excerpt is limited, but the study
  design (multicenter RCT, 6-year follow-up) carries genuine weight. For students
  and clinicians, this supports flexibility in margin selection based on tooth anatomy
  and preparation convenience rather than dogmatic adherence to one design. It also
  reinforces ZLS as a durable endocrown material, complementing the existing evidence
  base for lithium disilicate.
