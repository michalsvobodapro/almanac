summary: A cohort study in the BDJ finds that water hardness is an independent modifier
  of the association between fluoride exposure, socioeconomic deprivation, and caries
  burden — meaning fluoride's protective effect is not uniform across populations
  with different water mineral profiles. The finding has implications for public health
  fluoridation policy and individual risk stratification.
summaryDeep: 'Fluoride and water fluoridation have long been evaluated in isolation
  from other water chemistry variables. This cohort study introduces water hardness
  — primarily calcium and magnesium ion concentration — as a meaningful confounder
  and effect modifier in the fluoride–caries–deprivation triangle. In harder water
  areas, the caries-protective signal from fluoride appears attenuated or modified,
  while the relationship between deprivation and caries burden also shifts. The mechanism
  is plausible: calcium ions in hard water may independently support remineralisation,
  partially substituting for fluoride''s role, and may also alter fluoride bioavailability.
  For a Czech dental student, this is relevant because Central Europe has highly variable
  water hardness by region, and blanket fluoride supplementation advice may need local
  calibration. The study is moderate-grade cohort evidence — causality cannot be established
  — but it raises a legitimate question about whether current fluoridation guidelines
  adequately account for baseline water mineral composition.'
