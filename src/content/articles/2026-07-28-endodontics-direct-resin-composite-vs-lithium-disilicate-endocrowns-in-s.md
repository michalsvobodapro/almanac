summary: A superiority RCT pitting direct resin composite endocrowns against lithium
  disilicate endocrowns in structurally compromised endodontically treated molars
  found no clinically decisive advantage for the ceramic option at two years. Survival,
  clinical quality, and patient satisfaction were the primary endpoints. The result
  challenges the reflex to always prescribe an indirect ceramic solution for heavily
  broken-down posterior teeth.
summaryDeep: 'This randomized controlled superiority trial is one of the few high-quality
  head-to-head comparisons of direct versus indirect endocrown restorations in molars
  with severe structural compromise after root canal treatment. Over two years, restoration
  survival, clinical quality scores, and patient-reported satisfaction were tracked
  for both direct resin composite and lithium disilicate endocrowns. The superiority
  design means the trial was powered to detect a meaningful advantage for the ceramic
  arm — and it apparently did not find one convincingly enough to declare a winner.
  For a dental student, this reframes the clinical decision: the cheaper, single-visit
  direct composite approach is not simply a compromise option. Material choice should
  be driven by remaining tooth structure, occlusal load, and patient factors rather
  than an assumption that ceramic is categorically superior. Two years is still a
  relatively short horizon for a definitive answer, but the data are enough to justify
  a more nuanced conversation with patients about cost and appointments.'
