  We examined whether two-wave patterns of the modified cardiometabolic index (MCMI)

  Chinese adults. Methods We analyzed participants aged 45 years or older from the

summaryDeep: 'Complete edentulism is a late-stage oral health outcome that aggregates
  decades of caries, periodontal disease, and tooth extraction decisions. This longitudinal
  analysis from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study tracks 3,626 adults
  aged 45+ through two MCMI measurements (2011, 2015) and an edentulism endpoint in
  2018. The key finding: participants who transitioned from high to low MCMI — meaning
  their cardiometabolic risk profile improved — had an adjusted risk ratio of 0.57
  for incident edentulism compared to those who remained stably high. Static high
  or low MCMI and cumulative MCMI showed weaker or null associations after full adjustment,
  suggesting that trajectory matters more than snapshot status. The modified Poisson
  regression with robust variance is methodologically appropriate for a binary outcome
  in a cohort design. Limitations include self-reported tooth loss and the possibility
  that improved MCMI reflects broader health behaviour change rather than a specific
  metabolic mechanism. For students, this reinforces the systemic–oral health integration
  message: patients who improve their metabolic health may be protecting their teeth,
  and vice versa.'
