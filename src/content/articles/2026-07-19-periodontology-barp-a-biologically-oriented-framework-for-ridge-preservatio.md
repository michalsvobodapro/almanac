summaryDeep: 'Alveolar ridge preservation after extraction is well-established for
  intact sockets, but buccal bone dehiscence dramatically complicates outcomes: the
  coronal ridge remodels preferentially, and most ARP protocols were not designed
  to address this. The BARP concept, published in the Journal of Periodontal Research,
  frames the problem biologically rather than just materially — emphasizing the interplay
  between graft material, membrane selection, and the healing biology of compromised
  sockets. The paper is classified as a narrative/concept piece rather than a clinical
  trial, so the evidence grade is descriptive. Its value is as a conceptual organizer
  for a field that has accumulated many individual techniques without a unifying framework.
  For implant planning, the practical question BARP addresses is real: when you extract
  a tooth with a dehisced buccal plate, what do you actually do differently? The framework
  provides a decision structure, though prospective validation is needed before it
  can be adopted as a protocol.'
