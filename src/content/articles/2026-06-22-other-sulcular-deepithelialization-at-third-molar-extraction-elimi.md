excerpt: Sulcular deepitheliazation (SD) means the formation, maintenance, and regeneration
  of the thin, non-keratinized epithelial lining (sulcular epithelium) that covers
  the soft tissue wall of the gingival sulcus. SD is a critical, rapid healing process
  following periodontal surgery, where epithelial cells migrate to cover denuded connective
  tissue and re-establish a barrier against bacteria. To assess the healing outcomes
  of SD during the extraction of partially impacted mandibular third molars. This
  clinical pilot study enrolled forty patients at the Oral Surgery Clinic, College
  of Dentistry, University of Sulaimani, Sulaimaniyah, Iraq, from January 2024 to
  June 2025. Participants were randomly assigned to a control group treated with a
  modified three-sided flap (n = 20) or a study group receiving additional SD (n =
  20), as an approach for surgical extraction of partially erupted lower third molars.

  different variables were determined and compared. SD did not significantly affect

summary: In this pilot RCT (n=40), adding sulcular deepithelialization to a modified
  three-sided flap for partially impacted lower third molar extraction eliminated
  dry socket (10% in controls), impaired healing (15% in controls), and second-molar
  sensitivity (25% in controls) — with no significant difference in pain, swelling,

  rate in the study group is striking.
summaryDeep: 'Dry socket and second-molar periodontal complications are the two outcomes
  patients and clinicians most want to avoid after lower third molar surgery. This
  pilot RCT tested whether sulcular deepithelialization — removing the non-keratinized
  sulcular epithelium before flap closure — could reduce these events. The modified
  three-sided flap alone (control) produced dry socket in 2/20, impaired healing in
  3/20, and second-molar sensitivity in 5/20 patients. The deepithelialization group
  had zero events across all three outcomes. Pain, swelling, and trismus were statistically
  equivalent between groups. The mechanism proposed is improved connective tissue-to-connective
  tissue healing at flap margins, reducing epithelial migration into the clot. The
  evidence grade is low: n=40, single centre, pilot designation, and the primary endpoint
  wasn''t pre-registered with power calculation detail. Replication in a larger multicentre
  RCT is needed before this becomes a standard step. Still, the technique is simple,
  adds minimal operative time, and the complication reduction — if real — would be
  clinically meaningful.'
