summaryDeep: 'As CBCT becomes routine for implant planning, endodontic assessment,
  and orthodontic diagnosis, the artifact behavior of restorative materials in the
  field of view is clinically relevant. This study restored 90 standardized 3D-printed
  mandibular canine models with six materials — including G-ænial Universal Injectable,
  OMNICHROMA, Charisma Diamond ONE, and EQUIA Forte HT — and scanned all under identical
  CBCT parameters. Grayscale value analysis at standardized ROIs adjacent to each
  restoration revealed statistically significant inter-material differences on mesial
  and other surfaces. Injectable composites did not uniformly produce less artifact
  than conventional resins; the relationship was material-specific. The practical
  implication: when interpreting CBCT scans of restored teeth, knowing the restorative
  material present helps anticipate artifact patterns and avoid misdiagnosis of adjacent
  structures.'
