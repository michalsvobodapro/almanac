summary: A triple-blind RCT of 189 patients with dentine hypersensitivity in multiple
  teeth found Gluma® (glutaraldehyde/HEMA) produced significant reductions in pain
  on tactile and air-jet stimulation at 30 days, while the saline placebo group showed
  no change. OHRQoL scores (DHEQ-15) improved significantly only in the Gluma arm.
  This is the strongest controlled evidence yet for Gluma's patient-centred benefit
  beyond pain scores.
summaryDeep: Dentine hypersensitivity is one of the most common chairside complaints,
  yet high-quality RCT evidence for specific desensitising agents has been patchy.
  This triple-blind RCT randomised 189 adults (mean age 31; 78% female) with DH in
  more than one tooth to Gluma® or saline placebo, applied once to the dried cervical
  area for three minutes. At 30 days, the Gluma group showed significant reductions
  in both tactile and air-jet pain scores (p<0.001) and in DHEQ-15 OHRQoL scores (p<0.001);
  the placebo group showed no significant change on either measure. A moderate positive
  correlation between DHEQ-15 domains and pain scores was confirmed in both groups.
  Limitations include the single follow-up timepoint (30 days) and a predominantly
  female, young sample that may not generalise. The study does not compare Gluma to
  other active agents. Still, for a student deciding between desensitising options,
  this RCT provides solid justification for Gluma as a first-line in-office treatment,
  particularly when patient-reported outcomes matter.
