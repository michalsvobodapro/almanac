summary: In a ligature-induced mouse model, periodontitis significantly aggravated
  high-fat diet-induced metabolic-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), with
  the gut microbiota identified as a key mediating pathway. The HFD-periodontitis
  group showed markedly higher liver fibrosis scores and lipid accumulation compared
  to diet-alone controls.
summaryDeep: 'This Scientific Reports study used a two-hit mouse model — 12 weeks
  of high-fat diet followed by 4 weeks of ligature-induced periodontitis — to probe
  the periodontitis-MASLD connection. Micro-CT, histology, and serum biochemistry
  confirmed that periodontitis amplified hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis
  beyond what the high-fat diet alone produced. Gut microbiota profiling implicated
  dysbiosis as a mechanistic bridge: periodontal pathogens or their products appear
  to translocate and alter hepatic metabolism. The low-fat diet groups showed minimal
  liver pathology regardless of periodontal status, suggesting the effect is conditional
  on metabolic stress. As a mouse study, clinical translation requires caution, but
  the findings add to a growing body of evidence positioning periodontitis as a systemic
  metabolic amplifier — relevant for patients presenting with both obesity and periodontal
  disease.'
