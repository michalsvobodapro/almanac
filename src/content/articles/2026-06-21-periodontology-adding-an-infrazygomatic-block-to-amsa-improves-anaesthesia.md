summary: An 80-patient RCT compares the infrazygomatic approach combined with AMSA
  nerve block versus AMSA alone for maxillary scaling and root planing. The combined

  minimizing palatal mucosal blanching. A practical refinement for hygienists and
  periodontists managing anxious patients during non-surgical therapy.
summaryDeep: Adequate anaesthesia for maxillary non-surgical periodontal therapy is
  a persistent clinical challenge — AMSA alone often leaves gaps in coverage and causes
  visible palatal blanching that unsettles patients. This single-blind parallel-arm
  RCT at SRM Dental College (n=80, ASA I–II, ages 18–70) tested whether adding an
  infrazygomatic approach to AMSA injection with 2% lidocaine/1:80,000 adrenaline
  improves outcomes. The combined technique outperformed AMSA alone on pain intensity
  scores and surgical duration, with less palatal mucosal blanching. The infrazygomatic
  approach targets the posterior superior alveolar nerve via a lateral approach, supplementing
  AMSA's coverage of the anterior and middle superior alveolar nerves. The technique
  is not widely taught in Czech dental curricula but is straightforward to learn.
  Limitations include single-centre design and short follow-up, but the clinical signal
  is clear enough to trial in practice.
