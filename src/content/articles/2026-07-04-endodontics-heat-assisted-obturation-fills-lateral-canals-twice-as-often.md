excerpt: Background To evaluate the effect of heat-assisted obturation on the distribution
  of filling material within apical and coronal lateral canals, the composition of
  the filling materials present within these canals, and the radiographic and microscopic
  characteristics of the premixed calcium silicate-based endodontic sealer after setting
  in standardized lateral canals. Methods Sixty simulated root canals with standardized
  apical and coronal lateral canals (n = 120) were prepared in resin blocks. Canals
  were obturated using either a single-cone technique or carrier-based technique,
  both combined with a premixed calcium silicate-based sealer (CeraSeal ® ). Radiographic
  and microscopic analyses were performed immediately after obturation and after complete

  according to obturation completeness and material composition. Statistical analysis
  was performed using chi-square, Fisher's exact, and McNemar tests (α = 0.05). Results

summaryDeep: Sixty simulated root canals with standardized apical and coronal lateral
  canals were obturated using either single-cone or carrier-based (heat-assisted)
  technique, both with premixed CeraSeal sealer. Apical lateral canal filling was
  complete in 90% of heat-assisted cases versus 46.7% for single-cone — a statistically
  significant and clinically meaningful difference. Coronal lateral canals showed
  less dramatic but directionally consistent results. The sealer's radiographic and
  microscopic characteristics were unchanged between immediate and 4-day evaluations,
  confirming setting stability. This is in vitro evidence in resin blocks, so the
  translation to curved, calcified human canals is uncertain. However, for a student
  choosing between obturation techniques, this adds to a growing body of evidence
  that single-cone with bioceramic sealer — while convenient — leaves lateral anatomy
  less well-filled than thermoplastic techniques.
