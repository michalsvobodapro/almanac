excerpt: 'Objectives This multicenter prospective cohort study follows a 1-year period
  after a previously published randomized controlled trial, comparing the radiographic
  and clinical outcomes of implants placed with simultaneous guided bone regeneration
  (GBR) using bioceramic (BC) versus xenograft (BO). Materials and methods Patients
  from the previous trial were recalled 1 year after crown delivery. The follow-up
  data focused on the buccal bone stability evaluated by CBCT and implant survival
  rate, Probing Depth (PD), Bleeding on Probing (BOP), Plaque Index (PI), and patient
  satisfaction through Oral Health Impact Profile-5 (OHIP-5). Results Out of 150 enrolled
  patients in a previous trial, a total of 97 patients completed the 1-year follow-up:
  44 in the BC group and 53 in the BO group. The implant survival rate was 100% in
  both groups. Change in horizontal buccal bone thickness (△HBBT) at 1 year post-crown
  delivery versus immediately post-surgery was -0.28 ± 0.10 mm for the BC group and
  -0.91 ± 0.09 mm (mean ± standard error) for the BO group, with the BC group exhibiting
  less bone resorption along the implant than the BO group (p = 0.0000). No significant
  differences were observed in PI, BOP, or PD. Patient satisfaction remained high
  in both groups. Conclusions GBR with bioceramic demonstrated stable radiographical
  bone on the buccal side of the implant 1 year after crown delivery.'

summaryDeep: 'This 1-year follow-up of a previously published RCT enrolled 97 of the
  original 150 patients (44 bioceramic, 53 xenograft) and assessed buccal bone stability
  via CBCT alongside standard clinical parameters. The difference in horizontal buccal
  bone thickness change was statistically significant (p<0.0001): the bioceramic group
  lost 0.28 ± 0.10 mm versus 0.91 ± 0.09 mm in the xenograft group. Probing depth,
  bleeding on probing, and plaque index did not differ between groups, and patient
  satisfaction was high in both. The 35% dropout from the original RCT is a meaningful
  limitation and could introduce selection bias. Bioceramic grafts — synthetic calcium
  silicate-based materials — are gaining traction as xenograft alternatives on both
  biological and ethical grounds; this data adds a quantitative bone-preservation
  argument. Whether the buccal bone advantage translates to lower peri-implantitis
  rates at 5 or 10 years remains to be seen.'
