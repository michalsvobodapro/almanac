summaryDeep: Oral squamous cell carcinoma of the tongue is among the most aggressive
  OSCC subsites, and the role of HPV in oral (as opposed to oropharyngeal) carcinogenesis
  remains contested. This cohort study characterised tongue carcinomas by HPV status
  and correlated findings with cancer stem cell markers and conventional prognostic
  indicators. HPV-positive cases showed distinct CSC marker expression patterns, suggesting
  that the biological behaviour of HPV-driven tongue cancers differs from HPV-negative
  tumours in ways that may affect treatment response and recurrence risk. The study
  is observational and limited by cohort size, but it contributes to the growing case
  for routine HPV testing in oral cancer workup — not yet standard in most European
  centres. For dental students, this is a reminder that the oral cancer screening
  conversation increasingly needs to include HPV vaccination history and sexual behaviour
  risk factors.
