summaryDeep: Participants were assessed with the full Research Diagnostic Criteria
  for Temporomandibular Disorders questionnaire and clinical examination protocol.
  Of the cohort, 35% had reduced OVD and 18% had increased OVD. Despite the range
  of vertical dimension variation, no statistically significant association with TMD
  emerged. The association with depression (p=0.035) is consistent with the biopsychosocial
  model of TMD and reinforces that psychological screening belongs in the TMD workup.
  The lack of association with tooth wear is also notable — it challenges the clinical
  habit of attributing wear-related OVD loss to TMD aetiology. Limitations include
  cross-sectional design (no causal inference), single-centre Turkish cohort, and
  the absence of imaging-based joint assessment. For students taught that OVD changes
  cause or predict TMD, this study is a useful corrective — though a single cross-sectional
  study in one population is not sufficient to rewrite the textbook.
