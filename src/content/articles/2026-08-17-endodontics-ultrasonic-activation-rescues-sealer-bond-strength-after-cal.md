excerpt: 'Abstract Background The aim of this study was to comparatively evaluate
  the effects of removing calcium silicate–based (Bio-C Temp) and calcium hydroxide–based
  (UltraCal XS) intracanal medicaments via manual and ultrasonic activation methods
  on the bond strength of bioceramic-based (Bioserra) and resin-based (AH Plus) root
  canal sealers to dentin. Methods Eighty single-rooted, single-canal human teeth
  were used in this study. After root canal preparation, the samples were randomly
  assigned to three main groups: UltraCal XS ( n = 32), Bio-C Temp ( n = 32), and
  a control group with no medicament application ( n = 16). The medicament groups
  were further subdivided according to the removal technique into manual irrigation
  and ultrasonic activation subgroups. The root canals were obturated via either AH
  Plus or Bioserra sealers. Push-out bond strength tests were performed on the obtained
  sections, and failure modes were evaluated under a stereomicroscope. Results Three-way
  ANOVA revealed that only the medicament type significantly affected the bond strength
  ( p = 0.001); the activation method and sealer type did not ( p &gt; 0.05). In the
  UltraCal XS groups, where the medicament was removed via manual irrigation, the
  bond strength values were significantly lower than those in the Bio-C Temp and control
  groups ( p &lt; 0.05). Compared with manual irrigation, ultrasonic activation significantly
  increased the bond strength of the AH Plus sealer in the UltraCal XS group ( p &lt;
  0.05)'

summary: An in vitro study of 80 single-rooted teeth shows that medicament type —
  not activation method or sealer type — is the dominant driver of post-obturation

summaryDeep: 'Eighty single-rooted, single-canal human teeth were prepared and randomly
  assigned to UltraCal XS, Bio-C Temp, or no-medicament control groups, then subdivided
  by removal technique (manual irrigation vs. ultrasonic activation) and obturated
  with either AH Plus or Bioserra bioceramic sealer. Three-way ANOVA identified medicament
  type as the only significant factor (p = 0.001); activation method and sealer type
  did not reach significance overall. However, in the UltraCal XS subgroup, manual
  irrigation produced significantly lower bond strength than Bio-C Temp and control
  groups, and ultrasonic activation significantly recovered AH Plus bond strength
  in that subgroup (p < 0.05). The practical message is nuanced: if you use calcium
  hydroxide, ultrasonic activation for removal is not optional — it materially affects
  adhesion. If you switch to a calcium silicate medicament like Bio-C Temp, the removal
  technique matters less. This aligns with the growing clinical shift toward calcium
  silicate-based intracanal medicaments and supports their use on evidence rather
  than just convenience.'
