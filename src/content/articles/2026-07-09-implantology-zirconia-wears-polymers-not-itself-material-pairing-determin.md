summaryDeep: 'Material selection for complete-arch implant-supported prostheses involves
  trade-offs between wear resistance, antagonist protection, and fabrication method.
  This in vitro study is one of the most systematic comparisons of milled PMMA, 3D-printed
  resin, and zirconia in a chewing simulator with thermocycling, covering 16 specimens
  per group across seven antagonist/sample pairings. Zirconia''s dominance in wear
  resistance is confirmed, but the more clinically nuanced finding is the directionality
  of wear in mixed pairings: when zirconia opposes polymers, the polymer absorbs ~95%
  of wear loss, which may be protective for natural teeth or implant components but
  accelerates prosthetic replacement cycles. The 3D-PR/mPMMA pairing''s anomalous
  wear pattern (antagonist wearing more than sample) warrants further investigation.
  Total height loss ranged from 0.27 mm (Z/Z) to 1.01 mm (mPMMA/3D-PR) — the latter
  approaching clinically significant occlusal change over a prosthetic lifespan. In
  vitro data cannot fully predict clinical wear, but these findings should inform
  material selection conversations for full-arch implant cases.'
