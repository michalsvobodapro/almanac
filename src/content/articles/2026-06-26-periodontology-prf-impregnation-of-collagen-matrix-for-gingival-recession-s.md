summary: A split-mouth RCT tests whether soaking a volume-stable collagen matrix (VSCM)
  in platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) improves recession depth reduction compared to VSCM
  alone at 6 months. The trial finds no significant superiority for the PRF-enhanced
  arm. PRF impregnation adds procedural complexity without measurable clinical gain
  in this design.
summaryDeep: Volume-stable collagen matrices have largely replaced connective tissue
  grafts in multiple-recession coverage protocols at many centres, and PRF has been
  widely promoted as a biological booster for soft-tissue regeneration. This split-mouth
  RCT — the cleanest possible design for controlling inter-patient variability — directly
  tests whether combining the two is worth the extra step. At 6 months, recession
  depth reduction is not significantly better with PRF impregnation. The result is
  clinically important because PRF preparation adds chair time, centrifuge cost, and
  patient discomfort (blood draw). If the benefit doesn't materialize at 6 months,
  the burden of proof now sits with proponents of the combination. Longer follow-up
  data and patient-reported outcomes (root sensitivity, esthetics) would strengthen
  the conclusion, but the RCT design already makes this one of the stronger negative
  findings in recent perio literature. Clinicians using VSCM can deprioritize PRF
  impregnation without feeling they are compromising outcomes.
