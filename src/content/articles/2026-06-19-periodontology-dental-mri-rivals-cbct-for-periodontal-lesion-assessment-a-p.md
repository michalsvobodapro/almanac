summaryDeep: 'Dental MRI has long been constrained by resolution and acquisition time,
  but multi-contrast protocols are changing that calculus. This pilot comparative
  study evaluated dMRI against CBCT in periodontitis patients, assessing reliability
  for periodontal lesion detection and correlating imaging findings with clinical
  indicators of inflammation (bleeding on probing, probing depth). MRI demonstrated
  reliable lesion assessment and captured soft-tissue inflammatory burden that CBCT
  — a bone-centric modality — inherently cannot. The pilot design limits generalizability,
  and MRI access in Czech dental clinics remains limited. However, for a student thinking
  about the next decade of periodontal diagnostics, this study is a signpost: radiation-free,
  soft-tissue-sensitive imaging that correlates with clinical disease activity is
  a compelling proposition. The EFP''s imaging recommendations currently favour CBCT
  for bone assessment; this pilot will feed into future reconsideration of that position.'
