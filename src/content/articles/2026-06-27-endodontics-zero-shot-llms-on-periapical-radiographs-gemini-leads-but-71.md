  multimodal large language models (MLLMs)-Claude 4.5 Sonnet, GPT-5.2 Thinking, and
  Gemini 3.0 Pro-in detecting apical periodontitis on periapical radiographs. One
  hundred twenty periapical radiographs were included (60 apical periodontitis-positive
  and 60 healthy), retrieved from routine clinical records at a university dental
  clinic. Teeth with root canal fillings and radiographs showing metallic artifacts
  were excluded to minimize superimposition in the apical region. Two experienced
  clinicians independently assessed all images to establish the reference standard
  based on two-dimensional periapical radiographic interpretation without CBCT confirmation.
  Disagreements were resolved by consensus, and interobserver agreement was high (Cohen's
  κ = 0.88). The same coded images were subsequently evaluated by the three MLLMs

summaryDeep: 'The study used a balanced set of 60 AP-positive and 60 healthy periapical
  radiographs from a university clinic, with a high-quality expert reference standard
  (κ = 0.88). Models received a standardized prompt and returned binary present/absent
  responses — no fine-tuning, no training images. Gemini 3.0 Pro achieved 71.67% accuracy;
  GPT-5.2 Thinking was intermediate; Claude 4.5 Sonnet was significantly worse (Bonferroni-adjusted
  p = 0.0015). Sensitivity and specificity data reveal the characteristic failure
  mode: general-purpose LLMs tend to over-call pathology, inflating false positives.
  The zero-shot framing is both the study''s strength and its ceiling — it tests what
  a clinician actually gets when they upload an image to a consumer AI tool, not a
  tuned diagnostic system. The takeaway is sobering: even the best current LLM gets
  roughly one in three periapical radiographs wrong, which is not acceptable for a
  screening or diagnostic role without human oversight.'
