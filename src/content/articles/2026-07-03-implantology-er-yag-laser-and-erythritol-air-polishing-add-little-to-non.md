summaryDeep: 'Forty-seven patients with peri-implantitis were randomised to three
  parallel arms: non-surgical submarginal instrumentation with titanium curettes alone
  (control), plus erythritol air-polishing (test 1), or plus Er:YAG laser (test 2).
  At six months, mean PPD reductions of 1–1.4 mm were observed in all groups with
  no intergroup differences, and BoP reductions of 24–36% were similarly equivalent.
  The therapeutic endpoint — PPD ≤5 mm, BoP ≤1 site, no suppuration — was achieved
  in only 12–18% of implants regardless of adjunct. Multilevel logistic modelling
  identified ≥2 mm vestibular keratinized mucosa and Er:YAG use as associated with
  higher success odds, but the pilot sample size limits confidence in these subgroup
  signals. The headline finding is sobering: non-surgical peri-implantitis treatment,
  with or without physical adjuncts, achieves disease resolution in fewer than one
  in five implants. This aligns with the broader literature and reinforces that surgical
  intervention is often necessary for moderate-to-severe peri-implantitis.'
