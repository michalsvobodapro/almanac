summaryDeep: 'Every year the GDC publishes its Registration Statistical Report, and
  every year the dental technician headcount quietly declines — but this cycle the
  numbers are stark enough that even official commentary can no longer sidestep them.
  The shortage isn''t new, but the compounding effect of an ageing technician workforce,
  limited training capacity, and poor pay relative to clinical roles means the gap
  is widening faster than it''s being filled. For a dentistry student in Prague, this
  is worth understanding beyond the UK context: Czech and broader EU lab networks
  face analogous pressures as digital workflows shift some work in-house but don''t
  eliminate the need for skilled human fabrication. Clinically, the downstream effects
  are real — extended lead times for crowns, bridges, and removable prosthetics, pressure
  to accept inferior work, and a growing temptation to over-rely on chairside CAD/CAM
  regardless of indication. The piece argues the profession needs to interrogate why
  technicians are leaving and why students aren''t entering, not just count the vacancies.
  That''s the right frame, and it applies well beyond the GDC register.'
