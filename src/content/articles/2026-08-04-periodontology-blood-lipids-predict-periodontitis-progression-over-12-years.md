summaryDeep: 'The Parogene cohort has already contributed substantially to understanding
  the periodontitis–systemic disease interface; this lipidomics analysis adds a new
  dimension. Ceramides — bioactive sphingolipids with pro-inflammatory and pro-apoptotic
  roles — were elevated relative to phospholipids in participants with more severe
  periodontitis, and baseline ceramide profiles predicted who progressed over the
  12-year window. The biological plausibility is strong: ceramides are known mediators
  of insulin resistance, cardiovascular risk, and inflammatory signalling, all of
  which intersect with periodontal pathobiology. The clinical translation is not yet
  actionable — lipidomic profiling is not a chairside test — but the findings reinforce
  the case for treating periodontitis as a systemic metabolic condition rather than
  a localised infection. For students, this is a high-quality cohort study from a
  well-characterised population that illustrates how systemic biomarkers might eventually
  stratify periodontal risk beyond conventional clinical indices.'
