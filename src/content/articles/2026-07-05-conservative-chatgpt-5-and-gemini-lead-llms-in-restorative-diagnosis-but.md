summaryDeep: 'LLMs are already being used by students and clinicians as diagnostic
  aids, making benchmarking against specialist consensus urgent. This study used a
  Lawshe-validated set of 20 restorative dentistry cases, posed identically to five
  current-generation LLMs, with responses scored by 42 restorative specialists on
  a Likert scale. Gemini 3 Flash and ChatGPT-5 led on total median scores, with statistically
  significant between-model differences on 75% of questions (Kruskal-Wallis, p<0.001).
  However, the reference verification component — where article authors checked cited
  sources — revealed hallucinated or inaccurate references across all models. This
  is the study''s most important finding for clinical practice: an LLM that sounds
  authoritative but cites non-existent evidence is potentially more dangerous than
  no AI at all. For a Prague student using AI for case prep: treat LLM diagnostic
  suggestions as a starting framework, never as a citable source, and always verify
  against primary literature.'
