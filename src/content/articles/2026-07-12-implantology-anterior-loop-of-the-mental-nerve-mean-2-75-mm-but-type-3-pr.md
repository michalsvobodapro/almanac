  segment where the neurovascular bundle courses anteriorly and inferiorly to the
  mental foramen (MF) before looping back to exit through it. This structure is a
  critical consideration in determining optimal implant placement in the mandibular
  premolar region. The MF itself is an essential anatomical landmark, particularly
  for effective local anesthesia and for minimizing the risk of iatrogenic injury
  during surgical procedures in the premolar area. Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT)
  enables enhanced visualization of anatomical landmarks, offering precise localization,
  delineation, and assessment of their spatial relationships. Accordingly, CBCT is
  recommended when detailed anatomical information is required, such as in the identification
  and evaluation of the AnL. This study was designed to evaluate the vertical and
  horizontal positioning of the MF and to assess the visibility, classification, and

summaryDeep: 'The anterior loop of the mental nerve — the segment that courses anteriorly
  before looping back to exit the mental foramen — is a critical safety zone for implant
  placement in the mandibular premolar region. This study analysed 260 CBCT scans
  across dentate and edentulous patients of varying ages, classifying the anterior
  loop into three types and measuring its length and the mental foramen''s vertical
  and horizontal position. Type 3 (a clearly visible, well-defined loop) was present
  in 71.5% of cases, with a mean loop length of 2.75 mm ranging up to several millimetres.
  The clinical safety margin conventionally recommended (2 mm anterior to the mental
  foramen) may be insufficient in a significant proportion of patients. Age, sex,
  and dental status associations were also analysed. For a student planning implants
  in the premolar region, the takeaway is unambiguous: CBCT identification of the
  anterior loop is not a luxury — it is the standard of care. Panoramic radiographs
  routinely underestimate or miss the loop entirely.'
