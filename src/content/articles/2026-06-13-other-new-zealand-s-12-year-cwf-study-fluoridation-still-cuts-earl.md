summaryDeep: 'Twelve years of national data on 4-year-old children in New Zealand
  provide contemporary evidence that community water fluoridation (CWF) reduces early
  childhood caries (ECC) at the population level. But the study''s most important
  finding is the ethnic effect modification: the benefit of CWF is not distributed
  equally across demographic groups, raising questions about whether fluoridation
  alone is sufficient as an equity tool. This sits in productive tension with the
  Nordic data published in the same cycle, which questions CWF''s marginal value in
  high-income settings — New Zealand''s result suggests context, baseline risk, and
  population heterogeneity all determine whether CWF moves the needle. For students
  thinking about preventive dentistry and public health, the takeaway is that no single
  intervention is universally sufficient: fluoride toothpaste, diet, access to care,
  and water fluoridation interact differently across populations.'
