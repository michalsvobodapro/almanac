excerpt: Objective To summarize time-related biomarker changes in orthodontically-induced
  root resorption (OIRR), classify biomarkers by function, and examine trajectories
  across OIRR stages. Materials and methods PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of
  Science, CENTRAL, CINAHL and grey literature, were searched from inception to October
  1, 2025. Reference lists were manually searched. The risk of bias was assessed using
  the Cochrane RoB 2 for RCTs and ROBINS-I for non-RCTs. The certainty of evidence
  was evaluated via GRADE. Because of heterogeneity, findings were synthesized narratively.
  Results From 11,434 records, 14 studies (225 participants) were included. Gingival
  crevicular fluid, saliva and serum were analyzed, mainly using enzyme-linked immunosorbent
  assay. RoB was 'moderate'- 'serious' (non-RCTs) or 'some concerns' (RCTs). Studies
  reported a rise of pro-inflammatory cytokines right after force application. Subsequent
  force application phases showed signals of osteoclastic activity and regulation,
  followed by detection of root hard tissue degradation products that correlated with
  OIRR volume. Higher baseline salivary secretory IgA and anti-dentin humoral responses
  and lower baseline mineralized-tissue regulators were associated with greater OIRR
  severity. Conclusion Evidence suggests time-related changes involving early inflammatory
  activation and later matrix degradation, which may be shaped by interaction between
  orthodontic force and patients' biological responses. Standard

summary: A systematic review with GRADE assessment maps how salivary, GCF, and serum
  biomarkers change across the stages of orthodontically induced root resorption (OIRR)

summaryDeep: 'Orthodontically induced root resorption is one of the field''s most
  feared complications, yet it is currently diagnosed only after the fact — on radiographs,
  when damage is already done. This systematic review asks whether biomarkers could
  enable earlier detection or risk stratification. Fourteen studies (225 participants)
  using ELISA on GCF, saliva, and serum reveal a coherent temporal cascade: early
  inflammatory activation (cytokines), then osteoclastic signalling, then collagen/mineral
  degradation products that actually correlate with OIRR volume on CBCT. The GRADE
  certainty is moderate, limited by small samples and methodological heterogeneity.
  The clinically exciting finding is the baseline predictor data: patients with higher
  salivary secretory IgA and lower mineralized-tissue regulators before treatment
  show greater OIRR severity. If validated prospectively, a simple pre-treatment saliva
  test could flag high-risk patients for modified force protocols or closer radiographic
  monitoring. Not ready for chairside use yet, but the biological framework is now
  solid enough to design the validation trials.'
