summaryDeep: Using five years of medical claims data from 1,997 individuals, this
  cohort study quantified the longitudinal relationship between periodontitis and
  stroke — not just as a binary outcome but as a driver of healthcare resource use.
  Patients with periodontitis had higher rates of stroke onset and, when they did
  suffer a stroke, incurred greater medical costs and spent longer in hospital. The
  claims-based design captures what happens in actual healthcare systems rather than
  in trial populations, which strengthens external validity. For the dental chair,
  this reinforces the case for framing periodontal treatment as systemic risk reduction,
  not just gum care — an argument that resonates with patients and increasingly with
  insurers. It also adds economic weight to the periodontitis–systemic disease link
  that goes beyond the familiar cardiovascular mortality associations.
