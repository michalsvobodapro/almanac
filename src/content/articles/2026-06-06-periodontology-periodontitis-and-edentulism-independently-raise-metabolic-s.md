  and edentulism are associated with significantly elevated risk of metabolic syndrome
  and its components — including obesity and dyslipidemia — independent of confounders.
  The evidence base is now strong enough to treat periodontal status as a metabolic

summaryDeep: 'This systematic review, registered on PROSPERO, pooled data across studies
  comparing metabolic syndrome (MetS) prevalence and its individual components — central
  obesity, dyslipidemia, hypertension, and dysglycemia — between individuals with
  and without periodontitis or edentulism. The meta-analyses confirm a statistically
  significant association between periodontal disease and MetS as a composite outcome,
  with edentulism showing an even stronger signal, likely reflecting cumulative disease
  burden over time. Dyslipidemia and obesity emerged as the components most consistently
  linked to periodontal status. Mechanistically, the chronic low-grade systemic inflammation
  driven by periodontal pathogens is the leading explanatory pathway. Clinically,
  this means periodontal examination findings should be communicated to the patient''s
  physician — and vice versa. For students entering practice, the implication is structural:
  perio isn''t a silo. The mouth is a metabolic window, and this meta-analysis gives
  you the numbers to back that up in an interdisciplinary referral letter.'
