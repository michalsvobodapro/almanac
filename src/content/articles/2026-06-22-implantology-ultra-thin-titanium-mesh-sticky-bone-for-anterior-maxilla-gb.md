  anterior maxilla remains a major challenge in implant dentistry because of the functional
  and esthetic demands associated with this region. Following tooth loss, rapid and
  progressive bone resorption often results in insufficient bone volume for ideal
  implant placement. Guided bone regeneration (GBR) using titanium mesh has been widely
  applied to address such defects; however, clinical evidence regarding ultra-thin
  titanium mesh remains limited. Furthermore, the potential synergistic effect of
  combining ultra-thin titanium mesh with biologically active grafts such as sticky
  bone has not been sufficiently explored. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate
  the clinical and radiographic outcomes of ultra-thin titanium mesh combined with

summaryDeep: 'The anterior maxilla is the hardest site in implant dentistry: thin
  buccal bone, high esthetic demand, and rapid post-extraction resorption. Ultra-thin
  titanium mesh offers space maintenance with reduced soft-tissue perforation risk
  compared to standard mesh, and sticky bone (xenograft mixed with A-PRF) provides
  both a scaffold and growth factor delivery. This prospective study enrolled eight
  patients with horizontal or combined defects, placed implants simultaneously, and
  covered with collagen membrane over the mesh. CBCT-measured bone gain and implant
  stability were reported as favorable, with no major complications noted. The evidence
  grade is low by design: n=8, no control group, short follow-up, single surgeon.
  It joins a growing body of small GBR cohort studies — the technique is not new,
  but the ultra-thin mesh variant and the sticky bone combination are worth tracking
  as larger series accumulate. For a student, the conceptual value is understanding
  the GBR armamentarium for challenging anterior sites.'
