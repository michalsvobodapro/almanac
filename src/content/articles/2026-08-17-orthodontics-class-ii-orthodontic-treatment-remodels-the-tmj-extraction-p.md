summaryDeep: Eighty-two adult patients (43 female, 39 male; aged 18–35) with Class
  II Division 1 (n=54) or Division 2 (n=28) malocclusion underwent CBCT and electronic
  articulator assessment before and after comprehensive orthodontic treatment. Extraction
  protocols within Division 1 included no extraction (n=20), single-arch (n=13), and
  double-arch (n=21); all Division 2 cases were treated without extraction. Post-treatment
  CBCT showed significant changes in coronal and sagittal joint spaces, condylar height,
  and glenoid fossa morphology, with the double-arch extraction group showing the
  most pronounced condylar displacement. Electronic articulator data revealed corresponding
  alterations in condylar movement trajectories and protrusive condylar inclination.
  The retrospective design and absence of a non-treatment control group are limitations,
  and the study cannot determine whether these morphological changes are adaptive
  or potentially harmful long-term. The finding that extraction protocol — not just
  malocclusion type — influences TMJ remodelling is clinically relevant for treatment
  planning in adult Class II cases, where condylar position is already a consideration.
