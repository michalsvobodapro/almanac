summaryDeep: 'Grade C periodontitis — the aggressive, rapidly progressing form associated
  with A. actinomycetemcomitans — is clinically distinct, but its molecular basis
  has been incompletely characterised. This study exposed primary gingival fibroblasts
  from Grade C patients and healthy controls to A. actinomycetemcomitans protein extract
  and measured inflammasome-related gene expression (NLRP3, AIM2, IL-1β, TNF-α, CASP1,
  IL-10, TLR2, TLR4) alongside DNA methylation and hydroxymethylation of NLRP3 and
  IL-1β promoters. Grade C fibroblasts showed higher baseline AIM2 expression and
  a blunted transcriptional response to bacterial stimulation — consistent with epigenetic
  silencing of inflammatory genes that paradoxically sustains chronic low-grade inflammation.
  This is a mechanistic study, not a clinical trial, but it has diagnostic implications:
  epigenetic biomarkers in gingival tissue could eventually stratify periodontitis
  risk more precisely than clinical parameters alone. For students, it reinforces
  why Grade C is treated more aggressively and why systemic adjuncts (antibiotics,
  host modulation) are part of the evidence-based protocol.'
