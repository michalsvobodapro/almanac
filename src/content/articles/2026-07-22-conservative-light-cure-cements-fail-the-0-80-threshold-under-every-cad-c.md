summaryDeep: 'Specimens (n=10 per group) were polymerized under resin-matrix ceramic
  (Cerasmart 270) or leucite-reinforced glass ceramic (Initial LRF) at 1 mm and 2
  mm thickness, with microhardness measured at 1 h, 24 h, 7 days, and 30 days. The
  0.80 bottom/top ratio is the standard proxy for clinically adequate through-cure.
  Light-cured cement failed this threshold at every time point under both ceramic
  types and both thicknesses — a clear and actionable finding. Dual-cured and flowable
  composite cements performed well throughout, and resin-matrix ceramics transmitted
  more light than leucite-reinforced glass ceramics, yielding higher hardness values
  overall. The time-dependent hardness increase in dual-cured and self-adhesive cements
  confirms that final properties are not reached at 24 h, which has implications for
  early loading protocols. For students: avoid light-cure-only cements under any indirect
  CAD/CAM restoration thicker than 1 mm.'
