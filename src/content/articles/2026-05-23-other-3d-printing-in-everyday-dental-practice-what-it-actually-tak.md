summary: 3D printing has moved from lab curiosity to chairside tool, but adoption
  in general practice remains patchy. This BDJ piece cuts through the hype to address
  workflow integration, material choices, and the regulatory baseline clinicians need
  before printing anything that goes in a patient's mouth. If your clinic doesn't
  have a printer yet, it's worth knowing what the realistic entry point looks like.
summaryDeep: 'The piece tackles the practical gap between 3D printing''s obvious potential
  and its uneven uptake in everyday dental settings. It covers the core workflow:
  intraoral scan or CBCT data → slicing software → printer selection → post-processing
  → regulatory compliance. Material categories — resins for surgical guides, splints,
  and provisional crowns versus harder polymers for longer-term restorations — are
  distinguished with clinical context rather than brand promotion. Critically, the
  article flags that EU MDR classification of in-house printed devices is still a
  grey area that many practices are navigating incorrectly, a point directly relevant
  to Czech clinics operating under the same regulatory framework. For a dental student
  entering practice, understanding where printing fits (and where it doesn''t yet)
  is increasingly a baseline competency, not a niche interest.'
