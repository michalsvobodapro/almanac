summaryDeep: Starting with 511 implants in 303 patients at 10 years, the Bernese cohort
  retained 252 implants in 159 patients at the 25-year mark — an attrition rate that
  itself reflects real-world longevity challenges. Implant failure rose from 1.2%
  at 10 years to 6.0% at 25 years, with loss of osseointegration accounting for approximately
  60% of those failures. Peri-implantitis prevalence increased over the observation
  window, yet peri-implant mucositis — the reversible precursor — actually decreased,
  possibly reflecting improved maintenance protocols in long-term recall patients.
  The study used calibrated examiners and standardized radiographic assessments throughout,
  lending the data unusual methodological consistency. For clinicians, the key message
  is that implant survival above 90% at a quarter-century is achievable with tissue-level
  designs, but the trajectory of peri-implantitis means that patients placed today
  need a lifetime maintenance commitment, not a 5-year one. No single RCT can replicate
  what 25 years of prospective follow-up delivers.
