summaryDeep: Elastomeric chains are ubiquitous in fixed orthodontics for space closure
  and tooth alignment, yet the clinical choice between thermoplastic and thermoset
  formulations has lacked robust intraoral evidence. This split-mouth RCT had 20 patients
  in the retention phase wear Hawley retainers carrying four chain segments simultaneously
  — grey thermoplastic, transparent thermoplastic, grey thermoset, and transparent
  thermoset — all stretched to a standardised 20 mm. After 30 days, thermoset chains
  retained significantly higher forces at the activation distance and showed less
  permanent deformation, meaning they deliver more consistent biomechanical loading
  throughout the clinical interval. Thermoplastics started stronger but degraded faster,
  a pattern that could translate to unpredictable tooth movement if chain replacement
  intervals are not shortened. Transparent elastomers showed greater colour change
  (ΔE) than grey, though all values remained within clinically acceptable limits.
  The sample size is modest and the retention-phase setting may not fully replicate
  active treatment loading, but the directional finding is clear and actionable.
