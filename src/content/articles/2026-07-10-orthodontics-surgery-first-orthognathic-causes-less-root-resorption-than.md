summaryDeep: 'Surgery-first orthognathic (SFA) eliminates presurgical orthodontic
  decompensation, shortening total treatment time — but its effects on root and bone
  were undercharacterized. This retrospective CBCT study segmented incisor roots into
  eight regions and measured volume and length changes from pre-treatment through
  debonding. Both SFA and conventional orthognathic surgery (COS) produced measurable
  root resorption and alveolar bone height reduction at mandibular incisors. SFA patients
  showed significantly less resorption and bone loss overall. Critically, incisor
  inclination change was positively correlated with root resorption in SFA patients
  and with alveolar bone loss in COS patients — meaning the biomechanical driver is
  the same regardless of sequence, but COS patients accumulate more total tooth movement
  and thus more damage. Initial buccal alveolar bone height was a negative predictor
  of bone reduction post-treatment. Clinically: SFA''s shorter orthodontic phase appears
  to be genuinely protective for root and bone, not just faster.'
