summaryDeep: 'Gingival recession co-occurring with non-carious cervical lesions (NCCLs)
  is a common clinical dilemma: treat the recession surgically, fill the NCCL restoratively,
  or do both? This meta-analysis pooled four RCTs — all with ≥1-year follow-up and
  assessed with RoB 2.0 — and found that nine out of ten periodontal parameters showed
  no significant difference between the combined and periodontal-only arms. The one
  exception was a slight probing depth increase in the combined therapy group, which
  is a flag rather than a benefit. Root coverage outcomes and esthetic scores were
  also equivalent. The practical implication is that routinely adding a cervical restoration
  to a root-coverage procedure does not improve the periodontal result, and may introduce
  a marginal risk. Clinicians should individualize: if the NCCL is symptomatic or
  structurally significant, restore it — but don''t do it expecting it to enhance
  the surgical outcome. The four-study pool is small, but the consistency across trials
  is reassuring.'
