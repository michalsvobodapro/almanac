summaryDeep: 'Trained immunity — the epigenetic reprogramming of innate immune cells
  to mount exaggerated responses to future stimuli — is an emerging framework for
  understanding how chronic infections cause distant organ damage. This study applies
  that framework to P. gingivalis, showing in a bone-marrow transplant mouse model
  that infection epigenetically reprograms myeloid progenitors via the glycolysis–SIRT1
  axis, producing a hyperinflammatory phenotype that aggravates experimental colitis.
  The bone-marrow transplant design is methodologically important: it isolates the
  trained immunity mechanism from direct bacterial translocation, demonstrating that
  the immune reprogramming itself — not just live bacteria — is pathogenic at distant
  sites. Paired with the oral–atrial axis paper above, today''s digest presents two
  distinct systemic pathways for P. gingivalis — direct translocation and immune reprogramming
  — operating in parallel. Human validation is the critical next step, but the mechanistic
  specificity here is notable.'
