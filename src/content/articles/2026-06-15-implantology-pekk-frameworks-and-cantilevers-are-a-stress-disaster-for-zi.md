summaryDeep: A CT-derived edentulous maxilla model was used to build 12 FEA configurations
  combining titanium or zirconia implants with Co-Cr, PEKK, or zirconia frameworks,
  with and without cantilevers. A 200 N oblique load at 45° was applied at the first
  molar fossa — a realistic masticatory scenario. PEKK's lower elastic modulus, often
  marketed as a stress-absorbing advantage, instead caused greater prosthesis displacement
  and stress concentration at the implant-abutment interface. Zirconia implants under
  cantilevered PEKK reached 378 MPa, approaching the material's flexural strength
  limits. Co-Cr and zirconia frameworks performed comparably and significantly better.
  The cantilever effect was universal and additive regardless of framework choice.
  FEA cannot capture fatigue, bone remodeling, or clinical variability, so these are
  directional findings rather than definitive contraindications. Nevertheless, the
  PEKK + cantilever + zirconia implant combination warrants caution until clinical
  long-term data exist.
