excerpt: 'Background Surgical extraction of mandibular third molars is a common procedure
  performed in clinical practice yet it commonly yields postoperative pain, facial
  swelling, and trismus. While multiple-suture primary closure has traditionally been
  used, suture-less wound closure has emerged as an alternative. Aim This systematic
  review and meta-analysis aimed to compare the effect of suture-less vs. multiple-suture
  wound closure on early postoperative pain, facial swelling, and trismus after mandibular
  third molar surgery. Material and methods A comprehensive literature search was
  conducted in several databases for articles from January 2000 to December 2025.
  Only randomized controlled trials comparing suture-less and multiple-suture closure
  after surgical extraction of mandibular third molars were included. Meta-analyses
  were performed in RevMan 5.4 using a random-effects model due to substantial heterogeneity
  ( I 2 &amp;gt; 50%). Rob 2 tool was used for quality assessment of the included
  studies. Results Ten RCTs were included. Meta-analysis showed that pain at 48 h
  was lower in the suture-less group (mean difference: −0.46; 95% CI: −3.69, 2.77;
  p = 0.33), but the difference was not statistically significant. Similarly, facial
  swelling at 48 h was lower in the suture-less group (mean difference: −0.56; 95%
  CI: −1.35, 0.22; p = 0.159), and trismus at 7 days was lower (mean difference: −0.38;
  95% CI: −1.78, 1.22; p = 0.09), though neither reached conventional significance.

summaryDeep: 'Third molar surgery is among the most common procedures in oral surgery,
  and the debate over wound closure technique has persisted for decades. This meta-analysis
  is the most comprehensive synthesis to date, pooling 10 RCTs with a random-effects
  model to account for substantial heterogeneity. The headline finding is a null result:
  suture-less closure does not produce significantly worse outcomes for pain, swelling,
  or trismus compared to multiple-suture primary closure. The trend actually favours
  suture-less across all three outcomes, though the confidence intervals are wide.
  For students in Prague learning oral surgery technique, this is practically relevant:
  the evidence does not mandate primary closure, and secondary intention healing after
  third molar extraction is a legitimate, evidence-supported option. The high heterogeneity
  — driven by variation in flap design, impaction difficulty, and outcome measurement
  tools — means a definitive recommendation awaits more standardized trials, but the
  fear of leaving a wound open is not evidence-based.'
