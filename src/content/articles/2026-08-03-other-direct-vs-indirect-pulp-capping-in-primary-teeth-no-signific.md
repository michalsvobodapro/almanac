summaryDeep: 'The debate over whether to cap directly or indirectly in primary teeth
  has lacked high-quality prospective data. This single-centre study from Bangladesh
  followed 60 primary molars across 42 children for 12 months with evaluations at
  3, 6, and 12 months under standardised aseptic conditions in a single visit. Neither
  group showed statistically significant superiority at any timepoint, though IPC
  trended slightly higher throughout. The 80–83% success range is consistent with
  the broader literature on vital pulp therapy in primaries. Limitations include small
  sample size, single centre, and 12-month follow-up only — primary molars can fail
  radiographically after 18–24 months. Still, for a student learning to triage carious
  exposures in paediatric patients, the practical message is reassuring: a well-executed
  IPC in a tooth without signs of irreversible pulpitis is a defensible, evidence-aligned
  choice.'
