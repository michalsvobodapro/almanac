summaryDeep: F. nucleatum has attracted intense oncology interest since its consistent
  enrichment in colorectal tumour tissue was first reported. This review critically
  evaluates the accumulating evidence, distinguishing between studies showing pro-tumorigenic
  mechanisms (immune evasion, epithelial invasion, chemotherapy resistance) and emerging
  data suggesting context-dependent or even tumour-suppressive effects in some cancer
  types. The dual-face framing is not a rehabilitation of F. nucleatum — the weight
  of evidence still positions it as a pathobiont — but it cautions against oversimplified
  causal narratives that could drive premature clinical interventions (e.g., aggressive
  periodontal treatment marketed as cancer prevention). For a periodontics-interested
  student, the key message is that the perio-systemic link is real and mechanistically
  grounded, but the clinical translation requires randomised evidence that does not
  yet exist at scale.
