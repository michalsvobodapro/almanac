summaryDeep: 'Robotic-assisted implant surgery is advancing rapidly, and this study
  pushes the envelope by combining an autonomous robot with osseodensification (compaction
  rather than cutting) for simultaneous transcrestal sinus elevation and implant placement.
  The series enrolled patients with 4–8 mm residual bone height at a single Chinese
  university hospital between January and November 2024. Postoperative CBCT confirmed
  high placement accuracy, AI segmentation quantified sinus augmentation volumes at
  surgery and 6 months, and force feedback data were collected for flat versus sloped
  sinus floor morphologies. Intraoperative Schneiderian membrane perforation rates
  were low. The critical limitations are the absence of a control arm (conventional
  osteotome technique or piezoelectric), a small and unspecified sample size, and
  follow-up that captures only early healing. The learning curve analysis is a useful
  addition. For a student in Prague: robotic sinus surgery is not available in most
  European centres and the evidence base is at case-series level — file under ''watch
  this space'' rather than ''change practice.'''
