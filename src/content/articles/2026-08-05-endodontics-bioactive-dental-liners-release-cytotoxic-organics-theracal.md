summaryDeep: Six liner materials — an experimental liner, TheraCal LC, Dycal, Lime-Lite
  Enhanced, Fuji Lining LC, and Medcem MTA — were incubated for 72 hours in methanol
  or water, and eluates analyzed by GC/MS. Nine organic substances were identified
  and quantified; selected compounds were then tested individually in hDPSCs using
  WST-1 cytotoxicity assays, with EC₅₀ values derived from four-parameter logistic
  regression. The study separates the biological effects of organic leachables from
  the well-characterized ion release profile of these materials — an important distinction,
  since the mineralizing ion release is the basis of their 'bioactive' marketing.
  TheraCal LC and Dycal emerged as the most concerning for organic cytotoxicity, while
  MTA-based materials performed better. The in-vitro model using isolated compounds
  rather than whole eluates is a limitation — real-world pulp exposure involves complex
  mixtures and dentinal buffering. Still, for students placing liners in deep cavities
  approaching the pulp, this is a reminder that material selection matters beyond
  setting time and handling.
