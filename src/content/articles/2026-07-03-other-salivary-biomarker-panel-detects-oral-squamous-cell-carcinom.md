summaryDeep: 'Sixty histologically confirmed OSCC patients and 60 healthy controls
  provided unstimulated saliva samples analysed by ELISA for nine biomarkers: IL-6,
  IL-8, TNF-α, IL-1β, MMP-9, CD44, soluble CD44, CYFRA 21-1, and lactate dehydrogenase.
  All nine were significantly elevated in OSCC patients (p values not fully reported
  in the excerpt), and ROC analysis confirmed strong individual discriminatory ability,
  with multi-marker panels performing best. The case-control design is appropriate
  for biomarker discovery but cannot establish sensitivity/specificity in a real screening
  population — selection bias is a known limitation. The sample size (n=120) is modest.
  Nevertheless, the convergence of inflammatory, proteolytic, and cell-surface markers
  in a single saliva test is clinically attractive: OSCC is frequently diagnosed late,
  and a chairside salivary screen could triage patients for biopsy before visible
  lesions develop. For students in Prague, where oral cancer incidence is rising,
  this is the kind of diagnostic innovation worth tracking through to clinical validation.'
