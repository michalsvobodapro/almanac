  clinical literature. This systematic review was conducted according to PRISMA. PubMed,
  EMBASE, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library were searched. Reference lists of included
  studies and relevant reviews were also checked, but no additional eligible records
  were identified. Eligible studies evaluated adult or predominantly adult cohorts
  with anterior open bite treated with clear aligners and reported overbite change
  or dentoskeletal outcomes. Risk of bias was assessed using ROBINS-I and RoB 2. Reported

summaryDeep: 'Anterior open bite is among the most relapse-prone malocclusions, and
  the question of whether clear aligners can reliably correct it in adults has been
  contentious. This systematic review searched four major databases, applied ROBINS-I
  and RoB 2 bias tools, and performed exploratory random-effects meta-analyses where
  data allowed. The pooled overbite gain of 3.07 mm (5-study synthesis) or 2.89 mm
  (7-study sensitivity analysis) is clinically meaningful, but the confidence intervals
  are wide and the underlying studies are overwhelmingly retrospective and nonrandomized.
  Dentoskeletal changes were mapped descriptively — molar intrusion and incisor extrusion
  appear to be the dominant mechanisms — but the review could not reliably separate
  true skeletal from dentoalveolar effects. GRADE certainty is low to very low across
  outcomes. The practical upshot: aligners are a viable tool for mild-to-moderate
  adult open bite, but patient selection criteria and long-term stability remain poorly
  defined. An RCT comparing aligners to fixed appliances for this indication is overdue.'
