summaryDeep: Orthodontic emergencies — unscheduled visits for appliance-related problems
  — are a significant burden on practice efficiency and patient experience, yet their
  determinants have been poorly characterized. This 10-year retrospective cohort from
  a single private clinic is the largest single-center analysis of its kind, covering
  373 patients with a median age of 14 years. The 28.4% emergency rate is higher than
  many clinicians might expect, and the concentration in the first year of treatment
  and in the mandible has practical scheduling implications. Debonded brackets dominate
  (44.1% of events), followed by ligation system malfunctions and archwire complications.
  The multivariable finding that male sex and hypodivergent (deep bite) skeletal pattern
  predict more emergencies is clinically actionable — these patients may benefit from
  more frequent early review appointments and reinforced bracket bonding protocols.
  The retrospective single-center design limits generalizability, but the sample size
  and decade-long follow-up give the findings reasonable weight.
