summary: A high-fidelity 3D finite element model of the maxillary anterior region
  shows that shield thickness in the socket shield technique directly governs stress
  distribution in cortical bone and initial implant stability under immediate loading.
  Too thin concentrates dangerous stress; too thick compromises the biological space.
  The study gives surgeons a biomechanical rationale for thickness selection rather
  than relying on feel.
summaryDeep: The socket shield technique is gaining traction for immediate implant
  placement in the esthetic zone, but shield thickness has been chosen empirically.
  This finite element analysis builds a realistic maxillary model — cortical bone,
  cancellous bone, periodontal ligament, shield fragment, and implant — and simulates
  immediate occlusal loading across a range of shield thicknesses. Thinner shields
  concentrate von Mises stress at the crestal cortical bone and the implant neck,
  raising fracture and marginal bone loss risk. Thicker shields redistribute stress
  more favourably but reduce the biologic width available for bundle bone preservation.
  The model identifies an intermediate thickness range that optimises both stability
  and stress dissipation. While FEA cannot replace clinical trials, these data provide
  the first quantitative framework for intraoperative decision-making and should inform
  future prospective studies on shield dimensioning.
