summaryDeep: 'Seventy patients followed for more than 30 years under structured supportive
  periodontal care at a university clinic provide a rare window into what actually
  drives tooth loss over a clinical lifetime. The key finding: residual disease severity
  at the point SPC begins — not just baseline diagnosis — is the strongest predictor
  of eventual tooth loss. This reframes the clinical conversation: it is not enough
  to complete active therapy; what matters is how well disease is controlled before
  handing the patient to maintenance. The data also implicitly validate the SPC model
  itself — patients who stayed in structured care retained most of their dentition
  even across three decades. For a student, the lesson is concrete: the quality of
  active treatment outcome, not just compliance with recall, determines prognosis.
  This cohort-level evidence should inform how you set expectations and document residual
  probing depths at SPC entry.'
