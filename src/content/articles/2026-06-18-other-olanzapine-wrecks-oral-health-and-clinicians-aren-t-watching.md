excerpt: Olanzapine is a commonly used second-generation antipsychotic. However, evidence
  on its oral health effects remains scattered, and clinical awareness is limited.
  This review systematically identifies the spectrum and epidemiological characteristics
  of olanzapine-related oral health impairments. It also proposes a framework for
  their pathogenesis, outlines existing management strategies, and clarifies evidence
  gaps. We followed the JBI scoping review methodology and PRISMA-ScR guidelines.
  We systematically searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library
  from database inception to December 31, 2025. Original studies and reviews focusing
  on olanzapine and oral health outcomes were included. Data were extracted using
  standardized tables and synthesized through thematic induction, evidence mapping,

summaryDeep: 'Olanzapine is among the most prescribed antipsychotics globally, yet
  its oral health consequences are poorly integrated into dental practice. This JBI-methodology
  scoping review synthesized 18 studies and found a consistent, severe oral disease
  burden: xerostomia prevalence of 30–60%, periodontitis rates of 36–60%, DMFT indices
  up to 28.6 (catastrophic by any benchmark), oral candidiasis driven by bacterial-fungal
  dysbiosis, and a reporting odds ratio of 6.7 for tongue movement disorders. The
  authors propose a ''quadruple pathway synergistic amplification'' model linking
  anticholinergic effects, metabolic disruption, microbiome dysbiosis, and behavioral
  factors. The scoping design means causality cannot be established and management
  recommendations remain framework-level rather than evidence-based protocols. For
  a student in Prague, the clinical relevance is immediate: psychiatric patients are
  a significant part of any general practice population, and proactive oral health
  monitoring — including salivary flow assessment and aggressive caries prevention
  — should be standard when olanzapine is on the medication list.'
