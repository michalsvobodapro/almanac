summary: Root-filled teeth fracture not just because of lost tooth structure but because
  of how the root canal system is sealed and what goes on top. This study isolates
  the independent contributions of sealer type and coronal restoration technique to
  fatigue resistance, finding that the combination — not either variable alone — drives
  fracture risk. It's a direct argument for treating obturation and coronal seal as
  a single clinical decision.
summaryDeep: Fracture of root-filled teeth is a leading cause of extraction, yet the
  literature has long treated sealer selection and coronal restoration as separate
  decisions. This fatigue study subjects extracted teeth to cyclic loading after varying
  both the endodontic sealer (resin-based vs. bioceramic) and the restorative approach
  (direct composite vs. cusp-coverage onlay), tracking crack initiation and propagation
  to failure. Resin-based sealers combined with cusp-coverage restorations produced
  the highest fatigue cycles to failure; bioceramic sealers with direct restorations
  performed worst. The mechanism appears to involve sealer modulus of elasticity and
  how stress is distributed through the root. Clinically, this reinforces the case
  for bioceramic sealers only when paired with adequate coronal protection — and challenges
  the habit of treating obturation and restoration as independent appointments with
  independent logic.
