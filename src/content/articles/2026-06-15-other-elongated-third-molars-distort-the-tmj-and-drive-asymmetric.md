excerpt: Elongated third molars have been associated with occlusal interference, mandibular
  deviation, and temporomandibular joint (TMJ) disorders. However, their impact on
  TMJ space morphology and posterior tooth wear remains insufficiently explored. This
  study aimed to assess the relationship between unilateral third molar elongation,
  TMJ space asymmetry, and asymmetrical posterior tooth wear. Thirty patients presenting
  with unilateral third molar elongation (experimental group) and 30 controls with
  fully aligned dentition and absent third molars were included (Ethical approval
  ID:2023015). Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) was utilized to measure anterior,
  superior, and posterior TMJ spaces. Some of these CBCT scans were obtained specifically
  for this study. Some of CBCT scans were taken for therapeutic purposes. Bilateral
  differences in mean wear index (DBMWI) and the mean standard deviation (MSD) of
  posterior tooth wear were calculated using Lagan’s modified scoring system. Statistical
  analysis was conducted using SPSS 22.0. Significant intergroup differences in TMJ

summaryDeep: 'Thirty patients with unilateral third molar elongation were compared
  against 30 controls with fully aligned dentitions using CBCT-derived anterior, superior,
  and posterior TMJ space measurements and Lagan''s modified wear scoring. All three
  TMJ space asymmetry indices (ΔAS, ΔSS, ΔPS) differed significantly between groups.
  The bilateral difference in mean wear index was 0.50 vs. 0.19 (p=0.015) and the
  mean standard deviation of wear was 0.81 vs. 0.36 (p=0.001) — both clinically meaningful
  gaps. The mechanism is plausible: an elongated third molar creates an occlusal interference
  that shifts the mandible, loading one condyle asymmetrically and concentrating wear
  on the contralateral posterior teeth. The study is small and cross-sectional, so
  causality cannot be established, but the effect sizes are large enough to warrant
  clinical attention. For students managing third molar decisions, this adds a TMJ
  and wear argument to the extraction conversation beyond pericoronitis and caries
  risk.'
