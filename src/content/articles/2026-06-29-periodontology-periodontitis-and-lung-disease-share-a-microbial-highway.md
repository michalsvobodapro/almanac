summary: A mechanistic review maps the biological pathways connecting poor oral hygiene
  and periodontitis to pneumonia, COPD, COVID-19, and asthma. Oral microbiota reach
  the lower respiratory tract via microaspiration, and the lung's own microbiome is
  spatially varied and non-sterile. The review reframes oral health as a modifiable
  risk factor for respiratory disease — relevant to every ICU and care-home patient.
summaryDeep: 'The lung is not sterile: it harbours a distinct microbial ecosystem
  shaped partly by microaspiration from the oropharynx. This review details how periodontitis-associated
  dysbiosis — dominated by Porphyromonas gingivalis, Fusobacterium nucleatum, and
  Treponema denticola — seeds the lower respiratory tract and amplifies local inflammatory
  responses. The pathways are distinct for each condition: aspiration pneumonia involves
  direct bacterial translocation; COPD involves chronic low-grade aspiration and neutrophil
  priming; COVID-19 severity correlates with periodontal bacterial co-detection in
  bronchoalveolar lavage. The review is narrative rather than systematic, so effect
  size estimates are not provided, but the mechanistic framework is well-supported.
  For clinical practice, the implication is that oral hygiene interventions in hospitalised
  or ventilated patients are not cosmetic — they are infection control. Dental students
  rotating through hospital settings should be aware of this evidence base when advocating
  for oral care protocols.'
