  resins. Although ISO 10993-5 and ISO 10993-12 provide guidance for in vitro cytotoxicity

  may hinder comparability across studies. Objective To map how in vitro cytotoxicity

  orthodontic aligners and retainers found that only 68% explicitly referenced ISO

summaryDeep: 'Three-dimensional printing is transforming aligner and retainer fabrication,
  but the photopolymer resins used raise legitimate biocompatibility questions — residual
  monomers, photoinitiators, and leachables vary by material and curing protocol.
  This PRISMA-ScR scoping review mapped 25 studies published 2020–2025. Most used
  extract-based testing only (60%), with fewer employing direct contact or combined
  approaches. ISO 10993-5 and/or 10993-12 compliance was explicitly stated in only
  17 of 25 studies, and even among those, execution varied substantially in surface-area-to-volume
  ratios, extraction vehicles, temperatures, and cell exposure durations. No two studies
  were directly comparable. For a student in Prague, this matters practically: 3D-printed
  appliances are already in widespread clinical use across EU orthodontic practices,
  yet the regulatory evidence base for their biocompatibility is fragmented. The review
  calls for a consensus testing protocol — a necessary precondition for meaningful
  EU MDR-aligned safety data.'
