summary: Toregem BioPharma has received PMDA clearance to proceed with a Phase IIa
  trial of its tooth-regeneration drug in 24 children with severe congenital tooth
  agenesis. This is the first time a pharmacological tooth-regrowth agent has reached
  a controlled paediatric efficacy trial. If it works, the implications for implantology
  and paediatric dentistry are seismic.
summaryDeep: 'Toregem BioPharma''s experimental molecule — a USAG-1 (uterine sensitization-associated
  gene-1) inhibitor that suppresses a brake on tooth-bud development — has now satisfied
  Japan''s PMDA with enough Phase I safety data to move into a Phase IIa efficacy
  study. The 24 participants all have severe congenital hypodontia, the population
  with the clearest unmet need and the least confounding from adult dental history.
  The trial design targets children precisely because the tooth-bud window is still
  open; adult application remains a separate and far harder problem. Clinically, this
  does not change what you do Monday — implants and prosthetics remain the standard
  of care for agenesis — but it is the most credible proof-of-concept moment the tooth-regeneration
  field has ever produced. Watch the primary endpoint (eruption of a new tooth confirmed
  radiographically) and the timeline: results are expected within two to three years.
  The companion review piece published the same day provides useful background on
  the broader regeneration landscape.'
