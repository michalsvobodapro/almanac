summaryDeep: 'CBCT has become the standard advanced imaging modality in dentistry,
  but it has fundamental limitations: it cannot differentiate tissue composition,
  struggles with metal artefacts, and provides no soft-tissue contrast. Dual-energy
  CT addresses these gaps by acquiring images at two different X-ray energies and
  using the differential attenuation to decompose materials — identifying gout crystals
  in TMJ, characterising jaw lesions, and producing virtual monoenergetic images that
  dramatically reduce metal artefact. This BDJ review surveys current evidence for
  DECT in oral and maxillofacial applications, finding credible use cases in TMJ pathology,
  salivary gland calculi, and pre-surgical implant planning in heavily restored patients.
  The technology remains hospital-based and expensive, but the review argues that
  miniaturisation and cost reduction trajectories suggest dental-specific DECT units
  within the decade. For students, this is a technology to understand conceptually
  now — it will be in the differential for complex imaging referrals within your career.'
