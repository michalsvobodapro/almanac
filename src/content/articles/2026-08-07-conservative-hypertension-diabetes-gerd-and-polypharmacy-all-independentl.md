summaryDeep: 'Caries risk tools like CAMBRA focus heavily on behavioral and microbiological
  factors, but this large EHR-based cohort study makes the case that systemic disease
  and polypharmacy deserve formal inclusion in risk stratification. With ~7,000 patients
  and multivariable regression controlling for age and social determinants, the associations
  are robust. GERD and xerogenic medications are particularly actionable: both are
  common, both are modifiable or manageable, and both are routinely documented in
  medical records that dentists rarely see. The study''s retrospective design limits
  causal inference, and the university clinic setting may not generalize to community
  practice. Nevertheless, the practical implication is clear — a medically integrated
  caries risk framework would catch high-risk patients that behavioral screening alone
  misses. For a student in a clinic with access to medical records, this is a prompt
  to look beyond the oral cavity.'
