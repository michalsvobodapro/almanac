summaryDeep: Lateral wall perforation during implant osteotomy in narrow ridges is
  a common intraoperative complication that can compromise implant placement and require
  additional augmentation. This ex vivo paired study used 20 standardized trabecular
  bone blocks, each receiving one conventional and one OD osteotomy, with sequential
  diameter expansion until perforation. The within-specimen design controls for bone
  density variability — a methodological strength. OD burs, which compact rather than
  remove bone, significantly delayed perforation onset and dramatically reduced perforation
  incidence at the 4.0 mm step (10% vs 80%). Perforation length was also significantly
  smaller with OD. The ex vivo model cannot fully replicate clinical bone behavior,
  and the study is underpowered for subgroup analysis, but the effect size is large
  enough to be clinically meaningful. For students learning implant surgery, this
  is a clear argument for considering OD technique when ridge width is marginal.
