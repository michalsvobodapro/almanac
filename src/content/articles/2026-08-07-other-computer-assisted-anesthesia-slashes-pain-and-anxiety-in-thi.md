summaryDeep: 'Computer-assisted local anesthesia systems (CALAS) deliver slow, pressure-controlled
  injections that reduce needle-related pain and autonomic stress responses. This
  split-mouth RCT — each patient serving as their own control across two sessions
  at least three weeks apart — is well-designed to isolate the anesthesia delivery
  effect. The magnitude of the difference is striking: VAS pain scores drop by roughly
  two-thirds and anxiety scores by nearly two-thirds as well. Hemodynamic monitoring
  at eight procedural stages confirmed more stable respiratory rate, pulse, and blood
  pressure with CALAS. Postoperative satisfaction scores also favored CALAS. The n
  = 40 sample is adequate for the split-mouth design. For a dental student managing
  anxious patients in oral surgery rotations, CALAS is no longer a novelty — this
  trial makes a strong chairside case for it wherever the equipment is available.'
