excerptFull: Implant dentistry has undergone a significant transformation with the
  integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into digital workflows. AI, a branch
  of computer science that enables machines to simulate human cognitive processes,
  has demonstrated the potential to improve the accuracy, efficiency, and consistency
  of dental implant procedures. In guided implant surgery, the design of surgical
  guides traditionally requires manual segmentation of anatomical structures and alignment
  of cone-beam computed tomography and surface scan data – a process that is time-consuming
  and operator-dependent. This clinical report presents an AI-powered static computer-assisted
  implant surgery workflow for a 25-year-old man requiring a single anterior implant.
  By using deep learning architectures for automated data processing, the digital
  guide design was completed within 20 minutes. This case demonstrates the application
  of AI beyond diagnostic support, extending to automated surgical guide design for
  accurate implant placement and immediate previsualisation in the high-stakes aesthetic
  zone. While AI-enhanced workflows demonstrate a promising approach to improving
  predictability, clinicians must maintain rigorous manual verification to mitigate
  risks associated with data artefacts and algorithmic limitations.

  single maxillary anterior implant in the aesthetic zone. The guide was produced
  in 20 minutes versus the hour-plus typical of manual workflows. Immediate 3D previsualization
  allowed the patient and clinician to assess emergence profile and axis before any
  surgery. The authors are appropriately cautious: algorithmic artefacts in CBCT data
  (beam hardening, scatter near metal restorations) can propagate silently through
  automated pipelines, and the absence of manual verification checkpoints is a genuine
  risk. This is a single case report with no comparative accuracy data against manually
  designed guides, so it cannot yet change protocol. What it does do is define the
  architecture of AI-assisted implant planning that will likely become standard within
  a few years — making it essential reading for any student entering a digitally oriented
