summary: A perspective piece in IEJ proposes that trained immunity — epigenetic reprogramming
  of innate immune cells after prior stimulation — modulates pulp healing responses
  after vital pulp therapy. The framework could explain inconsistent outcomes with
  identical materials and techniques, and points toward immunomodulatory adjuncts
  as future research targets.
summaryDeep: 'This perspective argues that the pulp''s innate immune cells (macrophages,
  dendritic cells) are not immunologically naive at the time of vital pulp therapy:
  prior microbial exposure can epigenetically reprogram them into a ''trained'' state
  that either amplifies or dampens the healing cascade. This trained immunity concept,
  well-established in systemic immunology, has been largely ignored in endodontics.
  If pulp macrophage phenotype at the time of capping determines whether a tertiary
  dentin bridge forms or inflammation persists, then patient-level variables (caries
  history, systemic inflammation, prior pulp exposures) become mechanistically important
  — not just confounders. The authors stop short of clinical recommendations, and
  the piece is explicitly preliminary. Still, for a student grappling with why MTA
  caps succeed in one patient and fail in an apparently identical case, this framework
  offers a biologically coherent explanation worth tracking as the field develops.'
