summaryDeep: 'Temporomandibular disorder diagnosis currently relies on the DC/TMD
  criteria, which have high specificity but acknowledged sensitivity limitations.
  This cross-sectional study enrolled 261 patients (522 joints) and used MRI to measure
  a comprehensive set of condylar and joint-space parameters, comparing TMD patients
  against controls with multivariable generalised estimating equations to account
  for bilateral joint correlation. TMD joints showed smaller anteroposterior and mediolateral
  condylar dimensions, reduced anterior and superior joint spaces, increased posterior
  condylar position, and higher prevalence of beak-like condyle morphology — all statistically
  significant. The GEE approach is methodologically appropriate for paired-joint data.
  The clinical implication is that quantitative MRI morphometry could supplement DC/TMD
  criteria, particularly in ambiguous cases. However, the preliminary framing is warranted:
  the study is cross-sectional (causality unclear), the patient population is from
  a single centre, and MRI is not a first-line TMD diagnostic tool in most health
  systems.'
