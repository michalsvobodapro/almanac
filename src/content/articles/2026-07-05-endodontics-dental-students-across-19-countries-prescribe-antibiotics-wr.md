  This study evaluated dental students'' knowledge, antibiotic selection patterns,

summaryDeep: 'Antibiotic overprescribing and misprescribing in dentistry is a documented
  driver of resistance, and this multinational study puts numbers on the educational
  failure behind it. Using three standardised clinical scenarios — first-line choice,
  second-line when first-line fails, and the penicillin-allergy alternative — the
  survey scored responses against ESE and AAE guidelines on a 0–5 scale. Amoxicillin
  was selected as first-line by 57.6%, which is guideline-concordant, but amoxicillin-clavulanic
  acid was overused as a first-line agent (18.4%). For second-line therapy, concordance
  was lowest in Switzerland (2.15), suggesting that even high-income, well-resourced
  dental schools are not teaching this well. Clindamycin dominated penicillin-allergy
  scenarios (47.8%), which is increasingly problematic given resistance and C. difficile
  risk — current ESE guidance favours azithromycin or clarithromycin in many contexts.
  Mixed-effects modelling confirmed country as a major random effect, meaning curriculum
  design, not individual student aptitude, is the primary driver. For a Prague student:
  Czech prescribing culture and ČSK/ESE alignment should be interrogated in your own
  clinical training.'
