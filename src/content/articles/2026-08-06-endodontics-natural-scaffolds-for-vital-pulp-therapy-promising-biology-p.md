excerpt: 'Vital pulp therapy (VPT) is a modern approach to conservative treatment
  aimed at preserving and regenerating vital dental pulp. Its success largely depends
  on biomaterial scaffolds that support cell migration, differentiation, and proliferation,
  as well as angiogenesis and dentin regeneration. An electronic database search of
  PubMed, Scopus, and Google Scholar databases was conducted for publications from
  2015 to 2025 using the following key words: “vital pulp therapy,” “natural scaffolds,”
  “regene­rative endodontics,” “dental pulp regeneration,” and “pulp capping.” This
  paper reviews current research on select natural biomaterials used in VPT, including
  methacrylated gelatin, chitosan, alginate, platelet-rich fibrin, and demineralized
  dentin matrix. Their biological, mechanical, and antibacterial properties are discussed,
  alongside their potential in regenerative endodontics. Special attention is given
  to their biocompatibility, modifiability, and capacity for controlled bioactive
  agent delivery. The paper also highlights the clinical limitations of these materials,
  such as a lack of standardization, high costs, and technical challenges. Despite
  promising in vitro and in vivo results, most of these biomaterials remain in the
  preclinical research phase. Consequently, the need for additional clinical trials
  and strategies that enable their routine clinical use is emphasized.'

summary: 'This narrative review (2015–2025) evaluates gelatin methacrylate, chitosan,
  alginate, PRF, and demineralized dentin matrix as scaffolds for vital pulp therapy
  and regenerative endodontics. All show favorable biocompatibility and controlled
  bioactive delivery in vitro and in vivo. The honest conclusion: most remain preclinical,
  with no standardized clinical protocols and significant cost and technical barriers.'
summaryDeep: 'Vital pulp therapy is undergoing a biomaterials renaissance, and this
  review provides a structured map of where natural scaffold research stands. Methacrylated
  gelatin (GelMA) and chitosan lead on modifiability and controlled release; alginate
  offers easy fabrication but limited mechanical strength; PRF bridges the bench-to-chair
  gap most readily given its autologous nature and existing clinical use; demineralized
  dentin matrix is compelling for its native signaling molecules but technically demanding.
  The review is honest about the translation gap: despite promising in vitro and animal
  data, none of these materials has robust clinical trial evidence for routine VPT
  use. Lack of standardization — in scaffold preparation, application technique, and
  outcome measurement — is the primary barrier. For a student, the practical takeaway
  is that MTA and calcium silicate cements remain the evidence-based standard for
  direct pulp capping, while natural scaffolds represent the next generation to watch.
  The review also highlights cost as a real-world barrier, particularly relevant in
  Czech public dental practice.'
