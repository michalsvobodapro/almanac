summaryDeep: 'Forty-eight patients with Stage IV periodontitis — each with at least
  one non-molar tooth showing pathologic migration and a probing depth ≥7 mm — entered
  a structured four-step protocol: supra/subgingival instrumentation, regenerative
  surgery using enamel matrix derivative alone or combined with bone graft and membrane,
  orthodontic treatment initiated 8–12 months post-surgery, and tailored supportive
  periodontal care. Thirty-three patients completed the 20-year follow-up (31% dropout).
  Estimated tooth survival was 89.1% (CI: 79.4–99.9%) and complication-free probability
  86.3%. Only four teeth were extracted over two decades; complications included one
  pulp necrosis, two root fractures, and two cases of disease recurrence. Full-mouth
  bleeding scores and probing depths improved significantly from baseline to T20.
  The study''s strength is its prospective design and extraordinary follow-up length;
  its limitation is the small, highly compliant cohort treated by specialists. Still,
  for a Prague student seeing Stage IV cases in clinic, this is the most compelling
  long-term evidence yet that integrated regenerative-orthodontic care can preserve
  teeth once considered lost.'
