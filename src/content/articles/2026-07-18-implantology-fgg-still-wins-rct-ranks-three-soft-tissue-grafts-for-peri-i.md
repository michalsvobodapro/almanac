summary: A 12-month RCT on 44 implants compared free gingival graft (FGG), xenogeneic
  collagen matrix (XCM), and acellular dermal matrix (ADM), all combined with an apically

summaryDeep: 'With keratinized mucosa width under 2 mm at baseline, 44 implants were
  randomized to FGG+APF, XCM+APF, or ADM+APF and followed for 12 months. The primary
  finding is dimensional: FGG preserved buccal soft-tissue volume and thickness significantly
  better than ADM, with XCM occupying an intermediate position — a hierarchy that
  held across every time point. Critically, peri-implant clinical parameters (probing
  depth, bleeding on probing, marginal bone level) were stable and comparable across
  all three groups, meaning the grafts differ in how much tissue they keep, not in
  whether they protect implant health short-term. Patient satisfaction was high in
  all groups. The practical implication is straightforward: when long-term dimensional
  stability is the priority — think thin biotype, high esthetic demand — FGG remains
  the benchmark. XCM is a defensible morbidity-sparing alternative when some volume
  loss is acceptable. ADM''s substantial shrinkage makes it the weakest choice for
  volume-critical sites.'
