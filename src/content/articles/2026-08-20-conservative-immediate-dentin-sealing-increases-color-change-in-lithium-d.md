excerpt: 'The optical stability of thin ceramic laminate veneers may be affected by
  both the adhesive interface and the underlying substrate. This in vitro study evaluated
  the influence of immediate dentin sealing (IDS) on the color stability of lithium
  disilicate laminate veneers bonded to sound and eroded dentin after accelerated
  aging. Forty bovine dentin specimens were allocated into four groups according to
  dentin condition and adhesive protocol: sound dentin without IDS, sound dentin with
  IDS, eroded dentin without IDS, and eroded dentin with IDS. Lithium disilicate disks
  were cemented using a light-cured resin cement. Color coordinates were measured
  with a spectrophotometer before and after thermocycling, and color change was calculated
  using the CIEDE2000 formula (ΔE00). Changes in ΔL*, Δa*, and Δb* were also analyzed.
  Data were analyzed using two-way ANOVA and Tukey’s test (α = 0.05). IDS significantly
  increased ΔE00 values (4.63 ± 0.89) compared with the NoIDS groups (2.51 ± 0.49)
  (p &lt; 0.001), whereas dentin condition and the interaction were not significant.
  IDS also affected ΔL*, Δa*, and Δb*, with the greatest change observed for ΔL*.
  All groups exceeded perceptibility and acceptability thresholds. IDS increased color
  change in lithium disilicate laminate veneers after accelerated aging, regardless
  of dentin condition.'

summaryDeep: 'Immediate dentin sealing (IDS) — applying an adhesive layer to freshly
  cut dentin before temporisation — is widely promoted for its bond-strength and pulp-protection
  benefits. This in-vitro study adds a complication: IDS significantly increases color
  change in overlying lithium disilicate laminate veneers after accelerated aging
  (thermocycling), with ΔE00 values of 4.63 ± 0.89 versus 2.51 ± 0.49 without IDS.
  Dentin condition (sound vs. eroded) and the interaction term were not significant
  — the effect is driven by IDS itself, primarily through lightness shift (ΔL*). All
  four groups exceeded both the perceptibility threshold (~1.0 ΔE00) and the acceptability
  threshold (~2.25 ΔE00), meaning the color change is clinically visible in every
  condition tested. The bovine dentin model and accelerated aging limit direct clinical
  translation, but the finding is directionally important: for thin, translucent veneers
  in the esthetic zone, IDS may compromise the optical result that motivated the restoration
  in the first place.'
