summary: Head and neck radiotherapy turns every subsequent dental extraction into
  a potential trigger for osteoradionecrosis — making nonsurgical root canal treatment
  the preferred tooth-preservation strategy in irradiated fields. This review maps
  the biological, diagnostic, and clinical decision points across the pre-, peri-,
  and post-radiotherapy timeline. The protocol it outlines should be standard knowledge
  for any clinician who will see oncology patients.
summaryDeep: 'Radiation to the head and neck causes progressive hypoxia, hypovascularity,
  and hypocellularity in bone — the triad that makes post-irradiation extraction in
  high-dose mandibular zones so dangerous. This review synthesises the biological
  rationale for why tooth preservation via nonsurgical RCT is strongly preferred over
  extraction in irradiated patients, and provides a structured clinical framework
  across three phases: pre-radiotherapy (identify and treat teeth with guarded prognosis
  before the field is irradiated), during radiotherapy (emergency-only, conservative
  management), and post-radiotherapy (strict protocols, hyperbaric oxygen consideration
  before any surgical intervention). Diagnostic considerations include the limitations
  of periapical radiography in detecting early pulpal and periapical changes in irradiated
  bone, where CBCT adds meaningful sensitivity. The review also addresses timing thresholds
  — how long after radiotherapy completion before elective endodontic or surgical
  procedures become safer. For a dental student, this is a clinical roadmap for a
  patient population that is growing as oncology survival rates improve and that is
  frequently mismanaged due to lack of awareness at the general practice level.'
