summary: A clinical and radiographic evaluation of 1,000 endodontically treated teeth
  using the eight-parameter Restorative Difficulty Evaluation System (RDES) finds
  49.8% classified as high difficulty, 36.5% moderate, and only 13.7% minor. Molars
  and teeth with compromised residual structure drive the high-difficulty burden.
  The data challenge the assumption that root canal completion is the hard part.
summaryDeep: Restoring endodontically treated teeth is frequently underestimated as
  a clinical challenge, and this 1,000-tooth cohort from 654 patients provides the
  most granular difficulty mapping to date using the RDES — a validated multidimensional
  scoring system covering endodontic complexity, residual vertical and horizontal
  structure, marginal seal, interdisciplinary conditions, treatment complexity, and
  functional/esthetic requirements. Nearly half of all teeth scored as high difficulty,
  with molars and premolars driving the distribution (49.5% and 39.7% of the sample
  respectively). Significant differences across tooth types were found for most RDES
  parameters, with the notable exception of interdisciplinary conditions. The study
  is cross-sectional and single-center, but the sample size is large enough to be
  informative. For students, the key insight is that post-endodontic restorative planning
  deserves the same systematic pre-treatment assessment as the root canal itself —
  and that 'difficulty' is multidimensional, not just anatomical.
