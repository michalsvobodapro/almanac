summary: Porphyromonas gingivalis activates the ATR DNA-damage response pathway in
  esophageal squamous cell carcinoma cells, which in turn polarises tumour-associated
  macrophages toward the immunosuppressive M2 phenotype — accelerating cancer progression.
  The mechanism is a concrete molecular link between a periodontal pathogen and a
  systemic malignancy. It adds biological plausibility to the epidemiological associations
  between poor oral health and upper GI cancers.
summaryDeep: 'The association between P. gingivalis and systemic cancers has been
  epidemiologically noted for years, but mechanistic evidence has lagged. This study
  identifies a specific pathway: Pg infection activates ATR (ataxia telangiectasia
  and Rad3-related) kinase in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) cells, and
  this ATR activation drives polarisation of tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs)
  toward the M2 phenotype — a state associated with immunosuppression, tumour immune
  evasion, and worse prognosis. The finding is significant because it moves the conversation
  from correlation to mechanism, identifying ATR as a potential therapeutic target
  and P. gingivalis as a modifiable risk factor. For a dental student, the translational
  message is that periodontal treatment is not merely oral health management — it
  may have oncological relevance in patients with ESCC risk factors (smoking, alcohol,
  reflux). The study also reinforces the broader theme running through today''s digest:
  the oral microbiome is a systemic actor, and periodontitis is a systemic disease.'
