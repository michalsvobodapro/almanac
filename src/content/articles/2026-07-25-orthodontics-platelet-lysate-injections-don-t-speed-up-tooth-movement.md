excerpt: Objectives To assess the effect of autologous platelet lysate (APL) on rate
  of orthodontic canine retraction and to measure levels of soluble receptor activator
  of nuclear factor κ-B ligand (sRANKL) and osteoprotegerin (OPG) in the gingival
  crevicular fluid (GCF) over a period of 2 months. Materials and methods This split-mouth
  trial included 13 patients, with 28 sites randomly allocated to either the experimental
  or control side. After leveling and aligning, 0.6 mL of APL was injected buccally,
  distally, and palatally on the experimental side at T0 (baseline, day 1) and T31
  (day 31). Canine retraction was performed using NiTi closed-coil springs (150 g).
  Digital models at T0 (baseline day 1), T30 (day 30), and T60 (day 60) were used
  to measure canine retraction rate. OPG and sRANKL levels from GCF samples were measured
  at days 0, 1, 7, 21, 30, 31, 37, 51, and 60 via enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.
  Results No statistically significant difference was found in the rate of canine
  retraction at any time interval. OPG levels showed a statistically significant difference
  at T0 and T21; however, no significant differences were observed across any other
  time intervals. sRANKL remained stable throughout all intervals, except for a small
  late decrease at T60 on the control side. Conclusions APL did not significantly
  enhance the rate of canine retraction, and OPG and sRANKL levels showed no consistent
  changes.

summary: A split-mouth RCT in 13 patients finds that autologous platelet lysate injections
  at the canine retraction site produce no significant increase in tooth movement

  crevicular fluid. The biological rationale was plausible; the clinical effect is

summaryDeep: Accelerating orthodontic tooth movement is a commercially attractive
  goal, and platelet-derived preparations have been tested across a range of regenerative
  applications. This split-mouth trial injected 0.6 mL of autologous platelet lysate
  buccally, distally, and palatally on the experimental side at baseline and day 31,
  with NiTi closed-coil springs providing 150 g of retraction force. Digital models
  at days 0, 30, and 60 measured canine retraction. Neither the movement rate nor
  the biomarker profile (sRANKL, OPG) showed consistent significant differences between
  sides. The sample size (13 patients, 28 sites) is small and the study is underpowered
  for modest effect sizes, so a definitive null conclusion would be premature. But
  the direction of evidence — no effect — is consistent with several prior negative
  trials of platelet preparations in orthodontic acceleration. Patients asking about
  'growth factor injections' to speed treatment can be counselled that current evidence
  does not support the practice.
