  chronic kidney disease (CKD) progression. Thus, this study aimed to evaluate non-surgical
  periodontal therapy (NSPT) in patients with CKD disease progression by monitoring
  oral parameters, pathogen levels, cytokine profiles, and kidney function. Methods
  Forty-five participants (age 60.0 ± 8.5 years) were examined at baseline and at
  3 months after NSPT for oral parameters, oral pathogens (Porphyromonas gingivalis,
  Fusobacterium nucleatum, Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans, Candida albicans),
  cytokines (IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α, IL-10), and kidney functions (serum creatinine, eGFR,
  urine protein-creatinine ratio). Results At baseline, the CKD patients were diagnosed
  with stage II-IV periodontitis and exhibited measurable periodontal pathogen levels

summaryDeep: 'This cohort study enrolled 45 CKD patients (mean age 60, stage II–IV
  CKD) with concurrent stage II–IV periodontitis and assessed oral and systemic parameters
  at baseline and three months after NSPT. Baseline pathogen levels included measurable
  P. gingivalis, F. nucleatum, A. actinomycetemcomitans, and C. albicans. After NSPT,
  pathogen counts dropped significantly, pro-inflammatory cytokines fell, and anti-inflammatory
  IL-10 rose. Kidney function markers improved in the majority of patients, though
  the study design cannot establish causality — confounding by concurrent medical
  management of CKD is a real concern. The sample size is small and there is no control
  arm, limiting conclusions. Nevertheless, for a student seeing medically complex
  patients, this study reinforces the clinical rationale for prioritizing periodontal
  treatment in CKD patients: the systemic benefit is plausible, the intervention is
  low-risk, and the oral health improvement alone justifies treatment. Larger RCTs
  are needed before this changes nephrology referral pathways.'
