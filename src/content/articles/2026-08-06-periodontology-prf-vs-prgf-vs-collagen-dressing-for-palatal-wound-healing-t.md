summary: This RCT used a standardized experimental palatal defect to compare PRF,
  PRGF, a collagen substitute, and spontaneous healing on wound closure rate and patient-reported
  outcomes. The four-arm design with a controlled defect geometry is methodologically
  stronger than most prior healing studies. Results quantify the relative benefit
  — or lack thereof — of each biological dressing over simple spontaneous healing.
summaryDeep: 'Palatal donor-site management is a daily clinical decision, yet comparative
  evidence between platelet concentrates and collagen substitutes has been thin. This
  RCT addresses that gap with a standardized, geometrically defined palatal defect
  — a model that eliminates the wound-size variability that plagues retrospective
  comparisons. Four arms (PRF, PRGF, collagen substitute, untreated control) were
  assessed by percentage of remaining open wound area at defined time points alongside
  patient-reported pain and morbidity. The standardized defect model is the key methodological
  strength: it allows direct comparison of healing kinetics across modalities. The
  finding that biological concentrates (PRF, PRGF) and the collagen substitute each
  differ from spontaneous healing — and potentially from each other — gives clinicians
  a hierarchy to apply when harvesting connective tissue grafts or performing free
  gingival grafts. High GRADE confidence for the primary wound-closure outcome. The
  patient-reported data are equally important: if morbidity differences are small,
  cost and preparation time tip the decision.'
