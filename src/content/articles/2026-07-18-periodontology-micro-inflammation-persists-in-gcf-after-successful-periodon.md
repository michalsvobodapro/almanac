summaryDeep: 'The concept of residual micro-inflammation after periodontal treatment
  is clinically important but poorly characterized. This study measured seven inflammatory
  markers in GCF and serum at baseline, 4 weeks, and 12 weeks post-treatment in 23
  periodontitis patients and 10 healthy controls. All six pathological markers (TNF-α,
  IL-1β, IL-6, IL-17A, MMP-8, LPS) declined significantly after treatment and correlated
  strongly with pocket depth and clinical attachment loss — validating them as responsive
  biomarkers. However, none normalized to healthy-control levels by 12 weeks. IFN-γ
  was the exception, showing no significant change. The pilot scale (n=33) limits
  conclusions, but the finding is biologically coherent: clinical healing and molecular
  healing are not synchronous. For students: this supports the rationale for allowing
  adequate healing time (beyond 3 months) before reassessment and any regenerative
  procedures, and it explains why patients who look clinically stable may still have
  an active inflammatory milieu.'
