summaryDeep: 'Twenty patients with 27 implants exhibiting refractory peri-implant
  mucositis (defined as failure of two consecutive non-surgical cycles, with keratinised
  mucosa width ≤1 mm and absent adherent mucosa) underwent apically positioned flap
  surgery with thorough decontamination and free gingival graft placement. At 12 months,
  22 implants (81.5%) were classified as clinically healthy. Bleeding on probing dropped
  by 85.2%, keratinised mucosa width increased by 4.42 mm, and mucosal thickness increased
  by approximately 1 mm. High-frequency ultrasonography revealed decreased perfusion
  and increased tissue stiffness — a promising non-invasive monitoring tool. The study
  is observational and small, so causality cannot be firmly established, but the results
  are clinically compelling for a condition that has few evidence-based surgical options.
  The inclusion criterion of inadequate keratinised mucosa width is important: this
  intervention is specifically indicated for the soft-tissue-deficient phenotype,
  not all mucositis cases.'
