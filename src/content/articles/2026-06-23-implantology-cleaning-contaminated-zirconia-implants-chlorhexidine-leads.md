summaryDeep: Zirconia implants are gaining traction as metal-free alternatives, but
  intraoperative contamination — saliva contact or metallic debris from instruments
  — is unavoidable and can compromise osseointegration by altering surface energy.
  This study systematically tested six cleaning protocols (control, autoclave, ultrasonic
  bath, ethanol, chlorhexidine, hydrogen peroxide) across three contamination states
  on 90 zirconia specimens, measuring contact angle, surface free energy, and elemental
  composition by EDS. Both contamination types significantly reduced surface hydrophilicity.
  Chlorhexidine was the standout performer, restoring low contact angles and high
  polar surface free energy regardless of contaminant. Ethanol and autoclave were
  effective for saliva contamination. Ultrasonic cleaning excelled at removing metallic
  debris. Hydrogen peroxide showed inconsistent results and should not be the default
  choice for metallic contamination. The in-vitro design limits direct clinical translation,
  but the protocol hierarchy is clear enough to inform surgical prep decisions when
  zirconia implants are being placed.
