summaryDeep: 'Anatomic scannable healing abutments promise to streamline digital implant
  workflows by eliminating the scan-body swap step and simultaneously shaping the
  emergence profile. This in vitro study used 3D-printed partially edentulous maxillary
  models with implant analogs, scanned 15 times per group with an intraoral scanner,
  and compared linear (mesiodistal, buccolingual, coronoapical), angular, and overall
  3D deviations against a desktop-scanner reference. For single-crown scenarios, AHAs
  and conventional scan bodies performed equivalently — a genuine finding that supports
  AHA use in straightforward single-implant cases. For three-unit fixed partial prostheses,
  AHAs showed statistically greater deviations, particularly in the coronoapical axis.
  The likely mechanism is cumulative error from the AHA''s anatomic geometry interacting
  with IOS stitching algorithms across a longer span. Practical guidance: AHAs are
  a legitimate workflow simplification for single implants; revert to conventional
  scan bodies for multi-unit cases where passive fit is critical.'
