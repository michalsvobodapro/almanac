  roughness (Ra), contact angle (CA) and S. mutans adhesion of subtractive manufacturing

  were fabricated using a resin nano-ceramic CAD/CAM block (Cerasmart; GC (SM) ( N
  = 42) and a definitive resin (Permanent Crown Resin; Formlabs (AM) ( N = 42). The
  specimens of each manufacturing group were further divided into three immersion
  subgroups ( n = 14 per subgroup); 1: Control (Fusayama-artificial saliva) (Group
  SM-F and AM-F), 2: Alcohol-containing mouthwash (Total Care, Listerine) (Group SM-A
  and AM-A), and 3: Alcohol-free mouthwash (Total Care Zero, Listerine) (Group SM-AF
  and AM-AF). Baseline Ra and CA measurements were performed on 10 specimens per subgroup,
  while surface morphology was evaluated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and
  atomic force microscopy (AFM) on two specimens each. Following immersion, all specimens
  underwent the bacterial adhesion protocol. S. mutans adhesion was quantified on
  the same 10 specimens used for Ra and CA measurements, whereas SEM and AFM analyses
  were repeated on the same designated specimens. Depending on the normality of the
  data, both parametric (independent-samples t -test, one-way ANOVA, Tukey’s/Dunnett’s
  T3 post hoc tests) and non-parametric (Mann-Whitney U test) analyses were performed,

summary: An in vitro study of 84 specimens compared the effect of alcohol-containing
  and alcohol-free Listerine on surface roughness, contact angle, and S. mutans adhesion
  for both milled (Cerasmart) and 3D-printed (Formlabs Permanent Crown Resin) definitive
  restorations. Alcohol-containing mouthwash increased surface roughness and bacterial
  adhesion more than the alcohol-free formulation across both materials.
summaryDeep: As CAD/CAM and 3D-printed resin restorations become standard in restorative
  practice, understanding how common patient-use products affect their longevity is
  clinically essential. This study fabricated 84 specimens across two manufacturing
  methods and three immersion conditions, then assessed surface roughness (Ra), contact
  angle, S. mutans adhesion, and surface morphology by SEM and AFM. Alcohol-containing
  mouthwash produced greater surface degradation and higher bacterial adhesion than
  the alcohol-free formulation for both materials, though the magnitude differed between
  subtractive and additive manufacturing groups. The additive (3D-printed) material
  appeared more susceptible to surface changes. Limitations include the in vitro design
  and single bacterial species used. For clinical practice, the finding supports advising
  patients with resin-based CAD/CAM or printed restorations to use alcohol-free mouthwash
  — a low-cost, low-risk recommendation that is easy to implement.
