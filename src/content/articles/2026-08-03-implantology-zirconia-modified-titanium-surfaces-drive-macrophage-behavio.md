summaryDeep: 'Osseointegration research has long focused on surface topography and
  hydrophilicity (SLA vs modSLA), but this study shifts the spotlight to surface chemistry.
  Human monocytes were differentiated into M1 or M2 macrophages on four surfaces:
  hydrophobic titanium (P), hydrophilic titanium (modSLA), zirconia-enriched titanium
  (R), and hydrophilic zirconia-enriched titanium (modR). Real-time PCR, fluorescent
  immunoassays, and Luminex cytokine profiling showed that zirconia incorporation
  — not hydrophilicity — drove the most distinct macrophage phenotype shifts, including
  upregulation of both pro- and anti-inflammatory transcripts. Conditioned media from
  these macrophages then drove osteoblast differentiation and mineralisation assays,
  with zirconia surfaces producing the most favourable osteogenic signal. This is
  bench science only — no animal or clinical data — so it cannot change implant selection
  today. But it provides mechanistic rationale for the growing clinical interest in
  zirconia-surface implants and helps explain why surface chemistry specifications
  matter when comparing implant systems.'
