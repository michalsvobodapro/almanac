  untreated teeth using a digital bite force transducer (GM10). Methods: A total of
  131 patients (48 males and 83 females) participated in the study. Bite force measurements

summary: A cross-sectional study of 447 teeth in 131 patients found that root-canal-treated

summaryDeep: Using a digital bite-force transducer on 447 teeth (248 RCT, 199 untreated)
  across 131 patients, this cross-sectional study measured pain/discomfort-limited
  bite force thresholds rather than true maximal force — an important methodological
  distinction. RCT teeth registered a mean of 197 N versus 245 N for vital teeth (adjusted
  p = 0.035), a clinically meaningful 20% reduction. Males bit harder than females
  (278 N vs. 187 N), and molars outperformed premolars regardless of treatment status.
  The authors attribute the RCT deficit to ablation of pulpal nociceptors and altered
  periodontal mechanoreceptor signaling, which reduces the protective feedback loop
  during heavy occlusal loading. The cross-sectional design cannot establish causation
  — pre-existing differences in tooth structure or occlusal loading history could
  confound results — but the finding aligns with clinical intuition and has implications
  for crown indication thresholds and occlusal adjustment after endodontic treatment.
