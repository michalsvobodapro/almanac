summary: A 5-year RCT comparing fiber post and short fiber-reinforced composite (SFRC)
  as core materials for anterior endodontically treated teeth (>50% residual structure)
  covered by lithium disilicate crowns found no significant difference in prosthodontic
  failures or oral-behavior-related complications. Both approaches performed equivalently
  across all modified USPHS criteria. SFRC is a legitimate, post-free alternative
  for this indication.
summaryDeep: This two-arm randomized clinical trial recruited patients requiring single-unit
  anterior crown restorations after endodontic treatment, including only teeth with
  more than 50% residual tooth structure. Patients were evenly allocated to either
  a conventional fiber post core or a short fiber-reinforced composite core, both
  restored with a lithium disilicate full crown. Prosthodontic outcomes were assessed
  using modified USPHS criteria, and oral parafunctional behaviors were tracked with
  the Oral Behavior Checklist. At five years, neither failure rates (p=0.471) nor
  high-risk oral behavior associations (p=0.197) differed between groups. The finding
  is clinically significant because fiber post placement requires additional tooth
  preparation and carries its own failure modes; SFRC avoids post-space drilling entirely,
  preserving more dentin. For anterior teeth with adequate residual structure, this
  RCT provides level-1 evidence to support a simpler, post-free workflow.
