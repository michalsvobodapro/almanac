excerpt: 'Abstract Objectives To evaluate effects of self-activating phthalocyanine
  mouthrinse on recipient sites of subepithelial connective tissue graft (SCTG), considering
  clinical outcomes, professional and patient assessments and quantitative somatosensory
  changes. Materials and methods This randomized controlled, crossover, split-mouth
  clinical trial included 20 patients treated with SCTG plus coronally advanced flap
  (CAF). Recipient sites received 0.12% iron phthalocyanine mouthrinse (test group-PHY)
  and 0.12% chlorhexidine (control group-CHX) during 14 postoperative days. The primary
  outcome was percentage of root coverage (%RC) at 6 months. Secondary outcomes included
  clinical parameters (probing depth, PD; recession depth and width, RD, RW; percentage
  of complete root coverage (CRC); clinical attachment level, CAL; keratinized tissue
  thickness and width, KTT, KTW), professional- and patient-reported outcomes, mechanical
  detection threshold (MDT) and mechanical pain threshold (MPT). Results PHY and CHX
  groups showed significant improvements in clinical parameters, including reduced
  RD and RW, CAL gain, and increased KTW and KTT ( p &lt; 0.05). %RC was similar between
  groups, at approximately 80%. Professional- and patient-reported outcomes, including
  dentin hypersensitivity and esthetic perception, as well as MPT and MDT, showed
  no significant differences between groups. Postoperative symptoms decreased progressively,
  and oral health-related quality of life remained unchanged '

summaryDeep: 'Chlorhexidine is the default post-surgical rinse after connective tissue
  grafting, but its side effects — staining, taste disturbance, mucosal irritation
  — drive interest in alternatives. This split-mouth RCT is the first to test self-activating
  phthalocyanine (PHY) head-to-head with CHX after SCTG plus coronally advanced flap.
  The equivalence finding across all outcomes, including root coverage percentage
  (~80%), keratinized tissue dimensions, dentin hypersensitivity, and patient-reported
  quality of life, is clinically meaningful: PHY appears to be a viable substitute.
  The sample size (n = 20) limits definitive conclusions, and the 14-day rinse window
  is short. Still, for patients who struggle with CHX side effects, this gives a clinician
  something to offer. The phthalocyanine mechanism — photodynamic antibacterial activity
  without chemical toxicity — also makes it interesting for immunocompromised patients.'
