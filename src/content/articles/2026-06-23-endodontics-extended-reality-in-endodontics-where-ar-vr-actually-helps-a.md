summaryDeep: 'Extended reality (XR) — the umbrella term for VR, AR, and mixed reality
  — has entered dental education faster than the evidence base has matured. This review
  synthesises current endodontic applications: VR simulators for access-cavity preparation
  and file handling, AR overlays for working-length confirmation, and MR for surgical
  endodontic planning. Educational applications are the most developed, with VR simulators
  demonstrating comparable skill acquisition to phantom-head training in some studies.
  Clinical AR for intraoperative canal guidance is promising but hampered by registration
  accuracy, latency, and sterility of headset equipment. The review is narrative rather
  than systematic, so it cannot quantify effect sizes, but it provides a useful taxonomy
  of where XR sits on the translational spectrum. For a Prague dental student, the
  practical takeaway is that VR simulation is already a legitimate adjunct to phantom
  training, while intraoperative AR should be treated as experimental. Institutions
  investing in XR infrastructure for education are ahead of the curve; those marketing
  it as a clinical tool are ahead of the evidence.'
