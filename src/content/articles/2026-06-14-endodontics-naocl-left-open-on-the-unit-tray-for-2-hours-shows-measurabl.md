  capacity. Since these properties are related to its physicochemical characteristics,
  changes during chairside handling may be clinically relevant. This study aimed to
  evaluate time-dependent changes in pH, active chlorine content, and surface tension

  canal treatment period. MATERIAL AND METHODS A commercially available 5% NaOCl solution
  was tested under 3 conditions: fresh (T₀), after 1 hour (T₁), and after 2 hours

  physicochemical properties, yet chairside handling practices are rarely scrutinized.
  This study exposed commercially available 5% NaOCl to standardized ambient clinic
  conditions (≈22°C) in open transparent containers and measured pH, active chlorine,
  and surface tension at 0, 1, and 2 hours (n=10 per group per timepoint). pH declined
