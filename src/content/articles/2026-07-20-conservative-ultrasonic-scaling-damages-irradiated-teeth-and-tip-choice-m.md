summaryDeep: 'Head-and-neck radiotherapy alters dentinal structure, but how much that
  matters during routine scaling has been poorly quantified. This in vitro study used
  robot-standardized scaling (1 N force, medium power, water spray) on 24 molars irradiated
  at 0, 30, or 70 Gy, comparing two tip types (G6 broad vs. P20 slim periodontal),
  two angulations (15° vs. 45°), and two orientations (lateral vs. back). Surface
  loss was graded on intraoral scans by three blinded observers. At 30 Gy, back orientation
  significantly increased odds of deeper surface loss; the 70 Gy contrast paradoxically
  did not reach significance, suggesting a dose-threshold rather than linear effect.
  Tip selection dominated all other variables: G6 at 45° back produced the worst outcomes
  across all doses, while P20 at 15° lateral was consistently safe. Root surfaces
  were substantially more susceptible than crowns. The clinical translation is direct:
  in any patient with a history of head-and-neck radiotherapy, default to slim periodontal
  tips at shallow angulation, and treat root surfaces with particular caution.'
