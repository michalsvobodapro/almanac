  charged with 10–15 wt% CaF₂ released fluoride ions and induced enamel remineralisation

  to the control, flagging a mechanical compromise that needs solving before clinical

summaryDeep: 'The holy grail of restorative dentistry is a composite that actively
  protects the restoration margin from secondary caries. This lab study takes a credible
  step toward it: prepolymerized TMPTMA-HEMA particles loaded with CaF₂ (5, 10, 15
  wt%) were incorporated into experimental composites at 20 wt% filler replacement.
  Micro-CT confirmed enamel remineralisation in the CaF10 and CaF15 groups after S.
  mutans biofilm challenge — a meaningful in-vitro endpoint. Degree of conversion
  was unaffected, preserving polymerisation quality. However, flexural strength and
  modulus were significantly reduced versus the barium borosilicate control, and water
  sorption increased. These are not trivial trade-offs: reduced flexural strength
  limits posterior use, and higher water sorption accelerates degradation. The study
  is in-vitro only, with no ageing or fatigue data. For a student, this is a useful
  illustration of why remineralising composites have not yet displaced conventional
  materials: the chemistry that releases ions tends to weaken the matrix.'
