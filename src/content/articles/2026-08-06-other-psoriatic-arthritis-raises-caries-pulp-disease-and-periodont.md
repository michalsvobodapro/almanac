excerpt: Psoriatic arthritis is increasingly recognized as a systemic inflammatory
  disease with multiple comorbidities, yet the full spectrum of dental complications
  associated with psoriatic arthritis has not been clearly defined. This nationwide
  population based cohort study evaluated whether patients with psoriatic arthritis
  are at elevated risk of diverse dental diseases. Using a nationwide population-based
  health insurance database, we identified 40,920 newly diagnosed psoriatic arthritis
  cases and 204,468 age- and sex-matched controls. Dental outcomes included dental
  caries, pulp and periapical disease, periodontal disease, gingival changes and tooth
  loss. Cox proportional hazards models were used to estimate adjusted hazard ratios
  (aHRs). PsA patients exhibited significantly increased risks of dental caries (aHR
  1.342), pulp and periapical disease (aHR 1.633) and periodontal disease (aHR 1.268).
  Subtype analyses revealed elevated risks for acute and chronic gingivitis, aggressive
  and chronic periodontitis, periodontosis and unspecified periodontal disease. These
  findings highlight the need for comprehensive oral health surveillance in patients
  with psoriatic arthritis.

summaryDeep: 'Psoriatic arthritis is a systemic inflammatory disease managed primarily
  by rheumatologists, but this large cohort study makes a strong case for routine
  dental integration in PsA care pathways. The 63% elevated risk of pulp and periapical
  disease is the most striking finding — substantially higher than the caries and
  periodontal signals — and likely reflects a combination of xerostomia from DMARDs,
  immune-mediated pulp inflammation, and reduced dental attendance in a chronically
  ill population. The periodontal risk elevation (aHR 1.268) aligns with the broader
  literature on inflammatory arthropathies and periodontitis. The study''s strength
  is its scale (245,000+ individuals) and use of Cox proportional hazards models with
  age/sex matching. Limitations include reliance on insurance coding rather than clinical
  examination, and the Korean population may not generalize directly to European patients.
  For Czech dental students, the practical implication is straightforward: when a
  patient discloses PsA or is on biologics for inflammatory arthritis, elevate recall
  frequency and screen proactively for periapical pathology.'
