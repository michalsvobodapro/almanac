summary: Osseointegration is increasingly understood as an immunological process,
  not just a mechanical one. This mouse study shows that CXCL9 — a chemokine that
  recruits M1 macrophages — accelerates early bone formation around implants and improves
  bone-implant contact at 4 weeks. It identifies a targetable immune pathway that
  could inform next-generation implant surface coatings.
summaryDeep: The osteoimmunology of implant healing has shifted the field's understanding
  of why some implants integrate faster and more reliably than others. CXCL9 is an
  interferon-γ-induced chemokine primarily known for T-cell and macrophage recruitment
  in inflammatory contexts, but its role in bone regeneration has been underexplored.
  This murine study places titanium implants in femoral defects with and without local
  CXCL9 delivery, then assesses bone-implant contact (BIC), bone volume fraction,
  and macrophage polarisation at 2 and 4 weeks via histomorphometry and immunofluorescence.
  CXCL9-treated sites show earlier M1-to-M2 macrophage transition, higher BIC at 4
  weeks, and greater peri-implant bone volume. The proposed mechanism is that CXCL9
  orchestrates the early inflammatory phase more efficiently, shortening the window
  before osteogenic signalling dominates. This is bench science, but it points toward
  surface functionalisation strategies — CXCL9-loaded coatings or hydrogels — that
  could reduce early implant failure rates in compromised bone.
