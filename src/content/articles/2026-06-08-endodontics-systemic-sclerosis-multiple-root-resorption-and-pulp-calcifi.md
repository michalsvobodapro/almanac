summary: A patient with systemic sclerosis (SSc) presented with multiple external
  invasive root resorption lesions alongside pulp calcification — a combination rarely
  documented in the literature. The case is the most comprehensively illustrated SSc-associated
  root resorption report to date, and it raises the question of whether SSc should
  be added to the differential when unexplained multi-site resorption appears on radiographs.
summaryDeep: 'External invasive root resorption (EIRR) is already diagnostically challenging
  in otherwise healthy patients; encountering it across multiple teeth simultaneously
  should prompt a systemic workup. Systemic sclerosis — an autoimmune connective tissue
  disease — causes vascular and fibrotic changes that can affect the periodontium
  and periradicular tissues, but the mechanistic link to EIRR remains poorly characterised.
  This case report is notable for documenting both resorption and calcification concurrently,
  suggesting competing pathological processes driven by the same systemic background.
  The clinical lesson is practical: when a patient''s medical history includes SSc
  or other connective tissue diseases, baseline periapical radiographs of the full
  dentition are warranted, and unexplained resorption should not be attributed to
  trauma or orthodontics by default. As a case report it carries limited evidential
  weight, but it fills a genuine gap in the literature and is the kind of rare presentation
  that is easy to miss without prior exposure.'
