summaryDeep: 'GERD''s oral consequences are underappreciated in dental practice. This
  case-control study used upper GI endoscopy to confirm and grade GERD severity (LA
  classification A–D), then applied DMFT, BEWE, and plaque index alongside salivary
  pH and flow rate measurements. GERD patients had significantly worse scores on every
  oral health metric, and the severity gradient was clear — LA grade C/D and Barrett''s
  esophagus cases showed the steepest deterioration. Multivariate regression confirmed
  GERD severity, salivary pH, and flow rate as independent predictors of both caries
  and erosion. The cross-sectional design prevents causal inference, and the single-centre
  Egyptian sample limits generalisability. But the clinical message is actionable
  now: when a patient reports GERD or PPI use, erosive wear and caries risk should
  both be formally assessed, not assumed to be low because the patient ''brushes well.'''
