excerpt: Introduction Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP) is a painful dental
  condition often diagnosed on the basis of subjective pain reports, which are frequently
  vague and poorly localized, leading to diagnostic uncertainty and potential overtreatment.
  Our study integrated multiomics data to identify objective molecular signatures
  related to pulp inflammation and pain modulation. Methods Proteomic profiling of
  symptomatic irreversible pulpitis and healthy pulp was conducted via liquid chromatography-tandem
  mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). Their transcriptional expression was examined in GEO
  dataset to determine consistency across omics levels. Then we used single-cell RNA
  sequencing data to map candidate expression to specific pulp cell types. We validated
  key proteins in pulp tissue by Western blotting. Finally, we quantified selected
  markers in saliva by Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) as a non-invasive,

summaryDeep: 'Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis is diagnosed largely on subjective
  pain history, which leads to both overtreatment and missed cases. This multi-omics
  study quantified 5,057 proteins in SIP versus healthy pulp tissue, cross-validated
  findings against public transcriptomic datasets, and used single-cell RNA sequencing
  to assign expression to specific pulp cell types — macrophages, fibroblasts, osteoblast-lineage
  cells, and dental pulp stem cells. Six molecules emerged as consistently upregulated
  across platforms: MMP9, NCF2, NF-κB1, CAMK1, TRAF1, and NACC1. The most clinically
  exciting finding is that salivary ELISA for selected markers showed diagnostic potential
  by ROC analysis, suggesting a future where pulp diagnosis is supplemented by a non-invasive
  fluid test rather than relying solely on thermal/electric pulp testing. The study
  is mechanistic and translational rather than immediately practice-changing, but
  it lays a credible molecular foundation for objective pulp diagnostics. For endodontics
  students, the cell-type-resolved inflammation map is also a valuable conceptual
  resource.'
