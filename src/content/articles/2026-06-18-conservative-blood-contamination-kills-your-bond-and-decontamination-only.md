summary: A PRISMA-compliant systematic review of 15 in-vitro studies (1,015 teeth)
  confirms that blood contamination consistently degrades dentin bond strength across
  all adhesive systems, with the worst damage occurring after polymerization. Adhesive
  reapplication and phosphoric acid re-etching work best for etch-and-rinse systems;
  NaOCl plus antioxidants are the top rescue strategy for self-etch. No method reliably
  restores bond strength to uncontaminated levels.
summaryDeep: 'Fifteen in-vitro studies covering 1,015 teeth were pooled in this PROSPERO-registered
  systematic review to map how blood contamination affects dentin bond strength and
  whether decontamination can undo the damage. The verdict is unambiguous: contamination
  at any stage reduces bonding, but post-polymerization contamination is the most
  destructive scenario. For etch-and-rinse adhesives, adhesive reapplication and phosphoric
  acid re-etching offer the strongest partial recovery; for self-etch systems, sodium
  hypochlorite combined with antioxidants (e.g., sodium ascorbate) performs best.
  Critically, no decontamination protocol reliably returns bond strength to baseline
  — meaning prevention is categorically more important than rescue. Eleven of fifteen
  studies had low risk of bias (QUIN tool), giving the conclusions reasonable confidence
  despite the inherent limitations of in-vitro design. For a student working in clinic,
  this translates directly: rubber dam is not optional in adhesive dentistry, and
  if contamination occurs, know which rescue protocol matches your adhesive chemistry.'
