summaryDeep: 'Panoramic radiographs are the workhorse screening tool in most dental
  practices, yet periapical lesions are notoriously under-detected on them. This meta-analysis
  systematically aggregates the growing body of deep learning (DL) studies targeting
  that exact gap. The review finds that contemporary DL architectures achieve pooled
  diagnostic performance sufficient to function as a second-reader tool, flagging
  radiolucencies that clinicians miss on busy pans. Critically, the evidence grade
  is rated high — unusual for an AI-in-dentistry paper — because the included studies
  share consistent reference standards and outcome definitions. For a Prague dental
  student, the implication is concrete: AI-assisted panoramic analysis is entering
  clinical workflows now, and understanding its operating characteristics (sensitivity
  vs. specificity trade-offs, false-positive rates) is a competency, not a curiosity.
  The review also highlights remaining gaps — most training datasets are monocentric
  and lack ethnic diversity — which tempers enthusiasm for uncritical adoption but
  doesn''t undermine the core finding.'
