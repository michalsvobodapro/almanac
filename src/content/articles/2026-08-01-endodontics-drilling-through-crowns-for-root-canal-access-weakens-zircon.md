summaryDeep: 'The clinical appeal of performing root canal treatment through an existing
  crown — avoiding costly remake — is real, but this systematic review and meta-analysis
  quantifies the structural cost. Across 23 in-vitro studies covering fracture resistance
  (n=15), retention (n=5), and microleakage (n=3), zirconia and lithium disilicate
  restorations showed statistically significant reductions in fracture resistance
  after access preparation (p=0.0002 and p=0.007, respectively), while resin-matrix
  ceramic restorations did not (p=0.25). Retention was significantly compromised only
  when access cavities were left unrepaired — appropriate repair protocols restored
  retention to acceptable levels. Microleakage data were too heterogeneous for meta-analysis
  but trended toward increased leakage with unrepaired access. The abutment tooth
  type contributed additional heterogeneity in the LD and RMC subgroups, a variable
  often overlooked in clinical planning. Practically: if the existing crown is zirconia
  or lithium disilicate and the patient wants to keep it, factor in the structural
  penalty and repair the access cavity meticulously. RMC restorations appear more
  forgiving.'
