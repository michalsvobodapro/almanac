summaryDeep: 'The field of dental tissue engineering has produced impressive in vitro
  and animal data with nanoparticle-loaded scaffolds, but clinical translation has
  been glacially slow. This review dissects why: inconsistent nanoparticle characterisation
  across studies, lack of standardised release kinetics testing, and the absence of
  a clear EU/FDA regulatory category for combination scaffold-drug devices. The authors
  evaluate the major scaffold chemistries — hydrogels, electrospun fibres, 3D-printed
  constructs — and the nanoparticle payloads most relevant to dentistry (hydroxyapatite,
  silver, silica, growth-factor-loaded PLGA). Industrial manufacturing perspectives
  are included, which is rare in this literature: batch-to-batch reproducibility and
  sterilisation compatibility are flagged as underappreciated barriers. For a Prague
  dental student, the EU regulatory angle is particularly relevant — the Medical Device
  Regulation (MDR 2017/745) and its combination-product provisions will govern any
  scaffold that eventually reaches European clinics.'
