summaryDeep: Twenty studies were included; only ten provided the 2×2 contingency data
  needed for meta-analysis, which is itself a red flag about reporting standards in
  the AI-dentistry literature. Among those ten, pooled sensitivity of 76% is clinically
  meaningful — one in four proximal lesions would be missed by AI alone — while specificity
  of 94% limits false positives well. Bitewing radiographs consistently outperformed
  panoramic views, reinforcing their status as the modality of choice for interproximal
  caries screening. The QUADAS-2 quality assessment flagged significant risk of bias
  across studies, and external validation on independent datasets remains rare. For
  a Prague dental student, the practical implication is that AI caries-detection software
  (increasingly available in European practices) should be treated as a second-reader
  aid, not a diagnostic authority. The sensitivity gap means clinical examination
  and patient history still carry the decision.
