excerpt: 'To evaluate the effects of metabolic syndrome (MetS) and periodontitis on
  mandibular cortical and trabecular bone structure and their association with periodontal
  inflammatory burden, 80 individuals were allocated to four groups: systemically
  and periodontally healthy (MetS - P-, n = 20), periodontitis only (MetS - P+, n
  = 20), MetS only (MetS + P-, n = 20), and both conditions (MetS + P+, n = 20). Trabecular
  bone was assessed by fractal dimension (FD) analysis in three regions, while cortical
  morphology was evaluated on panoramic radiographs using radiomorphometric indices:
  symphysis index (SI), anterior index (AI), molar index (MI), panoramic mandibular
  index (PMI), and Klemetti index (KI). Periodontal inflamed surface area (PISA) and
  periodontal epithelial surface area (PESA) were calculated to quantify inflammatory
  load. Condyle and angulus FD values were higher in MetS groups (p 0.05). KI distribution
  differed significantly, with more C1 in controls and more C2 in MetS + P+ (p < 0.05).
  PISA was greatest in MetS + P+ (p < 0.05). Positive correlations were observed between
  PESA and AI, and between PISA and both AI and SI (p < 0.05). Coexistence of MetS
  and periodontitis is associated with regional mandibular trabecular and cortical
  bone alterations and increases periodontal inflammatory burden, suggesting a combined
  effect of metabolic stress and periodontal inflammation on bone microarchitecture.'

  cortical morphology was evaluated using standard panoramic radiomorphometric indices
  (PMI, SI, AI, MI, Klemetti index). The MetS+periodontitis group showed the highest
  PISA values and the most unfavorable cortical classification (more C2 on Klemetti),
  with positive correlations between PISA and both AI and SI. The combined group''s

  rather than merely additive effect of metabolic stress and periodontal inflammation
  on mandibular bone microarchitecture. Cross-sectional design limits causal inference,
  but the clinical message is clear: patients with metabolic syndrome presenting for
  periodontal treatment carry a higher skeletal risk burden, and panoramic radiographs
  may offer a low-cost screening window.'
