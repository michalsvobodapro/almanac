excerpt: 'Background/Objectives: Historical concerns regarding permanent dental staining
  have restricted the use of tetracycline antibiotics in children younger than 8 years.
  However, newer-generation tetracycline derivatives, particularly doxycycline, differ
  pharmacologically from first-generation tetracyclines and may present a lower risk
  of discoloration. This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the risk of
  dental staining associated with newer-generation tetracycline derivatives. Methods:
  It was conducted according to the PRISMA 2020 statement and registered in PROSPERO.
  PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, and Embase were searched. Methodological quality was assessed,
  and the certainty of evidence using the GRADE approach. A random-effects meta-analysis
  of comparative studies was performed using the risk difference (RD) as the summary
  effect measure. Results: Six observational studies fulfilled the eligibility criteria.
  Three studies reported no cases of dental staining following doxycycline exposure,
  whereas one study identified parent-reported discoloration in 2 of 18 evaluated
  children (11.1%). Dental staining was reported in 2 of 41 children (4.9%) treated
  with minocycline and in 2 of 12 children (16.7%) with tigecycline. Meta-analysis
  of the three comparative studies demonstrated no significant increase in the risk
  of dental staining among exposed children compared with controls (RD = 0.007; 95%
  CI −0.015 to 0.028; I2 = 0%). Methodological quality ranged from moderate to high.

summary: A PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis of six observational
  studies finds no significant increase in dental staining risk in children exposed
  to newer-generation tetracyclines (doxycycline, minocycline, tigecycline) compared
  to controls (RD = 0.007; 95% CI −0.015 to 0.028). The historical ban on tetracyclines
  in under-8s was based on first-generation compounds and may not apply to doxycycline.
summaryDeep: The prohibition on tetracycline use in children under 8 years has been
  a fixture of prescribing guidance for decades, rooted in evidence of permanent dental
  staining from first-generation compounds like oxytetracycline. Doxycycline differs
  pharmacologically — it has lower calcium-chelating affinity — and paediatric infectious
  disease specialists have long argued the restriction is overcautious. This meta-analysis
  pools six observational studies and finds a risk difference indistinguishable from
  zero for dental staining with doxycycline, with no heterogeneity (I²=0%). Minocycline
  and tigecycline showed small numbers of staining cases but without control comparisons
  adequate for meta-analysis. The evidence base is still modest — six studies, mostly
  observational — but the direction and consistency are clear. For Czech students,
  this is relevant to paediatric prescribing scenarios in oral medicine and paediatric
  dentistry rotations, and aligns with evolving guidance from paediatric infectious
  disease bodies internationally.
