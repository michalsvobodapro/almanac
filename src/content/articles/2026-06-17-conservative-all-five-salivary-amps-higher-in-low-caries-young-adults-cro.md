  through parameters such as flow rate, pH, and buffering capacity. However, additional
  components, particularly proteins of the innate immune system, may also contribute
  to caries development or prevention. This study evaluated whether antimicrobial
  peptides (AMPs) in saliva are associated with caries status in young adults. Methods
  Forty healthy volunteers (18-35 years) attending the University Dental Clinics provided
  stimulated saliva samples and were assigned to either a low-caries group or a high
  caries experience group, based on ICDAS criteria and DMFS index scores. Levels of
  human β-defensins 1-3, histatin-5, and cathelicidin LL-37 were quantified using
  peptide-specific ELISA kits. Results All AMP concentrations were significantly higher

  These findings indicate that salivary AMPs levels are associated with caries experience

summaryDeep: 'Stimulated saliva was collected from 40 volunteers (18–35 years) at
  a university dental clinic and divided into low-caries and high-caries groups using
  ICDAS criteria and DMFS index. ELISA quantification of five AMPs showed uniformly
  higher concentrations in low-caries individuals for all peptides measured. The cross-sectional
  design cannot establish causality — low AMP levels could be a cause or a consequence
  of caries activity — and the sample size is small. However, the finding that all
  five peptides move in the same direction is notable and corroborates the meta-analysis
  in this digest. For students, this is a clean illustration of how the innate immune
  system modulates oral ecology: individuals with robust AMP secretion appear to suppress
  cariogenic biofilms more effectively. Future longitudinal studies tracking AMP levels
  before caries onset are needed to confirm predictive validity.'
