summary: 'A deceptively simple question with real clinical stakes: does agitating
  the etchant against enamel during the etching step alter surface topography and
  bonding interface quality compared with passive application? This European Journal
  of Oral Sciences study provides quantitative surface data that should inform how
  you teach — and perform — the bonding protocol.'
summaryDeep: 'Acid etching of enamel is one of the most practised steps in restorative
  dentistry, yet the protocol details — concentration, time, and whether to rub or
  leave static — vary widely between operators and teaching institutions. This study
  from the European Journal of Oral Sciences isolates the rubbing variable, measuring
  surface roughness parameters and interfacial characteristics of intact enamel etched
  with and without mechanical agitation. The rationale for rubbing is intuitive: agitation
  might refresh the acid at the surface, remove dissolved mineral more efficiently,
  and create a more uniform etch pattern. Whether that translates into measurably
  different roughness or a better bonding interface is what the study tests. For a
  student in a Czech dental school where bonding protocols may differ from Western
  European norms, this kind of granular technique study is directly applicable — it''s
  the sort of evidence that should underpin standardised clinical teaching rather
  than instructor preference. The findings, whatever direction they point, are a reminder
  that even the most routine steps in operative dentistry have an evidence base worth
  interrogating.'
