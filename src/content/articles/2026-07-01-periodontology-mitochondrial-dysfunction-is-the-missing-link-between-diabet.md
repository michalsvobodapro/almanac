  due to impaired wound healing associated with concomitant failure to resolve inflammation
  and chronic infection. Studies from the Joslin Medalist Cohort, people with >50
  years of type 1 diabetes, have reported that protective factors exist to delay the

summary: Proteomic analysis of gingival tissue from the Joslin Medalist Cohort — people
  with >50 years of type 1 diabetes who avoided severe complications — reveals that
  preserved mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (complexes I & II) is the key
  protective factor against periodontitis. In diabetic mice, activating pyruvate kinase
  M2 restored mitochondrial function, slashed inflammatory cytokines, and halted periodontal
  bone loss — without touching glycolysis. This is the first mechanistic explanation
  for why some hyperglycemic patients never develop severe perio.
summaryDeep: 'The Joslin Medalist Cohort has long puzzled researchers: some individuals
  with decades of uncontrolled hyperglycemia simply don''t develop the severe end-organ
  damage — including periodontitis — that theory predicts. This study finally offers
  a molecular answer. Proteomic profiling of healthy gingival tissue from hyperglycemic
  Medalists versus periodontitis patients with good glycemic control showed massive
  upregulation of oxidative phosphorylation in the protected group, specifically mitochondrial
  complexes I and II. In a parallel mouse model, diabetes suppressed mitochondrial
  enzyme expression and oxygen consumption in gingival tissue, amplifying ligature-induced
  bone loss and driving IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-17a elevation. Critically, pharmacological
  activation of pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2) reversed all of this — restoring mitochondrial
  function, reducing cytokine load, and blocking osteoclast activation — without disrupting
  glycolytic pathways. The clinical implication is double-edged: it explains why HbA1c
  alone is a poor predictor of perio severity in individual patients, and it opens
  a genuinely new therapeutic avenue — mitochondria-targeted adjuncts for perio treatment
  in diabetic patients — that doesn''t require glycemic normalization first.'
