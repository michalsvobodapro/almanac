summaryDeep: This PROSPERO-registered review searched PubMed, Scopus, EBSCOhost, and
  SpringerLink for studies published 2015–2025 applying AI to skeletal maturation
  assessment. Seven studies met eligibility; five used lateral cephalograms for CVM,
  two used hand-wrist radiographs. A random-effects meta-analysis with REML estimation
  was performed on the CVM studies using logit-transformed accuracy proportions; hand-wrist
  studies were excluded from quantitative synthesis due to outcome heterogeneity.
  Pooled AI accuracy for CVM staging was high, with performance broadly comparable
  to expert clinicians, though confidence intervals remain wide given the small study
  pool. Risk of bias was assessed with QUADAS-2, revealing concerns around patient
  selection and reference standard blinding in several studies. Practically, AI-assisted
  CVM staging could reduce inter-examiner variability in growth assessment — a meaningful
  gain in orthodontic clinics where timing of functional appliance therapy depends
  on accurate maturation staging. Adoption should wait for prospective validation
  on diverse populations before replacing expert judgment.
