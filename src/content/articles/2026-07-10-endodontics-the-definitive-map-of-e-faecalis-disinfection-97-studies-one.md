  using a 13-item checklist adapted from JBI/CRIS guidance. Evidence was synthesized
  qualitatively and graded based on methodological limitations, relevance, consistency,

summaryDeep: 'Ninety-seven in vitro studies using extracted single-rooted human teeth
  were pooled to evaluate every major disinfection strategy against E. faecalis biofilms.
  Mechanical preparation alone reduced bacterial load but never achieved complete
  eradication. Sodium hypochlorite remains the gold-standard irrigant, but its efficacy
  is highly concentration- and contact-time-dependent; EDTA and CHX add complementary
  mechanisms when combined. Physical activation methods — passive ultrasonic irrigation,
  laser-activated irrigation, PIPS — consistently enhanced irrigant penetration and
  antimicrobial outcomes across studies. Antimicrobial photodynamic therapy (aPDT)
  showed adjunctive benefit but was parameter-sensitive and not ready to replace conventional
  irrigants. Calcium hydroxide as an interappointment medicament showed limited biofilm
  penetration. Nanoparticle and natural irrigants showed promise but remain inferior
  to NaOCl without further clinical validation. A critical caveat: only 28% of included
  studies were low risk of bias, limiting direct clinical translation — but the directional
  evidence is consistent enough to inform protocol design now.'
