summary: A comprehensive narrative review synthesises evidence that prior periodontal
  infection permanently reprograms local and systemic immune cells through epigenetic
  mechanisms — a phenomenon called trained immunity. Gingival fibroblasts, resident
  immune cells, and even hematopoietic stem cells acquire lower activation thresholds,
  explaining why periodontitis recurs despite successful bacterial load reduction.
  This reframes recurrence as a biological inevitability, not a compliance failure.
summaryDeep: 'The review draws on mechanistic, multi-omics, and clinical data to argue
  that periodontal recalcitrance is driven by at least four levels of immunological
  imprinting: local epigenetic reprogramming of gingival fibroblasts and resident
  immune cells, formation of tertiary lymphoid structures that sustain local inflammation,
  systemic transmission of pro-inflammatory signals (IL-1β, type I interferons) to
  bone marrow hematopoietic progenitors, and clonal hematopoiesis. Together, these
  create a hyper-responsive microenvironment that reactivates at lower bacterial thresholds
  than in treatment-naive tissue. The clinical implication is sobering: supportive
  periodontal therapy is not just maintenance — it is active management of a biologically
  altered host. The review is narrative and mechanistic rather than clinical-trial
  based, so it does not change protocols directly, but it provides the biological
  rationale for why lifelong SPT intervals should be short and why systemic anti-inflammatory
  strategies are an active area of research.'
