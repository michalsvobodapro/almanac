summaryDeep: Digital transfer workflows in orthognathic surgery have long debated
  PSI versus CAD/CAM splints, but prior meta-analyses were hampered by methodological
  heterogeneity and axis-pooled analyses that obscured directional errors. This review
  disaggregated accuracy into mediolateral, anteroposterior, and superoinferior deviations
  — and PSI outperformed splints on all three (all P < 0.05). Angular accuracy (pitch,
  roll, yaw) also favored PSI. The 12 included RCTs were assessed with RoB 2.0; 18
  cohort studies used Newcastle–Ottawa. Operative time and postoperative complication
  rates were comparable between workflows, meaning the accuracy advantage of PSI comes
  without a time or safety penalty. For surgical planning and execution in complex
  bimaxillary cases, the evidence now clearly favors PSI. Cost and access remain the
  practical barriers in most Czech and EU hospital settings.
