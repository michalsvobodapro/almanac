excerpt: 'This study aimed to assess the skeletal, dental, and soft-tissue changes
  after total maxillary arch distalization using infrazygomatic crest (IZC) miniscrews
  followed by fixed orthodontic treatment in patients with moderate Class II malocclusion.
  31 patients (including 11 males and 20 females, mean age of 15.48 ± 2.81 years)
  were treated with fixed orthodontic treatment combined with IZC miniscrew-assisted
  distalization. After the leveling of the maxillary dental arch, IZC screws (diameter:
  2 mm; length: 12 mm) were inserted between the maxillary first and second molars,
  applying 300 g of force with elastomeric chains. Pre-treatment (T1) and post-treatment
  (T2) lateral cephalometric radiographs were obtained. The average treatment duration
  was 2.40 ± 0.86 years. In skeletal measurements, no significant change was detected
  in the mandibular plane angle. In dentoalveolar measurements, the maxillary first
  molars were distalized by 2.59 mm, intruded by 0.52 mm, and showed 1.71° of distal
  tipping. Maxillary incisors were retruded (−1.16 mm), while mandibular incisors
  demonstrated protrusion (L1-NB: +0.66 mm). A significant increase of 1.67° was observed
  in the SN/OP angle. Overjet and overbite were reduced by 2.33 mm and 1.28 mm, respectively.
  Upper lip retrusion (−0.88 mm) indicated favorable soft tissue changes. Total maxillary
  arch distalization using IZC miniscrews followed by fixed orthodontic treatment

summaryDeep: 'Infrazygomatic crest (IZC) miniscrews offer skeletal anchorage for total
  maxillary arch distalization, avoiding the compliance demands of headgear and the
  tooth sacrifice of extraction protocols. This retrospective cohort (n=31, mean age
  15.5 years, 2.4-year treatment) quantifies the dentoalveolar and soft-tissue outcomes:
  maxillary first molars distalized 2.59 mm with 1.71° of distal tipping and 0.52
  mm intrusion; overjet reduced 2.33 mm; overbite reduced 1.28 mm. Skeletal measurements
  showed no significant change in mandibular plane angle — a key advantage for hyperdivergent
  patients. Soft-tissue response was favourable (upper lip retrusion −0.88 mm). Limitations
  include the retrospective design, absence of a control group, and the relatively
  small sample. The SN/OP angle increase of 1.67° warrants monitoring for occlusal
  plane canting. Overall, IZC-assisted distalization is a viable non-extraction option
  for moderate Class II correction in growing patients.'
