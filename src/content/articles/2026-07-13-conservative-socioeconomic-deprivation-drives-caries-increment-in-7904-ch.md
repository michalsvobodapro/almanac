summary: A prospective cohort tracked 7,904 schoolchildren in Liaoning Province over
  one year; caries prevalence jumped from 31.1% to 50.6% and incidence reached 36.2%.
  Structural equation modelling identified socioeconomic status as the dominant upstream
  driver, acting both directly and through dietary habits. Social isolation at school
  was also independently associated with caries increment.
summaryDeep: Caries epidemiology rarely uses structural equation modelling (SEM) to
  untangle causal pathways, so this prospective cohort stands out methodologically.
  With 7,904 children followed from 2024 to 2025, the one-year caries incidence of
  36.2% (ΔDMFT/dmft ≥1) is alarming and consistent with Chinese national data. SEM
  confirmed socioeconomic factors as the apex driver — shaping diet, which in turn
  shapes caries risk — while also showing a direct socioeconomic effect independent
  of diet. The unexpected protective association with higher weight status is flagged
  by the authors as needing further investigation and should not be over-interpreted.
  Social isolation as an independent predictor is novel and plausible (stress-related
  dietary changes, reduced parental oversight). The one-year observational window
  precludes strong causal claims, but the SEM framework is a template for designing
  targeted interventions at the population level.
