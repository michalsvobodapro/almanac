  practice, and it is frequently required in forensic and legal investigations. This
  study aimed to evaluate the intra- and inter-observer reliability of dental development
  staging among forensic practitioners using two widely applied staging systems, according
  to Demirjian et al. 1973 and Moorrees et al. 1963. The study included 250 anonymized
  panoramic radiographs of individuals between 6 and 14 years (median age, 10 years),
  comprising 51.2% males and 48.8% females. Eighteen observers with prior training
  in DAE independently assessed tooth development on panoramic radiographs using the
  Demirjian and Moorrees methods, with each method evaluated in two separate rounds.

summaryDeep: 'The study is the most methodologically rigorous head-to-head reliability
  comparison of the two dominant dental age estimation systems to date, using 18 trained
  observers, 250 anonymized OPGs, and both weighted and unweighted kappa alongside
  Gwet''s AC1/AC2 — a combination that exposes adjacent-stage disagreement that simpler
  analyses mask. The key finding: both systems are highly reliable in expert hands,
  but the Gwet''s AC1 values (as low as 0.306 for some procedures) reveal that when
  observers disagree, they do so on adjacent developmental stages, not distant ones.
  This matters for forensic and legal contexts where a one-stage error can shift an
  age estimate by months and affect legal outcomes. For dental students entering forensic
  or paediatric rotations, the practical message is that both systems are defensible,
  but documentation of staging rationale is essential when cases are borderline. The
  study does not resolve which system is more accurate against known ages — that would
  require a ground-truth dataset.'
