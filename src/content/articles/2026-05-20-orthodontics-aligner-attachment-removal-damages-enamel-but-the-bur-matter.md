summary: Removing composite attachments at the end of aligner treatment inevitably
  involves some enamel sacrifice, but how much depends heavily on the removal bur
  rather than the adhesive system used. This study quantifies enamel surface changes
  across different adhesive-bur combinations, finding that carbide finishing burs
  cause significantly less enamel loss than tungsten carbide or Arkansas stones. The
  adhesive brand is largely irrelevant to the outcome.
summaryDeep: 'Clear aligner therapy has normalised composite attachment placement
  and removal as routine procedures, yet the enamel consequences of removal are underreported
  in patient consent and clinical training. This study bonds attachments using three
  adhesive systems to extracted premolars, then removes them with four different bur
  types under standardised conditions, quantifying enamel loss via profilometry and
  SEM surface characterisation. The bur type accounts for the overwhelming majority
  of variance in enamel surface damage — carbide finishing burs (12-bladed) produce
  the smoothest surfaces and least depth of cut, while coarse tungsten carbide burs
  and Arkansas stones leave significant roughness and subsurface cracking. Adhesive
  system choice has no statistically significant effect on post-removal enamel integrity.
  The clinical message is direct: invest in the right finishing bur, not the premium
  adhesive. This is particularly relevant in Czech dental education, where attachment
  removal technique is rarely formalised in the curriculum.'
