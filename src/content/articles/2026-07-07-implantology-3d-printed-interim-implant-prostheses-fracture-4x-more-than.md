summaryDeep: The 3D-printing revolution in dental prosthetics has outpaced the clinical
  evidence, and this crossover RCT is a useful corrective. Twenty fully edentulous
  patients each wore both a milled PMMA and a hybrid ceramic 3D-printed complete-arch
  interim prosthesis for three months in randomised sequence. The fracture/complication
  rate for 3D-printed restorations was 45% versus 10% for milled PMMA — a 35 percentage-point
  difference that is both statistically and clinically significant. Passive fit, assessed
  objectively, was equivalent between materials, as was colour change (ΔE00 ~4.7 for
  both). Patient-reported quality of life improved with both. The mechanical failure
  rate for 3D-printed restorations is concerning for a material increasingly marketed
  as the future of digital prosthetics. The likely culprit is the layer-by-layer polymerisation
  creating internal stress concentrations under full-arch occlusal loading. Until
  material science catches up, milled PMMA should remain the default for full-arch
  interim implant work.
