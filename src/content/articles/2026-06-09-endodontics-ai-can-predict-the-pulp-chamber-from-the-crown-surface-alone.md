summaryDeep: 'Localising the pulp chamber before cutting is one of the underappreciated
  safety problems in endodontics, especially in calcified or heavily restored teeth
  where the usual visual landmarks disappear. This study trained DeepSDF models —
  a class of neural network that learns signed distance functions to represent 3D
  shapes — on paired datasets of external molar crowns and their corresponding pulp
  chamber geometries. The model learns the statistical relationship between outer
  crown morphology and internal pulp anatomy, then predicts the chamber volume from
  a surface scan alone. The reported performance metrics suggest clinically meaningful
  spatial accuracy, though the paper is limited to molars and the training set will
  need to be large and diverse before this generalises. The practical upshot is significant:
  a chairside or lab scan of the crown could generate a patient-specific pulp chamber
  prediction that feeds directly into guided endodontic access systems, reducing the
  risk of perforation without mandatory pre-op CBCT radiation.'
