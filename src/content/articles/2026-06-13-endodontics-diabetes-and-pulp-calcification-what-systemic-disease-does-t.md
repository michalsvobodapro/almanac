summary: Diabetes mellitus drives pulp calcification — 'hardening the heart of the

summaryDeep: 'This BDJ piece examines the pathophysiological relationship between
  diabetes and pulpal changes, with a focus on calcification and fibrosis that progressively
  obliterate the pulp chamber and canal space. Hyperglycaemia promotes advanced glycation
  end-products and oxidative stress within pulp tissue, accelerating mineralisation
  and reducing the pulp''s regenerative capacity. For the endodontist or GP attempting
  root canal treatment on a diabetic patient, this translates to narrower, more calcified
  canals, higher rates of missed canals, and potentially altered pain responses that
  complicate diagnosis. The clinical message is practical: when treatment planning
  a diabetic patient, factor in the likelihood of calcified anatomy, consider CBCT
  for complex cases, and counsel the patient on the increased procedural difficulty.
  It also reinforces why periodontal and endodontic disease in diabetic patients tends
  to be more severe and harder to resolve.'
