summary: A new FDI World Dental Federation report makes the case that employers and
  occupational health systems are an underused lever for oral health promotion — reaching
  adults who rarely initiate dental visits on their own. The policy angle matters

summaryDeep: The FDI report draws on global data to argue that workplace settings
  offer consistent, repeated access to adult populations with poor dental attendance
  — a demographic that falls outside school-based programmes and often skips primary
  care. Proposed interventions range from on-site screening and subsidised dental
  benefits to oral health literacy campaigns embedded in occupational health curricula.
  The report is explicitly aimed at policymakers and employers rather than clinicians,
  but its framing of oral health as a productivity and systemic-health issue is the
  kind of argument that moves budgets. For students in the Czech context, where workplace
  health programmes exist within the broader EU occupational safety framework, this
  is relevant background for public health coursework and any future advocacy role.
  It won't change what you do chairside, but it shapes the system you'll practise
  in.
