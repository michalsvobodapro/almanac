summary: An in-vitro study optimises the concentration of TPO photoinitiator and post-curing
  duration in a 3D-printed restorative resin, finding that both variables independently
  affect flexural strength and colour stability. There is no single universal setting
  — the optimal combination depends on the target property. Labs and clinicians adopting
  in-office 3D printing need to validate their own post-cure protocols rather than
  defaulting to manufacturer defaults.
summaryDeep: 'As desktop resin printers move chairside, the assumption that manufacturer-recommended
  post-cure settings are universally optimal is being tested. This study systematically
  varied TPO (diphenyl(2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl)phosphine oxide) concentration and post-curing
  time, measuring the resulting flexural strength, hardness, and colour shift (ΔE).
  The findings show a non-linear relationship: increasing TPO concentration improves
  degree of conversion up to a threshold, beyond which excess photoinitiator acts
  as a plasticiser and degrades mechanical properties. Post-curing time follows a
  similar curve for colour — longer exposure improves strength but accelerates yellowing.
  The practical implication is that in-office 3D-printing workflows require material-specific
  validation, not just adherence to a generic protocol. For a dental student in a
  clinic adopting printed temporaries or inlays, this means understanding that the
  resin brand, printer light spectrum, and post-cure unit are a system — changing
  one element invalidates the others. The study is purely in vitro and does not address
  clinical wear or marginal adaptation.'
