summaryDeep: 'Surgically assisted rapid maxillary expansion is a well-established
  procedure for adult transverse deficiency, but the optimal retention protocol after
  the expansion phase remains contested. This observational cohort of 65 patients
  compares four post-SARME retention strategies — no appliance, transpalatal arch
  alone, fixed orthodontic appliances alone, and the combination — using digitised
  maxillary models at preoperative, 4-month, and 10-month timepoints. The primary
  metrics are palatal area, volume, and height. The headline result: no between-group
  differences reached statistical significance for any variable, and within-group
  changes in palatal area were significant in three of the four groups — meaning relapse
  occurred regardless of what retention device was used. The transpalatal arch, long
  prescribed as a post-SARME staple, does not appear to confer measurable skeletal
  benefit within this timeframe. The observational design and 10-month ceiling are
  limitations; skeletal changes beyond a year and dental arch width data would strengthen
  the picture. For orthodontic students, this is a useful prompt to question protocol
  inertia: retention choices after SARME may need to be driven by dental alignment
  goals rather than skeletal stability expectations.'
