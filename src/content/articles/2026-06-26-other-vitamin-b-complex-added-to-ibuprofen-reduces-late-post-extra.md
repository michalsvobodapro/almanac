summary: A double-blind split-mouth RCT in 17 patients tests vitamin B complex as
  an adjunct to ibuprofen after bilateral mandibular third molar extraction. The B-complex
  arm shows significantly lower pain at 48–72 h and better soft-tissue healing at
  3 weeks. The sample is small and the effect size needs replication, but the intervention
  is cheap, safe, and easy to prescribe.
summaryDeep: 'Post-operative pain management after third molar surgery typically relies
  on NSAIDs, with or without corticosteroids. This split-mouth RCT introduces vitamin
  B complex — a combination of B1, B6, and B12 with established neuroregenerative
  and anti-inflammatory properties — as an adjunct to ibuprofen. With only 17 patients,
  statistical power is limited, but the within-patient design controls for confounders
  effectively. Pain reduction is significant at 48–72 h (the period when NSAID efficacy
  often wanes) and soft-tissue healing is improved at 3 weeks on a validated scale.
  The biological rationale is plausible: B vitamins modulate prostaglandin synthesis
  and support peripheral nerve repair. The authors appropriately call for larger RCTs
  before practice change. For students, this is a good example of how a low-cost,
  low-risk adjunct can be tested rigorously — and why n=17 is not enough to change
  prescribing habits, even when the p-values are favourable.'
