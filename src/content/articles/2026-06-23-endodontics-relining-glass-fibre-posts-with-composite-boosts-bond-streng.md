summaryDeep: Non-circular root canals — common in premolars and anterior teeth — leave
  gaps between a standard circular fibre post and the canal wall, creating stress
  concentrations and weak adhesive interfaces. This in-vitro study compared conventional
  glass fibre posts against composite-relined (customised) posts in 40 premolars,
  testing push-out bond strength immediately and after 6,000 thermocycles. Relined
  posts produced significantly higher bond strength (p = 0.006), while thermocycling
  had no significant effect on either group — a reassuring durability finding. The
  relining technique involves adapting uncured composite to the post before luting,
  creating a custom-fit that maximises dentine contact. The study is limited by its
  in-vitro design and single-canal morphology type, but the technique is low-cost,
  requires no additional armamentarium, and the bond-strength advantage is clinically
  plausible. For a student restoring a post-RCT premolar with an oval canal, this
  is a practical technique worth adding to the repertoire.
