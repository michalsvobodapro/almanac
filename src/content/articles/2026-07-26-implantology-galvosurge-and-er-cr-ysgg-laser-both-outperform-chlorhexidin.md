summary: An in vitro study on 48 Straumann SLA implants contaminated with S. aureus
  biofilm found that both GalvoSurge electrolytic cleaning and Er,Cr:YSGG laser irradiation
  reduced bacterial load significantly more than 0.12% chlorhexidine. CHX, the most
  widely used chairside option, performed worst among active treatments. The findings
  add to a growing body of evidence questioning CHX monotherapy in peri-implantitis.
summaryDeep: Peri-implantitis decontamination remains one of implantology's unsolved
  problems, and SLA surfaces — with their rough, thread-retentive geometry — are particularly
  hostile to chemical antiseptics. This controlled in vitro study used 3D-printed
  peri-implant defect models to standardize geometry, then applied three clinically
  available decontamination protocols. The untreated control averaged 5.09 log₁₀ CFU/implant;
  CHX reduced this to 4.03 log₁₀; GalvoSurge and Er,Cr:YSGG laser achieved greater
  reductions (exact values not fully reported in the excerpt but statistically significant
  versus CHX). The study is in vitro and uses a single-species S. aureus biofilm rather
  than the polymicrobial biofilm of clinical peri-implantitis, which limits direct
  translation. Nevertheless, the consistent underperformance of CHX across multiple
  in vitro models is a signal that clinical protocols relying solely on CHX irrigation
  during peri-implantitis surgery may be inadequate. GalvoSurge is already CE-marked
  and clinically available in Europe.
