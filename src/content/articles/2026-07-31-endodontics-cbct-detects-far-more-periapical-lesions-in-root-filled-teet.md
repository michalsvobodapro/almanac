summary: A systematic review and meta-analysis compared the prevalence of apical periodontitis
  in root-filled teeth as detected by periapical radiography versus CBCT. CBCT consistently
  identified substantially more lesions, meaning that conventional radiographic follow-up
  significantly underestimates treatment failure rates. This has direct implications
  for how endodontic outcomes are reported and how retreatment decisions are made.
summaryDeep: 'The review pooled prevalence data from studies using either periapical
  radiographs or CBCT to assess apical periodontitis in root-filled teeth, finding
  that CBCT-based estimates were markedly higher — a gap that reflects the well-known
  limitations of 2D imaging in detecting early or buccally/lingually positioned lesions.
  The clinical consequence is two-fold: first, published success rates for root canal
  treatment based on periapical radiographs are likely inflated; second, patients
  presenting with persistent symptoms but a ''clear'' periapical radiograph may still
  harbour active disease detectable by CBCT. For endodontic training and practice,
  this reinforces the case for CBCT in equivocal follow-up scenarios, while also demanding
  that future outcome studies specify their imaging modality. The finding also contextualises
  the single-visit versus two-visit RCT in today''s digest — both studies used CBCT,
  which is the appropriate standard.'
