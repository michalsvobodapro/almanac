summary: 'Osteoporotic patients on bisphosphonates or denosumab face a measurable
  MRONJ risk when dental implants are placed — but the absolute numbers are lower
  than clinical anxiety suggests. This evidence-based review quantifies that risk
  and offers a framework for informed consent and treatment planning. The so-what:
  blanket contraindication is not supported; individualised risk stratification is.'
summaryDeep: 'Antiresorptive drugs — bisphosphonates and denosumab — are prescribed
  to millions of osteoporotic patients, many of whom also need implant-supported prostheses.
  The central clinical question has always been whether implant surgery tips these
  patients into MRONJ, and this Evidence-Based Dentistry synthesis finally puts numbers
  on it. The review draws on the best available cohort and case-control data to estimate
  incidence rates and identify modifying factors: drug type, cumulative dose, route
  of administration, and systemic comorbidities all shift the risk profile. Crucially,
  the evidence does not support a blanket contraindication to implants in this population
  — absolute MRONJ incidence remains low when patients are appropriately selected
  and managed. The practical upshot for clinic: a structured pre-implant protocol
  including a drug holiday discussion with the prescribing physician, thorough informed
  consent documenting the residual risk, and close post-operative monitoring is the
  defensible standard of care. For a Prague dental student, this is also directly
  relevant to the EU''s pharmacovigilance framework, which increasingly expects documented
  risk assessment in the patient record before elective surgical procedures.'
