summaryDeep: 'The review systematically walks through the RCT workflow — preoperative
  diagnosis, access, canal negotiation, shaping, irrigation, and obturation — and
  asks, for each step, what AI or digital augmentation is available and how well it
  performs. CBCT-based AI for periapical lesion detection and working-length confirmation
  via electronic apex locators are flagged as clinically validated. Robotic handpiece
  systems and real-time irrigation monitoring are positioned as near-term but not
  yet standard-of-care. The authors are candid that many ''smart'' claims in the literature
  are backed by in-vitro or small-cohort data only. For a dentistry student in Prague,
  this is a useful framework: it separates the tools worth learning now from the ones
  worth watching. The review also highlights that AI-assisted diagnosis is already
  influencing referral decisions in specialist practice, which has direct implications
  for how general practitioners document and triage pulpal cases.'
