  lesions remains a clinical challenge. This retrospective cohort study aimed to evaluate
  the clinical and radiographic outcomes of a staged protocol utilizing extended calcium
  hydroxide medication followed by a bioceramic apical barrier, and to identify prognostic
  factors influencing strict radiographic healing. Methods A total of 124 immature
  permanent teeth with necrotic pulps and open apices, treated at a single center
  between January 2021 and December 2024, were included. The protocol mandated intracanal
  calcium hydroxide dressing for a minimum of 1 month, periodically replaced until
  absolute canal dryness was achieved, followed by the placement of an iRoot BP Plus
  apical barrier. Periapical lesion areas were quantitatively measured on calibrated

summaryDeep: 'Managing open-apex necrotic teeth is one of endodontics'' toughest problems:
  the canal won''t seal, the apex won''t close, and regenerative protocols often stall.
  This single-centre retrospective cohort (n=124, 2021–2024) tested a staged approach
  — intracanal Ca(OH)₂ changed repeatedly until absolute dryness, then iRoot BP Plus
  bioceramic barrier placement. Strict radiographic success (complete lesion resolution,
  asymptomatic) reached 83.1%; adding ''improved'' cases (functional, quantifiable
  shrinkage) pushes the figure to 96.8%. Mean periapical lesion area collapsed from
  50.8 ± 47.7 mm² to 4.9 ± 25.8 mm². Multivariate regression identified large initial
  lesion size and persistent exudate as predictors of non-strict success — useful
  for pre-treatment counselling. The retrospective design and single centre limit
  generalisability, and 12 months is short for immature teeth. Still, the numbers
  are compelling enough to inform protocol choice when regeneration is not feasible.'
