summary: 'Universal adhesives degrade primarily at the hybrid layer, where incomplete
  resin infiltration leaves exposed collagen vulnerable to hydrolysis. This review
  maps biomimetic remineralisation strategies — including ACP-loaded primers, peptide
  analogs, and MMP inhibitors — that can rebuild mineral within that zone and measurably
  extend bond longevity. The clinical upshot: material selection and application protocol
  matter far more than the adhesive brand name.'
summaryDeep: 'Bond failure at the hybrid layer is the single biggest driver of composite
  restoration longevity, yet most clinical guidance still focuses on etching time
  and adhesive generation rather than what happens to the demineralised dentine over
  years. This paper systematically reviews biomimetic approaches — amorphous calcium
  phosphate (ACP) carriers, leucine-rich amelogenin peptide (LRAP) analogs, carbodiimide
  (EDC) cross-linking, and chlorhexidine-loaded nanoparticles — that target the exposed
  collagen scaffold left behind after acid etching. Key finding: combining an MMP
  inhibitor with a remineralising agent produces synergistic gains in microtensile
  bond strength after thermocycling aging, outperforming either strategy alone. For
  a student in clinic, this reframes the adhesive step: the goal isn''t just wetting
  dentine, it''s creating conditions for secondary mineralisation. Expect these strategies
  to migrate into next-generation universal adhesive formulations within the next
  product cycle.'
