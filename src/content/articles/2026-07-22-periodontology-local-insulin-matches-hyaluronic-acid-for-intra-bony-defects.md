  of locally applied insulin versus hyaluronic acid (HA), each combined with absorbable
  collagen sponge (ACS), using the single flap approach (SFA) in the management of
  periodontal intra-bony defects. Materials and methods 21 periodontitis patients
  with intra-bony defects were randomly assigned to 3 groups: group I (SFA + insulin
  + ACS; n = 7), group II (SFA + HA + ACS; n = 7), and group III (SFA + ACS; n = 7).

  measured at baseline and 6 months; postoperative pain, using visual analogue scale
  (VAS) at 24 and 48 h; and radiographic parameters, including radiographic linear
  defect depth (RLDD) and bone density, assessed at baseline and 6 months. Results

summary: A three-arm pilot RCT (n=21) compared single-flap approach alone versus with
  hyaluronic acid or insulin soaked in absorbable collagen sponge for periodontal
  intra-bony defects. Both adjuncts improved CAL and PPD significantly versus control
  at 6 months, with no significant difference between insulin and HA. Insulin costs
  a fraction of commercial HA products.
summaryDeep: 'Seven patients per arm received single-flap approach (SFA) with either
  insulin + collagen sponge, hyaluronic acid + collagen sponge, or collagen sponge
  alone. Primary outcome was clinical attachment level gain at 6 months; secondary
  outcomes included probing depth, radiographic linear defect depth, bone density,
  and early postoperative pain. Both active groups outperformed control on all primary
  and radiographic measures. The insulin group''s performance is the headline: insulin
  is cheap, widely available, and already used off-label in wound healing. The trial
  is registered (NCT05746676) and the methodology is sound for a pilot, but n=7 per
  arm means it is underpowered to detect between-group differences. Larger confirmatory
  RCTs with longer follow-up are essential before insulin enters routine periodontal
  regeneration protocols. Still, this is the kind of low-cost biomaterial signal worth
  tracking.'
