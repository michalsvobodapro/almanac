summary: A mixed-methods RCT at Karolinska Institutet randomized 44 fifth-semester
  dental students to 3 hours of self-directed haptic VR simulator (HVRS) training

summaryDeep: 'Haptic VR simulators are increasingly marketed as adjuncts to preclinical
  dental training, but evidence for skill transfer to physical preparation tasks has
  been limited. This RCT — one of the more rigorous designs in dental education research
  — randomized 44 students at Karolinska to either 3 hours of self-directed HVRS training
  on a maxillary first molar crown preparation or a control condition, then assessed
  both groups on a phantom-head typodont preparation scored by a blinded examiner
  across 8 PrepCheck criteria. The null result is the finding: HVRS training did not
  improve objective preparation quality. Students in the HVRS group did report greater
  self-confidence, which is not trivial — but confidence without competence is a clinical
  risk. Manual dexterity (Grooved Pegboard Test) was assessed exploratorily and showed
  no group differences. The study is limited by small sample size and a single 3-hour
  dose of training; longer or more structured HVRS protocols might yield different
  results. For now, HVRS should be positioned as a supplement to, not a replacement
  for, conventional typodont training.'
