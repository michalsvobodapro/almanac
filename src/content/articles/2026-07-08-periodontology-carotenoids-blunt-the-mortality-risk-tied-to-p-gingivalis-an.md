summary: In 7,043 NHANES III participants followed for a median 25.8 years, high serum
  P. gingivalis IgG was associated with elevated all-cause and cause-specific mortality
  — but that association was significantly attenuated in individuals with high serum
  carotenoids. The interaction is robust across Cox models accounting for complex
  survey design. Dietary antioxidant status appears to be a genuine modifier of periodontal
  pathogen-linked systemic risk.
summaryDeep: 'This prospective cohort leverages NHANES III''s long follow-up (2,646
  deaths identified) to test whether antioxidant status modifies the well-documented
  link between P. gingivalis burden and mortality. The answer is yes, and the effect
  is not trivial: participants with both high P. gingivalis IgG and low carotenoids
  carried the greatest mortality hazard, while those with high carotenoids showed
  a substantially reduced risk even at elevated IgG levels. Survey-weighted Cox models
  were used to handle NHANES''s complex sampling, adding methodological credibility.
  The mechanism is plausible — carotenoids quench reactive oxygen species that P.
  gingivalis-driven inflammation generates — but causality cannot be established from
  observational data. What this does is sharpen the systemic-periodontology conversation:
  it''s not just ''does perio disease increase mortality risk?'' but ''in whom, and
  what modifies it?'' For clinicians, it adds a dietary/nutritional dimension to periodontal
  risk counseling that is rarely discussed chairside.'
