excerpt: Background Periodontitis involves dysregulated immunity where the NLRP3 inflammasome
  plays a key role, while the role of TRIM31, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, remains unknown
  in periodontitis. Methods Human gingival fibroblasts (HGFs) and macrophages were
  stimulated with LPS and ATP; TRIM31 was overexpressed via AAV, and NLRP3 was knocked
  out via CRISPR; periodontitis was induced in WT and NLRP3-KO mice treated with AAV-TRIM31;
  bone loss, osteoclasts, and apoptosis were assessed. Results TRIM31 was downregulated
  in inflammation and correlated with M2 polarization. TRIM31 overexpression protected
  HGFs, promoted M2 polarization, and bound to NLRP3, thereby promoting K48-linked
  ubiquitination and degradation. In vivo, TRIM31 reduced bone loss, osteoclasts,
  and apoptosis; these effects were abolished in NLRP3-KO cells and mice. Conclusion
  TRIM31 negatively regulates periodontal inflammation via ubiquitin-dependent NLRP3
  degradation.

summary: TRIM31, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, is downregulated in inflamed periodontal
  tissue and acts as a brake on the NLRP3 inflammasome by promoting its K48-linked
  ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. Overexpressing TRIM31 in a mouse periodontitis
  model reduced bone loss, osteoclast activity, and apoptosis. A mechanistic finding
  — too early for clinical translation, but it identifies a druggable target in the
  NLRP3 pathway.
summaryDeep: 'The NLRP3 inflammasome is a central driver of IL-1β-mediated periodontal
  bone destruction, and pharmacological NLRP3 inhibition is an active therapeutic
  target. This study introduces TRIM31 as an endogenous negative regulator: it is
  downregulated in LPS/ATP-stimulated human gingival fibroblasts and macrophages,
  and its overexpression via AAV vector promotes M2 macrophage polarisation, protects
  fibroblasts from apoptosis, and physically binds NLRP3 to drive its K48-ubiquitination
  and proteasomal degradation. In a murine ligature-induced periodontitis model, AAV-TRIM31
  significantly reduced alveolar bone loss and osteoclast numbers; these effects were
  abolished in NLRP3-knockout animals, confirming on-target specificity. The evidence
  grade is low — cell and mouse data only — and AAV delivery is not a near-term clinical
  strategy. However, the TRIM31–NLRP3 axis is a credible mechanistic node that could
  inform small-molecule or gene-therapy approaches to host-modulation therapy in periodontitis.'
