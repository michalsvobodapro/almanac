summaryDeep: 'The study is the latest entry in the ongoing MTA-versus-Biodentine debate,
  which has produced inconclusive clinical trial data for years. By shifting to a
  murine model with histological and cellular-level analysis, the authors can interrogate
  the mechanism rather than just the endpoint. The key finding is that bridge formation
  rates are comparable but the inflammatory and regenerative cell populations differ
  between materials in the early healing phase. MTA and Biodentine appear to recruit
  different cellular actors to achieve a similar structural outcome — which may explain
  why long-term pulp vitality data sometimes diverge even when short-term bridge formation
  looks equivalent. The murine model limits direct translation: pulp anatomy, immune
  response, and clinical contamination conditions differ substantially from human
  molars. Still, this mechanistic data is useful context for the clinical choice,
  and it reinforces that Biodentine''s faster setting and easier handling don''t come
  at the cost of regenerative potential.'
