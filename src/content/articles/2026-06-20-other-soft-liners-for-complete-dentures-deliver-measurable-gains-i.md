summary: A meta-analysis of 32 studies (710 patients) confirms that soft-lined complete
  dentures significantly outperform hard-based dentures on bite force (+35 N), masticatory
  performance, pain (VAS), and quality of life. The effect holds regardless of soft
  liner type. For edentulous patients struggling with conventional dentures, this
  is an underused intervention with solid evidence.
summaryDeep: Soft liners and relining materials are often positioned as a niche or
  temporary solution, but this systematic review — the most comprehensive to date
  — challenges that framing. Thirty-two clinical studies covering 710 patients were
  included, with follow-up up to 6 months. Bite force improved by a mean of 35 N,
  masticatory performance and quality of life scores were significantly better, and
  pain on the VAS was reduced by over 14 points in the soft liner group. Electromyographic
  amplitude and duration did not differ significantly, suggesting the benefit is biomechanical
  and comfort-driven rather than neuromuscular. Crucially, the effect was consistent
  across different soft liner materials, meaning the class effect is robust. The 6-month
  follow-up ceiling is a limitation — long-term durability and maintenance burden
  remain open questions. For prosthodontic practice, soft lining should move from
  afterthought to first-line consideration in patients with atrophic ridges or persistent
  pain.
