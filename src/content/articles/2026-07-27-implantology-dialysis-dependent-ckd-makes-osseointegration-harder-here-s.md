summaryDeep: This review synthesizes the pathophysiology of osseointegration failure
  in dialysis-dependent chronic kidney disease, a patient group increasingly presenting
  for implant rehabilitation. Renal osteodystrophy disrupts bone turnover and hydroxyapatite
  crystallization; elevated PTH drives excessive resorption; uremic toxins suppress
  osteoblast activity and angiogenesis while promoting osteoclastogenesis; and anticoagulation
  during hemodialysis risks disrupting the early clot that initiates osseointegration.
  Comorbidities — diabetes, hypertension, anemia — compound microcirculatory compromise.
  The review notes that published success rates in CKD patients are lower than in
  healthy populations, with higher marginal bone loss and late failure rates, though
  it stops short of meta-analytic synthesis. Practical guidance includes scheduling
  surgery the day after dialysis (to minimize anticoagulant effect and fluid shifts),
  optimizing calcium-phosphate product and PTH preoperatively, and setting realistic
  patient expectations. As the CKD population grows in Europe, this is a clinical
