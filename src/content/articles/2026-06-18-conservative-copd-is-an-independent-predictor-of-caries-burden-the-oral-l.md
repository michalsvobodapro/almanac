summaryDeep: 'Using interviewer-administered questionnaires and clinical oral examinations
  in a Bangladeshi chest hospital, this study compared DMFT indices between 106 COPD
  patients and 106 matched non-COPD adults. COPD patients had significantly higher
  decayed, missing, and filled tooth counts (p < 0.001 for all components), and multiple
  linear regression confirmed COPD status as a strong independent predictor of total
  DMFT (B = 8.715) after adjusting for key covariates. The proposed mechanism runs
  through the oral–lung axis: oral dysbiosis and chronic aspiration of oral pathogens
  may exacerbate pulmonary inflammation, while COPD-related mouth breathing, inhaler
  use (which reduces salivary pH), and systemic inflammation all worsen oral health.
  Cross-sectional design prevents causal inference, and the single-center developing-country
  setting limits generalizability. Still, for a student, this reinforces a growing
  body of evidence linking respiratory and oral disease — and argues for routine dental
  screening in respiratory medicine settings.'
