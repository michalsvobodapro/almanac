summaryDeep: 'Deep margin elevation has been described in the literature as a conservative
  alternative to crown lengthening for subgingival restorations, allowing adhesive
  procedures to be performed supragingivally after composite elevation of the cervical
  margin. This cross-sectional survey (response rate 77.6%) found that while three-quarters
  of respondents knew the technique, clinical adoption was patchy. Most considered
  adhesive bonding to cervical or root dentin predictable (66%), yet when favorable
  conditions were present, a substantial proportion still defaulted to other approaches.
  The survey did not capture why — whether it is material uncertainty, technique sensitivity,
  lack of hands-on training, or medicolegal concern — which is its main limitation.
  For a dental student in Prague, this is a technique worth mastering early: it is
  conservative, preserves periodontium, and is increasingly expected in adhesive-focused
  European practice. The multicentric design (including European centers) adds some
  geographic relevance.'
