summary: 'A pilot in vitro study compared robot-assisted haptic guidance (RA) against
  freehand by a resident and by an experienced endodontist across three procedures:
  calcified canal access, fiber post removal, and endodontic microsurgery. RA-guided
  residents were faster and removed less tooth structure than freehand residents —
  and performed comparably to the expert. The technology constrains movement along
  a predefined path rather than providing real-time navigation.'
summaryDeep: 'Unlike dynamic navigation systems that display real-time positional
  feedback, the robot-assisted haptic platform physically constrains the handpiece
  to a pre-planned trajectory, preventing the operator from deviating. Extracted teeth
  were divided into RA-resident, freehand-resident, and freehand-expert groups for
  each of the three modalities. Across all three, RA significantly reduced operative
  time and CBCT-measured tooth structure loss compared with the freehand resident,
  and the RA-resident results were statistically indistinguishable from the experienced
  endodontist''s freehand work. This is a pilot study with a small sample and no clinical
  validation — results cannot be extrapolated to patients. But the concept is striking:
  haptic constraint as a leveler of operator skill. If this translates clinically,
  it has implications for training, for high-risk procedures in calcified teeth, and
  potentially for supervised student practice.'
