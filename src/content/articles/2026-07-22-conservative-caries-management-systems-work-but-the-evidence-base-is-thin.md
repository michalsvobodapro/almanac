excerpt: Introduction Caries care protocols (CCPs), commonly known as caries management
  systems, adopt a patient-centred, hygiene-focused approach to caries prevention.
  Although individual preventative strategies are well-studied, evidence on their
  combined efficacy is limited. The aim of this scoping review was to map and evaluate
  current evidence from clinical studies on the efficacy of CCPs in children and adults.
  Methods Primary outcomes were caries incidence (increment), progression and regression,
  and secondary outcomes were changes in caries risk from baseline to follow-up. Inclusion
  criteria were randomised controlled trials (RCTs) comparing CCP interventions with
  control groups receiving only standard preventive care within routine dental services.
  Titles and abstracts were screened from MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL and Web of Science
  databases, and data were extracted from full texts on study setting, methodology
  and outcomes. Quality was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute checklist.

  lower caries increment and progression from baseline, along with a greater reduction

summary: A scoping review of RCTs testing combined caries care protocols (CCPs) found
  only three eligible trials (n=1,186) from Australia, Greece, and Colombia. The two

  systems makes generalization difficult.
summaryDeep: 'CCPs bundle individual preventive strategies — risk assessment, dietary
  counseling, fluoride, sealants, recall intervals — into a coordinated patient-centred
  system. Despite widespread clinical advocacy for this approach, only three RCTs
  met inclusion criteria after screening MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, and Web of Science.
  Risk of bias was moderate in two studies and high in one. The positive outcomes
  in the higher-quality trials are encouraging, but the small evidence base, heterogeneous
  CCP definitions, and varying follow-up periods mean no specific protocol can be
  recommended over another. For a dental student in Prague, the takeaway is structural:
  the concept is sound and aligns with current preventive philosophy, but the field
  urgently needs larger, better-standardised RCTs before CCPs can be formally recommended
  in guidelines. The ČSK preventive framework implicitly endorses bundled prevention;
  this review quantifies how thin the RCT support actually is.'
