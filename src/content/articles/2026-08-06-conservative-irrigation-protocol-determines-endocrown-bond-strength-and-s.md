summary: In this in vitro study of 40 extracted molars, irrigation protocol had a
  highly significant effect on microtensile bond strength of CAD/CAM endocrowns (F
  = 127.01, p < 0.001). NaOCl + EDTA + sodium ascorbate (antioxidant) and QMix 2-in-1
  both outperformed NaOCl + EDTA alone, which in turn far exceeded distilled water

  ceramic.
summaryDeep: 'Endocrowns are increasingly the restoration of choice for endodontically
  treated posterior teeth, but the bond to pulp chamber dentin is vulnerable to the
  oxidizing effects of sodium hypochlorite irrigation. This in vitro study systematically
  tests four irrigation protocols against two CAD/CAM materials, providing a practical
  hierarchy. The antioxidant rescue effect of sodium ascorbate after NaOCl + EDTA
  is the headline finding: it restores bond strength to levels comparable with QMix,
  suggesting a simple chairside addition to the irrigation sequence before bonding.
  The low baseline bond strength in the distilled water control (8.6–8.9 MPa) underscores
  that EDTA smear-layer removal is non-negotiable for endocrown bonding. Thermocycling
  (10,000 cycles) was included, adding some durability signal. Limitations are inherent
  to in vitro design — clinical bond strength data in functioning endocrowns are still
  needed. Material type (LDS vs. VE) did not significantly modify the irrigation effect,
  which simplifies the clinical message.'
