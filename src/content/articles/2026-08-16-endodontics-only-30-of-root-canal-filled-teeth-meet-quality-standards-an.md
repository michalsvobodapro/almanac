excerpt: Objective The aim of this cross-sectional study was to assess the root canal
  filling quality and the frequency of apical periodontitis (AP) in root-canal-filled
  teeth in a randomly selected adult non-patient population in Sweden. Materials and
  methods In 2023, 295 root-canal-filled teeth were identified in 126 adults in a
  cross-sectional study in Jönköping, Sweden. Periapical radiographs were assessed
  by two calibrated observers. The periapical status was scored according to the periapical
  index (PAI) and then dichotomized as either healthy (PAI 1-2) or diseased (PAI 3-5).
  The root canal filling quality was deemed as adequate/inadequate. An adequate root
  canal filling was considered to end 0.5-2 mm from the radiographic apex and to have
  no radiolucent voids. Results Adequate root canal fillings were seen in 89 (30.2%)
  of the teeth. AP was present in 94 (31.9%) of the teeth, and of these, 72 (76.6%)
  had an inadequate root canal filling. Of the 122 root-canal-filled molars, 101 (82.8%)
  had inadequate root canal filling and 57 (46.7%) had AP. Conclusion A large proportion
  of root-canal-filled teeth have inadequate root canal filling quality and AP, especially
  in molars. Both contributing factors and strategies for improvement require further
  study.

summaryDeep: 'Researchers in Jönköping examined periapical radiographs of 295 root-canal-filled
  teeth from 126 non-patient adults — a design that avoids the selection bias of clinic-based
  samples. An adequate root canal filling was defined as terminating 0.5–2 mm from
  the radiographic apex with no radiolucent voids; only 89 teeth (30.2%) qualified.
  Apical periodontitis (PAI ≥3) was detected in 94 teeth (31.9%), and 76.6% of those

  46.7% had AP. The findings align with prior European epidemiological data and reinforce
  that the quality gap is not marginal — it is the norm. For students and GDPs, this
  is a reminder that working length control and three-dimensional obturation are not
  academic niceties; they are the primary determinants of long-term periapical health.
  The authors call for further study of contributing factors, but the clinical implication
  is already clear: current standards of root canal treatment in general practice
  are failing most patients.'
