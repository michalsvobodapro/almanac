summaryDeep: Third- and fourth-year dental students, dental hygiene degree students,
  periodontics residents from five programmes, and clinical instructors at two Canadian
  universities each diagnosed five de-identified clinical cases spanning the spectrum
  of periodontal health and disease. Gold-standard diagnoses were established by board-certified
  periodontists. Underdiagnosis was the dominant error pattern (32.4%), followed by
  incorrect periodontal status identification (28.7%). Training level was a significant
  predictor of accuracy, but even residents made systematic errors. The study highlights
  a gap between the 2017 classification's theoretical adoption and its real-world
  implementation — a finding directly relevant to Czech dental education, where the
  EFP framework underpins both ČSK guidelines and the ESE curriculum. The cross-sectional
  design and two-institution sample limit generalisability, but the error patterns
  are consistent with earlier reports from European settings. For students, the practical
  implication is to practise staging and grading on real cases, not just memorise
  the classification table.
