summaryDeep: 'Short implants have long been offered as an augmentation-sparing alternative
  in the atrophic posterior jaw, but clinicians have lacked long-term survival data
  to back the conversation. This 12-year prospective multi-centre cohort enrolled
  45 patients receiving 95 splinted 6-mm implants loaded early in posterior regions.
  Clinical and radiographic outcomes — including marginal bone loss trajectories —
  remained stable well beyond the critical 5-year window, and patient-reported outcomes
  mirrored the objective findings. The splinting protocol appears to distribute occlusal
  load effectively enough to compensate for reduced implant length. For a dental student
  in Prague, the practical takeaway is clear: 6-mm implants with splinted restorations
  are no longer a compromise option backed only by short-term data — they are a documented
  12-year solution. This shifts the risk-benefit calculus against routine sinus lifts
  and ridge augmentations in suitable posterior cases.'
