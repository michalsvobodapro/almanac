summaryDeep: This study exposed BBB cell models to serum from patients with apical
  periodontitis (AP) before and after endodontic treatment, measuring tight junction
  protein expression, permeability, and inflammatory cytokine profiles. Pre-treatment
  serum disrupted BBB integrity and elevated IL-6 and TNF-α; post-treatment serum
  showed partial restoration of tight junction proteins and reduced inflammatory signalling.
  The model is a genuine step beyond correlation — it establishes a causal in vitro
  link between AP-derived systemic inflammation and BBB disruption. However, in vitro
  BBB models are notoriously poor predictors of in vivo CNS effects, and confounders
  (pain, stress, medication) in the patient serum are uncontrolled. This is low-grade
  evidence that generates a hypothesis worth testing in animal models. For a student,
  it's a compelling mechanistic story connecting endo to neurology — but 'too preliminary
  to change practice' is the honest summary.
