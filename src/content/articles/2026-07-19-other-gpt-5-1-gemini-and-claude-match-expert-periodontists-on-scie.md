summaryDeep: 'Nine independent periodontists blind-rated responses from ChatGPT (GPT-5.1),
  Gemini (2.5 Flash), Claude (Sonnet 4.5), and three expert periodontists across 20
  validated patient questions using a 5-point Likert scale. Scientific accuracy showed
  no significant difference between groups (p = 0.425) — a result that will surprise
  many clinicians still treating AI as a novelty. The chatbots outperformed experts
  on conciseness and clarity, while human experts retained an edge on empathy. Inter-rater
  agreement was assessed with ICC, and Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc comparisons controlled
  for multiple testing. The study is limited by its single-session design (first response
  only, no follow-up dialogue) and a question pool developed by the authors rather
  than drawn from real patient queries. Still, the practical implication is clear:
  current frontier LLMs are factually reliable enough for periodontal patient education,
  and the remaining gap is relational, not informational. Practices considering AI-assisted
  patient communication should focus their quality-control efforts on tone and empathy,
  not fact-checking.'
