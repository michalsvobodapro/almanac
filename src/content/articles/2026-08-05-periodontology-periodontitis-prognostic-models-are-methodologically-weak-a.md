summaryDeep: 'The review searched three databases and classified eligible studies
  into conceptual tools, category-based tools, and quantitative prognostic models,
  then critically appraised their methodological quality and historical evolution.
  The findings expose a gap between the clinical ubiquity of periodontitis prognosis
  tools and the evidence base underpinning them. Many widely used classification systems
  were developed without prospective validation cohorts, and quantitative models often
  lack external validation or transparent reporting of calibration and discrimination.
  For a student learning to assign tooth prognosis in the clinic, this review is a
  useful corrective: the confidence with which prognosis is communicated to patients
  often outstrips the evidence behind the tool being used. Published in JCPE, this
  carries authority and may catalyze guideline-level discussion about standardizing
  prognostic methodology in periodontology.'
