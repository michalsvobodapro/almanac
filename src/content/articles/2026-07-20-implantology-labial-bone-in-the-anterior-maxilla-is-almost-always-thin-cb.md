summaryDeep: Labial alveolar bone thickness in the maxillary anterior esthetic zone
  was assessed on sagittal CBCT sections at three standardized root levels (coronal
  third, mid-root, apical third) across all six maxillary anterior teeth in a Saudi
  university clinic cohort. Mean values were below 2 mm at all measurement points,
  with the thinnest readings at mid-root — the level most relevant to immediate implant
  placement and facial soft tissue support. No significant differences were found
  between right and left sides or across age groups, suggesting this is a stable anatomical
  characteristic rather than an age-related change. Sex-based differences emerged
  in the canine region. The findings are consistent with prior CBCT studies in other
  populations, reinforcing that surgeons should not assume adequate labial bone without
  imaging. For anterior implant planning, this data supports a default assumption
  of thin or absent labial plate, with treatment planning oriented toward buccal gap
  grafting and delayed provisionalization in most cases.
