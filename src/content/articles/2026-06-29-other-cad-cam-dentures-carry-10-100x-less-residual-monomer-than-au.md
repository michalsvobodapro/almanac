summaryDeep: 'Residual monomers — principally methyl methacrylate (MMA) and 2-HEMA
  — elute from incompletely polymerised PMMA denture bases and are the primary driver
  of allergic contact stomatitis, burning mouth syndrome, and occupational dermatitis
  in dental personnel. This systematic review (15 studies, 2,287 participants/specimens,
  PRISMA 2020, Cochrane RoB 2.0) establishes a clear hierarchy: autopolymerised resins
  carry 2.2–7.5% w/w residual MMA; heat-cured PMMA 0.11–0.90%; CAD/CAM-milled PMMA
  just 0.02–0.12%. The dose-dependent relationship between elution and mucosal/immunological
  reactivity is confirmed at HIGH GRADE certainty. For prosthodontic practice, the
  message is unambiguous: patients with suspected denture-related stomatitis or burning
  mouth should be offered CAD/CAM-milled alternatives, and dental technicians working
  with autopolymerised resins need rigorous occupational exposure controls. The 4–27%
  patch-test positivity range in symptomatic wearers is clinically actionable — referral
  for patch testing is warranted before fabricating replacement prostheses.'
