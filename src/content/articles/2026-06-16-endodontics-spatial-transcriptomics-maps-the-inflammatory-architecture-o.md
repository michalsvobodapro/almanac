summary: Using Visium HD spatial transcriptomics on healthy and inflamed human pulp
  tissue, this study charts the regional cellular crosstalk that drives pulpitis —
  identifying distinct inflammatory microenvironments and candidate therapeutic targets.

summaryDeep: 'Spatial transcriptomics goes beyond bulk RNA-seq by preserving tissue
  architecture: you see not just which genes are expressed, but where. Applied to
  human dental pulp, Visium HD reveals that inflamed pulp is not homogeneous — distinct
  regional niches show differential activation of immune cell populations, stromal
  signaling, and intercellular crosstalk that bulk methods would average away. The
  study delineates how healthy pulp transitions to irreversible pulpitis at the molecular
  level, and flags specific receptor-ligand interactions as potential intervention
  points. For a Prague dental student, the immediate clinical relevance is limited
  — this is foundational science — but it directly informs the long-standing question
  of why vital pulp therapy succeeds in some cases and fails in others. Future biomarker-based
  chairside tests for pulp vitality and reversibility could be built on exactly this
  kind of spatial atlas. The moderate evidence grade reflects the lab/tissue study
  design; translation to clinical diagnostics will require prospective validation.'
