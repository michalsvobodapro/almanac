summaryDeep: Implant-supported monolithic zirconia crowns are now a mainstream restorative
  choice, but the ti-base interface introduces a system-level variable that is easy
  to underestimate. This clinical cross-sectional study enrolled 31 patients receiving
  single-unit zirconia crowns in either a Flat or Tab ti-base system, using a fully
  digital workflow and metrology-grade software to quantify manufacturing trueness
  (RMS deviation), internal fit (triple-scan gap analysis), and proximal contact discrepancy.
  Both systems delivered clinically acceptable internal fit, but the comparison revealed
  that differences in proprietary scan bodies, CAD libraries, abutment height, and
  recommended cement gap — not just interface geometry — drive the outcome. The authors
  correctly frame this as a system-level comparison rather than an isolated test of
  ti-base design. For students entering a world of proliferating digital implant workflows,
  the lesson is that component mixing across manufacturers is where fit problems originate,
  and that 'digital' does not automatically mean 'accurate.'
