summaryDeep: 'Fiber post adhesion to root canal dentin is compromised by residual
  NaOCl, which oxidizes collagen and interferes with resin infiltration. Bioactive
  dentin modulators — natural compounds that interact with dentin matrix proteins
  or mineral phase — have been proposed as a conditioning step to counteract this
  effect. Using 40 bovine roots divided into four groups, this study evaluated bond
  strength and adhesive interface quality after NaOCl treatment with and without modulator
  application. While the excerpt does not detail which specific modulators were tested
  or the magnitude of bond strength differences, the study design (bovine roots, four
  groups, standardized endodontic treatment) is representative of the field''s standard
  in vitro approach. The clinical relevance is clear: if a simple conditioning step
  can meaningfully restore bond strength after NaOCl irrigation, it could be incorporated
  into post-space preparation protocols without adding significant chair time. The
  in vitro nature limits direct clinical translation, and long-term fatigue testing
  would be needed before protocol adoption.'
