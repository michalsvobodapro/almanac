  while gingival abrasion (GA) is the pathologic wear of gingiva by a foreign substance/object.

  caries and compromised aesthetics. This study aimed to investigate the prevalence

summaryDeep: 'Three hundred seventy-two subjects spanning periodontal health, gingivitis,
  and periodontitis were recruited at a Baghdad teaching clinic. Gingival recession
  prevalence was 42.1%, with significantly greater defect depth in periodontitis versus
  healthy subjects (3.4 vs. 2.1 mm). Gingival abrasion lesions were categorized by
  size; large lesions (>5 mm) were most prevalent in periodontitis patients. Adjusted
  logistic regression identified three independent predictors of gingival abrasion:
  hard-bristle toothbrush use (OR 3.19, 95% CI 1.38–7.38), presence of periodontitis
  (OR 12.02, 95% CI 1.83–79.13), and tooth type. The cross-sectional design prevents
  causal inference — recession and hard-bristle use may co-occur without one causing
  the other — but the magnitude of the toothbrush association is consistent with prior
  literature. The practical message is unchanged: soft-bristle brushes should be the
  default recommendation for all patients, and the conversation about brushing technique
  is especially important in patients already showing recession.'
