summary: An in-vitro study compared extracellular vesicles (EVs) from dental pulp
  stem cells (hDPSC-EVs) versus human blood plasma (hBP-EVs) for their ability to
  drive proliferation and differentiation of hDPSCs. hDPSC-derived EVs showed superior
  pro-regenerative effects. The findings support cell-free approaches to pulp regeneration
  as an alternative to direct stem-cell transplantation.
summaryDeep: 'The study addresses a practical limitation of stem-cell-based pulp regeneration:
  direct cell transplantation carries immunogenicity and regulatory hurdles that make
  clinical translation difficult. Extracellular vesicles — membrane-bound nanoparticles
  carrying proteins, lipids, and RNA — offer a cell-free alternative that is easier
  to standardize and store. The comparison between hDPSC-EVs and hBP-EVs is clinically
  relevant because blood-plasma-derived EVs (from PRP/PRF protocols already in clinical
  use) represent a more accessible source. The finding that hDPSC-EVs are superior
  in proliferative and differentiative capacity is mechanistically plausible given
  their tissue-specific cargo, but the in-vitro setting means translation to clinical
  pulp regeneration is still multiple steps away. For endodontics students, this is
  a useful window into the direction the field is moving — away from MTA-only apexification
  and toward biologically active regenerative protocols — but it does not yet change
  how you treat an immature necrotic tooth.'
