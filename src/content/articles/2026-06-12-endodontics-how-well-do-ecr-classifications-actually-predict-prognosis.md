summaryDeep: 'External cervical resorption (ECR) sits at the intersection of endodontics
  and periodontology, and its management hinges almost entirely on staging: how extensive
  is the lesion, has it breached the canal, and what does that mean for survival?
  Heithersay''s four-class system has been the clinical standard for decades; Patel
  et al.''s CBCT-based scheme added a third dimension and a canal-invasion subclassifier.
  This narrative review asks the harder question — do either system''s categories
  actually map onto predictable outcomes in the published literature? The answer is
  nuanced: higher Heithersay classes and Patel et al. grades with canal involvement
  consistently correlate with worse prognosis, but the evidence base is fragmented,
  retrospective, and heterogeneous in treatment protocols, making direct comparison
  difficult. For a student or clinician, the practical takeaway is that classification
  should inform — not replace — a case-by-case risk conversation, and that CBCT-based
  staging is now effectively mandatory before committing to conservative management.
  The review also highlights a gap: prospective outcome data with standardised treatment
  protocols is urgently needed to give these classifications real prognostic teeth.'
