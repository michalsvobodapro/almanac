summary: A randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled trial in 60 adults with IAN hypoesthesia
  after third molar extraction found that both laser acupuncture and direct nerve-trajectory
  PBM (808 nm, 9 J/site, 15 sessions) significantly outperformed sham on two-point

  modality was superior to the other. All patients also received mecobalamin.
summaryDeep: Participants aged 18–60 with unilateral IAN hypoesthesia lasting 7 days
  to 12 weeks were randomized 1:1:1 to laser acupuncture (LA), direct PBM along the
  external IAN projection, or sham. The 808-nm diode laser delivered 9 J per site
  every other day for 15 sessions; LA used a fixed five-plus-two acupoint prescription.
  Primary outcomes — two-point discrimination (TPD) and Semmes-Weinstein monofilament
  examination (SWME) — showed significant group-by-time interactions favoring both
  active arms over sham at day 30, with gains maintained at day 60. The two active
  protocols did not differ from each other. The sham-controlled design and blinding
  of both patients and assessors are methodological strengths rarely seen in PBM research.
  For oral surgery practice, this provides the clearest evidence yet that PBM is an
  active treatment for post-extraction nerve injury, not merely a placebo effect —
  though the optimal protocol (LA vs. direct) remains open.
