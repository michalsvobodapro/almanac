summaryDeep: Periodontitis has long been framed as a bacterially driven dysbiosis,
  but this review makes the case that members of the Herpesviridae family — particularly
  Epstein–Barr virus and human cytomegalovirus — are active participants in disease
  initiation and perpetuation, not passive bystanders. Viral reactivation can shift
  the subgingival microbiome toward a more pathogenic composition, amplify the host
  inflammatory response, and may mediate some of the systemic associations (cardiovascular,
  metabolic) traditionally attributed to periodontal bacteria alone. The review is
  narrative rather than systematic, so causal claims remain tentative, but the breadth
  of associations is striking. For students, the key conceptual shift is that periodontal
  diagnostics and treatment planning may eventually need to account for viral status
  — a direction that would require new chair-side assays and potentially antiviral
  adjuncts. The evidence base is still maturing, and no clinical protocol changes
  are warranted yet.
