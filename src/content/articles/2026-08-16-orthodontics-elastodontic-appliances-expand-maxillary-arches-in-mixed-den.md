summaryDeep: 'Elastodontic appliances are marketed as a low-force, removable alternative
  for early arch development, but the mechanism of action has been poorly characterized.
  This CBCT study is one of the first to separate dental tipping, alveolar remodelling,
  and true skeletal expansion using three measurement levels. The results are nuanced:
  inter-canine width gains of ~3 mm at the cusp level and ~1.4 mm at the alveolar
  crest are clinically meaningful, but the apical basal bone showed no significant
  change in either arch. This pattern is consistent with dentoalveolar bending and
  alveolar remodelling rather than mid-palatal suture opening. The mandibular alveolar
  response was minimal (0.38 mm), which is expected given the absence of a midline
  suture. The study is limited by its retrospective design, small sample, and lack
  of a control group. Still, it provides the first CBCT-level mechanistic data for
  this appliance class and suggests that elastodontic therapy should be understood
  as a dentoalveolar — not skeletal — intervention in mixed dentition.'
