summaryDeep: 'The claim that composite restorations are ''metal-free'' has always
  been a slight oversimplification — conventional composites rely on metal oxide pigments
  (titanium dioxide, iron oxides) to achieve their shade range. Omnichroma''s structural-colour
  approach is genuinely different: 260 nm spherical fillers produce structural colour
  through light scattering rather than pigment absorption, theoretically allowing
  a single shade to chameleon-match across the A–D Vita scale. This article examines
  the optical mechanism in accessible terms and interrogates whether the metal-oxide-free
  marketing holds up to scrutiny. The practical upshot for a student: single-shade
  composites reduce inventory and shade-matching anxiety, but their performance is
  most reliable in mid-range shades and in cavities with sufficient surrounding enamel
  to drive the chameleon effect. Very dark or very light teeth, and large restorations
  with minimal remaining tooth structure, remain challenging. Understanding the underlying
  physics — not just the brand claim — is what lets you counsel patients honestly
  and choose the right material for the right cavity.'
