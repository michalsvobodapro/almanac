summaryDeep: 'Screening 10,566 records down to a final RCT-only dataset, this systematic
  review and meta-analysis evaluated how abutment cross-sectional profile — narrowed
  (concave, curved), straight (cylindrical, convex), or widened — affects marginal
  bone loss (MBL), probing depth (PD), and Pink Esthetic Score (PES). Narrowed profiles
  consistently outperformed straight and widened designs on MBL and PES; the biological
  rationale is that a concave emergence profile creates space for the peri-implant
  soft-tissue cuff rather than compressing it. Secondary outcomes including mucosal
  zenith position and keratinized mucosa width also trended in favour of narrowed
  designs. Risk of bias was assessed with Cochrane RoB 2.0 and certainty with GRADE.
  For a student learning implant prosthetics, the takeaway is concrete: when you have
  a choice at the abutment selection stage, a concave or narrowed emergence profile
  is not just an aesthetic preference — it is the biologically defensible option.
  The limitation is that most included RCTs had short follow-up; long-term MBL equivalence
  remains to be confirmed.'
