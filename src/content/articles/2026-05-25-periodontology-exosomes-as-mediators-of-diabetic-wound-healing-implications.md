summary: Diabetes impairs periodontal wound healing through mechanisms that are only
  now being mapped at the cellular level. This Periodontology 2000 review focuses
  on exosomes — extracellular vesicles that carry signalling cargo — as both diagnostic
  markers and potential therapeutic agents in diabetic tissue repair. It's translational
  science, not yet chairside, but it frames where adjunctive perio therapy is heading.
summaryDeep: 'Patients with poorly controlled diabetes present a persistent clinical
  headache in periodontology: their tissue healing is blunted, surgical outcomes are
  less predictable, and maintenance intervals need to be tighter. This review examines
  the molecular underpinning of that impairment through the lens of exosome biology.
  Exosomes are nano-sized extracellular vesicles secreted by most cell types; they
  carry proteins, lipids, and non-coding RNAs that modulate inflammation, angiogenesis,
  and cell proliferation — all processes central to periodontal wound repair. In diabetic
  conditions, exosome cargo is altered in ways that suppress healing, but engineered
  or stem-cell-derived exosomes are being explored as delivery vehicles to restore
  normal signalling. The review maps current in-vitro and animal-model evidence, acknowledging
  that human clinical trials are sparse. For a student, the value is conceptual: understanding
  why diabetic patients heal differently at a mechanistic level sharpens clinical
  decision-making around surgical timing and patient counselling. Watch this space
  — exosome-based adjuncts could reach periodontal practice within a decade.'
