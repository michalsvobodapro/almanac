summary: In 14 patients with 28 maxillary augmentation sites, customized PEEK mesh
  and prebent titanium mesh produced equivalent horizontal bone gain (2.42 vs. 3.02
  mm, p=0.065) and similar volumetric outcomes at 6 months. PEEK's advantages — no

  at least in the short term.
summaryDeep: Patients with predominantly horizontal maxillary ridge deficiencies were
  randomized to prebent titanium mesh (n=7, 14 sites) or milled patient-specific PEEK
  mesh (n=7, 14 sites), both combined with autogenous bone and xenograft. CBCT volumetric
  analysis was performed preoperatively, immediately postoperatively, and at 6 months
  before implant placement. No statistically significant differences were found for
  horizontal bone gain (titanium 3.02±0.68 mm vs. PEEK 2.42±0.38 mm), vertical bone
  gain, total bone volume gained, or graft resorption. The study is underpowered —
  14 patients is far too few to detect moderate effect sizes — so equivalence cannot
  be firmly concluded. PEEK's theoretical advantages include no second surgery for
  mesh removal and lower soft-tissue perforation rates, though complication data were
  not the primary focus here. This is an early-phase RCT; larger trials with implant
  survival as an endpoint are needed before PEEK mesh becomes a routine alternative.
