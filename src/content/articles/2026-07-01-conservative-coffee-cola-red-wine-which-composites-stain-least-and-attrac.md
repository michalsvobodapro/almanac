summary: An in-vitro comparison of two microhybrid and two nanohybrid composites after
  12-day immersion in coffee, cola, and red wine finds that red wine causes the greatest

summaryDeep: 'Composite resin longevity in the esthetic zone depends heavily on resistance
  to staining and surface degradation from dietary beverages — a concern patients
  raise constantly. This study tested four composites (two microhybrid: DRM, HCM;
  two nanohybrid: DRN, CME) across four immersion media (distilled water, coffee,
  cola, red wine) with n=12 per subgroup, measuring color (ΔE) and surface roughness
  (Ra) at baseline and after 12 days, then assessing S. mutans adhesion via CFU counts
  and SEM. Red wine was the most damaging across all metrics; cola and coffee produced
  intermediate effects. Nanohybrid composites showed better color stability, consistent
  with their smaller filler particle size reducing surface pitting. The bacterial
  adhesion data are the most clinically interesting: rougher surfaces after beverage
  exposure hosted significantly more S. mutans, creating a plausible pathway from
  dietary habits to secondary caries around restorations. Limitations: 12-day static
  immersion doesn''t replicate oral dynamics; saliva pellicle was simulated rather
  than real. Still, the ranking of beverages by damage potential is useful chairside
  information for patient counseling.'
