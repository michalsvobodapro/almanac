  and is associated with postoperative sensitivity, marginal discoloration, secondary
  caries, and restoration failure. Various strategies, including the use of bulk-fill
  composites and fiber reinforcement, have been proposed to reduce polymerization
  shrinkage stress and improve marginal sealing. This study aimed to evaluate the
  effect of composite type and polyethylene fiber reinforcement on the microleakage
  of Class II resin composite restorations. This in vitro study was conducted on 40
  extracted human premolars randomly assigned to five groups ( n = 8): conventional
  composite, conventional composite with polyethylene fiber, bulk-fill composite,
  bulk-fill composite with polyethylene fiber, and short fiber-reinforced composite
  (SFRC). Standardized Class II cavities were restored according to the respective
  protocols. Following thermocycling (500 cycles, 5–55 °C), microleakage was evaluated

summaryDeep: 'Forty extracted premolars with standardized Class II cavities were divided
  into five groups: conventional composite, conventional + polyethylene fiber, bulk-fill,
  bulk-fill + polyethylene fiber, and SFRC. After 500-cycle thermocycling, methylene
  blue dye penetration was scored under stereomicroscopy. SFRC achieved the lowest
  microleakage scores (Kruskal-Wallis, p<0.001), while bulk-fill alone performed worst
  — a counterintuitive result given bulk-fill''s marketing around reduced shrinkage
  stress. Polyethylene fiber reinforcement improved marginal sealing in both conventional
  and bulk-fill groups, narrowing the gap with SFRC. The study''s limitations are
  the small group sizes (n=8) and in-vitro design, which cannot replicate intraoral
  thermal and mechanical complexity. Still, the consistent ranking across groups strengthens
  the signal: SFRC and fiber reinforcement deserve more clinical attention for posterior
  Class II restorations where marginal integrity is critical.'
