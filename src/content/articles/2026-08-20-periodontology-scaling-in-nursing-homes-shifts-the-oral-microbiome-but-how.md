summaryDeep: 'Periodontitis prevalence in nursing-home populations is rising as tooth
  retention improves, yet evidence on microbiome response to periodontal therapy in
  this group is sparse. This secondary analysis of an RCT assessed the oral microbiota
  before and after outreach-delivered supra- and subgingival instrumentation in elderly
  institutionalised patients. The key question is whether the microbial shift parallels
  what is seen in community-dwelling adults, or whether age, polypharmacy, and systemic
  comorbidities dampen the response. Published in the Journal of Clinical Periodontology
  — the EFP''s flagship journal — this carries weight for European perio practice.
  For students, it highlights a growing clinical reality: geriatric dentistry is no
  longer a niche, and understanding how periodontal therapy performs in frail elderly
  patients is increasingly core curriculum. The secondary-analysis status means the
  study was not powered for microbiome endpoints, so effect estimates should be interpreted
  cautiously.'
