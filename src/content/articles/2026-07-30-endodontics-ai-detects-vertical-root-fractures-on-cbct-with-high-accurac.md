summaryDeep: Vertical root fractures are among the most consequential diagnostic misses
  in endodontics — they typically mean extraction — yet their radiographic signs are
  subtle and modality-dependent. This prospective PROSPERO-registered systematic review
  applied QUADAS-2 risk-of-bias assessment and GRADE-adapted certainty ratings to
  six studies, four of which contributed to quantitative synthesis via bivariate random-effects
  models and summary ROC curves. CBCT-based AI models showed high diagnostic accuracy;
  2D modalities (periapical and panoramic) did not reach the same threshold. Post-hoc
  sensitivity analyses controlled for multiple datasets per study. The review is limited
  by the small number of eligible studies and heterogeneity in AI architectures and
  training sets. Nevertheless, for clinicians already using CBCT in suspected VRF
  cases, integrating an AI detection layer is now evidence-supported. For 2D-only
  workflows, AI does not yet compensate for the inherent limitations of the imaging
  modality itself.
