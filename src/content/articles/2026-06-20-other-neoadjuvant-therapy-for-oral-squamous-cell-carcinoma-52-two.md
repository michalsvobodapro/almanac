excerpt: Objectives Neoadjuvant therapy has been increasingly explored as a treatment
  strategy for oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) and may be associated with favorable
  pathological responses. However, its long-term efficacy and safety have not yet
  been systematically evaluated. Therefore, this study aimed to comprehensively synthesize
  the available evidence regarding the clinical outcomes of neoadjuvant therapy in
  patients with OSCC. Methods We systematically searched three electronic databases
  (PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library) for relevant studies evaluating neoadjuvant
  therapy in OSCC. Eligible studies published up to June 1, 2025 were included. A
  random-effects meta-analysis was performed to estimate pooled proportions of disease-free
  survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). Safety outcomes were assessed based on
  the incidence of grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). Data
  extraction and quality assessment were independently performed by two reviewers
  in accordance with the PRISMA guidelines. Results A total of ten studies involving
  578 patients met the eligibility criteria and were included in the analysis. The
  pooled DFS rates at 12 and 24 months were 68% and 41%, respectively. Correspondingly,
  the pooled OS rates were 83% at 12 months and 52% at 24 months. The pooled incidence
  of grade 3-4 treatment-related adverse events was 19%. Conclusions Neoadjuvant therapy

summaryDeep: 'Neoadjuvant therapy — delivering systemic treatment before definitive
  surgery — has gained traction in head-and-neck oncology as a strategy to downstage
  tumours and potentially improve resectability and survival. This meta-analysis is
  the first to systematically pool long-term outcomes specifically for oral squamous
  cell carcinoma, including 578 patients across 10 studies. The 12-month OS of 83%
  and 24-month OS of 52% are broadly comparable to historical surgical series for
  locally advanced OSCC, but direct comparison is confounded by patient selection
  and staging differences across studies. The 19% rate of grade 3–4 treatment-related
  adverse events is clinically significant and must be weighed against potential benefit.
  The authors appropriately label this an exploratory review — the included studies
  are heterogeneous in regimen (chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy) and
  staging. For dental students, the relevance is contextual: understanding that OSCC
  management is evolving toward multimodal pre-surgical strategies shapes how oral
  medicine and maxillofacial surgery will look in practice.'
