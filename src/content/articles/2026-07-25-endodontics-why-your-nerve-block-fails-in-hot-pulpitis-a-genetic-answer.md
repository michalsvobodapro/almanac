summary: A prospective genetic association study links the SCN9A rs6746030 (R1150W)
  polymorphism — a sodium channel variant — to inferior alveolar nerve block failure
  in patients with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis. This is the first credible molecular
  explanation for a phenomenon that frustrates every clinician who has ever chased
  anaesthesia in an acutely inflamed molar.
summaryDeep: 'IANB failure in hot pulpitis is one of endodontics'' most clinically
  vexing problems, with failure rates reported as high as 30–80% depending on diagnostic
  criteria. Inflammatory mediators lower the activation threshold of nociceptors,
  but individual variation in failure rates has always suggested a genetic component.
  The SCN9A gene encodes the Nav1.7 sodium channel, a key player in pain signalling;
  the R1150W variant has previously been associated with altered pain sensitivity
  in other contexts. This study prospectively recruited patients with symptomatic
  irreversible pulpitis, genotyped them, and correlated genotype with IANB success
  defined by electric pulp tester and cold test after injection. The association with
  block failure in R1150W carriers, if replicated, opens a path toward pre-treatment
  genotyping or at minimum a clinical heuristic: patients reporting historically difficult
  anaesthesia may carry this variant. Practically, it reinforces the case for supplemental
  techniques (intraligamentary, intrapulpal) as first-line adjuncts rather than rescue
  measures in hot pulpitis.'
