summaryDeep: 'Split-tooth fracture is the most catastrophic failure mode for endodontically
  treated posterior teeth restored with direct composite, and it is almost always
  non-restorable. This fractographic study — small but methodologically rigorous —
  applied digital 3D microscopy and SEM to 9 recovered fractured specimens with 3–8
  years of clinical service. All 9 showed Mode I fracture (wedge-opening), with crack
  origins consistently at the occlusal surface in areas of contact loading and wear.
  Classic fatigue features — arrest lines, hackle, twist hackle, wake hackle, compression
  curl, and cavitations — were identified on the resin composite fracture surfaces,
  confirming that failure accumulated over many loading cycles rather than from a
  single traumatic event. This has direct implications for restoration design: occlusal
  contact management, cusp coverage, and the decision between direct and indirect
  restoration in high-risk teeth. While the sample size limits statistical inference,
  the mechanistic clarity is valuable for understanding why these failures occur and
  how to counsel patients.'
