summaryDeep: 'Conventional pulp-vitality tests have a fundamental ceiling: they assess
  neural function, not the degree of irreversible inflammatory change that determines
  whether vital pulp therapy or root canal treatment is the right call. This review
  systematically addresses the biological rationale for molecular diagnostics, covering
  preoperative approaches (salivary and crevicular biomarkers) and intraoperative
  sampling of pulp tissue or exudate for cytokine profiling. The practical and biological
  considerations discussed include sample contamination, chairside assay feasibility,
  and the gap between research-grade molecular tools and point-of-care reality. For
  a dental student, the key takeaway is conceptual: the field is moving toward a future
  where a positive cold test is not sufficient justification for vital pulp therapy
  in a symptomatic tooth, and where molecular confirmation of reversible inflammation
  will be expected. Clinically, these tools are not yet chairside-ready, but understanding
  the framework now positions you ahead of the curve when they arrive.'
