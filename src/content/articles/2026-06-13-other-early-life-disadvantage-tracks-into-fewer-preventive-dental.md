summaryDeep: 'This BDJ study traces the long shadow of early-life disadvantage on
  adult dental behaviour, finding that childhood socioeconomic deprivation is associated
  with reduced preventive dental visit rates in adulthood — independently of the person''s
  current socioeconomic position. The mechanism is likely a combination of health
  beliefs, dental anxiety, and habitual non-attendance patterns established in childhood
  that persist regardless of later financial circumstances. For public health and
  dental education, this is a critical finding: interventions aimed solely at adult
  access and affordability will not fully close the oral health inequality gap if
  childhood dental experience remains neglected. It also has direct relevance for
  Czech dental students who will encounter patients whose dental avoidance has deep
  roots — understanding the life-course context changes how you approach motivational
  interviewing and recall systems.'
