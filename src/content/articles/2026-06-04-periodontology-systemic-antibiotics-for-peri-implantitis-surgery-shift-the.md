summaryDeep: 'Systemic antibiotics are frequently prescribed alongside surgical peri-implantitis
  treatment, but their effect on the broader oral microbiome — rather than just the
  subgingival niche — has been largely ignored. This exploratory study analysed salivary
  microbiome composition before and after surgery in 27 patients receiving one of
  two combined antibiotic regimens. Both regimens altered salivary microbial diversity
  and community structure, but the magnitude and direction of shifts differed between
  protocols. The study is explicitly exploratory and underpowered for definitive conclusions,
  but it surfaces an important question: if antibiotic regimens for peri-implantitis
  reshape the salivary ecosystem, do those shifts influence re-colonisation of the
  peri-implant sulcus post-treatment? For students, this is a reminder that antibiotic
  prescribing in implant surgery is not microbiologically neutral — and that the field
  needs larger trials with microbiome endpoints built in from the start.'
