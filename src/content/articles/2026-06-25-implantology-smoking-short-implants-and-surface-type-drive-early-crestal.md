excerpt: 'Abstract Aim: The occurrence of crestal bone loss during the healing period
  of a two-stage implant affects the prognosis of an implant. This study aimed to
  evaluate the factors associated with ECBL and identify predictors of early and late
  implant failures. Materials and Methods: A retrospective analysis was conducted
  on 884 implants placed in 271 patients treated between November 2021 and September
  2024. Panoramic radiographs taken at the time of surgery and the 3-month follow-up
  were used to assess ECBL. Clinical and radiographic data, including demographic
  factors, implant characteristics, and systemic health status, were analyzed. Chi-square
  and logistic regression analyses were performed. Results: The resorption rate was
  higher in implants placed in males (54.5%) than in females (45.5%). ECBL was found
  to be 43.1% in implants placed in smokers and 24.4% in non-smokers (p=0.0001). The
  mean length of the implants was 8.96 mm (±1.8 mm) in the group with resorption and
  9.62 mm (±1.63 mm) in the group without resorption (p = 0.0001). Conclusions: Smoking,
  implant surface type, and implant length are key predictors of early crestal bone
  loss and implant failure. These factors should be considered during implant planning,
  particularly in high-risk patients. Keywords: Alveolar Bone Loss; Bone Resorption;
  Dental Implants; Peri-Implantitis; Risk Factors'

summaryDeep: 'Panoramic radiographs taken at surgery and at 3-month follow-up were
  used to assess ECBL in 884 implants placed between 2021 and 2024. Chi-square and
  logistic regression analyses identified smoking as the strongest modifiable predictor:
  smokers had nearly double the resorption rate of non-smokers. Implant length also
  mattered — mean length was 8.96 mm in the resorption group versus 9.62 mm in those
  without, a statistically significant difference (p=0.0001). Surface type was independently
  associated with ECBL, though the specific surface characteristics conferring protection
  are not detailed in the abstract. The retrospective design and reliance on panoramic
  radiographs (rather than CBCT or periapical films) limit precision, but the sample
  size and the consistency of findings with existing literature strengthen the clinical
  message. For students planning implant cases, this reinforces the non-negotiable
  importance of smoking cessation counseling and conservative implant length selection
  in compromised bone.'
