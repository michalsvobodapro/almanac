summaryDeep: 'Photobiomodulation is gaining traction as a non-pharmacologic pain adjunct,
  but the evidence quality varies dramatically by application. This review usefully
  stratifies the signal: for third-molar and minor oral surgery, multiple RCTs and
  meta-analyses converge on reduced pain, swelling, and analgesic consumption — the
  most clinically actionable finding. For injection pain reduction, the effect is
  modest and most consistent in pediatric patients, with an emerging signal for improved
  inferior alveolar nerve block success in symptomatic irreversible pulpitis. The
  post-endodontic pain data are the weakest: systematic reviews show a favorable trend,
  but at least one recent well-controlled trial found no benefit, and heterogeneity
  in laser parameters (wavelength, dose, timing, delivery) makes pooling unreliable.
  The review''s honest conclusion — that protocol standardization is the field''s
  primary need — is correct. For a student considering PBM in clinic: the post-surgical
  application has the best evidence base; the post-endo application is too preliminary
  to justify routine adoption.'
