excerpt: The achievement of a durable and hermetic seal at the tooth restoration interface
  remains a significant challenge in restorative dentistry, particularly with Class
  II composite restorations. Therefore, it is of interest to compare microleakage
  and internal voids in Class II restorations using different intermediate materials
  in the open sandwich technique. The materials evaluated included ACTIVA Bioactive,
  Resin-modified Glass Ionomer Cement (RMGIC), Smart Dentin Replacement (SDR) and
  a control group with nanohybrid composite. Data showed that ACTIVA Bioactive showed
  the least microleakage and void formation, followed by RMGIC and SDR. Thus, we show
  the superior performance of bioactive materials in improving marginal sealing and
  reducing void formation in composite restorations.

summaryDeep: The open sandwich technique places a liner at the gingival floor of Class
  II composite restorations to improve marginal sealing in the critical cervical zone
  — but the optimal liner material remains debated. This in vitro study compared ACTIVA
  Bioactive, resin-modified glass ionomer cement (RMGIC), SDR flowable composite,
  and a nanohybrid composite control across microleakage and internal void outcomes.
  ACTIVA Bioactive ranked first on both metrics, attributed to its ionic resin matrix
  and bioactive filler that promotes hydroxyapatite formation. RMGIC placed second,
  consistent with its established cervical sealing performance. SDR, despite its low-shrinkage
  chemistry, underperformed relative to the bioactive option. The study is in vitro
  and uses dye penetration — a method with known limitations for predicting clinical
  microleakage. Clinical translation requires RCT confirmation, but the finding is
  directionally consistent with the broader bioactive materials literature and supports
  ACTIVA as a first-choice liner in the open sandwich approach.
