summary: Selective caries removal (SCR) preserves remineralizable, affected dentine
  near the pulp while eliminating infected tissue at the cavity margins — a distinction
  that complete excavation ignores. A narrative review synthesizing PubMed, Scopus,
  and Web of Science controlled studies confirms that SCR reduces iatrogenic pulp
  exposure without compromising cavity cleanliness. For deep lesions, the biology-guided
  approach is now the evidence-backed default.
summaryDeep: 'The review reframes caries management around the biological heterogeneity
  of dentine: infected, superficial dentine must go; demineralized but remineralizable
  dentine near the pulp should stay. Across controlled clinical studies, SCR consistently
  achieves clean cavity margins while protecting pulp vitality — the key outcome that
  complete removal sacrifices in the name of thoroughness. The authors integrate caries
  detection technologies (dye, hardness, fluorescence) with microbiology to show that
  the soft, wet dentine closest to the pulp is not necessarily infected and retains
  regenerative potential. Clinically, this shifts the decision framework from ''how
  much can I see?'' to ''what is the biological status of this tissue?'' The review
  stops short of meta-analysis, so effect-size comparisons with stepwise removal remain
  approximate, but the directional evidence is consistent enough that SCR should be
  the default approach for deep lesions in vital teeth. For a student in Prague, this
  is the conceptual backbone behind the minimally invasive protocols increasingly
  taught in European dental schools.'
