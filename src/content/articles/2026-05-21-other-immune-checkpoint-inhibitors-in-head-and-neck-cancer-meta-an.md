summary: A meta-analysis of phase III trials evaluates adjuvant immune checkpoint
  inhibitor therapy in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, pooling survival and
  toxicity data across regimens. The overall survival benefit is real but modest,
  and toxicity profiles vary substantially by agent. Dentists treating oncology patients
  need to understand these drugs' oral side-effect burden.
summaryDeep: Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) is the oral medicine specialty's
  most consequential diagnosis, and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are now entering
  adjuvant protocols after definitive surgery or chemoradiation. This meta-analysis
  pools data from phase III trials to quantify overall survival and disease-free survival
  gains, finding a statistically significant but clinically modest improvement in
  high-risk resected disease. Crucially for dental practitioners, ICI-related adverse
  events include immune-mediated mucositis, xerostomia, and — less commonly — osteonecrosis-like
  presentations that can be confused with osteoradionecrosis. As more patients arrive
  in dental chairs on adjuvant ICIs, recognising these oral manifestations and coordinating
  with oncology teams becomes a core competency. The meta-analysis provides the evidence
  base for understanding which patients are on these regimens and what the risk-benefit
  calculus looks like.
