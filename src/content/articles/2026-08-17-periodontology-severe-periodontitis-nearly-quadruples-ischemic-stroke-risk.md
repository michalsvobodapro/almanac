summaryDeep: 'This PRISMA 2020-registered meta-analysis searched four major databases
  from 2000 to 2026 and pooled data from 15 studies assessing periodontitis severity
  and ischemic stroke. The headline hazard ratio of 1.24 (95% CI 1.11–1.38) comes
  from cohort data; case-control studies yield a much larger OR of 4.59 (95% CI 1.81–11.62),
  reflecting design differences and likely selection effects. The severity gradient
  is the clinically actionable finding: severe periodontitis (OR 4.19, 95% CI 3.14–5.59)
  confers more than double the stroke-associated odds of moderate disease (OR 1.63,
  95% CI 1.16–2.29). Studies applying the modern AAP/EFP 2018 classification produced
  the strongest associations, suggesting earlier research underestimated risk by using
  looser diagnostic criteria. Causality remains unproven — residual confounding by
  shared risk factors (smoking, diabetes, socioeconomic status) cannot be excluded
  — but the dose-response pattern across severity stages strengthens the biological
  plausibility. For the periodontal clinician, this reinforces the case for communicating
  systemic risk to patients with Stage III/IV disease and for interprofessional referral
  pathways with neurology and cardiology.'
