summaryDeep: 'This systematic scoping review searched PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science,
  and Google Scholar through May 2026, ultimately including 45 studies on orthodontic
  appliance fabrication using conventional acrylics, thermoplastics, and directly
  printed resins. Thermoplastic materials consistently outperformed printed resins
  on stiffness, transparency, colour stability, and surface characteristics — properties
  directly relevant to aligner performance and patient acceptance. Printed resins
  offered advantages in manufacturing flexibility and appliance customisation, and
  digital workflow integration was broadly superior to conventional methods. Biocompatibility
  data were sparse and heterogeneous, a gap that matters given prolonged intraoral
  contact. Aging behaviour of printed materials under thermal and mechanical cycling
  remains incompletely characterised. For a Prague dental student working in a clinic
  that has adopted digital workflows, the practical message is: 3D-printed retainers
  and auxiliaries are clinically viable, but printed aligner-equivalent appliances
  should be approached with awareness that their mechanical properties differ meaningfully
  from thermoplastic benchmarks.'
