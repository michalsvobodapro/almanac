summaryDeep: 'Endodontic education depends on case-based reasoning, but faculty time
  is a bottleneck. This RCT deployed an LLM configured to ask Socratic questions —
  probing students'' differential diagnoses, treatment rationale, and complication
  management — rather than providing direct answers. Students in the LLM arm demonstrated
  significantly better case-analysis performance compared to controls receiving standard
  instruction. The Socratic design is key: it avoids the well-documented problem of
  LLMs simply generating plausible-sounding but unchecked answers, instead forcing
  the student to do the cognitive work. For a dental student in Prague with limited
  access to one-on-one faculty time, this model is immediately relevant — the architecture
  is replicable with any major LLM API. Limitations include single-institution setting
  and short follow-up; transfer to clinical skill remains unproven.'
