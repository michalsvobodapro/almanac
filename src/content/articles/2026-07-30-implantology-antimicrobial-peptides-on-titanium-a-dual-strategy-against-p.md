summaryDeep: 'Peri-implantitis treatment is notoriously difficult because mechanical
  debridement cannot fully decontaminate implant surfaces and systemic antibiotics
  carry resistance risks. Antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) — cationic, membrane-disrupting
  molecules with low resistance-selection pressure — have been explored as surface
  coatings, but mostly for osseointegration contexts. This review reframes AMPs through
  a wound-healing lens, arguing that the peri-implant transmucosal interface behaves
  like a healing epithelial barrier, making it an ideal target for host-defense peptides
  like LL-37 and β-defensins that both kill pathogens and promote tissue repair. The
  review gives particular attention to the abutment and transmucosal collar as the
  primary sealing zone, and extends the analysis to zirconia and hybrid components.
  The honest caveat: evidence is predominantly in vitro and animal-derived. No clinical
  trials of AMP-functionalized implant components are yet available. This is a conceptual
  advance, not a clinical recommendation — but it points toward where implant surface
  science is heading.'
