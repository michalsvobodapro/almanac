summaryDeep: 'The SCAPIS study is one of the largest population-based investigations
  to date linking oral and cardiovascular disease, and it goes beyond association
  to probe mechanism. Among 29,056 Swedish adults, 6% had severe periodontitis; these
  individuals showed 69% higher odds of severe coronary artery calcium scores and
  40% higher odds of extensive segment involvement on CT. Mediation analysis confirmed
  that subclinical atherosclerosis partially explains the periodontitis–CHD pathway,
  not merely confounds it. The novel finding is the link to epicardial adipose tissue
  attenuation: lower EAT Hounsfield units signal pericardial fat inflammation, and
  periodontitis patients showed significantly lower values (β = −0.27 HU). Crucially,
  the cardiovascular signal was strongest in periodontitis patients without caries
  — pointing to a systemic, immune-driven phenotype rather than a general oral health
  effect. For clinical practice, this is the strongest mechanistic evidence yet that
  treating severe periodontitis is not just oral health management but potentially
  cardiovascular risk reduction. The data are observational, so causality remains
  unproven, but the effect sizes are clinically meaningful.'
