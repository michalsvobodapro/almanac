  in early childhood, but existing tools are difficult to standardize in routine practice.
  The ability of large language model (LLM)-based artificial intelligence (AI) systems
  to recognize and correctly apply validated CRA tools remains unclear. This study
  aimed to evaluate the ability of ChatGPT and Google Gemini, LLM-based AI systems,
  to apply and interpret CRA tools in early childhood under different prompting conditions.
  Thirty standardized clinical vignettes of children aged 0-5 years were evaluated
  using Caries Management by Risk Assessment (CAMBRA) and the Cariogram by ChatGPT-5.1
  (OpenAI, San Francisco, CA, USA) and Google Gemini-3.0 Pro (Google LLC, Mountain
  View, CA, USA) under three prompting conditions: unguided, guideline-informed, and
  guideline-only. An expert panel provided reference classifications. AI outputs were
  assessed for categorical agreement, mean absolute error (MAE), quality, accuracy,

summaryDeep: 'An expert panel established reference CAMBRA and Cariogram classifications
  for 30 vignettes of children aged 0–5 years. AI outputs were scored for categorical
  agreement, MAE, quality, accuracy, and readability under unguided, guideline-informed,
  and guideline-only conditions. Inter-rater reliability was high (ICC 0.89–0.93).
  The two CRA tools themselves showed only moderate correlation (ρ = 0.509), which
  complicates AI evaluation — if the gold standards disagree, the AI can''t win either
  way. Guideline-only prompting (pasting the full tool into the prompt) produced the
  most consistent outputs. The study sits alongside the LLM apical periodontitis paper
  as a paired lesson: LLMs are sensitive to how you ask, not just what you ask. For
  a dental student using AI to practice CRA, the implication is practical — always
  provide the full guideline text in the prompt, not just the tool name.'
