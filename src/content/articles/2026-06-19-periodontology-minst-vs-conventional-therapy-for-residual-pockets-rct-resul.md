summaryDeep: 'Residual pockets after Step 1–2 periodontal therapy remain a clinical
  headache, and MINST has been proposed as a more targeted alternative to re-instrumentation
  with conventional curettes. This RCT randomizes patients with stage II–III periodontitis
  and residual pockets ≥4 mm to MINST (ultrafine sonic/ultrasonic tips, magnification,
  minimal tissue distension) versus conventional non-surgical retreatment. While the
  full results text was not available in the excerpt, the trial design — randomized,
  with periodontal clinical parameters as primary outcomes — represents the highest
  feasible evidence level for this comparison. MINST''s theoretical advantage is preservation
  of the junctional epithelium and reduced post-operative discomfort, which may improve
  patient compliance with supportive therapy. For a student in a periodontal clinic,
  this trial is worth tracking: if MINST achieves equivalent or superior pocket reduction
  with less patient morbidity, it strengthens the case for investing in magnification
  loupes and ultrafine tip sets early in training. The EFP''s S3-level guidelines
  on periodontal therapy already acknowledge MINST; this RCT will feed directly into
  future updates.'
