summaryDeep: 'Forty-five three-unit posterior FDPs were split equally between additive
  manufacturing from a 3Y-TZP slurry and two subtractive routes — translucent 4Y-TZP
  and a strength-gradient 3/5Y-TZP. Digital surface deviation analysis and gap measurement
  revealed a clear hierarchy: AM produced the worst external, intaglio, and overall
  surface trueness, while the two milled groups were statistically indistinguishable
  from each other. Counterintuitively, AM FDPs had the smallest average marginal gaps
  — a finding that likely reflects how slurry-based printing can fill fine marginal
  detail — but their gap precision (consistency across specimens) was significantly
  worse than the milled strength-gradient group. For a student or clinician choosing
  a fabrication route, this means AM zirconia is not yet a drop-in replacement: the
  average fit number looks good on paper, but the variability is a clinical liability.
  Milled strength-gradient and milled translucent zirconia remain equivalent and predictable.
  The study is moderate-evidence by design (n=45, in vitro), so long-term clinical
  survival data are still needed.'
