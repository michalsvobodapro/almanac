summary: A prospective cohort followed 30 patients for a decade after surgical peri-implantitis
  treatment combining deproteinised bovine bone mineral with 10% collagen (DBBMC),
  enamel matrix derivative, and doxycycline powder. Ten-year implant survival data
  from a regenerative peri-implantitis protocol are exceptionally rare, making this
  one of the longest follow-ups in the field. The results provide a real-world benchmark
  for a combined regenerative-antimicrobial surgical approach.
summaryDeep: Peri-implantitis surgical studies rarely survive past five years in the
  literature, so this 10-year prospective cohort from the Journal of Clinical Periodontology
  is a landmark data point. Thirty patients underwent a regenerative surgical protocol
  combining DBBMC (a widely used bone substitute), enamel matrix derivative for soft-tissue
  and bone regeneration, and doxycycline powder as a local antimicrobial adjunct.
  The 10-year implant survival outcome tests whether early radiographic gains from
  regenerative peri-implantitis surgery translate into durable implant retention —
  the question every clinician actually cares about. The moderate evidence grade reflects
  the single-arm design and modest sample size, but no RCT in peri-implantitis has
  come close to this follow-up length. For a Prague dental student, this is the kind
  of long-term cohort that shapes understanding of what 'successful' peri-implantitis
  treatment actually looks like over a clinical career. It also feeds directly into
  ongoing EFP guideline discussions on regenerative versus resective peri-implantitis
  surgery.
