summary: An in vitro study of 192 full-arch implant scans across three intraoral scanners
  found that even light saliva contamination significantly increases deviation and
  scan time; high moisture levels compound the problem across all tested systems.
  Scan body height and splinting wire aids also matter, but moisture is the dominant
  variable. Dry-field protocol is non-negotiable for full-arch implant IOS.
summaryDeep: 'Full-arch intraoral scanning for implant-supported prostheses is increasingly
  replacing conventional impressions, but clinical conditions rarely match the dry
  in vitro benchmarks on which scanner accuracy claims are based. This study used
  a metrology-grade industrial scanner as ground truth and tested TRIOS 5, NeoScan
  1000, and Primescan under dry, lightly moistened, and highly moistened conditions,
  with short (4.5 mm) and tall (8.0 mm) scan bodies and splinted versus non-splinted
  protocols — 192 scans total. Moisture was the strongest predictor of RMS deviation
  and acquisition time across all three scanners (p<0.001). Tall scan bodies and splinting
  aids improved trueness, but neither compensated for high moisture. The TRIOS 5 performed
  best overall, though all systems degraded under wet conditions. The practical message
  for clinic: invest in rigorous moisture control before worrying about which scanner
  to buy.'
