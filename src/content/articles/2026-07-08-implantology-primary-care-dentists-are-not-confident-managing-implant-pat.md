summaryDeep: 'As implant prevalence rises across Europe, the assumption that general
  practitioners can absorb routine implant maintenance is increasingly tested. This
  Yorkshire survey quantifies the confidence deficit: practitioners report uncertainty
  in recognizing peri-implant disease, knowing when to refer, and executing basic
  maintenance protocols. The cross-sectional design captures a snapshot rather than
  outcomes, and self-reported confidence is an imperfect proxy for competence — but
  the directional signal is consistent with similar surveys from other European countries.
  For a Czech dental student, this is a preview of a structural challenge that ČSK
  and EU dental education frameworks will need to address: implant maintenance must
  be embedded in undergraduate curricula, not left to postgraduate specialists. The
  referral pathway confusion identified here also has patient safety implications
  — delayed diagnosis of peri-implantitis is the most common driver of late implant
  failure.'
