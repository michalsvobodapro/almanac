summaryDeep: 'The association between periodontitis and systemic disease is well-established
  epidemiologically, but causality has been hard to demonstrate. This study takes
  a germ-free mouse model — the gold standard for isolating microbial causality —
  and colonises animals with salivary microbiota from periodontitis donors versus
  healthy controls. The periodontitis-derived microbiota produced measurable changes
  in IgA-related immune features in salivary gland tissue, suggesting that the dysbiotic
  oral microbiome actively reprogrammes mucosal immune responses rather than simply
  reflecting pre-existing systemic vulnerability. The findings have implications well
  beyond periodontology: if oral dysbiosis causally alters mucosal immunity, periodontal
  treatment could have genuine immunomodulatory effects. For students, this is the
  mechanistic bridge between the clinical observation that treating periodontitis
  improves systemic inflammatory markers and the biological explanation for why. It
  also strengthens the rationale for aggressive periodontal prevention in immunocompromised
  patients.'
