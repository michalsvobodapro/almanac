summary: A commentary on a 6-month RCT finds that soft tissue augmentation at immediate
  implant sites reduces volumetric buccal bone loss compared to implant placement
  alone. The effect is measurable within the first six months — the critical window
  for buccal plate remodeling. This adds prospective evidence to the growing consensus
  that soft tissue grafting at immediate placement is not optional in the esthetic
  zone.
summaryDeep: 'The underlying RCT (Jung et al., J Periodontol 2026) used volumetric
  CBCT analysis to quantify buccal bone changes at immediate implant sites with and
  without soft tissue augmentation over six months. The commentary contextualizes
  the finding: buccal bone resorption at immediate implant sites is driven partly
  by the thin buccal plate''s vascular dependence, and soft tissue bulk appears to
  buffer that resorption — possibly through improved blood supply and mechanical protection.
  The 6-month window is clinically meaningful because most buccal plate remodeling
  occurs early; what is lost here is rarely recovered. The evidence grade remains
  moderate (single RCT, short follow-up), and longer-term volumetric data are needed
  to confirm durability. But for immediate implant protocols in the esthetic zone,
  the direction of evidence is now consistent: graft the soft tissue simultaneously.'
