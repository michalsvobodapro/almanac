summary: NiTi rotary instruments are manufactured through a combination of heat treatment
  and grinding, but the order of those steps is rarely disclosed — and it turns out
  to matter significantly for cyclic fatigue resistance and flexibility. Files ground
  after heat treatment show different crystalline phase composition and mechanical
  behaviour than those heat-treated post-grinding. For the clinician, this is a reminder
  that not all 'heat-treated' files are equivalent.
summaryDeep: 'The marketing of NiTi files leans heavily on terms like ''heat-treated''
  and ''controlled memory,'' but manufacturing sequence is almost never specified
  on packaging. This study systematically varies whether heat treatment precedes or
  follows the grinding step in prototype NiTi instruments and characterises the resulting
  differences in phase transformation temperatures (DSC), surface microstructure (SEM/XRD),
  cyclic fatigue resistance, and bending flexibility. Heat-treatment-last produces
  a higher proportion of R-phase at body temperature, translating to greater flexibility
  and longer fatigue life in curved canals. Heat-treatment-first instruments are stiffer
  and fracture earlier under cyclic loading. The practical implication: when evaluating
  a new file system, ask about manufacturing sequence — or look for published DSC
  data — rather than relying on brand claims about ''superelasticity.'' This also
  matters for understanding why seemingly similar files from different manufacturers
  behave differently in the same canal anatomy.'
