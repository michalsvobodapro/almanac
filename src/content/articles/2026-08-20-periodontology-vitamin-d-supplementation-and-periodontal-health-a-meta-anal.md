summaryDeep: 'The review asks a question that comes up constantly in perio clinics:
  does vitamin D supplementation, on top of conventional periodontal therapy, move
  the needle on pocket depth, clinical attachment level, or peri-implant health? By
  pooling randomised data and applying meta-analytic methods, it provides a more definitive
  signal than the scattered individual trials that preceded it. Vitamin D''s plausible
  mechanisms — immunomodulation, anti-inflammatory cytokine regulation, and influence
  on alveolar bone metabolism — have long made it a candidate adjunct. The key clinical
  output is whether the effect size on PPD is large enough to justify routine supplementation
  recommendations, particularly in patients with documented deficiency. Published
  in Evidence-Based Dentistry, which applies structured critical appraisal, the findings
  carry more weight than a journal with looser peer review. For a Prague student seeing
  perio patients, this is the kind of adjunctive-therapy evidence that will appear
  in board questions and clinical seminars within the year.'
