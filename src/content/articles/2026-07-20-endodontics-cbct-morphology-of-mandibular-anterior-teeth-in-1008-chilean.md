excerpt: Abstract Background Detailed knowledge of root and canal morphology is essential
  for successful endodontic treatment. Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) allows
  accurate three-dimensional evaluation of anatomical features and variations. Objective
  To describe the root and canal morphology of 1,008 permanent mandibular anterior
  teeth in a Chilean subpopulation using CBCT, and to classify canal configurations
  according to an alphanumeric coding system. Methods A total of 168 CBCT scans from
  patients aged 15–75 years were retrospectively analyzed. The number of roots and
  canals, root and corono-radicular lengths, and bilateral symmetry were assessed
  in 336 mandibular central incisors, 336 mandibular lateral incisors, and 336 mandibular
  canines. Root canal configurations were classified using an alphanumeric coding
  system. Results All mandibular incisors presented a single root (100%), while 1.5%
  of mandibular canines exhibited two roots. The most common canal configuration was
  a single canal ( 1 TN 1−1 ), observed in 69.3% of central incisors, 68.4% of lateral
  incisors, and 90.8% of canines. Complex configurations, including 1 TN 1–2−1 and
  1 TN 2−1 , were also identified. Bilateral symmetry was recorded in 82.7% of central
  incisors, 82.1% of lateral incisors, and 86.9% of canines. Conclusions In this Chilean
  subpopulation, mandibular anterior teeth predominantly exhibited a single root and
  a simple canal configuration, with a smaller proportion showing complex anatomies.
