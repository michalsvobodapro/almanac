summaryDeep: 'Head-and-neck cancer survivors face relentless caries progression driven
  by radiation-induced xerostomia, and until now the relative contributions of fluoride
  varnish and saliva substitutes had not been tested head-to-head in a powered RCT.
  This trial randomised 482 patients into three groups — saliva substitute alone,
  fluoride varnish alone (applied before RT, immediately after, then every 3 months),
  and the combination — tracking DMFS from baseline through 12 months post-radiotherapy.
  All groups showed significant DMFS increases, confirming the severity of the challenge,
  but the combination arm consistently produced the lowest increment at every time
  point. The finding aligns with mechanistic logic: fluoride addresses remineralisation
  while saliva substitutes buffer pH and lubricate. Critically, the trial used a pragmatic
  design with real-world RT patients, boosting external validity. For dental students
  rotating through oncology or hospital dentistry, the take-home is concrete: neither
  intervention alone is sufficient — the combination should become the standard of
  care for irradiated patients.'
