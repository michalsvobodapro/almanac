summaryDeep: 'Neurofibromatosis type 1 is a relatively common autosomal dominant condition
  (1:3,000) that most dental curricula address only in the context of oral neurofibromas.
  This study reframes NF1 as a significant caries-risk condition driven by salivary
  pathology. The DMFT gap of nearly 6 points between NF1 patients and age- and sex-matched
  controls is striking, and the salivary data explain it mechanistically: unstimulated
  flow rate was reduced in 57% of NF1 patients versus just 2.4% of controls, and salivary
  viscosity was markedly elevated. Salivary pH, buffering capacity, and mineral composition
  were also altered. Behavioural and socioeconomic factors were assessed and did not
  fully account for the difference. For a clinician, NF1 should trigger the same preventive
  protocol as Sjögren''s syndrome or head-and-neck radiation: high-fluoride toothpaste,
  salivary substitutes, frequent recall, and dietary counselling. This is a condition
  where proactive prevention can prevent a catastrophic caries trajectory.'
