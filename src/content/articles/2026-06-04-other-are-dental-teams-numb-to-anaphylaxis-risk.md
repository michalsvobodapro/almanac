summaryDeep: 'Dental practices administer local anaesthetics, latex-containing products,
  and prophylactic antibiotics daily, yet anaphylaxis preparedness in primary dental
  care is inconsistent at best. This commentary contends that the low incidence of
  anaphylaxis in dentistry has bred a dangerous complacency — teams are not drilled,
  adrenaline auto-injectors are not always immediately accessible, and recognition
  of early anaphylactic signs is undertrained. The piece draws on resuscitation guidelines
  and incident data to argue that the gap between protocol and practice is widening
  as practices become busier and staff turnover increases. For dental students about
  to enter clinical placements, the message is direct: knowing the anaphylaxis algorithm
  is not optional, and checking the emergency drug kit should be as routine as checking
  the autoclave. The piece also touches on the medicolegal exposure of practices that
  cannot demonstrate adequate preparedness — a dimension that matters regardless of
  which country you practise in.'
