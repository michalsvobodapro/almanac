excerpt: Dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) play a critical role in maintaining dental
  pulp homeostasis and supporting dentin-pulp complex regeneration, while tumor necrosis
  factor-α (TNF-α)-mediated inflammation severely impairs their biological functions.
  This study explored the role of the miR-335-5p/DKK1/autophagy axis in TNF-α-induced
  DPSCs dysfunction. Human DPSCs were stimulated with 20 ng/mL TNF-α; miR-335-5p overexpression
  and DKK1 silencing were achieved via transfection. qRT-PCR, Western blot, SA-β-gal
  staining, immunofluorescence, transmission electron microscopy, ALP/ARS staining,
  and dual-luciferase assay were used to detect related indicators. Results showed
  TNF-α downregulated miR-335-5p, upregulated DKK1, inhibited autophagy, induced senescence,
  disrupted cytoskeleton, and suppressed osteogenesis; miR-335-5p directly targeted
  DKK1's 3'-UTR. Overexpressing miR-335-5p or silencing DKK1 restored DPSCs' autophagic
  flux, alleviated senescence, and rescued osteogenic potential. In conclusion, the
  miR-335-5p/DKK1/autophagy axis mediates TNF-α-induced DPSCs dysfunction, and targeting
  this axis may improve stem cell-based dental pulp and bone reconstruction under
  inflammation.

summaryDeep: 'Dental pulp stem cells hold regenerative promise, but their function
  is severely compromised in the inflamed pulp environment that characterizes irreversible
  pulpitis — precisely the condition where regenerative endodontics would be most
  useful. This study dissects one molecular pathway by which TNF-α impairs DPSCs:
  it downregulates miR-335-5p, which normally suppresses DKK1 (a Wnt pathway inhibitor).
  DKK1 upregulation then blocks autophagy, induces cellular senescence, disrupts the
  cytoskeleton, and suppresses osteogenic differentiation. Overexpressing miR-335-5p
  or silencing DKK1 via transfection restored autophagic flux and rescued osteogenic
  potential. The study is entirely in vitro and uses transfection-based overexpression,
  which is far from a clinical delivery strategy. The GRADE evidence level is low.
  For a student, this is valuable basic science context for understanding why regenerative
  endodontic procedures have variable outcomes in teeth with established periapical
  pathology — the inflammatory microenvironment actively sabotages the stem cells
  you are trying to harness.'
