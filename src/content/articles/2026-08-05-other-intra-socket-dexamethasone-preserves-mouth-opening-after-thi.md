  associated with postoperative pain, facial swelling, and trismus. Local intra-socket
  drug delivery provides targeted therapy with minimal systemic drug exposure. This
  study compared the efficacy of dexamethasone and hyaluronic acid–soaked gelfoams
  in reducing postoperative sequelae. Materials and Methods A prospective randomized
  controlled study was conducted on 54 patients who underwent surgical extraction
  of impacted mandibular third molars. Participants were allocated into three groups
  (n = 18 each): Group A (control, normal saline-soaked gelfoam), Group B (dexamethasone-soaked
  gelfoam), and Group C (hyaluronic acid-soaked gelfoam). Facial swelling (linear
  measurements), interincisal mouth opening, and pain were recorded preoperatively
  and on postoperative days 3 and 7 using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS). Data were
  analyzed using one-way and repeated-measures ANOVA (p &lt; 0.05). Results All fifty-four

summaryDeep: Fifty-four patients were randomized equally across three groups (n =
  18 each) for surgical extraction of impacted mandibular third molars. Facial swelling
  (linear measurements), interincisal opening, and VAS pain were recorded preoperatively
  and on days 3 and 7. Dexamethasone-soaked gelfoam significantly outperformed both
  control and hyaluronic acid for mouth opening at day 3 (p = 0.021) and day 7 (p
  = 0.049) — the most functionally disabling sequela for patients. Swelling and pain
  differences did not reach significance, though the trend favored intervention groups.
  The small group size (n = 18) limits power to detect differences in pain and swelling,
  and the 7-day follow-up misses later healing events. Hyaluronic acid, despite its
  anti-inflammatory reputation, offered no significant benefit over saline in this
  design. For students performing third molar extractions, intra-socket dexamethasone
  gelfoam is a simple, targeted adjunct that addresses trismus without systemic steroid
  exposure.
