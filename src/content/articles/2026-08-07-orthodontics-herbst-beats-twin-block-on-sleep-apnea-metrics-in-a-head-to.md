summary: A randomized trial in 46 patients with mandibular retrognathia and OSA finds
  the Herbst appliance significantly reduces AHI, supine AHI, and ODI at 8-month follow-up;
  Twin Block fails to reach significance on the same metrics. Both appliances correct
  the skeletal discrepancy, but only Herbst moves the needle on sleep-disordered breathing.
summaryDeep: This RCT is one of the few head-to-head comparisons of functional appliances
  specifically powered for sleep outcomes. Forty-six patients with confirmed OSA and
  mandibular retrognathia were randomized to Herbst or Twin Block, with polygraphy
  and PSQI at baseline and ~8 months. The Herbst group showed statistically significant
  improvements in AHI (p = 0.031), supine AHI (p = 0.001), and ODI (p = 0.013); the
  Twin Block group did not reach significance on any of these. Minimum oxygen saturation
  did not change significantly in either group, suggesting the improvement is in event
  frequency rather than depth of desaturation. The sample size is modest and follow-up
  short, so durability is unknown. Still, for an orthodontist managing a growing patient
  with both retrognathia and OSA, this is the clearest trial-level signal yet that
  appliance choice matters for the airway — not just the bite.
