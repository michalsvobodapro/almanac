summaryDeep: Poor osseointegration in diabetic patients is a well-established clinical
  problem, and this study explores whether exercise — independent of glycaemic control
  — can mitigate it. Type 2 diabetes was induced in rats via high-fat/high-sucrose
  diet plus low-dose streptozotocin, and a subset underwent 40 minutes of daily treadmill
  running. Implants were placed in the proximal tibia. Running significantly improved
  osseointegration metrics versus sedentary diabetic controls, but did not improve
  hyperglycaemia or insulin resistance. The improvement correlated with reduced triglycerides
  and LDL, pointing to lipid metabolism as a mediator of bone-implant healing. The
  study is animal-only and the forced-running model has obvious translational limitations.
  However, it adds to a growing body of evidence that physical activity has skeletal
  benefits beyond glycaemic control, and it provides a biological rationale for advising
  diabetic implant patients to exercise — advice that is already appropriate for general
  health. Too preliminary to change implant protocols, but worth discussing with diabetic
  patients pre-surgically.
