summary: A BDJ opinion piece argues that the clinical narrative around 'regenerating'
  early caries lesions overstates what remineralisation actually achieves — surface
  rehardening is not structural restoration of the enamel rod architecture. The piece
  is a direct provocation to how non-operative caries management is communicated to
  patients and taught in dental schools.
summaryDeep: 'The remineralisation story has become a cornerstone of modern preventive
  dentistry: catch it early, apply fluoride, arrest the lesion, avoid the drill. This
  BDJ commentary pushes back hard on the language of ''regeneration,'' distinguishing
  between ion redeposition into a demineralised surface (which does occur) and true
  structural repair of the enamel prism architecture (which does not). The clinical
  implication is that arrested white-spot lesions remain structurally compromised
  and may be more susceptible to future acid challenge than intact enamel. For dental
  students in Prague, this is directly relevant to how you counsel patients about
  fluoride varnish and dietary modification — the message should be ''stabilise and
  protect,'' not ''heal.'' The piece is opinion-grade, not a systematic review, so
  it should sharpen your thinking rather than change your protocol. But it is a useful
  corrective to marketing language that has crept into clinical communication.'
