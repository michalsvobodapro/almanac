summaryDeep: 'Bisphosphonates are well-established as a risk factor for impaired periapical
  healing, but denosumab — a RANKL inhibitor with a different mechanism and reversible
  effect — has been less studied in this context. This case-control study is the largest
  to date specifically examining RCT outcomes in denosumab users, with a median follow-up
  exceeding 500 days. The 15-percentage-point healing gap (75% vs 90%) is clinically
  meaningful but the study is underpowered to confirm it: n=20 per group, wide confidence
  intervals, and a p-value of 0.407 on the primary outcome. Multivariable regression
  found no significant predictors, though high initial PAI and sealer extrusion showed
  non-significant trends toward worse healing. The practical implication is cautious:
  denosumab patients are not equivalent to healthy controls for RCT prognosis, and
  the drug holiday question — whether pausing denosumab improves healing — remains
  unanswered. Larger prospective studies are needed before protocol changes are warranted.'
