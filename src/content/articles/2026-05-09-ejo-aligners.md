summaryDeep: |
  The meta-analysis aggregated 14 prospective studies and RCTs published 2018–2025 comparing clear aligner therapy with fixed appliances in upper first premolar extraction cases. Total cohort n=1,180. Outcomes: total treatment time, space closure quality, root parallelism, anterior torque control.

  Aligner cases averaged 0.4 mm less space closure than fixed (95% CI 0.2–0.6 mm) and 4.2° worse root parallelism on post-treatment panoramic. Anterior torque loss was 2.3° greater. Treatment time was 1.7 months shorter on average for aligner cases — but with notably higher refinement rates (average 1.8 refinement sets vs 0.4 cycles of fixed adjustment). The gap closes meaningfully with optimized attachments and Class II elastics, though it doesn't disappear. The honest summary: aligners are clinically acceptable for moderate extraction cases with experienced operators; for severe crowding or complex mechanics, fixed appliances retain a meaningful edge.
excerptFull: |
  Aim: To compare treatment outcomes of clear aligner therapy versus fixed appliances in patients undergoing upper first premolar extractions.

  Materials and methods: Systematic review and meta-analysis. PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, and EMBASE searched for prospective studies and RCTs comparing aligner and fixed appliance treatment in extraction cases (2018–2025). Risk of bias assessed with ROBINS-I and Cochrane RoB 2. Random-effects meta-analysis on weighted mean differences for primary outcomes: space closure, root parallelism (panoramic angulation), anterior torque control, and treatment duration.

  Results: 14 studies included (5 RCTs, 9 prospective cohorts), n=1,180. Aligner-treated cases showed mean 0.4 mm (95% CI 0.2–0.6) less space closure, 4.2° (3.1–5.4) worse root parallelism, and 2.3° (1.5–3.2) greater anterior torque loss. Mean treatment time was 1.7 months shorter for aligners (p=0.02), but mean refinement sets per case was 1.8 vs fixed appliance adjustment cycles of 0.4. Subgroup analysis: cases using optimized attachments and Class II elastics showed reduced (but not eliminated) outcome gaps.

  Conclusion: Clear aligners produce clinically acceptable but measurably inferior mechanical outcomes versus fixed appliances in extraction cases. Outcome gap narrows with optimized auxiliaries.
