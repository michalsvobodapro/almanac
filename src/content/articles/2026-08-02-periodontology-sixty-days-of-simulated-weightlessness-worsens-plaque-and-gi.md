summaryDeep: 'Long-duration spaceflight poses real oral health risks, and ground-based
  bedrest analogs allow controlled study of microgravity''s physiological effects.
  This prospective cohort study enrolled 24 healthy volunteers in a strictly controlled
  60-day, 6° head-down tilt protocol (DRKS00033882). Quigley-Hein Plaque Index rose
  from 1.8 to 2.3 and gingival inflammation increased significantly by end-HDT, recovering
  partially post-reambulation. Salivary secretion rates did not change significantly,
  pointing away from xerostomia as the driver and toward the practical difficulty
  of effective toothbrushing in a recumbent, microgravity-simulating position. For
  the average dental student, the direct clinical relevance is limited — but the study
  has broader implications: it confirms that gingival tissue is a sensitive early
  biomarker of systemic physiological stress, and that mechanical plaque control is
  the dominant variable in periodontal health even when diet and systemic health are
  controlled. Adjunctive chemical plaque control (antiseptic rinses, powered brushes)
  should be part of any long-duration mission protocol.'
