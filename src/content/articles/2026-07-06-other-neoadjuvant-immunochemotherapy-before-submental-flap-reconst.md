summary: A propensity-matched cohort of 170 oral squamous cell carcinoma patients
  (85 per group) shows neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy before submental island flap

summaryDeep: The submental island flap is a workhorse for oral cavity reconstruction,
  but its use has been debated in the context of neoadjuvant therapy because the flap
  traverses level I lymph node territory — a potential oncological risk. This propensity-matched
  retrospective cohort (279 patients total, 85 matched per arm) directly addresses
  that concern. Neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (NICT) was associated with a hazard
  ratio of 0.38 for loco-regional recurrence and 0.51 for overall survival on multivariable
  analysis. Critically, zero recurrences occurred within level I nodes or along the
  flap subcutaneous tract in either group, providing reassurance about the flap's
  oncological safety even in the NICT era. Pathological analysis confirmed complete
  level I clearance. The study is retrospective and single-center, but the propensity
  matching and competing-risks regression methodology are rigorous. For dental students
  rotating through oral surgery or maxillofacial units, this reframes the submental
  flap as not just safe but potentially synergistic with modern immunotherapy protocols.
