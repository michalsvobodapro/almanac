  syndrome, longitudinal evidence remains limited. This nationwide population-based
  cohort study aimed to investigate whether periodontal status is associated with
  the incidence of metabolic syndrome in Korea. We analyzed data obtained from the
  National Health Insurance Service-Health Screening cohort (2009-2019), including
  43,713 participants aged ≥40 years. Periodontitis was assessed using diagnostic
  codes and periodontal treatment procedures. Metabolic syndrome was defined based
  on the modified National Cholesterol Education Program Adult Treatment Panel III
  criteria. This longitudinal cohort analysis used Cox proportional hazards models
  to examine the association between baseline periodontitis and the subsequent incidence

summaryDeep: Using the Korean National Health Insurance Service cohort — one of the
  largest and most complete population health databases in the world — this study
  tracked 43,713 adults aged ≥40 from 2009 to 2019. Periodontitis was defined via
  diagnostic codes and treatment procedures; metabolic syndrome via modified NCEP-ATP
  III criteria. Cox proportional hazards models showed that severe periodontitis conferred
  a hazard ratio of 1.10 (95% CI 1.05–1.16) for incident metabolic syndrome, with
  a particularly pronounced effect in the 50–59 age bracket (HR 1.16, 95% CI 1.10–1.23).
  The interaction between age and periodontal severity was statistically significant
  (p=0.015). Limitations include reliance on administrative codes rather than clinical
  examination for periodontitis classification, and residual confounding from lifestyle
  factors. Nevertheless, the scale of the cohort and the 9-year longitudinal design
  make this one of the stronger epidemiological contributions to the oral-systemic
  literature. For dental students, it reinforces the case for treating periodontitis
  as a systemic health intervention, not just an oral one.
