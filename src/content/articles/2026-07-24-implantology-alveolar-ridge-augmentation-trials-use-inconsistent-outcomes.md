summaryDeep: 'Core outcome sets exist precisely to prevent the fragmentation of evidence
  that makes systematic reviews of surgical interventions frustrating. This JCPE systematic
  review audited outcome measures across ARA clinical trials and mapped them to the
  Implant Dentistry Core Outcome Set and Measurement (ID-COSM) framework alongside
  FDA terminology. The finding — substantial heterogeneity in what trials measure
  and how they report it — is not surprising but is now formally documented. For a
  student learning implantology, this is a useful orientation to why the ARA literature
  is hard to synthesise: trials measure bone width, height, volume, histomorphometry,
  and implant survival in incompatible ways. The practical implication is that clinicians
  reading ARA studies should check whether reported outcomes align with ID-COSM before
  drawing clinical conclusions, and researchers planning trials should adopt the framework
  from the outset. The guidelineFlag is set because ID-COSM is an EFP/ITI-endorsed
  framework directly relevant to clinical trial standards.'
