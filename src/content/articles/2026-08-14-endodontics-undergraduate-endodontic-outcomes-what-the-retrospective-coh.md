summaryDeep: 'Dental schools occupy an unusual position in the evidence base: they
  generate large volumes of endodontic treatment under supervision, yet outcomes are
  rarely systematically tracked and published. This retrospective observational cohort
  mines electronic dental records to evaluate success rates for pulpectomy, initial
  RCT, and orthograde retreatment performed by undergraduates, then models the influence
  of variables such as tooth type, preoperative periapical status, obturation quality,
  and case complexity. The findings matter for two reasons: they provide a realistic
  benchmark for student-level outcomes (which tend to be underestimated in the literature),
  and they identify which case characteristics predict failure — information that
  should directly inform case selection and supervision intensity in clinical training.
  Retrospective design and potential record incompleteness are the main limitations.
  Still, for a student currently performing or about to perform their first RCTs,
  this paper offers a grounded, evidence-based picture of what to expect and where
  to focus technical effort.'
