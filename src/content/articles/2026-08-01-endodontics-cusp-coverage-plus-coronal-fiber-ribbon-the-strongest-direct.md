summaryDeep: 'Endodontically treated premolars with single-wall defects are notoriously
  fracture-prone, and the restorative decision — direct versus indirect, cusp coverage
  versus not — carries significant long-term consequences. This study combined flexural
  strength testing of composite and fiber-reinforced composite blocks, finite element
  analysis across five restorative designs, and fracture testing of extracted teeth.
  The design combining cusp reduction with coronal fiber-ribbon placement (CCFC) consistently
  outperformed alternatives across all three methodologies. Quartz fiber reinforcement
  significantly increased flexural strength versus composite alone (p<0.05). The FEA
  confirmed more favorable stress distribution with the CCFC design. Failure modes
  were also more favorable — restorable rather than catastrophic fractures. The clinical
  relevance is direct: for a premolar with a mesio-occlusal cavity after RCT, a fiber-reinforced
  cusp-coverage direct restoration may delay or avoid the need for a full crown, preserving
  tooth structure. This is an in-vitro study, so clinical longevity data are still
  needed.'
