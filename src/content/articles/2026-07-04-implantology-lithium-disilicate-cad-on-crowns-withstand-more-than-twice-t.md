summary: Mechanical testing of 24 implant-supported CAD-on crowns finds lithium disilicate
  veneered zirconia (ZL) fails at 4,649 N versus 2,086 N for feldspathic veneered

summaryDeep: Two CAD-on systems — zirconia cores veneered with either feldspathic
  ceramic (ZF) or lithium disilicate (ZL) — were fabricated via Cerec InLab, cemented
  on titanium abutments, and loaded to failure. ZL's mean failure load (4,649 N) was
  more than double ZF's (2,086 N), a statistically significant difference. FEA using
  Von Mises and principal stress analysis showed that veneer material choice does
  not alter stress distribution at the implant or bone level — the difference is entirely
  in the crown itself. Notably, ZF's lower failure load came with veneer delamination
  as the failure mode, which is clinically preferable to catastrophic fracture. This
  is in vitro lab evidence (n=12 per group), so clinical longevity data are absent.
  For material selection in implant-supported single crowns, ZL offers a substantial
  fracture resistance advantage; ZF's delamination failure mode may be more retrievable
  but at lower loads than seen in posterior function.
