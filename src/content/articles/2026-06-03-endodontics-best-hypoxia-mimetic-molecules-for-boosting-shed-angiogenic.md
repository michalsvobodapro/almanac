summary: Hypoxic preconditioning enhances the angiogenic capacity of stem cells from
  human exfoliated deciduous teeth (SHED), but true hypoxia is impractical clinically.

  regenerated pulp tissue.
summaryDeep: Vascularisation remains the rate-limiting step in regenerative endodontic
  outcomes — without adequate angiogenesis, regenerated tissue is sparse and poorly
  organised. Hypoxic preconditioning of transplanted stem cells is known to upregulate
  pro-angiogenic factors, but maintaining true low-oxygen conditions during cell preparation
  is logistically difficult for clinical labs. This study systematically screens hypoxia-mimetic
  small molecules for their ability to stabilise HIF-1α in SHED without inducing cytotoxicity,
  comparing DMOG, CoCl₂, and other candidates across dose and exposure time. The lead
  compounds significantly upregulate VEGF, bFGF, and SDF-1α secretion and improve
  tube formation in co-culture assays. Crucially, the study defines the therapeutic
  window — the concentration range where HIF stabilisation is maximal and cell viability
  is preserved — giving future translational protocols a concrete starting point.
  Combined with the OCT4-PER1 senescence work, this positions SHED as a more clinically
  manageable cell source than SCAP for some regenerative scenarios.
