summary: A Journal of Periodontal Research review consolidates current mechanistic
  evidence for the periodontitis–rheumatoid arthritis link, centering on shared chronic
  inflammation pathways and the role of subgingival bacteria — particularly P. gingivalis
  — in citrullination and autoantibody generation. The bidirectional relationship
  has moved from epidemiological association to plausible causal mechanism.
summaryDeep: 'The periodontitis–RA association has been observed epidemiologically
  for decades, but mechanistic clarity has lagged. This review synthesizes evidence
  showing that P. gingivalis-derived peptidylarginine deiminase (PPAD) drives protein
  citrullination, generating anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPAs) that are
  central to RA pathogenesis. Shared inflammatory mediators — TNF-α, IL-6, IL-17 —
  amplify tissue destruction in both joints and periodontium. The review also addresses
  the reverse direction: RA medications (methotrexate, biologics) may modulate periodontal
  inflammation. For a dental student, the practical upshot is twofold: RA patients
  warrant heightened periodontal vigilance, and patients with severe periodontitis
  presenting with joint symptoms deserve prompt medical referral. The review does
  not present new primary data, limiting its evidence grade, but it synthesizes a
  coherent mechanistic framework.'
