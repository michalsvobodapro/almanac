summaryDeep: 'As the diabetic patient population grows, dentists increasingly treat
  people on complex anti-hyperglycemic regimens — yet the oral side-effect profiles
  of these drugs are poorly integrated into dental curricula or clinical protocols.
  This scoping review covers the evidence on how drug classes including biguanides
  (metformin), SGLT-2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and insulin affect teeth,
  periodontal tissues, salivary composition, wound healing, and implant osseointegration.
  Some findings are clinically immediate: SGLT-2 inhibitors are associated with oral
  candidiasis and altered salivary pH; GLP-1 agonists may reduce appetite in ways
  that change dietary cariogenic exposure; metformin has shown some evidence of periodontal
  benefit in animal models. The scoping methodology means causality is not established
  for most associations, and the review appropriately flags evidence quality. For
  a dental student, the practical takeaway is to take a thorough medication history
  from diabetic patients and consider drug-specific oral risks when planning restorative,
  periodontal, or implant treatment.'
