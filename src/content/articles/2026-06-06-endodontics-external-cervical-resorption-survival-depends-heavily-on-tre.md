summary: This multi-centre retrospective study evaluates survival and success of teeth
  with external cervical resorption (ECR) across three distinct management strategies,
  using periapical radiographs and small-FOV CBCT for diagnosis and follow-up. ECR
  is notoriously under-diagnosed and under-treated; this dataset gives clinicians
  the outcome data needed to stratify cases and justify treatment decisions. The CBCT-based
  diagnostic pathway is a key takeaway.
summaryDeep: 'External cervical resorption is one of the most diagnostically challenging
  conditions in endodontics — often asymptomatic, frequently misclassified as caries
  or internal resorption, and capable of progressing silently to tooth loss. This
  multi-centre retrospective study is among the largest to compare outcomes across
  three distinct treatment approaches (the specific strategies are detailed in the
  full paper), using both periapical radiography and small field-of-view CBCT for
  baseline classification and follow-up assessment. Survival and success rates varied
  meaningfully by treatment strategy and by Heithersay classification stage at diagnosis,
  reinforcing that early detection via CBCT is not just diagnostically superior but
  prognostically decisive. The data support a tiered approach: less invasive strategies
  are viable for early-stage lesions, while advanced resorption requires more aggressive
  intervention or extraction planning. For students, the clinical lesson is twofold
  — know the CBCT signs of ECR, and understand that the Heithersay classification
  directly maps to treatment selection and expected outcomes.'
