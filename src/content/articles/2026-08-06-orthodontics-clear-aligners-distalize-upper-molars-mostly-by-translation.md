summaryDeep: 'The persistent clinical question about clear aligner molar distalization
  — are you moving the root or just tipping the crown? — finally gets a quantitative
  framework. This systematic review introduces the bodily fraction (BF = bodily displacement
  / crown displacement), derived from a rigid-body approximation using reported crown
  displacement and angulation change. Across 12 included studies, median BF of 0.70
  for U6 and 0.78 for U7 suggests that, on average, aligners achieve predominantly
  translational movement — a more favorable biomechanical outcome than often assumed.
  However, the distribution matters: 37.5% of U6 arms showed mixed behavior and 6.3%
  were tipping-dominant, rising to 13.3% for U7. Internal validation against root
  displacement data showed 88.9% of predictions within 0.75 mm of observed apical
  movement, supporting the model''s utility. For the student and clinician, the takeaway
  is that aligner distalization is not inherently tipping-prone, but individual case
  variation is substantial enough that root angulation monitoring remains essential.
  The BF descriptor itself is a useful addition to future study reporting standards.'
