summary: An in vitro study of 90 hybrid abutment crowns (45 zirconia, 45 PICN) finds
  that adding a circumferential macroretention groove to the internal crown surface

  and may risk surface damage.
summaryDeep: Two-piece hybrid abutment crowns (HACs) bonded to titanium bases represent
  a common CAD/CAM implant restoration, but the adhesive interface is a recognised
  failure point. This study tested whether a circumferential internal groove (macroretention)
  or more aggressive Ti-base sandblasting (250 μm Al₂O₃ at 2.0 bar vs. standard 50
  μm at 1.0 bar) could improve tensile shear bond strength after thermocycling. Six
  groups of 15 specimens each were tested. Macroretention grooves significantly increased
  bond strength in both zirconia and PICN crowns compared to conventionally prepared
  controls. Coarser sandblasting of Ti-bases did not improve bond strength in either
  material group, suggesting that surface roughness beyond the standard protocol yields
  diminishing returns. Failure mode analysis and SEM examination supported these findings.
  The study is in vitro and uses a single cementation protocol (Panavia V5 for zirconia,
  Adiva for PICN), limiting direct generalisation to other cement systems. For clinicians
  and lab technicians, the macroretention groove is a simple, low-cost modification
  worth considering for cases where long-term adhesive durability is a concern.
