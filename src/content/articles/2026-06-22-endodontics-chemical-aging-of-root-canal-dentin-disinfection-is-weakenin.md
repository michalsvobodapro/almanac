summaryDeep: 'Endodontic success has historically been defined by bacterial eradication,
  but this review makes the case that the irrigants achieving that goal are simultaneously
  compromising the tooth''s long-term survival. Sodium hypochlorite degrades collagen
  in the organic matrix; EDTA and citric acid demineralize the inorganic phase; combined
  or prolonged use accelerates both. The result is chemically aged dentin with reduced
  elastic modulus and fracture toughness — a substrate that fails under masticatory
  load years after a technically successful root canal. The review maps the concentration-
  and time-dependent nature of these effects and evaluates mitigation strategies:
  reduced NaOCl concentrations, antioxidant rinses (e.g., sodium ascorbate) post-bleaching,
  and collagen cross-linkers such as proanthocyanidins and riboflavin/UV. While this
  is a narrative review rather than a meta-analysis, the mechanistic evidence is well-grounded
  and the clinical protocols proposed are actionable. For a student entering clinical
  rotations, this is essential framing: every irrigation decision is a trade-off between
  disinfection and structural integrity.'
