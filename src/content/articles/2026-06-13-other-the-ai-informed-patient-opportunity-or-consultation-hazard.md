summaryDeep: 'AI chatbots have crossed from novelty to routine patient behaviour:
  a growing proportion of people now consult a large language model before — or instead
  of — seeking professional advice. This BDJ commentary interrogates what that shift
  means for the dental consultation, where patient-reported symptoms, self-diagnosis,
  and treatment expectations increasingly arrive pre-shaped by AI output. The risks
  are real: AI systems hallucinate, lack access to clinical examination findings,
  and can generate plausible-sounding but incorrect diagnoses. But the opportunity
  is also real — a well-informed patient who has done genuine research is easier to
  engage in shared decision-making. The practical advice for clinicians is to treat
  AI-sourced information as a starting point for conversation rather than a threat
  to professional authority, while remaining alert to cases where misinformation has
  led to delayed presentation or inappropriate self-treatment.'
