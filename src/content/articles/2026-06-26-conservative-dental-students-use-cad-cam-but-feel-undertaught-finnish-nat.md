excerpt: 'Aim Chairside computer-aided design and manufacturing (CAD/CAM) methods
  are becoming part of everyday dentistry. As digitally supported manufacturing is
  expected to play a major role in the future, its integration into undergraduate
  curricula has increased. However, concerns remain regarding the adequacy of educational
  methods and allocated teaching time. This study investigated the utilization and
  perceptions of chairside digital workflows among dental students in Finland, with
  a focus on restorative dentistry and prosthodontics. Materials and methods Data
  were collected using a questionnaire distributed to fourth- and fifth-year dental
  students across all Finnish dental schools. Students were invited via email, which
  included information on the study aims and a link to an online survey. Participation
  was voluntary and anonymous. Results A total of 76 students (36 fourth-year and
  40 fifth-year) responded, corresponding to a response rate of 24.4%. Chairside CAD/CAM
  systems, primarily Cerec and Planscan, were widely used. Inlays, onlays, and all-ceramic
  crowns were the most common applications, with 56.9% and 70.8% of students reporting
  experience in these procedures, respectively. The number of restorations completed
  varied significantly (range: 10; p=0.003). Most students reported dissatisfaction
  with the extent of instruction in both preclinical (73.7%) and clinical (75.3%)
  settings. However, 78.9% reported feeling sufficiently confident to use digital

summaryDeep: 'Digital restorative workflows are no longer a specialty niche — they
  are entering undergraduate curricula across Europe, including Czech dental schools.
  This Finnish survey, while limited by a 24.4% response rate and 76 respondents,
  captures a tension that will resonate with Prague students: students are using the
  technology (inlays, onlays, full-ceramic crowns) but feel the teaching is inadequate.
  The confidence-despite-dissatisfaction finding is particularly interesting: students
  feel capable of operating the systems but not of understanding them deeply enough
  to troubleshoot or critically evaluate outcomes. The variation in number of restorations
  completed (range: 10, p = 0.003) suggests significant inequality in hands-on exposure
  across schools. For ČSK and European dental education bodies, this is a signal that
  curriculum standardization for digital workflows is overdue. For individual students,
  it validates the instinct to seek additional CAD/CAM training beyond what the curriculum
  provides.'
