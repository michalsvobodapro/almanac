summaryDeep: |
  The systematic review pooled 22 in vitro and clinical studies (n ≈ 4,800 canals) comparing heat-treated NiTi instruments — controlled-memory wire, R-phase, blue thermal — against conventional austenitic NiTi files for cyclic fatigue, separation, and shaping outcomes in molar canals.

  Heat-treated instruments cut separation incidence roughly in half (1.4% vs 2.9% pooled), with the largest absolute gains in severely curved second molar mesial canals. Shaping metrics (centering ratio, transportation, taper preservation) were equivalent or slightly better. The authors hedge on a guideline-level recommendation because two trials reported higher screw-in tendency for one heat-treated brand, but their summary leans clearly toward heat-treated as the new default for routine molar endo.
excerptFull: |
  Background: Nickel-titanium (NiTi) rotary instruments revolutionized canal shaping but intracanal separation remains a clinical risk, particularly in curved canals. Heat-treated NiTi alloys (controlled-memory, R-phase, blue thermal) promise improved cyclic fatigue resistance.

  Methods: PRISMA-guided systematic review of MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane databases (2015–2025). Inclusion: comparative studies of heat-treated vs conventional NiTi instruments for shaping or separation outcomes in molar canals. Risk of bias assessed with QUIN tool for in vitro and Cochrane RoB 2 for clinical studies. Random-effects meta-analysis where data permitted.

  Results: 22 studies (15 in vitro, 7 clinical) totaling approximately 4,800 canals. Heat-treated instruments showed pooled separation rate 1.4% (95% CI 1.0–1.9%) vs 2.9% (2.3–3.7%) for conventional NiTi. Shaping efficacy and centering ratios were equivalent. Subgroup analysis suggested largest benefit in second molar mesial canals (>30° curvature).

  Conclusion: Heat-treated NiTi rotary instruments meaningfully reduce intracanal separation without compromising shaping outcomes. Evidence supports their adoption as routine for molar endodontics.
