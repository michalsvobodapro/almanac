summaryDeep: 'This systematic review searched PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science through
  November 2025 and pooled observational studies using ROBINS-I quality assessment
  and random-effects meta-analysis. The headline result: OR >2 for periodontitis in
  OSA patients, with a moderate correlation coefficient (r = 0.39) between OSA severity
  and periodontal breakdown. Shared pathways — systemic inflammation, intermittent
  hypoxia, dysbiosis, and impaired neutrophil function — provide a credible mechanistic
  scaffold. Causality cannot be established from observational data alone, and heterogeneity
  (I² = 57.4%) is non-trivial. Still, the clinical implication is clear: when a patient
  discloses OSA or arrives with a mandibular advancement device, periodontal risk
  should be flagged and a full periodontal examination prioritised. The reverse is
  also true — periodontitis patients should be screened for sleep-disordered breathing
  symptoms. This is increasingly relevant as dental sleep medicine grows in European
  practice.'
