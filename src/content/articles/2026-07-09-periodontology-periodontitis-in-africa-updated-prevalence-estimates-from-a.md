summaryDeep: 'Periodontitis remains one of the most prevalent non-communicable diseases
  globally, yet African epidemiological data have been fragmented and methodologically
  inconsistent. This systematic review addresses that gap by pooling studies up to
  2025 across five major databases, applying rigorous inclusion criteria and meta-analytic
  methods to generate updated continental and sub-regional prevalence estimates. The
  African context matters because risk factor profiles — smoking, diabetes, access
  to care, oral hygiene infrastructure — differ substantially from European or North
  American populations where most guideline evidence originates. For a dental student
  in Prague, the clinical relevance is indirect but real: understanding global periodontitis
  epidemiology contextualizes the 2018 EFP classification and informs how prevalence
  data should be interpreted across populations. The review also serves as a methodological
  template for how systematic epidemiological synthesis should be conducted in underrepresented
  regions.'
