summaryDeep: 'Flowable composites are increasingly used for cervical restorations
  and small posterior fillings in patients who may also have erosive dietary or gastric
  acid exposure. This in-vitro study used 96 disk specimens across four materials
  — Omnichroma Flow, G-ænial Universal Injectable, Universal Flo, and EverX Flow —
  subjected to sequential 48-hour hydrochloric acid immersion (simulating gastric
  acid) followed by 24-hour coffee immersion. Surface roughness and color coordinates
  were measured at baseline, post-exposure, and after one of three cleaning protocols
  (toothbrushing, water flosser, mouthrinse). All four composites degraded under the
  combined challenge, but Omnichroma Flow showed the lowest roughness values throughout.
  Color stability varied by material and cleaning method. The clinical translation
  is nuanced: in-vitro acid concentrations and immersion times are proxies, not perfect
  simulations of the oral environment. But for patients with documented GERD or heavy
  coffee habits, material selection for flowable restorations is not trivial. Omnichroma
  Flow''s filler architecture appears to confer a surface durability advantage in
  this specific challenge model.'
