summary: A PRISMA-ScR scoping review of 50 studies confirms that removable, fixed,
  and implant-supported prostheses all harbor diverse microbial communities, including
  pathogens linked to peri-implantitis and systemic infection. Surface material, roughness,
  and prosthesis type influence colonization patterns. The review frames prosthetic
  surfaces as an underappreciated infection vector in dental practice.
summaryDeep: 'Starting from 1,149 database records, this scoping review retained 50
  studies presenting primary microbiological data collected directly from prosthetic
  surfaces or implant-supported components. Covered prosthesis types included removable
  full and partial dentures, fixed crowns and bridges, and implant abutments and superstructures.
  Across all types, microbial colonization was universal — species profiles varied
  by surface material and roughness, with rougher surfaces and certain polymers supporting
  richer biofilms. Pathogens associated with peri-implantitis (Porphyromonas gingivalis,
  Fusobacterium nucleatum, Candida spp.) were identified on implant components even
  in the absence of clinical disease. The clinical implication is dual: prosthesis
  hygiene protocols need to be specific to surface type, and implant components removed
  for maintenance or repair should be treated as contaminated. For students, this
  review is a useful reminder that the prosthetic surface is not biologically inert
  — it is an ecosystem that requires active management. The scoping design means causality
  cannot be established, but the breadth of evidence is sufficient to justify updated
  hygiene counseling.'
