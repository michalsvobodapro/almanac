summary: An in-vitro dye-penetration study in 80 extracted molars compared three injectable
  bioceramic putties for furcation perforation repair. Bio C-Repair (Angelus) demonstrated

summaryDeep: 'Furcation perforation repair is one of endodontics'' more demanding
  procedural challenges, and the proliferation of injectable bioceramic putties has
  outpaced comparative evidence. This study standardised perforations in 80 mandibular
  first molars, repaired them with one of three bioceramic putties or left them as
  controls, incubated at 37 °C for 24 hours, then exposed pulp chambers to 2% methylene
  blue for 48 hours. Linear dye penetration was measured stereomicroscopically. Bio
  C-Repair showed the least penetration, with statistically significant differences
  versus the other two materials on post-hoc testing. Limitations are inherent to
  the dye-penetration model: it does not replicate clinical moisture conditions, tissue
  pressure, or the dynamic sealing demands of a functioning tooth. Material composition
  differences (calcium silicate ratios, radiopacifiers) likely explain the sealing
  variance but were not mechanistically explored. The finding is directionally useful
  for product selection but should not be over-interpreted until corroborated by clinical
  or at minimum push-out bond strength data.'
