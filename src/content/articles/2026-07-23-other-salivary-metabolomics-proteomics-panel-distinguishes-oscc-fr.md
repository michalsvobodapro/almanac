excerpt: 'Background Non-invasive biomarker-based approaches are urgently needed to
  support early identification and monitoring of Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC)
  and its precursor, oral submucous fibrosis (OSMF). Methods We employed combined
  metabolomic and proteomic analyses to profile saliva and lesion surface swabs from
  clinically diagnosed OSCC (n = 15), OSMF (n = 20), and healthy controls (n = 27).
  Polar and nonpolar metabolites were extracted separately from saliva, while proteins
  were isolated from exfoliated mucosal epithelial cells. Differential expression,
  pathway enrichment, and biomarker classification were performed across four comparisons:
  OSCC vs non-OSCC (controls + OSMF), OSCC vs OSMF, OSMF vs control, and OSCC vs control.
  Results Distinct molecular changes were observed across the health-to-cancer spectrum.
  Top discriminatory salivary metabolites included linoelaidic acid, gamma-glutamylcysteine,
  and 3-hydroxybutyryl-CoA, implicating dysregulated linoleic acid metabolism, redox
  imbalance, and fatty acid β-oxidation in pathogenesis. Proteomic data revealed significant
  upregulation of histone variants, cytoskeletal keratins, and BPI-fold-containing
  proteins in OSCC, reflecting chromatin remodelling and epithelial-mesenchymal transition.
  Multivariate biomarker panels achieved high accuracy (AUC ≥ 0.96) for classifying

summaryDeep: 'Early oral cancer detection remains a clinical gap: most OSCC is diagnosed
  at stage III–IV, when survival drops sharply. This cross-sectional multiomics study
  is small (n=62) but methodologically ambitious — combining polar and nonpolar salivary
  metabolomics with proteomic profiling of exfoliated mucosal cells across four diagnostic
  comparisons. The AUC ≥0.96 for OSCC vs. OSMF is striking, though small-sample multivariate
  models are prone to overfitting and the result needs prospective validation in a
  larger, independent cohort. The identified metabolites implicate linoleic acid metabolism
  and redox dysregulation, consistent with known OSCC biology. Proteomic upregulation
  of histone variants and keratins aligns with epithelial-mesenchymal transition signatures.
  For a dental student: saliva-based diagnostics are moving from concept to near-clinical
  reality — understanding the biomarker landscape now will matter when point-of-care
  devices reach the clinic within the next decade.'
