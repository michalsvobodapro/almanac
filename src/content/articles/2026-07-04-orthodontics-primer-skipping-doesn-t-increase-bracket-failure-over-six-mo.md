  patients undergoing fixed appliances treatment. This split-mouth randomized controlled
  trial was conducted on 42 participants. Diagonal quadrants were randomly assigned
  to either the primer (control) or non-primer (experimental) group. Allocation was
  concealed in opaque envelopes and revealed at the time of intervention. Both genders,
  aged 12-30 years, and visiting for the management of malocclusion were included.
  Patients with dental anomalies, caries, or restorations were excluded. The direct
  bonding technique was used for the placement of conventional metallic brackets.
  Participants were followed up for six months, with monthly visits, and bracket failure
  within each group was recorded. Generalized estimating equation logistic regression
  was used to account for clustering of multiple attachments within participants and
  to estimate odds ratios. A total of 42 participants contributing 840 orthodontic
  attachments were analyzed over six months. Mean age was 17.81 ± 4.09 years, and
  52.4% were female. Monthly attachment failure rates were low in both groups, ranging
  from 3.8% to 4.3% in the primer group and 3.6% to 6.0% in the non-primer group,
  with no significant unadjusted differences at any month. In multivariable generalized
  estimating equation analysis, bonding method was not significantly associated with

summaryDeep: This split-mouth RCT randomised diagonal quadrants to conventional primer
  or primer-free direct bonding of metallic brackets in 42 patients aged 12–30. Over
  six monthly visits, failure rates were numerically slightly higher in the no-primer
  group but the difference never reached significance in multivariable GEE analysis
  accounting for clustering. Male sex was associated with higher failure in exploratory
  analysis. The study is adequately powered for its primary outcome and uses allocation
  concealment. Limitations include a 6-month horizon (longer follow-up might reveal
  differences) and exclusion of patients with caries or restorations — the very cases
  where primer might matter most. For a student, this is reassuring evidence that
  primer-free bonding is a viable time-saver in straightforward cases, but the exclusion
  criteria mean it shouldn't be generalised to all patients.
