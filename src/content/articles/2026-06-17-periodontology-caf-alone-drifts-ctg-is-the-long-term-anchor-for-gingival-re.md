summaryDeep: 'This review synthesized randomized trials, controlled studies, case
  series, and retrospective cohorts with minimum 5-year follow-up to compare CAF alone
  versus CAF + CTG for gingival recession. CAF alone achieves high mean root coverage
  at 12 months, but long-term data — some extending beyond 20 years — document a steady
  apical drift of the gingival margin, particularly where baseline gingival thickness
  is inadequate. The addition of CTG reverses this trajectory: keratinized tissue
  width and gingival thickness gains are maintained, and complete root coverage rates
  remain significantly higher at extended follow-up. Surgical modifications (tunnel,
  modified CAF) and patient-specific factors such as recession depth and tooth position
  modulate outcomes but do not overcome the phenotype deficit. The clinical implication
  is unambiguous: in patients with a thin or medium phenotype, omitting CTG to save
  chair time is a false economy — relapse will likely require retreatment. Early gingival
  margin position post-surgery is the single strongest surrogate for long-term stability
  and should guide postoperative monitoring protocols.'
