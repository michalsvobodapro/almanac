excerpt: Identifying factors linked to multi-visit nonsurgical root canal treatment
  (RCT) may help distinguish cases that warrant staging from those suitable for single-visit
  treatment. This study examined patient-, tooth-, and dentist-level characteristics
  associated with multi-visit RCT. Data were analyzed from 1,723 patients enrolled
  in the National Dental Practice-Based Research Network study. The primary outcome
  was completion of RCT in more than one visit. Patient demographics, preoperative
  symptoms, clinical and tooth characteristics, procedural difficulty, and dentist
  factors were evaluated for their association with multi-visit RCT completion using
  multivariable generalized estimating equation models accounting for clustering of
  patients within dentists. Overall, 316 patients (18%) received more than one visit.
  In the final adjusted model, multi-visit RCT was independently associated with academic
  or public practices (OR= 2.98; p=.03 ) in the Northeast region (OR=2.59; p=.02).

summaryDeep: 'The single-visit vs. multi-visit RCT debate is often framed around infection
  control dogma, but this large practice-based cohort reframes it empirically. With
  1,723 patients across the National Dental Practice-Based Research Network, the 18%
  multi-visit rate is lower than many textbooks imply. The adjusted model is the key
  contribution: after controlling for clustering within dentists, tooth-level factors
  (molar, mandibular, non-vital, swelling, tooth mobility) and patient-level factors
  (severe pain, Asian/Hispanic ethnicity) independently predicted staging. The OR
  of 0.48 for vital teeth is particularly actionable — vital pulp cases are strongly
  single-visit candidates. The system-level finding is striking: academic and public
  practices were nearly three times more likely to stage treatment (OR 2.98), independent
  of case complexity. This suggests training environment and workflow norms shape
  staging decisions as much as clinical indicators. For a student entering practice,
  this data provides an evidence-based framework for triage rather than defaulting
  to institutional habit.'
