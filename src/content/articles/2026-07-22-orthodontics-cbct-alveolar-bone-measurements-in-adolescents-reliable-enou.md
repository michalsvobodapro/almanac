summary: A substudy of an RCT on fixed-appliance treatment (n=48 adolescents, five
  CBCT units) found moderate-to-excellent intrarater reliability for alveolar bone
  level measurements, but cross-unit agreement was insufficient for detecting small
  longitudinal changes. Fenestration detection agreement was poor across raters and
  units.
summaryDeep: 'Ninety-six CBCT volumes (baseline and post-treatment) from 48 adolescents
  were measured by three independent raters using a standardised multiplanar reconstruction
  protocol. Intrarater ICC was moderate to excellent, confirming the protocol itself
  is learnable and reproducible. The problem is between-unit agreement: Bland-Altman
  plots and Dahlberg''s formula revealed systematic differences between CBCT machines
  that exceed the minimal detectable change at several sites, meaning that a patient
  scanned on one unit at baseline and a different unit at follow-up could show apparent
  bone loss that is actually machine noise. For fenestration detection, percentage
  agreement was low regardless of unit. The practical implication for longitudinal
  orthodontic research — and for any Prague clinic running multi-unit CBCT workflows
  — is that same-unit scanning at baseline and follow-up is not optional; it is a
  methodological requirement.'
