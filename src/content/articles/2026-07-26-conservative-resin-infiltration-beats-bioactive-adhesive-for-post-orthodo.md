summary: A randomized split-mouth RCT compared resin infiltration against a bioactive
  adhesive for treating white spot lesions after fixed appliance therapy. Resin infiltration

  comparisons available.
summaryDeep: 'Post-orthodontic white spot lesions are one of the most common and frustrating
  sequelae of fixed appliance treatment, and clinicians have lacked high-quality comparative
  data to guide material choice. This split-mouth RCT — where each patient served
  as their own control — pitted resin infiltration (Icon, DMG) directly against a
  bioactive adhesive on contralateral lesions. Resin infiltration produced significantly
  better esthetic outcomes and higher patient satisfaction scores. The bioactive adhesive''s
  theoretical advantage — remineralizing the lesion rather than masking it — did not
  translate into superior perceived esthetics at the follow-up interval studied. For
  the practicing clinician, this reinforces resin infiltration as the first-line intervention
  for cosmetically significant post-orthodontic WSLs. The commentary in Evidence-Based
  Dentistry contextualizes the trial''s limitations: follow-up duration and lesion
  severity classification both matter, and longer-term remineralization data from
  the bioactive arm are still pending.'
