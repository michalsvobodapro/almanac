summaryDeep: 'Polyethylene fiber reinforcement (Ribbond®) has been promoted as a way
  to reduce fracture risk in large composite restorations in primary teeth, where
  occlusal loads and moisture control challenges are significant. This double-blind
  parallel RCT randomized 60 children (5–10 years) to CR+PF or conventional CR for
  occluso-proximal restorations, evaluating outcomes at 3, 6, and 12 months using
  FDI criteria. At 12 months, 53 restorations were evaluable; retention rates were
  statistically indistinguishable (HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.27–1.01). Cox regression identified
  age — not material type — as the significant predictor of success. The one genuine
  difference: the fiber group took 5.5 fewer minutes on average, possibly because
  the fiber matrix stabilizes the restoration during placement. For students in pediatric
  dentistry rotations: fiber reinforcement is not indicated for retention improvement,
  but the time saving may be clinically relevant in anxious young patients where chair
  time is a limiting factor.'
