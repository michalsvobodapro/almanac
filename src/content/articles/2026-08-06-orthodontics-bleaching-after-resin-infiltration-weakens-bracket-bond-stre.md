summary: 'In this randomized in vitro study (64 extracted teeth), resin infiltration
  followed by bleaching significantly reduced shear bond strength of orthodontic brackets.
  Home bleaching (10% carbamide peroxide) produced the lowest SBS values (14.99 MPa,
  dropping to 10.84 MPa after thermocycling), differing significantly from untreated
  controls. In-office bleaching (25% H₂O₂) showed a similar trend. The clinical implication:
  sequence matters when combining Icon treatment with bracket bonding.'
summaryDeep: 'Resin infiltration (Icon) is increasingly used to mask white-spot lesions
  before or during orthodontic treatment, and patients frequently request concurrent
  whitening. This study tests what happens to bracket bond strength when bleaching
  follows infiltration — and the answer is a meaningful reduction, particularly with
  home bleaching. The mechanism is well-established: peroxide residues within the
  resin-infiltrated enamel inhibit adhesive polymerization, reducing bond strength.
  The thermocycling data (5,000 cycles, 5–55°C) are particularly relevant because
  they simulate clinical aging: home bleaching SBS dropped from 14.99 to 10.84 MPa,
  approaching the lower threshold for clinical acceptability. The in vitro design
  limits direct translation — clinical bracket failure rates depend on many additional
  variables — but the directional signal is clear. For a student managing white-spot
  lesions in orthodontic patients: if Icon has been applied, delay bracket bonding
  for at least two weeks after any bleaching, or apply antioxidant treatment before
  bonding.'
