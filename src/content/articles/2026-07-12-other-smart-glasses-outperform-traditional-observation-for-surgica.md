summaryDeep: 'Surgical education in dentistry has a persistent visibility problem:
  students at the back of an observation group see almost nothing of the operative
  field. This study randomised 95 fifth-year students into traditional observation,
  live smart-glasses video streaming, and a hybrid group during implant surgery. Kruskal-Wallis
  analysis showed significant differences in observational awareness and surgical
  field visibility favouring the video-streaming groups; qualitative interviews reinforced
  that students felt more engaged and better prepared. The hybrid group performed
  best overall, suggesting that wearable video supplements rather than replaces physical
  presence. For a Prague dental student, this is directly relevant: if your faculty
  is evaluating surgical observation technology, this is the strongest available evidence
  base. The study is single-institution and uses self-reported learning outcomes,
  which limits objectivity, but the effect sizes are large enough to be credible.
  The technology is accessible and low-cost relative to simulation alternatives.'
