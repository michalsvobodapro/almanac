summaryDeep: 'Combination injuries — where a single traumatized tooth sustains two
  or more injury types simultaneously (e.g., crown fracture plus luxation) — are clinically
  significant because each component requires separate management and the interaction
  between them affects prognosis. This PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis searched
  PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, and Scopus without language or date restrictions,
  including 30 studies from 20 countries covering 34,382 traumatized permanent teeth.
  Pooled prevalence was 14% (95% CI: 11–19%), though extreme heterogeneity (I² = 98.2%)
  reflects genuine variation in study populations, trauma etiology, and documentation
  practices. Denmark showed the highest pooled prevalence among multi-study countries.
  The GRADE certainty assessment is reported. For clinical practice, 14% means roughly
  one in seven trauma patients presenting with a ''simple'' injury actually has a
  combination injury — making systematic, multi-component examination and documentation
  non-negotiable at every trauma presentation.'
