  the relationship between edentulism and NCDs in the CHARLS study, a large prospective

summary: A prospective cohort of 6,585 Chinese adults followed for 6.5 years found
  edentulous participants had a 17% higher overall NCD risk, with particularly striking
  associations for asthma (HR 2.83) and cancer (HR 2.66). Confounding is the obvious
  caveat, but the effect sizes are large enough to demand attention.
summaryDeep: Using the nationally representative CHARLS dataset, this study tracked
  middle-aged and older Chinese adults from 2011 to 2018, with the primary endpoint
  being first diagnosis of any non-communicable disease. After adjustment for potential
  confounders, edentulism was independently associated with incident NCD (HR 1.17),
  asthma (HR 2.83), hypertension (HR 1.21), and cancer (HR 2.66). The asthma and cancer
  hazard ratios are striking and biologically plausible — chronic oral infection,
  altered microbiome, and nutritional compromise from chewing impairment are candidate
  mechanisms — but residual confounding in an observational cohort of this design
  cannot be excluded. Stratified analyses revealed age modified the asthma association
  and sex modified dyslipidemia. For the dental student, this is a compelling argument
  for framing tooth retention as a systemic health intervention, not just a quality-of-life
  issue. It also reinforces the case for prosthetic rehabilitation in edentulous patients
  beyond mere function.
