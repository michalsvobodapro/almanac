summary: Shotgun metagenomics on primary endodontic infections reveals that both taxonomic
  makeup and functional gene content of the root canal microbiome correlate with periapical
  lesion size. Larger lesions host distinct microbial communities, not just higher
  bacterial loads. The data push toward microbiome-informed treatment planning rather
  than one-size-fits-all chemo-mechanical protocols.
summaryDeep: 'Most endodontic microbiology has relied on 16S amplicon sequencing,
  which misses functional capacity. This observational shotgun metagenomic study characterises
  the full taxonomic and functional landscape of primary root canal infections and
  tests whether community composition — not just diversity — predicts periapical destruction.
  The answer is yes: specific taxa and metabolic pathways are enriched in cases with
  larger apical lesions, suggesting that virulence is a community-level property rather
  than the work of a single pathogen. For the clinician, this matters because it implies
  that standard irrigation protocols may be insufficient when high-virulence consortia
  are present, and that pre-treatment imaging combined with microbiome profiling could
  eventually stratify cases by difficulty. It also opens the door to targeted antimicrobial
  adjuncts aimed at keystone species identified in destructive communities.'
