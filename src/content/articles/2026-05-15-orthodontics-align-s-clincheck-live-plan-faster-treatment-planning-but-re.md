  software, designed to let clinicians generate and present an initial Invisalign
  plan while the patient is still in the chair. The pitch is speed and personalisation:
  rather than sending records away and following up at a second appointment, the clinician
  can show a preliminary tooth-movement simulation in real time, theoretically improving
  case acceptance and reducing administrative lag. The technology is a logical extension
  of Align''s existing digital ecosystem and fits the broader industry trend toward
  chairside digital workflows. However, this item is a product announcement, not a
  clinical study — there is no published evidence on whether live-plan presentations
  improve treatment outcomes, patient satisfaction, or adherence compared to conventional
  planning. For a student, it''s worth knowing the tool exists and understanding its
  commercial logic, but the absence of independent validation means it shouldn''t
  drive clinical decisions. Treat it as a feature to evaluate critically when you
  encounter it in practice.'
