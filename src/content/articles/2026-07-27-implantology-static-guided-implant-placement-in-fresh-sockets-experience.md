summaryDeep: 'Using two fresh-frozen cadaver heads, researchers extracted 20 teeth
  (incisors, premolars, molars) and had two operators — one expert, one non-expert,
  neither with prior guided surgery experience — place implants using a digital workflow
  integrating CBCT and intraoral scanning. Post-operative superimposition revealed
  mean head deviations of 1.26 mm (expert) vs. 1.54 mm (non-expert), tip deviations
  of 1.70 vs. 1.81 mm, and angular deviations of 3.83° vs. 3.32° — none statistically
  significant. Surgical time was also equivalent. The real signal: anterior implants
  had significantly greater head and tip deviations than posterior ones (p=0.03 and
  p=0.04), likely reflecting reduced guide stability and narrower bone envelopes.
  The study is limited by small numbers (n=20) and a cadaver model, but the operator-independence
  finding supports static guided surgery as a leveler for less experienced clinicians
  — with the caveat that anterior esthetic zone cases carry inherently higher positional
  risk.'
