summaryDeep: 'Upper airway resistance syndrome in mouth-breathing adolescents with
  high-angle Class II malocclusion presents a dual challenge: correcting the skeletal
  pattern while improving airway patency. This comparative cohort (30 VB, 18 controls,
  mean age not specified) used lateral cephalometry, CBCT, polysomnography, and OSA-18
  scores to evaluate the van Beek headgear activator. Mandibular growth was stimulated
  (Co-Go median increase 3.70 mm, p<0.001), transverse discrepancy improved (T-W +4.47
  mm), and SNA decreased 1.62° — a combined dentoalveolar and skeletal response. Airway
  metrics improved on PSG. The non-randomised design and unequal group sizes (30 vs
  18) are limitations, and the absence of long-term retention data leaves durability
  uncertain. Nevertheless, the convergence of skeletal, airway, and growth data makes
  this a compelling case for early functional appliance intervention in this specific
  phenotype — relevant for orthodontic screening in paediatric patients presenting
  with mouth breathing.'
