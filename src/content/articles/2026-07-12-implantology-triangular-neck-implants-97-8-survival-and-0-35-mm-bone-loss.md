summary: A PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis of eight studies reports
  a pooled 1-year marginal bone loss of 0.35 mm and a survival rate of 97.8% for triangular-neck
  implants — figures that compare favourably with conventional designs. Heterogeneity

summaryDeep: Triangular-neck implants feature a non-circular cross-section at the
  implant–abutment interface, theoretically distributing crestal stress more evenly
  and preserving interproximal bone. This meta-analysis pooled five studies with sufficient
  quantitative data from an initial 115 titles, applying GRADE methodology and Cochrane/JBI
  risk-of-bias tools. The 1-year MBL of 0.35 mm (95% CI 0.24–0.46, I² = 23.2%) sits
  well within the accepted ≤1.5 mm threshold, and the 97.8% survival rate (I² = 0%)
  is reassuring. The near-zero heterogeneity in survival is notable. The critical
  limitation is that all included studies are single-arm — there are no head-to-head
  RCTs against standard implants — so the favourable numbers reflect performance,
  not superiority. For a student evaluating implant system choices, this is a green
  light for the design's short-term safety profile, but long-term crestal bone data
  and comparative trials are still needed before triangular-neck geometry becomes
  a differentiating clinical argument.
