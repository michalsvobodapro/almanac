  biological complications of implant therapy, yet the relative contribution of patient-
  and implant-related factors to their development remains incompletely understood.
  This retrospective study examined the associations between demographic, clinical,
  and implant-related variables and peri-implant disease status in a large clinical
  cohort. Methods A total of 199 patients with 968 dental implants placed between
  January 2015 and January 2023 were evaluated. Variables recorded included demographic
  characteristics, systemic conditions, smoking status, oral hygiene habits, implant-related

  gingiva width, and gingival thickness), and radiographic marginal bone loss. Peri-implant
  disease status - classified as peri-implant health, peri-implant mucositis, or peri-implantitis

summaryDeep: 'Peri-implant disease risk stratification has long been debated, and
  this cohort — 968 implants placed 2015–2023, classified by 2017 World Workshop criteria
  — provides one of the cleaner multivariate pictures to date. GEE modelling accounts
  for the clustering of multiple implants per patient, a methodological step many
  smaller studies skip. The headline finding: patient-level behavioral factors dominate
  over implant design variables. Heavy smoking nearly quintuples peri-implantitis
  odds; inadequate brushing adds a similar multiplier for mucositis. The 8.5% peri-implantitis
  rate sits at the lower end of published estimates, possibly reflecting the center''s
  patient selection. Retrospective design limits causal inference, and the study does
  not report implant surface or connection type in detail. Nevertheless, the magnitude
  of the behavioral risk signals reinforces that implant maintenance counseling —
  especially smoking cessation — is not optional. For a student: when you place or
  restore implants, the maintenance conversation starts at the treatment-planning
  appointment.'
