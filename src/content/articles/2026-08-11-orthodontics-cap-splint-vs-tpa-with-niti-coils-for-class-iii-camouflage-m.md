summaryDeep: 'Maxillary arch mesialization using palatal mini-screws is an established
  camouflage strategy for mild-to-moderate Class III malocclusion, but appliance choice
  has lacked direct comparative data. This retrospective study from Istanbul Okan
  University compared 11 patients treated with an acrylic cap splint covering the
  full dentition and 15 treated with a TPA plus NiTi closed-coil springs, both anchored
  to palatal mini-screws. Cephalometric analysis at T0 and T1 showed the TPA-coil
  system produced 2.6× more molar mesialization (3.57 mm vs. 1.36 mm) at a faster
  monthly rate. Critically, the skeletal effects diverged: SNA and ANB increased in
  the cap splint group (suggesting some forward maxillary displacement), while ANB
  declined in the TPA group (more dentoalveolar tipping). The small sample (n=26)
  and retrospective design limit generalisability, and patient selection differences
  between groups cannot be excluded. Still, for a clinician choosing between these
  two systems, the data suggest the TPA-coil approach is more efficient for tooth
  movement while the cap splint may offer a modest skeletal benefit — a distinction
  worth factoring into treatment planning.'
