summaryDeep: 'This cohort study is notable for its granularity: 395 molars assessed
  at pre- and post-treatment, with 180 followed to a later time point, all in patients
  who had completed phase I non-surgical periodontal therapy and were on regular maintenance.
  The 5% deterioration rate sounds modest, but it is concentrated in identifiable
  risk profiles — compromised baseline support, history of regenerative surgery, and
  specific orthodontic movements (molar extrusion, antagonist intrusion, significant
  axial uprighting). The finding that prior regenerative periodontal surgery predisposes
  to orthodontic-induced deterioration is clinically important and underappreciated:
  regenerated bone may not respond to orthodontic forces the same way native bone
  does. Post-orthodontic furcation involvement was the strongest predictor of eventual
  tooth loss. The practical message is not to avoid orthodontics in periodontitis
  patients — the literature supports it when disease is controlled — but to select
  movements carefully, avoid extrusion of compromised molars, and intensify monitoring.'
