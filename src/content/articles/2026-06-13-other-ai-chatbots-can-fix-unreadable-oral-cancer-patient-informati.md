  survival, yet patient-facing information materials are consistently written at reading
  levels that exclude the least health-literate patients — precisely those at highest
  risk. This study tested whether AI chatbots could bridge that gap by rewriting existing
  materials to recommended readability standards. The results are encouraging: AI-rewritten
  content hit target readability scores while preserving clinical accuracy, suggesting
  a practical, scalable tool for dental practices and public health campaigns. For
  a student or clinician thinking about patient communication, the implication is
  immediate: if you''re handing out printed oral cancer information, check its readability
  score, and consider AI-assisted simplification as a routine step. The broader point
  — that AI can democratise health literacy rather than just automate admin — is one
  of the more genuinely useful clinical applications of the technology.'
