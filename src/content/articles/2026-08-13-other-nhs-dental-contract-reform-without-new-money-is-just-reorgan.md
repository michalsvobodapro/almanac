summaryDeep: 'Pabary''s argument is straightforward: the NHS dental contract is not
  primarily a design problem but a funding problem. Successive governments have proposed
  new contract models — capitation, blended payments, reformed UDAs — but none have
  come with sufficient new money to make participation economically rational for dentists.
  The result is continued drift to private practice and worsening access. The podcast
  was recorded before the Burnham government took office, which makes it a useful
  baseline: the new dental minister (Frith, appointed this week) inherits exactly
  the same fiscal constraint. For students, this is a masterclass in health-system
  economics: clinical quality and access are downstream of payment model design, and
  payment model design is downstream of political will to fund it. The same tension
  exists in Czech public dental insurance debates.'
