  of a surface pre-reacted glass-ionomer (S-PRG)–based resin varnish and a bioactive
  glass–based air-polishing system in the management of dentin hypersensitivity and
  their effects on tooth color. Methods This randomized clinical trial included 34
  participants diagnosed with dentin hypersensitivity. Participants were randomly
  allocated (1:1) to receive either an S-PRG–based varnish or a bioactive glass–based
  air-polishing treatment. The primary outcome was pain intensity measured using the
  Visual Analog Scale (VAS), while the secondary outcome was tooth color change. Pain

  using a 0–10 VAS. Tooth color change was evaluated using modified United States

summaryDeep: Thirty-four patients with confirmed dentin hypersensitivity were randomised
  1:1 to either S-PRG (surface pre-reacted glass-ionomer) varnish or bioactive glass
  air polishing. VAS pain scores were recorded at baseline, immediately post-treatment,
  and at 1, 6, and 12 months. The S-PRG group showed significantly lower VAS scores
  at every time point after baseline. Both interventions produced acceptable tooth
  colour outcomes by USPHS criteria. The sample size is modest and single-centre,
  but 12-month follow-up with five assessment points is methodologically solid for
  this indication. S-PRG varnishes release fluoride, strontium, sodium, and silicate
  ions that occlude dentinal tubules and may also remineralise the smear layer — a
  mechanism that appears more durable than the physical plugging achieved by bioactive
  glass polishing. For the clinic, this suggests S-PRG varnish as the preferred in-office
  option when patients present with symptomatic hypersensitivity, particularly after
  scaling or bleaching.
