summaryDeep: 'Zirconia implants are increasingly marketed on the basis of superior
  biocompatibility and esthetic properties, with an implicit claim of lower peri-implant
  disease risk. This meta-analysis — 19 studies, 687 patients, 995 implants, mean
  follow-up ~49 months — provides the most comprehensive prevalence estimate to date.
  The pooled peri-implantitis rate is reassuringly low at ≤3 years (2%, 95% CI 0–6%)
  and ≤5 years (2%, 95% CI 0–8%), but rises to 8% in studies with follow-up beyond
  5 years. High heterogeneity (I²=79.3% in the short-term subgroup) limits confidence
  in the point estimates. Crucially, no head-to-head comparison with titanium implants
  could be performed with sufficient statistical power to declare material superiority.
  The evidence base remains thin: 19 studies with a mean follow-up under 4 years is
  not enough to make definitive claims about a device expected to last decades. Clinicians
  should counsel patients that zirconia implants are a legitimate option, not a peri-implantitis-proof
  one.'
