excerpt: Abstract This randomized clinical trial aimed to assess the relation of insertion
  torque value (ITV) and implant stability quotient (ISQ) between bone-level (BL)
  and tissue-level (TL) dental implants (ISQ recorded immediately and 3 months after
  implant placement). A total of 90 dental implants (44 BL, 46 TL; BioHorizons Camlog,
  Iberica) were placed in 21 patients. ITV was recorded at placement. ISQ (Penguin
  device, Klockner) was measured horizontally (ISQ-H) and vertically (ISQ-V) at baseline
  and at the 90-day follow-up. The Mann–Whitney U test and Spearman's correlation
  tests (α = 0.05) were used for the analyses. No significant difference in ITV was
  found between the BL (36.0 ± 11.5 Ncm) and TL (38.0 ± 11.5 Ncm) groups (p = 0.381).
  Similarly, no significant differences were observed in baseline ISQ-H (p = 0.928)
  or ISQ-V (p = 0.874). At 90 days, ISQ-H values were marginally higher in the TL

summary: In this pilot RCT of 90 implants (44 bone-level, 46 tissue-level), insertion
  torque and baseline ISQ were statistically equivalent between designs. At 90 days,
  tissue-level implants showed marginally higher horizontal ISQ (66.5 vs. 57.1; p
  = 0.029). Insertion torque correlated with primary stability but not with 90-day
  ISQ, confirming that secondary stability is governed by osseointegration biology,
  not initial mechanical engagement.
summaryDeep: 'The bone-level vs. tissue-level implant debate has long been driven
  by preference and prosthetic workflow rather than stability data. This pilot RCT
  provides the first randomized comparison of resonance frequency analysis outcomes
  between the two designs at 90 days. The equivalence at placement is reassuring —
  neither design confers a primary stability advantage. The 90-day horizontal ISQ
  difference favoring tissue-level implants (66.5 vs. 57.1) is statistically significant
  but modest, and the authors appropriately flag it as hypothesis-generating rather
  than practice-changing given the pilot sample size. The dissociation between insertion
  torque and 90-day ISQ is the most generalizable finding: high torque at placement
  does not predict better osseointegration, which has direct implications for loading
  protocols that use ITV as a proxy for readiness. The study is underpowered for subgroup
  analyses, and longer follow-up is needed to determine whether the ISQ difference
  translates to clinical outcomes like marginal bone loss.'
