excerptFull: 'Lucchesi, VH, Giorgetti, APO, Corrêa, MG. et al. Impact of systemic
  resveratrol on non-surgical periodontal treatment of smokers: A 12-month randomized
  clinical trial. Clin Oral Invest 2025;29:428. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00784-025-06517-9
  This double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized clinical trial evaluated whether
  systemic resveratrol (500 mg/day for 180 days) improves outcomes of non-surgical
  periodontal therapy in smokers with Stage III or IV, Grade C periodontitis. Thirty-eight
  adult smokers were recruited from a university periodontal clinic and randomly assigned
  to receive full-mouth ultrasonic debridement combined with either systemic resveratrol
  or placebo (n = 19 per group). Eligible participants had at least 20 teeth, smoked
  a minimum of 10 cigarettes daily for at least five years, and presented with multiple
  periodontal sites exhibiting probing depth&nbsp;(PD) and clinical attachment loss
  ≥5 mm; patients with recent periodontal therapy, systemic diseases, pregnancy, or
  recent use of antibiotics or anti-inflammatory medications were excluded. Clinical
  parameters, including PD, clinical attachment level (CAL), position of gingival
  margin (PGM), full-mouth plaque score (FMPS), and full-mouth bleeding score (FMBS),
  were assessed at baseline and at 3-, 6-, and 12-month follow-up, alongside microbiological
  and immunoinflammatory markers. The primary outcome was CAL, while secondary outcomes
  included PD, PGM, FMPS, subgingival microbiological profiles, and inflammatory cytokine
  concentrations. Data distribution was assessed using exploratory and normality testing,
  with appropriate transformations applied where necessary. Repeated clinical measures
  were analyzed using restricted maximum likelihood models and generalized estimating
  equations, while microbiological and immunological outcomes were compared using
  non-parametric tests. Missing data were addressed using multiple imputation, and
  statistical significance was set at p &lt; 0.05. Compared with placebo, the resveratrol
  group demonstrated significantly lower PD and improved CAL throughout follow-up.
  At 12 months, PD was 2.80 mm in the resveratrol group versus 3.02 mm in controls,
  while CAL was 3.87 mm versus 4.39 mm, respectively. Resveratrol was also associated
  with lower levels of Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans, reduced IL-1β at 3 months,
  and reduced IL-6 at 3 and 12 months. Both groups showed substantial improvements
  following therapy, and no major adverse effects were reported. Systemic resveratrol
  may improve clinical, microbiological, and inflammatory outcomes when used as an
  adjunct to non-surgical periodontal therapy in smokers with Stage III or IV, Grade
  C periodontitis.'

summary: A 12-month double-blind RCT in 38 smokers with Stage III/IV Grade C periodontitis
  found that 500 mg/day resveratrol added to full-mouth ultrasonic debridement produced
  statistically significant gains in clinical attachment level and reductions in probing
  depth versus placebo. The effect was real but modest, and the small sample size
  limits generalisability. Still, this is one of the cleanest adjunct-therapy trials
  in the smoker-periodontitis space.
summaryDeep: 'Smokers with severe periodontitis are notoriously poor responders to
  non-surgical therapy, and adjunctive pharmacology has struggled to show consistent
  benefit in this cohort. This RCT randomised 38 heavy smokers (≥10 cigarettes/day
  for ≥5 years) with Stage III or IV, Grade C disease to full-mouth ultrasonic debridement
  plus either 500 mg/day systemic resveratrol or placebo for 180 days, with follow-up
  to 12 months. The resveratrol group showed significantly greater CAL gain and PD
  reduction at all follow-up timepoints, alongside favourable shifts in subgingival
  microbiological and immunoinflammatory markers. The biological rationale is solid
  — resveratrol''s anti-inflammatory and antioxidant pathways are well-characterised
  — but n=19 per arm means confidence intervals are wide. No serious adverse events
  were reported. For now, this is hypothesis-confirming rather than practice-changing:
  a larger multicentre RCT is the logical next step before resveratrol earns a place
  in the smoker-periodontitis protocol.'
