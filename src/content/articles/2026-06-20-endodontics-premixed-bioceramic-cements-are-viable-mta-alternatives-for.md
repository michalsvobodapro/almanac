excerpt: ABSTRACT This study investigated the biocompatibility and osteogenic potential
  of premixed bioceramic cements (PBCs)—CeraPutty, EndoCEM MTA Premixed, Well‐Root
  PT, and One‐Fil PT Injectable—as alternatives to mineral trioxide aggregate for
  retrograde filling in endodontic microsurgery. Setting time was measured in saline
  and fetal bovine serum (FBS). Cytocompatibility was assessed using MTT assays on
  human periodontal ligament fibroblasts with serial extract dilutions at multiple
  time points. Osteogenic differentiation was evaluated by alkaline phosphatase (ALP)
  activity and Alizarin Red S staining for mineralized nodule formation. One‐way ANOVA
  was used for biological data and two‐way ANOVA for setting time analysis ( p &lt;
  0.05). All PBCs demonstrated over 90% cell viability. CeraPutty showed significantly
  higher ALP activity, indicating enhanced early osteogenic differentiation. All materials
  promoted mineral deposition. Setting time varied by material and medium, generally
  increasing in FBS. Overall, PBCs exhibited favourable biological and setting properties,
  supporting their clinical use as reliable MTA alternatives.

summaryDeep: Mineral trioxide aggregate remains the gold standard for retrograde filling
  in endodontic microsurgery, but its long setting time and handling difficulties
  have driven interest in premixed bioceramic alternatives. This study systematically
  compared four commercially available PBCs on biocompatibility (MTT assay, human
  periodontal ligament fibroblasts), osteogenic potential (ALP activity, Alizarin
  Red S mineralisation), and setting time in saline versus fetal bovine serum. All
  four materials cleared the 90% cell viability threshold at all dilutions and time
  points. CeraPutty showed significantly higher ALP activity than the others, indicating
  enhanced early osteogenic differentiation — clinically relevant because faster bone
  regeneration reduces the risk of fibrous healing at the resection site. Setting
  times generally increased in FBS (simulating tissue fluid), which has implications
  for surgical technique. The study is limited to in vitro conditions, but the breadth
  of comparators and the use of validated osteogenic endpoints make it a useful reference
  for clinicians choosing among PBCs for apical surgery.
