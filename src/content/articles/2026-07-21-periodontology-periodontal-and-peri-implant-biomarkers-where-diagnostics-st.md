summaryDeep: 'The review traces biomarker discovery from the first gingival crevicular
  fluid (GCF) studies through contemporary multi-omic approaches, contextualising
  why translation to chairside diagnostics has been slow despite decades of research.
  Key barriers include biological variability, lack of standardised sampling protocols,
  and the absence of validated cut-off values for clinical decision-making. Emerging
  platforms — lateral flow assays, microfluidic devices, and AI-assisted salivary
  proteomics — are identified as the most promising near-term paths to clinical utility.
  Peri-implantitis biomarkers lag behind periodontal ones, partly because the disease
  is more heterogeneous and partly because the implant surface itself confounds sampling.
  As a narrative review, it carries no new primary data, but it is a useful orientation
  for students entering the field and for clinicians wondering whether to invest in
  commercial biomarker tests currently on the market (the implicit answer: not yet,
  for most).'
