  shape-memory aligners (TC-85™) in terms of thermomechanical behaviour, chemical
  extractables, and shape-recovery performance. In this in vitro comparative study,
  standardized specimens (n=22/group) were evaluated using differential scanning calorimetry
  (Tg, ΔCp), gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (ISO 4049 extractables), and shape-recovery
  testing at multiple temperatures; time-dependent stress relaxation under constant
  strain (2% tensile strain, 180min, 37°C) was additionally evaluated as a viscoelastic
  measure of orthodontic force decay. Measurements were recorded at baseline and after
  7 days of artificial saliva ageing. Statistical analysis was performed using parametric
  and non-parametric tests (P≤0.05). Both materials showed increased extractables

summary: An in-vitro head-to-head of thermoformed Zendura and directly 3D-printed
  TC-85 aligners shows TC-85 retains 72.8% of initial force after 180 minutes versus
  58.7% for Zendura — a clinically meaningful difference in sustained tooth movement.
  However, TC-85 showed lower cumulative extractables after ageing, while Zendura
  leached significantly more overall. Shape recovery also favoured TC-85 post-ageing.
summaryDeep: 'Direct 3D printing of clear aligners is the manufacturing frontier,
  and this study is one of the first to put thermoformed polyurethane (Zendura) and
  a shape-memory 3D-printed material (TC-85) through a rigorous comparative battery.
  Thermomechanical testing via differential scanning calorimetry, ISO 4049 extractables
  by GC-MS, shape-recovery testing, and viscoelastic stress-relaxation under constant
  2% tensile strain at 37°C were all performed at baseline and after 7-day artificial
  saliva ageing. TC-85''s lower stress relaxation (27.2% vs 41.3%) means it delivers
  more sustained orthodontic force per wear cycle — theoretically reducing the number
  of aligner changes needed or improving compliance sensitivity. The extractables
  finding is nuanced: Zendura showed higher cumulative extractables overall, but both
  materials increased after ageing, raising biocompatibility questions that in-vitro
  chemistry cannot fully resolve. The slight Tg decrease in TC-85 post-ageing warrants
  monitoring in clinical use. For a student: 3D-printed aligners are not yet universally
  superior, but the force-delivery data are compelling.'
