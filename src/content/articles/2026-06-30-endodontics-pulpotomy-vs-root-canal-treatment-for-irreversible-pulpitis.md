summaryDeep: 'The PROVE study aggregates individual patient data from multiple RCTs
  to produce the most statistically powered comparison yet of full pulpotomy against
  root canal treatment (RCT) for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIRP). Short-term
  success rates — pain resolution, periapical healing, tooth survival — were non-inferior
  for pulpotomy, reinforcing what smaller trials have individually suggested. Crucially,
  this is a pooled RCT analysis, not a meta-analysis of heterogeneous observational
  data, so the signal is cleaner than most. For a Prague dental student, this matters
  because the reflex to go straight to full RCT in SIRP is being systematically challenged:
  pulpotomy preserves more tooth structure, is faster, and may reduce procedural complexity.
  The caveat is that ''short-term'' is doing real work here — long-term data (beyond
  2–3 years) remain sparse, and case selection criteria across the pooled trials varied.
  Still, this is the kind of high-grade evidence that shifts guidelines, and the ESE
  is watching this thread closely.'
