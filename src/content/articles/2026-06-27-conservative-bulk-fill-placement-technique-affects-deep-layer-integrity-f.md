summaryDeep: 'The study evaluated flexural strength, modulus, and energy dissipation
  parameters at the bottom layer of restorations after simulated aging. The clinical
  relevance is direct: in deep posterior cavities, the bottom layer is furthest from
  the curing light and most prone to inadequate polymerization. Fiber-reinforced bulk-fills
  showed less technique sensitivity — their deep-layer properties were more consistent
  between bulk and incremental placement. Particulate-reinforced materials degraded
  more at the bottom layer when placed in bulk, particularly after water storage,
  which simulates the hydrolytic aging that occurs in vivo over years. The in vitro
  design means masticatory loading, cavity geometry, and adhesive interface effects
  are not captured. But for a student choosing between bulk-fill options for a deep
  Class II, the message is that material choice matters as much as technique — and
  that not all bulk-fills are created equal at depth.'
