excerpt: The aim of this systematic review and meta-analysis was to evaluate the longitudinal
  temporal pattern of periodontal probing depth (PPD) reduction in intrabony periodontal
  defects following non-surgical periodontal therapy (NSPT), both as a monotherapy
  and with adjunctive locally delivered pharmacological agents. A comprehensive electronic
  search was performed in PubMed/MEDLINE and Embase databases to identify randomized
  controlled trials (RCTs). Eligible studies included adult patients with vertical
  defects treated with NSPT and reporting defect-level PPD values at predefined follow-up
  intervals (3, 6, 9, or 12 months). Pairwise temporal comparisons were conducted
  within treatment arms (6 vs. 3, 9 vs. 6, and 12 vs. 6 months) using standardized
  mean differences (SMD) in a random-effects model. Risk of bias was assessed with
  the Cochrane RoB 2 tool and certainty of evidence with GRADE. Eight RCTs met the
  inclusion criteria. Significant PPD reduction occurred between 3 and 6 months (SMD
  = -1.07; 95% CI -1.70 to -0.45, p < 0.001). Further significant improvements were
  detected between 6 and 9 months (SMD = -1.35; 95% CI -2.08 to -0.63) and sustained
  at 12 months compared to 6 months (SMD = -1.70; 95% CI -2.73 to -0.66). High heterogeneity
  was observed (I2 = 83-89%). Temporal PPD reduction across consecutive follow-up
  intervals was considered the primary outcome. Subgroup analyses suggested similar

summary: 'A systematic review and meta-analysis of 8 RCTs shows that probing depth
  reduction in intrabony defects after non-surgical periodontal therapy does not plateau
  at 6 months — statistically significant gains continue through 9 and even 12 months.
  The implication is direct: re-evaluating patients at 6 weeks or 3 months and escalating
  to surgery may be premature for vertical defects. Clinical protocols built around
  a single 6-month checkpoint need revisiting.'
summaryDeep: 'This GRADE-assessed meta-analysis pooled 8 RCTs to map the temporal
  trajectory of periodontal probing depth (PPD) reduction in intrabony defects following
  non-surgical periodontal therapy (NSPT), with or without adjunctive locally delivered
  pharmacological agents. The primary finding is that PPD reduction is not a one-time
  event: significant improvements occurred between 3 and 6 months (SMD −1.07), continued
  between 6 and 9 months (SMD −1.35), and were sustained at 12 months versus 6 months
  (SMD −1.70). High heterogeneity (I² = 83–89%) tempers certainty, but the directional
  signal is consistent across subgroups for both NSPT alone and adjunctive therapy
  arms. For the Prague student: the reflex to re-evaluate at 6 months and immediately
  refer for surgical correction of residual pockets in vertical defects may be cutting
  the healing curve short. The data support extending the observation window to at
  least 9–12 months before concluding that NSPT has failed. This has direct implications
  for treatment sequencing and patient communication.'
