excerpt: Introduction Despite favourable outcomes of non-surgical endodontic retreatment
  (NS-ReTx), treatment decisions for previously treated posterior teeth with apical
  periodontitis remain inconsistent. This ambispective cohort study evaluated NS-ReTx
  outcomes in posterior teeth. Methodology Permanent posterior teeth undergoing NS-ReTx
  in a residency program (2010-2023) with ≥24-month follow-up or an untoward event
  were included; otherwise, recall was attempted. Teeth were classified as healed,
  healing, or diseased. Periapical healing was defined using strict and loose criteria,
  and event-free survival as time to reintervention or last event-free follow-up.
  Analyses used logistic regression and Kaplan-Meier methods (α = 0.05). Results Periapical
  healing analyses included 423 teeth (367 patients; median follow-up 3.7 years),
  comprising retrospectively (n=276; median follow-up 3.4 years) and prospectively
  (n=147; median follow-up 4.3 years) ascertained subgroups. Overall strict/loose
  periapical healing rates were 82.7%/89.8%, compared with 80.8%/87.0% and 86.4%/95.2%

  shows strict periapical healing after non-surgical retreatment at 82.7%, rising

summaryDeep: 'This is one of the larger and longer retreatment cohorts in the recent
  literature, drawing on a residency programme dataset from 2010–2023 with prospective
  recall attempted for all eligible cases. Strict healing (complete radiographic resolution)
  reached 82.7% overall; the prospective subgroup — with better follow-up fidelity
  — hit 86.4% strict and 95.2% loose. Kaplan-Meier event-free survival was 96.5% at
  two years, declining to 92.7% at six, which compares favourably with published implant
  survival in equivalent sites. Two factors independently predicted healing: a smaller
  or absent preoperative periapical radiolucency, and an adequate-quality final restoration.
  That second finding is the clinical headline — retreatment is a team effort between
  the endodontist and whoever places the crown. For students, this data supports a
  confident conversation with patients: non-surgical retreatment of posterior teeth
  is a high-probability intervention, not a last resort before extraction.'
