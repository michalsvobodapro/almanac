summaryDeep: Peri-implant bone defect assessment currently relies on CBCT or probing,
  both with limitations — radiation exposure and soft-tissue interference, respectively.
  This in vitro study placed 72 implants in fresh porcine rib bone with standardized
  artificial defects (buccal dehiscence, circumferential, combined, and controls)
  and compared ultrasonography against CBCT and direct measurement as the gold standard.
  USG achieved >95% accuracy for defect type classification across most categories,
  with high ICC for depth (0.934) but poor agreement for height measurement. CBCT
  was perfect (100%) for defect detection. Sensitivity for circumferential defects
  was lower with USG (83.3%), and positive predictive value for no-defect sites was
  87% — meaning false positives are a real concern. The in vitro model is a significant
  limitation; soft tissue, implant surface geometry, and prosthetic components will
  all affect real-world performance. Still, for monitoring known peri-implant defects
  longitudinally without repeated CBCT exposure, USG is a credible adjunct that deserves
  clinical validation.
