summaryDeep: 'The piece engages seriously with the equity argument for AI in dentistry:
  that LLMs could function as a first-contact resource for patients in dental deserts,
  provide multilingual patient education, and reduce the information asymmetry between
  well-resourced and under-resourced populations. Against this, the commentary weighs
  the counterarguments — training data skewed toward English-language, high-income-country
  clinical contexts; the digital divide that means the populations most in need of
  access are least likely to have reliable smartphone or broadband access; and the
  risk that AI-generated advice substitutes for rather than supplements professional
  care. For a student in Prague, this connects directly to the EU digital health agenda
  and the question of how AI tools will be regulated and reimbursed across member
  states. The piece does not resolve the tension but frames it honestly, which is
  more useful than either techno-optimism or reflexive scepticism.'
