summaryDeep: 'Secondary caries detection is notoriously difficult, and this study
  addresses a specific clinical scenario: restorations already condemned by clinical
  and radiographic criteria. Among 120 repair- or replacement-indicated posterior
  composites in 83 adults, secondary caries was confirmed histologically in 80 (66.7%).
  QrayPen C at QS >0 delivered sensitivity 85.0%, specificity 90.0%, LR+ 8.50, and
  LR− 0.17 — strong discriminatory performance. Clustered bootstrap analysis accounting
  for multiple restorations per patient produced similar but wider confidence intervals,
  which is methodologically honest. Higher QS thresholds (>1, >2) traded sensitivity
  for specificity, useful when the clinician wants to minimize unnecessary removal.
  The critical caveat: this is an enriched cohort where caries prevalence was 67%,
  far above what you''d encounter in routine screening. The tool''s value here is
  localization — identifying which margin or surface to target — not primary diagnosis.
  For a student learning cavity preparation decisions, QrayPen adds a useful adjunct
  layer but cannot replace Nyvad criteria or bitewing radiographs.'
