summaryDeep: 'Depth of cure (DOC) is a critical parameter for composite placement
  protocols, particularly for bulk-fill materials where manufacturers claim 4–5 mm
  cure depths. The ISO 4049 scraping method — which uses organic solvents to remove
  uncured material — is the standard test, but this study demonstrates that the solvent
  itself interacts with the composite matrix and alters the hardness readings used
  to define the cure boundary. Both conventional (Filtek Universal, Beautifil II)
  and bulk-fill (Filtek One Bulk Fill, Beautifil-Bulk) composites were affected, though
  the magnitude varied by material. This is a methodological critique with real consequences:
  DOC values in the literature and on product data sheets may be systematically biased
  depending on the solvent used. For clinicians, the practical message is to treat
  manufacturer DOC claims with scepticism and to default to conservative layer thicknesses
  — especially for conventional composites in deep cavities.'
