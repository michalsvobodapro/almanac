summary: A layer-by-layer coating system embedding tetracycline complexed with anionic
  β-cyclodextrin in a pH-responsive polymer demonstrated antimicrobial activity and
  acceptable biocompatibility in vitro. The pH-triggered release mechanism means drug
  delivery ramps up precisely when peri-implant inflammation acidifies the local environment
  — an elegant match of biology and chemistry.
summaryDeep: Peri-implantitis management is hampered by the difficulty of delivering
  antimicrobials to a biofilm-protected implant surface at therapeutic concentrations
  without systemic side effects. This in-vitro study tested a layer-by-layer (LbL)
  coating that loads tetracycline as a β-cyclodextrin complex within a poly(methacrylic
  acid) matrix. The pH-responsive polymer swells and releases drug as local pH drops
  — exactly what happens during bacterial acid production in early peri-implant infection.
  Antimicrobial efficacy and cell biocompatibility were both confirmed at this bench
  stage. The evidence grade is low (in vitro only), and the path from coating stability
  in a test tube to survival under masticatory load and osseointegration conditions
  is long. Still, the concept of a 'smart' implant surface that self-medicates in
  response to the inflammatory microenvironment is clinically compelling and represents
  a meaningful advance over passive antibiotic-eluting coatings. Animal and then clinical
  studies are the necessary next steps.
