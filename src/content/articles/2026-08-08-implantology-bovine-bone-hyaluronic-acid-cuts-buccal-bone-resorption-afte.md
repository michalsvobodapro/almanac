summary: A small RCT (n=16 immediate implants) finds that grafting fresh extraction
  sockets with bovine bone mineral plus hyaluronic acid dramatically reduces buccal
  bone width loss at 6 months (3.9% vs. 16.0% in controls) and yields higher ISQ values
  and bone density. The effect size is striking, but the sample is too small to change
  protocol alone.
summaryDeep: Sixteen immediate implants were placed in single-rooted maxillary anterior
  or premolar extraction sockets and randomized to grafting with bovine bone + hyaluronic
  acid (n=8) or no graft (n=8). ISQ was measured at placement and 4 months; CBCT assessed
  bone density and buccal bone width at placement and 6 months. The study group showed
  significantly higher ISQ at 4 months (72.25 vs. 64.88, p<0.001), higher bone density
  at 6 months (1163 vs. 1034 HU, p=0.016), and dramatically less buccal bone width
  reduction (3.9% vs. 16.0%). Both groups lost buccal bone, but the control group
  lost four times more. Hyaluronic acid's role in osteogenesis and mineralization
  is biologically plausible, and the results align with the broader literature on
  socket grafting. The critical limitation is the tiny sample (n=8 per group), which
  inflates effect sizes and limits generalizability. This is hypothesis-generating,
  not practice-changing — but the direction of effect is consistent and worth watching
  in larger trials.
