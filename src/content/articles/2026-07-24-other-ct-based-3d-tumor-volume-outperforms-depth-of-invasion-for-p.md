  (TV) improves prediction of cervical spread in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC)
  beyond conventional size metrics. Materials and methods We retrospectively analyzed
  132 consecutive primary OSCC patients (2014-2019). Contrast-enhanced CT datasets
  were semi-automatically segmented to obtain 3D TV (cm 3 ), verified by a second
  observer. Multivariable logistic regression related log-TV to pathologic lymph-node
  metastasis (LNM), extranodal extension (ENE), and skip metastasis. Performance of

summaryDeep: 'Depth of invasion is currently the dominant metric for neck dissection
  decisions in oral squamous cell carcinoma staging, but this retrospective cohort
  of 132 consecutive OSCC patients challenges that primacy. Semi-automatic CT segmentation
  to derive 3D tumor volume (median 12.6 cm³) produced an independent predictor of
  pathologic lymph node metastasis with AUC 0.815 in multivariable logistic regression
  — versus AUC 0.541 for DOI. The non-linear relationship with skip metastasis is
  particularly striking: both very small (<threshold) and large (>16 cm³) tumors showed
  elevated skip-spread probability, which has direct implications for neck dissection
  planning. Radiologic and pathologic volumes showed near-zero concordance (R²=0.0002),
  confirming that CT volumetry captures something distinct from pathology measurements.
  For dental students rotating through oral surgery or oncology, this study is a concrete
  example of how CBCT/CT data can be leveraged beyond implant planning into oncological
  staging.'
