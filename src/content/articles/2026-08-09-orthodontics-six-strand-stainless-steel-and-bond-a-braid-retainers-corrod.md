summaryDeep: 'Lingual retainers sit in a cariogenic oral environment for years, yet
  material selection has been driven largely by mechanical and handling properties
  rather than corrosion behavior under microbiological challenge. This study fills
  that gap with a controlled electrochemical protocol: 60 specimens across six materials
  (3-strand SS, 6-strand SS, titanium Grade 1, titanium Grade 5, Bond-A-Braid, gold)
  were exposed to S. mutans–containing medium or sterile artificial saliva for 28
  days, then tested by potentiodynamic polarization. Six-strand stainless steel showed
  significantly increased corrosion current density and rate, and was the only material
  with a significant reduction in polarization resistance. Bond-A-Braid also deteriorated.
  Gold performed best, with lower corrosion and higher resistance after exposure.
  Titanium grades showed intermediate behavior. The study is in-vitro and uses a single
  bacterial species, so real-mouth complexity is not captured. For a student bonding
  retainers, the practical signal is that 6-strand SS — widely used — may be the most
  vulnerable material in high-caries-risk patients.'
