summary: A Phase III RCT published in JAMA Pediatrics (n=830 children under six) found
  that biannual silver diamine fluoride application arrested active caries in more
  than 50% of affected primary teeth. This is the largest and most rigorous trial
  of SDF in early childhood caries to date. It substantially strengthens the case
  for SDF as a first-line, non-invasive option in young, uncooperative, or high-risk
  patients.
summaryDeep: The Phase III study enrolled 830 children under six with severe early
  childhood caries and applied silver diamine fluoride every six months, tracking
  arrest rates against controls. Arrest was achieved in over half of treated lesions
  — a clinically meaningful result at scale. SDF has been used empirically for years,
  but evidence at this level of design and sample size was missing; this trial fills
  that gap decisively. For paediatric and general practice, the protocol is simple,
  cheap, and requires no anaesthesia, making it particularly valuable in settings
  where access to operative care is limited or where behaviour management is a barrier.
  The dark staining of arrested lesions remains a cosmetic trade-off that must be
  discussed with caregivers, but the functional benefit is now hard to argue against.
  Expect this to accelerate SDF adoption in European paediatric guidelines, where
  it has lagged behind North American and Australian recommendations.
