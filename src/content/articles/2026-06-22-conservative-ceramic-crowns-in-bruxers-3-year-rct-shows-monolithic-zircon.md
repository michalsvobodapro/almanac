summary: A 3-year prospective RCT found no significant difference in complication
  or failure rates between monolithic lithium disilicate and zirconia molar crowns
  in sleep bruxism patients. Both materials performed acceptably, challenging the
  assumption that bruxism is a hard contraindication to all-ceramic restorations.
  Occlusal splint use was controlled for, strengthening the clinical relevance.
summaryDeep: The question of which ceramic to reach for in a bruxer has long been
  answered by habit rather than evidence. This RCT changes that. Over three years,
  monolithic lithium disilicate and monolithic zirconia molar crowns in confirmed
  sleep bruxism patients showed comparable survival and complication profiles — chipping,
  fracture, and loss of retention were tracked systematically. The moderate GRADE
  confidence reflects the 3-year horizon (longer follow-up would be ideal) and the
  inherent difficulty of blinding in materials trials, but the prospective randomized
  design is the strongest available for this question. For restorative practice, the
  takeaway is that material choice in bruxers can be guided by esthetic and preparation-depth
  considerations rather than a blanket preference for zirconia. Patients should still
  be counselled on splint therapy, and the study does not cover implant-supported
  crowns or anterior teeth. A useful companion to the growing body of evidence rehabilitating
  lithium disilicate's reputation in high-load situations.
