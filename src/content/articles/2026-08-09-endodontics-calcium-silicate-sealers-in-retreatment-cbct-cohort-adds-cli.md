summaryDeep: 'Calcium silicate sealers have become the dominant obturation material
  in specialist endodontic practices, yet nearly all outcome data come from primary
  treatment. Retreatment introduces additional variables: residual sealer, altered
  dentinal tubule permeability, and the challenge of removing a hydraulic, bioactive
  material. This prospective cohort uses CBCT — more sensitive than periapical radiography
  for detecting periapical healing — to assess outcomes following nonsurgical retreatment
  of CSS-obturated teeth. While the full dataset is not yet published in the excerpt,
  the study design is notably stronger than the retrospective case series that dominate
  this literature. For a student in a specialist endo clinic, the key question this
  study begins to answer is whether the bioceramic revolution in primary treatment
  creates downstream complexity in retreatment — a question that matters every time
  a previously treated tooth presents with persistent apical pathology.'
