excerpt: To synthesize evidence from systematic reviews and meta-analyses on low-level
  laser therapy/Photobiomodulation (LLLT/PBM) and vitamin D as non-invasive adjuncts
  for accelerating orthodontic tooth movement (OTM) and mitigating associated side
  effects. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library databases
  were searched from inception to November 18, 2025. SRMAs primarily comprising human
  RCTs evaluating LLLT/PBM or vitamin D in orthodontics were included; in some mixed-design
  reviews, clearly separable human orthodontic data from a small number of CCTs were
  retained narratively. (one pre-registered exception with 4 RCTs for vitamin D to
  ensure completeness). Randomized controlled trials were prioritized; some retained
  reviews also included a small number of controlled clinical trials when relevant
  human orthodontic data were clearly separable. Quality was assessed using AMSTAR-2,
  ROBIS, and GRADE. Narrative synthesis was performed because of high heterogeneity.
  Twenty systematic reviews and meta-analyses covering a large number of human orthodontic
  trials were included. Low-certainty evidence suggests that LLLT/PBM may be associated
  with accelerated OTM, slight reductions in orthodontically induced inflammatory
  root resorption (OIIRR), and short-term pain relief. Vitamin D showed inconsistent
  findings across outcomes with no reliable overall clinical benefit. Current evidence
  is insufficient to recommend the routine clinical use of LLLT/PBM or vitamin D

summary: An umbrella review of 20 systematic reviews and meta-analyses on photobiomodulation
  (LLLT/PBM) and vitamin D in orthodontics concludes that evidence quality is too
  low to recommend either as routine adjuncts. LLLT may marginally accelerate tooth
  movement and reduce pain short-term, but effect sizes are small and certainty is
  low. Vitamin D shows no reliable benefit across outcomes.
summaryDeep: 'Photobiomodulation devices are actively marketed to orthodontic practices
  as tooth-movement accelerators, making this umbrella review — the highest level
  of evidence synthesis — particularly valuable. Covering 20 SRMAs assessed with AMSTAR-2,
  ROBIS, and GRADE, the authors find that LLLT/PBM is associated with slightly faster
  orthodontic tooth movement and modest short-term pain reduction, but the underlying
  trials are heterogeneous, often small, and rated low certainty by GRADE. Root resorption
  data are similarly inconclusive. Vitamin D fares worse: findings are inconsistent
  across all outcomes with no reliable clinical benefit identified. The authors explicitly
  state current evidence is insufficient to recommend routine clinical use of either
  intervention. For a student evaluating whether to recommend or use these adjuncts:
  the biological plausibility is real, but the clinical evidence hasn''t caught up.
  Save the patient the cost until better-powered RCTs emerge.'
