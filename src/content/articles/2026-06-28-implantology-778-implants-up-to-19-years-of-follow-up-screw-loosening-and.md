excerpt: The aim of this retrospective study was to evaluate the long-term clinical
  outcomes of implant-supported prostheses and to analyze the type, frequency, and
  distribution of prosthetic complications in relation to prosthesis-related factors.
  Clinical records of patients who received implant-supported prosthetic treatment
  between 2017 and 2024 at the Department of Prosthodontics, Faculty of Dentistry,
  Gazi University, were retrospectively reviewed. A total of 269 patients with 778
  implants were included. The follow-up period ranged from 14 to 228 months after
  functional loading, with a mean follow-up duration of 54.6 months. Fixed, hybrid,
  and implant-supported overdentures were evaluated. Prostheses were classified according
  to prosthesis type, restorative material, retention type, and impression level.
  Prosthetic complications were recorded. Statistical analyses included chi-square
  tests to evaluate complication distributions and Cox proportional hazards regression
  analysis to identify factors associated with time to first prosthetic complication.
  Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. Implant-supported prostheses showed
  favorable long-term clinical outcomes; however, biological, mechanical, and technical
  complications were observed. The most common complications were screw loosening,
  prosthetic fractures and chipping of the veneering material. Complication rates

summaryDeep: 'This Gazi University retrospective study is one of the larger single-center
  implant prosthetics datasets in recent literature, covering fixed, hybrid, and overdenture
  prostheses across a follow-up window of 14 to 228 months. Cox proportional hazards
  regression identified restorative material, prosthesis type, retention type, and
  impression level as significant predictors of time to first prosthetic complication.
  Screw loosening was the most frequent mechanical event, followed by veneering chipping
  — a pattern consistent with the broader literature but here quantified across a
  clinically heterogeneous prosthetic mix. Biological complications were also recorded
  but the mechanical burden dominated. For students entering prosthodontics or implant
  planning rotations: the message is that implant survival and prosthetic success
  are different endpoints, and the latter requires material and design choices that
  are evidence-informed rather than preference-driven. The retrospective design limits
  causal inference, but the sample size and follow-up duration give the complication
  rates genuine weight.'
