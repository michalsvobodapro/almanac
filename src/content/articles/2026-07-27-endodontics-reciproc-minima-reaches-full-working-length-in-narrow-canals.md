summaryDeep: 'This comparative study tested three reciprocating instruments — Reciproc
  Minima #20, Minima #25, and the established Reciproc Blue R25 — across 330 mesial
  canals of extracted mandibular molars, all attempted without prior scouting or glide
  path creation. Reciproc Minima instruments reached full working length at significantly
  higher rates than R25, with the size-20 variant performing particularly well in
  the narrowest canals. The asymmetric cross-section of the Minima series appears
  to reduce taper lock and improve negotiation in curved, constricted anatomy. If
  confirmed in clinical studies, this could streamline single-file protocols by removing
  the glide path step in challenging canals — a meaningful time and instrument-cost
  saving. The study''s in-vitro design and extracted-tooth model limit direct clinical
  extrapolation, but the 330-canal sample gives the comparison reasonable statistical
  power.'
