summary: Using electronic health records and target trial emulation methodology, long-term
  NSAID use in osteoarthritis patients was associated with a reduced risk of developing
  periodontitis. The COX-inhibition pathway that blunts joint inflammation appears
  to confer periodontal protection too — a finding with real implications for how
  we interpret perio status in medicated patients.
summaryDeep: 'NSAIDs suppress prostaglandin-mediated inflammation via COX inhibition,
  and prostaglandins are key mediators of alveolar bone resorption in periodontitis.
  This study emulated randomised target trials using US electronic health records
  of osteoarthritis patients, comparing those on long-term NSAIDs with those not taking
  them. NSAID users showed a meaningfully lower incidence of periodontitis over the
  follow-up period. Target trial emulation is a rigorous causal inference method that
  reduces confounding compared to standard observational analysis, lending the finding
  more credibility than a simple association study. The clinical implication cuts
  both ways: patients on chronic NSAIDs may have artificially suppressed periodontal
  inflammation that unmasks rapidly on discontinuation, and their perio risk should
  be reassessed when medication changes. Conversely, the data hint at a potential
  adjunctive anti-inflammatory role in periodontal management — though prescribing
  NSAIDs for perio is far from evidence-based practice yet.'
