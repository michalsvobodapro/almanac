summary: A retrospective 5-year cohort of 83 immediately placed implants at a Vietnamese
  military hospital reports an 88.0% success rate and 94.6% survival rate by Albrektsson
  criteria. Marginal bone loss increased over time but remained within accepted physiological
  limits, and peri-implant soft tissue indices stabilized after early improvement.
summaryDeep: Of 112 implants placed immediately post-extraction between 2015 and 2021,
  83 in 64 patients reached the 5-year follow-up threshold and form the analytical
  cohort. Peri-implant parameters — bleeding on probing, plaque index, probing depth
  — improved in the early phase and remained stable. Marginal bone loss showed a gradual
  increase consistent with physiological remodeling rather than pathological resorption.
  The 88% success and 94.6% survival figures align with the broader immediate-placement
  literature, reinforcing that the technique is safe and effective when patient selection
  and surgical protocol are controlled. Limitations include the retrospective design,
  single-center setting, and the absence of a delayed-placement control arm. For a
  student, this adds to the now-substantial cohort evidence supporting immediate implant
  placement as a routine option — not an experimental one — in appropriate sockets.
