summary: This systematic review audits every clinically relevant agent capable of
  releasing bioactive molecules from dentine matrix — the signalling reservoir that
  drives stem-cell recruitment in regenerative endodontic procedures. EDTA remains
  the reference standard, but several alternative chelators and acids show comparable
  or superior release profiles. The evidence base is still largely in vitro, which
  limits immediate protocol changes.
summaryDeep: 'Regenerative endodontic procedures depend on dentine-bound growth factors
  — TGF-β, BMPs, IGF — to recruit, proliferate and differentiate stem cells into pulp-like
  tissue. The choice of conditioning agent determines how much of that bioactive payload
  is actually released. This systematic review catalogues EDTA alongside phosphoric
  acid, citric acid, MTAD, and emerging bioceramic-based conditioners, grading each
  on release efficiency, cytotoxicity, and clinical evidence. EDTA at 17% for one
  minute remains the best-supported protocol, but the review identifies several agents
  with superior growth-factor release in vitro that lack clinical validation. The
  conflict between high in-vitro release and acceptable cytotoxicity is the central
  tension: some aggressive demineralisers liberate more TGF-β but damage the surviving
  cell population. For students and practitioners, the takeaway is that the conditioning
  step in REP is not interchangeable — agent choice, concentration, and contact time
  all matter and should follow the best-available protocol until RCT data matures.'
