summaryDeep: 'The Hybrid Funnel Technique pairs a cortical funnel preparation with
  medullary bone condensation rather than subtractive drilling, aiming to preserve
  bone density at the implant neck. In this prospective non-randomized controlled
  trial, 43 patients received 87 bioactive-surface implants split between HFT (n=45)
  and conventional osteotomy (n=42). At one year, MBL was already significantly lower
  in the HFT group (0.33 vs 0.78 mm); by three years the gap widened to 0.46 vs 1.34
  mm (p<0.001). Linear mixed-effects modelling identified site preparation technique
  as the sole independent variable associated with MBL — implant diameter, length,
  bone density, and patient factors did not reach significance. Bleeding on probing
  and plaque index were comparable between groups, ruling out hygiene confounding.
  The non-randomized design is the main caveat: surgeon selection bias cannot be excluded.
  Still, a near-threefold difference in bone loss over three years with bioactive
  implants is hard to dismiss and warrants an RCT.'
