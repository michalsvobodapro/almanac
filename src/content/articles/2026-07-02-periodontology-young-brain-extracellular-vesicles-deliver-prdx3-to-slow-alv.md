summary: Extracellular vesicles from young brain tissue carry the antioxidant protein
  PRDX3 and reduce alveolar bone resorption in a periodontitis animal model. The finding
  links neurobiological aging to periodontal bone homeostasis — a conceptually novel
  axis. Clinical translation is distant, but the mechanism is worth tracking.
summaryDeep: Aging is a recognized risk factor for periodontitis severity, but the
  biological bridges between systemic aging and local periodontal bone loss are poorly
  understood. This experimental study isolates extracellular vesicles from young brain
  tissue and demonstrates that they carry peroxiredoxin-3 (PRDX3), a mitochondrial
  antioxidant, which when delivered to a periodontitis model attenuates alveolar bone
  resorption. The proposed mechanism involves reduction of oxidative stress in the
  periodontal microenvironment, which modulates osteoclast activity. The study is
  animal/experimental — no human data — and the delivery route (systemic injection
  of brain-derived EVs) is not clinically feasible in its current form. The conceptual
  contribution is the identification of an aging-related, brain-derived signal that
  influences periodontal bone homeostasis, adding to a growing literature on the systemic
  regulation of local oral tissues. For students, this is a reminder that periodontitis
  biology extends well beyond the gingival sulcus.
