summaryDeep: 'Previous GWAS efforts in malocclusion were underpowered and largely
  confined to European ancestry. This study pooled 3,405 participants from the US,
  Philippines, Colombia, and Nigeria, applying standardized Angle classification with
  quantitative overjet and overbite thresholds. Meta-analysis across cohorts identified
  two genome-wide significant hits: rs372564249 at 11q22.3 (Class II, OR 3.12) and
  rs13060317 at 3p24.3 (multiplanar malocclusion, OR 2.4), plus a near-significant
  signal at 12q15. The loci implicate genes involved in craniofacial morphogenesis,
  consistent with known biology. Clinically, this does not yet change treatment —
  orthodontic correction remains phenotype-driven — but it opens the door to genetic
  risk stratification for early interception and may eventually explain why some Class
  II patients relapse after treatment. For a Prague dental student, the broader lesson
  is that malocclusion heritability is real and quantifiable, which matters for patient
  counseling and future precision-orthodontics research.'
