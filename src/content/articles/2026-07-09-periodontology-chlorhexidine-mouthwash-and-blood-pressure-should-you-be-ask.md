summary: 'A commentary on a new systematic review and meta-analysis evaluates whether
  chlorhexidine mouthwash — the periodontal gold-standard adjunct — meaningfully affects
  blood pressure in dental patients. The nitrate-nitrite-nitric oxide pathway is the
  proposed mechanism: CHX suppresses oral bacteria that reduce salivary nitrate to
  nitrite, potentially raising blood pressure. The clinical relevance for hypertensive
  patients is under active debate.'
summaryDeep: 'Chlorhexidine is the most widely prescribed adjunct in periodontal therapy,
  but its effect on the oral microbiome extends beyond periodontal pathogens. The
  nitrate-nitrite-nitric oxide (NO) pathway — in which oral bacteria convert dietary
  nitrate to nitrite, which is then reduced to vasodilatory NO — is disrupted by broad-spectrum
  antiseptics including CHX. The underlying systematic review and meta-analysis (Toonen
  et al., Int J Dent Hyg 2026) is the evidence base this commentary interrogates.
  The commentary''s value is in translating the meta-analytic findings into a clinical
  question: should dentists be factoring CHX use into the management of hypertensive
  patients, particularly those on antihypertensive medication? The effect sizes reported
  in the meta-analysis appear modest, and the commentary appropriately contextualizes
  the clinical significance. For a dental student, this is a useful reminder that
  even familiar agents have systemic pharmacological footprints worth understanding.'
