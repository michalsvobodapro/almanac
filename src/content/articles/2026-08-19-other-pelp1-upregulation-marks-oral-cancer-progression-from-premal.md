excerpt: Purpose Chronic inflammation is an important contributor to the development
  of oral cancer, particularly in Oral Potentially Malignant Disorders (OPMDs) progressing
  to Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma (OSCC). PELP1 is an inflammation-responsive nuclear
  coregulator identified in multiple cancers; however, its role in oral cancer remains
  unclear. This study evaluates the expression of PELP1 and determines its clinical
  significance in inflammation-associated oral tumorigenesis. Methods PELP1 transcript
  levels were examined in the TCGA-HNSC dataset using different online tools. Immune
  infiltration analysis using TIMER and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis plots was also
  performed. Protein expression was validated by immunohistochemistry (IHC) in human
  normal oral mucosa, OPMDs, and OSCC tissues and in chemically induced murine OSMF
  models. Functional validation was performed by shRNA-mediated PELP1 knockdown in
  SCC131 cells, followed by western blot analysis of Cyclin B1 and NF-κB signaling.

summary: PELP1, an inflammation-responsive nuclear coregulator, is significantly upregulated
  across the OPMD-to-OSCC continuum and correlates with adverse clinicopathological
  features including lymph node involvement and poor survival. Functional knockdown
  in SCC131 cells suppressed Cyclin B1 and NF-κB signaling. The data position PELP1
  as a candidate biomarker for malignant transformation risk in oral potentially malignant
  disorders.
summaryDeep: 'The study combines TCGA-HNSC transcriptomic analysis, immunohistochemistry
  in human tissue spanning normal mucosa through OPMD to OSCC, a chemically induced
  murine OSMF model, and shRNA functional validation — a multi-layered approach that
  gives the PELP1 finding more credibility than a single-method study would. The NF-κB
  connection is particularly relevant: it links PELP1 to the chronic inflammation
  pathway that drives malignant transformation in conditions like oral submucous fibrosis
  and leukoplakia. For oral medicine students, the clinical implication is not yet
  a change in biopsy protocol, but PELP1 could eventually stratify which OPMD patients
  need more aggressive surveillance. The TCGA dataset provides population-level validation
  that the IHC findings are not institution-specific. Limitations: the functional
  data are in a single cell line, and no prospective transformation data are yet available.'
