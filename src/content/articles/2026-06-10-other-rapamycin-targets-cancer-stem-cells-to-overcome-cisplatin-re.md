summaryDeep: 'Cisplatin remains the backbone of systemic therapy for advanced head
  and neck squamous cell carcinoma, but resistance — driven largely by a subpopulation
  of cancer stem cells (CSCs) — limits its effectiveness in a substantial proportion
  of patients. This study uses a mouse xenograft model to show that rapamycin, an
  mTOR inhibitor already approved for other indications, preferentially targets CSCs
  and reduces their capacity to confer cisplatin resistance. The mechanistic logic
  is sound: CSCs rely heavily on mTOR signaling for self-renewal, and blocking it
  destabilizes their survival advantage. While this is preclinical data and the jump
  to human trials is non-trivial, the finding is significant because rapamycin is
  a known drug with an established safety profile, making combination trials feasible
  in the near term. For dental students, HNSCC is not a distant specialty topic —
  dentists are often the first to detect suspicious lesions, and understanding the
  treatment landscape improves your ability to counsel and refer appropriately.'
