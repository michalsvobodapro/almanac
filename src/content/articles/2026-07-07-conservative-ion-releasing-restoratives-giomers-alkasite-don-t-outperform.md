summaryDeep: 'This frequentist network meta-analysis, conducted per PRISMA-NMA and
  assessed with CINeMA, is the most comprehensive head-to-head comparison of ion-releasing
  restoratives to date. Thirteen RCTs with follow-up ranging from 12 months to 8 years
  contributed data on five USPHS/FDI outcomes. Across all outcomes, no ion-releasing
  material consistently outperformed conventional resin composite or resin-modified
  GIC. Glass carbomer could not even be incorporated into the connected treatment
  network, limiting it to qualitative synthesis — a damning signal about the state
  of its evidence base. Certainty of evidence was generally low to moderate due to
  small trial sizes and risk of bias. For a student choosing a restorative material
  in permanent teeth, the take is straightforward: the biological rationale for ion-releasing
  materials is sound, but the clinical payoff has not materialised in head-to-head
  trials. Reserve them for high-caries-risk patients where the fluoride-release mechanism
  has theoretical value, not as a routine upgrade.'
