summaryDeep: Twenty-four growing patients per group received either clear aligner
  (CA) or slow maxillary expander (SME) treatment for maxillary transverse deficiency.
  Arch dimensions were measured at crown and gingival levels, with arch perimeter
  and molar buccolingual inclination assessed at T0, T1 (post-expansion), and T2 (post-retention).
  The primary non-inferiority analysis on intermolar expansion was met, with no statistically
  significant difference between groups (p=0.26). The CA group showed significantly
  greater expansion at the canine cusp tip, and age was a negative predictor of canine-region
  response — younger patients expanded more. This is a small multicenter cohort and
  not an RCT, so confounding cannot be excluded. Nevertheless, the data challenge
  the assumption that removable aligners cannot achieve skeletal-level expansion in
  growing patients. For a Prague student, the practical takeaway is that aligners
  are a legitimate first-line option for mild-to-moderate transverse deficiency in
  the mixed dentition, particularly when compliance with a fixed expander is a concern.
