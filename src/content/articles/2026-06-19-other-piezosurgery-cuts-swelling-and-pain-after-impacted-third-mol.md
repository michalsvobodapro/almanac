summaryDeep: Forty-seven patients underwent bilateral extraction of mesio-angulated
  or horizontally impacted mandibular third molars — one side with a piezosurgery
  protocol, the contralateral side with conventional rotary instruments and manual
  elevators. Facial swelling was objectively quantified using three-dimensional facial
  scans rather than the traditional tape-measure method, providing a more reliable
  outcome metric. At 48 hours, the piezosurgery group showed significantly less swelling
  (p=0.01) and less trismus (p=0.016); pain scores were lower at every measured timepoint;
  and analgesic consumption was higher in the conventional group. Surgical time was
  longer with piezosurgery — a known trade-off. Patient-reported outcomes via the
  PoSSe scale also favoured piezosurgery. For a student who will perform third molar
  extractions in oral surgery rotations, this RCT provides solid justification for
  piezosurgery when the equipment is available, particularly for mesio-angular and
  horizontal impactions where bone removal is substantial. The main barrier remains
  cost and operative time.
