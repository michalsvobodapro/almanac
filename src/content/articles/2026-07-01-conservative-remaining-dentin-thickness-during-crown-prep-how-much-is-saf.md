summaryDeep: 'Traumatic pulpitis is the most common serious complication of crown
  preparation on vital teeth, yet clinical decisions about how aggressively to reduce
  tooth structure are still largely intuitive. This review consolidates fundamental
  and contemporary evidence on the mechanisms of preparation-induced pulp damage —
  mechanical, thermal, and vibrational — and the concept of a ''safe'' RDT threshold.
  The literature does not converge on a single number, but values around 0.5–1.5 mm
  are repeatedly cited as critical boundaries below which pulp damage risk escalates
  sharply. The paper evaluates measurement modalities: conventional radiography is
  limited by angulation and resolution; digital subtraction and dedicated software
  improve precision; CBCT offers the most reliable pre-operative RDT mapping but involves
  radiation and cost. Patented intraoperative devices for real-time RDT monitoring
  are described but remain niche. For a student, this is a solid conceptual framework:
  understand that RDT is not a fixed safe number but a risk gradient modulated by
  preparation speed, cooling, and individual pulp anatomy.'
