summaryDeep: Public knowledge and attitudes toward root canal treatment shape treatment
  acceptance and tooth retention rates across Central Europe. This Croatian cross-sectional
  survey — geographically and culturally proximate to the Czech Republic — used a
  validated 38-item questionnaire distributed via social media to 1,004 adults. The
  mean knowledge score was 5.6 out of 8; prior RCT experience was the strongest predictor
  (OR 2.91), followed by medical profession affiliation (OR 3.16) and female sex (OR
  1.78). Fear and the belief that RCT is painful were common even among those who
  had undergone treatment. Crucially, 88.3% of treated participants would recommend
  the procedure — a satisfaction signal that clinicians can leverage in patient communication.
  The social-media recruitment method introduces selection bias toward more health-literate
  respondents, likely inflating knowledge scores. For a Prague student, this is a
  useful reference point for understanding the patient population they will counsel
  — and a reminder that chairside communication about pain expectations remains the
  highest-yield intervention.
