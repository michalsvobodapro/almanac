summaryDeep: 'Tooth agenesis and colorectal cancer share a genetic intersection through
  the WNT signalling pathway, most concretely via pathogenic AXIN2 variants that cause
  oligodontia and predispose to colorectal polyposis. This systematic review synthesises
  epidemiological, cohort, and genetic studies and finds that the association is real
  but heterogeneous: large-scale cohort analyses report increased hazard ratios for
  early-onset CRC in patients with missing teeth, while several case-control studies
  find no significant difference in agenesis prevalence between CRC patients and controls.
  The review is balanced in acknowledging both positive and null findings. For dental
  students, the clinical implication is not to panic about every hypodontia case,
  but to be aware that patients presenting with oligodontia — especially in the context
  of a family history of colorectal cancer — may warrant a conversation with their
  GP. Dentists are often the first clinicians to document tooth agenesis systematically,
  positioning them as an underutilised node in cancer surveillance pathways.'
