summary: Calcium silicate cements (CSCs) and glass ionomer cements (GICs) are both
  marketed for their remineralising potential, but direct comparisons in simulated
  caries-affected dentine are scarce. This in vitro study finds CSCs release more
  calcium and produce greater mineral density gains, while GICs maintain a fluoride-release
  advantage. Neither dominates across all parameters — the choice should be lesion-
  and context-specific.
summaryDeep: With the shift toward minimally invasive caries management, the remineralising
  capacity of restorative liners and bases has become a genuine clinical differentiator.
  This study places calcium silicate cements (Biodentine, MTA) and resin-modified
  and conventional GICs against standardised caries-affected dentine specimens, measuring
  ion release (Ca²⁺, Si⁴⁺, F⁻), surface microhardness recovery, and mineral density
  via micro-CT over 28 days. CSCs produce significantly higher calcium and silicon
  release and drive greater subsurface mineral density gains, consistent with their
  known alkaline setting chemistry. GICs, however, sustain fluoride release over the
  full observation period, which CSCs cannot match. Clinically, this suggests CSCs
  as the preferred liner under deep lesions where pulp protection and rapid remineralisation
  are priorities, while GICs retain an edge in high-caries-risk patients where sustained
  fluoride delivery matters. Neither material should be chosen on brand alone.
