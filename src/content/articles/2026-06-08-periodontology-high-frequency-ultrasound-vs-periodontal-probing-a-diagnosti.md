summary: High-frequency ultrasound was tested against conventional periodontal probing
  for pocket depth measurement in a formal diagnostic accuracy study. The results
  put a number on how well ultrasound can replicate — or outperform — the probe, which
  has been the unchallenged standard for decades. If accuracy holds up in larger trials,
  non-invasive pocket assessment becomes a realistic near-term clinical tool.
summaryDeep: 'Periodontal probing has been the backbone of pocket depth assessment
  since the 1970s, but it carries well-known limitations: operator variability, patient
  discomfort, and inability to image subgingival tissue architecture. This diagnostic
  accuracy study pits high-frequency ultrasound directly against probing, generating
  sensitivity, specificity, and agreement metrics rather than just descriptive comparisons.
  The technology images soft-tissue contours in real time without penetrating the
  sulcus, which could reduce both measurement error and patient discomfort. For a
  dental student in clinic, the immediate takeaway is that ultrasound is moving from
  research curiosity to plausible adjunct — understanding its accuracy ceiling now
  will matter when the devices start appearing in practice. The study''s design (diagnostic
  accuracy rather than RCT) means it answers the right question first: does it measure
  correctly before asking whether it changes outcomes.'
