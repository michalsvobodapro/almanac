excerpt: Background Evidence-based caries management emphasises identification of
  disease drivers and causal, preventive, and behaviour-oriented care. However, limited
  evidence is available on how such care is documented for adults with high caries
  activity in routine public dental care. This study examined documentation of caries
  treatment and preventive measures consistent with evidence-based caries management,
  and whether documented care addressed underlying causes of caries disease. Methods
  This retrospective observational record-review study included 418 adults aged 25-65
  years from 19 Public Dental Service clinics in Region Västra Götaland, Sweden. High
  caries activity was defined as ≥ 4 manifest caries lesions at a complete oral examination
  in 2015 or 2016. Documented caries-related treatment, preventive measures, causal
  investigations, and counselling content were extracted from electronic dental records
  between the index examination and the subsequent complete oral examination. Descriptive
  statistics summarised documentation patterns. Sex-related differences were analysed

summaryDeep: 'Evidence-based caries management requires identifying disease drivers
  — diet, saliva, hygiene, socioeconomic factors — and addressing them alongside restorative
  care. This audit of electronic dental records in Region Västra Götaland, Sweden,
  selected 418 adults with ≥4 manifest caries lesions and tracked documentation between
  two complete oral examinations. The headline finding is stark: 21.1% of high-caries
  patients had no documented preventive or treatment measure whatsoever. Among those
  who did, oral hygiene advice (43.5%) and fluoride advice (40.9%) were the most common
  entries — but documentation of causal investigation (why this patient has high caries
  activity) was rare. Sex-related differences were present but modest. The study measures
  documentation, not care delivered — but in a system where records are the legal
  and clinical record, undocumented care is effectively absent care. For students
  entering public dental service rotations: the gap between evidence-based caries
  management and routine practice is wide, and it starts with the record.'
