excerptFull: 'Chen H, Ji L, Wang Y, Klineberg I, Chen H. Dentition-Cognition Relationship
  in Aging Populations: A Meta-Analysis of Longitudinal Data. J Oral Rehabil 2026;
  53: 924–38. Systematic review and meta-analysis of longitudinal observational studies.
  Longitudinal cohort and case-control studies assessing dentition status (tooth loss
  or edentulism) and cognitive outcomes (dementia or mild cognitive impairment [MCI])
  were included. Twenty-one studies with approximately 35.7 million participants and
  a mean follow-up of 8.9 years were analysed. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence
  intervals (CIs) were calculated using random- or fixed-effects models depending
  on heterogeneity. Study quality was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale. Tooth
  loss was associated with an increased risk of both dementia (OR 1.26, 95% CI 1.07–1.49)
  and MCI (OR 1.40, 95% CI 1.14–1.71), with stronger associations observed in individuals
  with edentulism, particularly for MCI (OR 1.90, 95% CI 1.07–3.35) and to a lesser
  extent dementia (OR 1.16, 95% CI 1.09–1.23). Findings also suggested a dose-response
  relationship, whereby increasing severity of tooth loss corresponded to greater
  cognitive risk. Subgroup analyses indicated higher risks in women and Western populations.
  Limited evidence also suggested that denture use attenuated this association, showing
  no significant increased dementia risk among denture wearers (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.82–1.28).
  Tooth loss is associated with an increased risk of cognitive decline and dementia,
  with more severe tooth loss conferring greater risk. Prosthodontic rehabilitation
  may mitigate this association, suggesting a potential role for oral health in supporting
  cognitive ageing.'

summaryDeep: 'This is the largest longitudinal synthesis to date on the dentition-cognition
  link. Pooled ORs from 21 studies covering 35.7 million people show tooth loss raises
  dementia odds by 26% (OR 1.26, 95% CI 1.07–1.49) and MCI odds by 40% (OR 1.40, 95%
  CI 1.14–1.71). Edentulism hits hardest for MCI (OR 1.90), and a dose-response gradient
  — more teeth lost, greater cognitive risk — strengthens the causal inference, even
  if confounding cannot be fully excluded in observational data. The denture finding
  is the most immediately actionable: wearers showed no significant excess dementia
  risk (OR 1.03), suggesting prosthetic rehabilitation may attenuate the pathway,
  possibly via restored masticatory function and sensory input. Women and Western
  populations showed higher effect sizes. For a Prague dental student, this reframes
  tooth preservation and timely prosthetic replacement not just as oral health goals
  but as potential neuroprotective interventions — a message worth communicating to
  every patient facing extraction.'
