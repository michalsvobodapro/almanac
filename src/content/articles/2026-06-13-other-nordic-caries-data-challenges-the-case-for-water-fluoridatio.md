summaryDeep: 'Scandinavian countries have some of the lowest caries rates in the world,
  yet none fluoridate their water supplies. This European Journal of Oral Sciences
  analysis uses that natural experiment to interrogate the population-level value
  of community water fluoridation (CWF) in settings where fluoride toothpaste penetration
  is near-universal. The conclusion — that CWF at 0.7 ppm provides only minor additional
  caries reduction in high-income populations — directly challenges the public health
  orthodoxy that has driven CWF policy in the UK, US, and Australia for decades. The
  implications for Czech public health policy are limited (CWF was never implemented
  here), but the findings matter for understanding which fluoride delivery mechanisms
  actually drive the caries decline seen across Europe. Read alongside the New Zealand
  CWF study in today''s batch, the picture is nuanced: CWF may still matter in lower-income
  or higher-risk populations even if it''s redundant in Nordic-style contexts.'
