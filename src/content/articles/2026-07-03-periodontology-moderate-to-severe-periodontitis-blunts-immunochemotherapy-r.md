  lung cancer(NSCLC) treatment. Periodontitis(PD) is one of the most prevalent oral
  diseases worldwide; however, its influence on the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy
  in NSCLC remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the association between PD
  status and severity with treatment outcomes following neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy
  in NSCLC. Methods Patients with initial stage IIA-IIIB NSCLC(excluding T4N2 and
  N3) who underwent surgery after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy at three institutions
  between 2019 and 2025 were retrospectively reviewed. Stabilized inverse probability
  of treatment weighting(IPTW) was employed to balance baseline characteristics. The

summaryDeep: 'This retrospective multi-centre study enrolled 382 patients with stage
  IIA–IIIB non-small cell lung cancer who underwent surgery after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy
  between 2019 and 2025. Stabilised inverse probability of treatment weighting balanced
  baseline characteristics between 51 non-periodontitis and 331 periodontitis cases.
  After adjustment, grade II–III periodontitis — but not grade I — was an independent
  predictor of lower major pathological response and complete pathological response
  rates, as well as poorer preliminary survival. Interaction analyses identified age
  and BMI as effect modifiers. The biological rationale is compelling: chronic periodontal
  inflammation elevates systemic cytokines and may alter the tumour immune microenvironment,
  potentially dampening the efficacy of PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitors. Limitations
  include the retrospective design and the absence of standardised periodontal staging
  across centres. For dental students, the takeaway is broader than oncology: this
  is the strongest real-world signal yet that untreated periodontitis carries systemic
  treatment consequences beyond cardiovascular disease.'
