summaryDeep: 'Pediatric liquid antibiotics are acidic, sugar-containing vehicles that
  sit on tooth surfaces repeatedly throughout a treatment course — yet their effect
  on restorative materials is rarely discussed chairside. This in-vitro study exposed
  90 specimens (primary enamel, Cention N, RMGI) to amoxicillin suspension, azithromycin
  suspension, or artificial saliva across three 7-day immersion cycles, mimicking
  realistic prescription durations. Both antibiotics raised surface roughness (Ra)
  significantly versus saliva (p<0.001 and p=0.001 respectively), with no difference
  between them (p=0.999). All three substrate types were affected. Higher surface
  roughness is clinically relevant because it correlates with increased plaque retention
  and bacterial adhesion — a particular concern in primary dentition where caries
  risk is already elevated. The study is in-vitro and uses static immersion rather
  than the dynamic oral environment, so effect sizes likely overestimate clinical
  impact. Nonetheless, the message for parents is clear: rinse with water after antibiotic
  doses, and don''t brush immediately.'
