summaryDeep: 'The Wolters classification stratifies pulpitis into initial, mild, moderate,
  and severe categories based on clinical and radiographic criteria, with the intent
  of matching each stage to the appropriate vital endodontic intervention. This exploratory
  cohort study compares those clinical classifications against histopathological examination
  of extracted pulp tissue to assess diagnostic accuracy. The results reveal meaningful
  discordance: clinical staging does not reliably predict histological severity, which
  matters enormously when the treatment decision — pulp cap versus pulpotomy versus
  RCT — hinges on that staging. This is a moderate-evidence exploratory study, not
  a definitive verdict, but it raises a legitimate concern: if the classification
  system underpinning vital pulp therapy protocols is histopathologically unreliable,
  the clinical decision tree built on it is shakier than assumed. Read alongside the
  PROVE pooled analysis, it suggests that while pulpotomy outcomes are good, our ability
  to pre-select the right cases may still be limited.'
