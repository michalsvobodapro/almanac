summaryDeep: 'The paper dissects why advances in pulp biology, stem-cell research,
  and scaffold biomaterials have not translated into routine clinical regenerative
  procedures. The central argument is that animal models used in preclinical studies
  — predominantly rodents — have pulp anatomy, immune responses, and healing dynamics
  that differ substantially from humans, meaning positive animal results frequently
  fail to replicate clinically. The authors call for standardised large-animal models
  and better-defined outcome metrics before further clinical trials are designed.
  For a student, this is important context: regenerative endodontics is taught as
  a promising frontier, but the review makes clear that the evidence base for mature
  permanent teeth remains thin. It also implicitly challenges the enthusiasm around
  case reports like the blunderbuss open-apex case elsewhere in today''s digest —
  single cases cannot substitute for the translational groundwork that is still missing.'
