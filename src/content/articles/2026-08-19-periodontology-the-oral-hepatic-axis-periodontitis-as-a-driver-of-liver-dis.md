summary: A comprehensive review in the Journal of Periodontal Research maps the epidemiological
  and mechanistic evidence linking periodontal dysbiosis to chronic liver diseases
  including NAFLD, NASH, and cirrhosis. Periodontal pathogens translocate to the liver
  via the portal circulation and amplify hepatic inflammation through TLR signaling
  and cytokine cascades. The axis is bidirectional — liver disease also worsens periodontal
  status.
summaryDeep: 'The oral-hepatic axis review synthesizes epidemiological data showing
  that periodontitis patients carry significantly elevated risk of non-alcoholic fatty
  liver disease and accelerated fibrosis, alongside mechanistic evidence from animal
  and human studies. Key pathways include translocation of Porphyromonas gingivalis
  and its LPS via the portal vein, hepatic TLR4 activation, and downstream NF-κB-driven
  inflammation that compounds metabolic liver injury. The bidirectionality matters
  clinically: impaired hepatic immune surveillance in cirrhotic patients reduces periodontal
  defense, creating a vicious cycle. For the periodontology student, this review consolidates
  the argument for screening periodontal patients for liver disease markers — and
  vice versa — though intervention trials showing that periodontal treatment measurably
  improves liver outcomes remain sparse. The review does not itself generate new primary
  data, but its synthesis of existing evidence is thorough enough to inform how you
  counsel medically complex patients with both conditions.'
