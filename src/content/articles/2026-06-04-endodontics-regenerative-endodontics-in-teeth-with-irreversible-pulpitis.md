summaryDeep: Regenerative endodontic procedures (REPs) built their evidence base on
  necrotic immature permanent teeth, where the goal is apexogenesis and root development.
  The emergence of bioactive calcium silicate-based materials — MTA, Biodentine, and
  their successors — has prompted clinicians to ask whether REPs can be extended to
  teeth with irreversible pulpitis, preserving some vitality rather than committing
  to full extirpation. This scoping review systematically maps the available literature
  and finds that while the biological rationale is sound and case series are promising,
  controlled clinical trials are sparse and heterogeneous in outcome reporting. The
  review highlights that patient selection criteria, material choice, and follow-up
  protocols vary widely, making synthesis difficult. For students, the key takeaway
  is that REPs in irreversible pulpitis are an emerging — not established — indication,
  and the decision to attempt one over conventional RCT should be driven by tooth
  maturity, patient age, and restorability, not material enthusiasm.
