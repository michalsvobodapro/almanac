summaryDeep: 'Infra-zygomatic crest (IZC) mini-screws are widely used for posterior
  maxillary anchorage, but insertion angle recommendations have varied in the literature.
  This retrospective CBCT study measured overall bone thickness (OBT) and cortical
  bone thickness (CBT) at four posterior maxillary sites in 20 patients aged 18–30,
  comparing 50° and 70° angulations from the occlusal plane. At 70°, OBT was significantly
  greater at all four sites (p ≤ 0.002); CBT was also significantly greater at the
  distobuccal root of the second molar and the first-second molar interdental region.
  The practical implication is straightforward: a 70° insertion angle engages more
  bone and more cortex, which should translate to greater primary stability and lower
  failure rates — though this study measures anatomy, not clinical outcomes. The n=20
  is small and the age range (18–30) limits applicability to older patients with different
  bone density. Nonetheless, the finding is consistent with biomechanical principles
  and aligns with the trend in recent IZC literature toward steeper insertion angles.'
