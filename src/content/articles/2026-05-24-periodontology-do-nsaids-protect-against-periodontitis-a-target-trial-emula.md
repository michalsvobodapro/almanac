summary: Using target trial emulation — the gold standard for squeezing causal inference
  out of observational data — this study finds that adults with osteoarthritis who
  take NSAIDs have a measurably lower risk of developing periodontitis. The anti-inflammatory
  mechanism is plausible; the clinical implication is that a patient's NSAID regimen
  deserves a place in your perio risk assessment.
summaryDeep: 'Target trial emulation mimics an RCT within a real-world cohort, controlling
  for confounders that standard observational analyses miss. Here, adults with osteoarthritis
  — a population with chronic systemic inflammation — were followed to compare periodontitis
  incidence between NSAID users and non-users. NSAID exposure was associated with
  a significantly reduced periodontitis risk, consistent with the hypothesis that
  prostaglandin-mediated bone resorption and gingival inflammation are partially suppressed
  by COX inhibition. This isn''t a green light to prescribe NSAIDs for perio prevention,
  but it reframes how you read a medication list: a patient already on long-term ibuprofen
  or naproxen may have a pharmacologically modulated perio trajectory. It also opens
  the door for prospective trials targeting the arachidonic acid pathway as an adjunct
  to conventional perio therapy. For a student building a systemic-disease–oral-health
  mental model, this is a clean example of how pharmacoepidemiology generates testable
  hypotheses.'
