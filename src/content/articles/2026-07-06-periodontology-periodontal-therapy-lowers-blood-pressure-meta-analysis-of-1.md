summaryDeep: 'This systematic review pooled 12 randomized controlled trials to ask
  whether periodontal therapy moves cardiovascular and inflammatory markers in patients
  with periodontitis. The answer is yes, on both counts. Systolic blood pressure dropped
  by a mean of 4.64 mmHg (95% CI −5.99 to −3.30) and diastolic by 1.84 mmHg (95% CI
  −2.65 to −1.02); C-reactive protein fell concurrently. Random-effects models and
  Cochrane RoB2 quality assessment were applied throughout. A 4–5 mmHg systolic reduction
  is the same order of magnitude achieved by lifestyle interventions like sodium restriction
  — and here it comes as a side-effect of standard non-surgical periodontal care.
  The mechanism is presumed to be reduced systemic inflammatory burden. For a dental
  student, the implication is straightforward: periodontal treatment is not just oral
  health maintenance, it is a legitimate cardiovascular risk-reduction tool, and that
  framing belongs in every patient conversation.'
