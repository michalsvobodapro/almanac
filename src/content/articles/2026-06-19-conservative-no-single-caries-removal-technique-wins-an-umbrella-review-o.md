summaryDeep: 'This PROSPERO-registered umbrella review applied AMSTAR-2 and GRADE
  to seven systematic reviews and meta-analyses comparing SR, NSR, and SW caries removal
  in permanent teeth. The headline finding is uncomfortable: no single technique dominates
  across all outcomes. SR is the rational choice when pulp exposure risk is high,
  yet avoiding exposure did not reliably prevent pulpal complications — a direct challenge
  to the assumption that keeping the pulp covered is sufficient. NSR and SW showed
  higher restorative success rates, and NSR produced marginally less post-operative
  pain than SW. Dentin remineralization and microbial load reduction were statistically
  equivalent across all three approaches. Critically, only two of the seven included
  reviews were rated moderate quality by AMSTAR-2; the remaining five were critically
  low. GRADE certainty ranged from low to very low throughout. The practical implication:
  technique selection should be individualized by lesion depth, pulp proximity, and
  patient risk profile — not by a blanket protocol — and clinicians should communicate
  genuine uncertainty to patients.'
