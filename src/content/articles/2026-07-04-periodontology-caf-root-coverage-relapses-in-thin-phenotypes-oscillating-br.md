summaryDeep: 'Thirty-one of the original 60 CAF patients were re-examined at 6 years,
  making this one of the longest follow-ups for root coverage surgery in the literature.
  Both manual and oscillating-rotating toothbrush groups showed significant loss of
  complete root coverage (CRC) by year 6 — 58.8% CRC in the manual group and a higher
  rate in the oscillating group, but the oscillating group''s breakdown started at
  2 years versus 6 months for manual users. Random-intercept models confirmed the
  phenotype effect: thin and medium phenotypes drove relapse regardless of brush type.
  The study is limited by 48% attrition and the observational nature of the follow-up
  phase, but the direction of effect is clinically coherent. For students counselling
  patients pre-surgery: phenotype assessment is non-negotiable, and post-surgical
  brush choice genuinely matters for medium-term stability even if it doesn''t prevent
  eventual recession recurrence.'
