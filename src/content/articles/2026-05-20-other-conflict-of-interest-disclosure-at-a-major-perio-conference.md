summary: A systematic audit of conflict-of-interest disclosures at an international
  periodontology conference finds significant inconsistencies — speakers with industry
  ties frequently under-disclose, and the conference's own enforcement mechanisms
  are weak. This matters because perio guidelines are heavily shaped by conference
  consensus. Knowing who funds the speakers is not optional.
summaryDeep: 'Conflicts of interest in academic dentistry are structurally embedded:
  the same companies that manufacture implants, regenerative materials, and periodontal
  drugs fund the conferences where treatment guidelines are debated. This audit cross-references
  speaker disclosure forms at a named international periodontology conference against
  publicly available industry payment databases and published financial disclosures
  in concurrent journal articles. Under-disclosure is common — roughly a third of
  speakers with verifiable industry relationships either omitted them entirely or
  disclosed only a subset. The conference''s stated enforcement policy (self-declaration
  only, no verification) is identified as the root cause. For a student consuming
  CPD content: this is a methodological reminder to check the disclosure slide, cross-reference
  it against the journal version of any cited study, and weight consensus statements
  accordingly. The broader implication is that professional societies need mandatory
  third-party verification of disclosures, not honour systems.'
