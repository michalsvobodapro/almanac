summary: A cancer-derived near-infrared photoimmunotherapy (NIR-PIT) technique has
  been adapted to selectively eliminate periodontal pathogens by binding an antibody-dye
  conjugate to target bacteria and activating it with near-infrared light. Unlike
  broad-spectrum antimicrobials, the approach leaves commensal species intact. If
  the bench results translate, this could redefine adjunctive periodontal therapy.
summaryDeep: 'Near-infrared photoimmunotherapy was originally engineered to destroy
  cancer cells with antibody-guided precision; this adaptation turns the same logic
  on periodontal pathogens. An antibody-dye conjugate is designed to bind selectively
  to target bacteria — likely keystone species such as P. gingivalis — and NIR light
  activation triggers cell death only in those bound cells. The critical differentiator
  from photodynamic therapy or chlorhexidine is microbiome selectivity: commensal
  bacteria that are not targeted by the antibody remain unaffected. The current evidence
  is pre-clinical and the report is news-level rather than a peer-reviewed primary
  paper, so mechanistic details and species specificity remain to be scrutinised.
  Still, the conceptual leap is significant — precision antimicrobial therapy at the
  subgingival level has been a long-standing unmet need in periodontology. Watch for
  a primary publication; if selectivity holds in vivo, this challenges the rationale
  for systemic antibiotics as adjuncts to subgingival debridement.'
