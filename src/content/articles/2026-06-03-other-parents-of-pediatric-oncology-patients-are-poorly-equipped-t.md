summary: Chemotherapy causes severe oral complications in children — mucositis, xerostomia,

summaryDeep: 'Oral complications affect the majority of children undergoing chemotherapy
  and are a leading cause of treatment interruption and systemic infection, yet oral
  health is rarely integrated into pediatric oncology care pathways. This study assesses
  parental awareness and hygiene practices across a cohort of families with children
  in active chemotherapy, finding significant knowledge deficits around mucositis
  management, appropriate toothbrush selection, fluoride use, and when to escalate
  to a dentist. Parents who received any structured dental counselling performed markedly
  better on both knowledge and practice metrics. The clinical implication is straightforward:
  dentists and dental hygienists should be embedded in pediatric oncology teams, and
  brief standardised parent-education interventions at diagnosis could meaningfully
  reduce oral morbidity. For dental students, this is also a reminder that the scope
  of dental practice extends well into medically complex paediatric settings.'
