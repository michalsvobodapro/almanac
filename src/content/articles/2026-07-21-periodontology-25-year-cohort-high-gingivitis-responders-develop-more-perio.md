summaryDeep: 'In the 1990s, 48 systemically and periodontally healthy adults were
  classified as high responders (HR) or low responders (LR) based on the degree of
  gingival inflammation they mounted during a standardised plaque-accumulation experiment.
  This 25-year follow-up tracked their actual periodontal trajectories. HR individuals
  showed significantly greater clinical attachment loss, deeper probing depths, and
  higher rates of periodontitis diagnosis compared with LR peers — a finding that
  held even after adjusting for plaque levels and smoking. The magnitude of the difference
  is clinically meaningful, not just statistically significant. For a dental student,
  the lesson is foundational: risk stratification at the first appointment should
  include inflammatory phenotype, not just plaque scores. The cohort is small (n=48)
  and attrition over 25 years is inevitable, but the signal is remarkably consistent
  and biologically coherent. It also supports the EFP''s 2017 classification framework,
  which explicitly incorporates susceptibility as a grade modifier.'
