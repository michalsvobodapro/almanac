summaryDeep: 'This retrospective chart review analyzed 78 peri-implantitis-affected
  implants and 100 adjacent implants in 53 patients, using linear and generalized
  linear mixed-effects models to account for patient-level clustering. The headline
  finding — 53% of adjacent implants developing peri-implantitis — is striking and
  clinically important. Distal neighbors were more severely affected than mesial ones:
  deeper probing depth (p = 0.037) and greater relative probing depth (p = 0.034)
  at near versus far sites, suggesting a directional inflammatory gradient. Mesial
  adjacent implants showed primarily increased recession rather than pocket deepening.
  Marginal bone loss differences between near and far sites were small and did not
  reach significance, which may reflect the relatively short follow-up or the early
  stage of spread. The mechanism is likely shared biofilm reservoir and local immune
  dysregulation rather than direct bone-to-bone transmission. Practically, this means
  a peri-implantitis diagnosis on one implant should trigger heightened monitoring
  of every adjacent fixture.'
