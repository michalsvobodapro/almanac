summaryDeep: 'This PROSPERO-registered umbrella review synthesized 11 systematic reviews
  and meta-analyses on calcium-silicate-based materials (CSMs) — including MTA, Biodentine,
  and related bioceramics — used for vital primary-tooth pulpotomy. Across the included
  reviews, MTA and Biodentine showed equivalent clinical success and radiographic
  outcomes at up to 24 months, consolidating the case for either as a first-line choice
  over formocresol. The Biodentine-versus-formocresol comparison is where the evidence
  fractures: one meta-analysis favoured Biodentine radiographically, another found
  no meaningful difference, and clinical success rates were inconsistent across reviews.
  Methodological quality was a real problem — most reviews rated critically low or
  low on AMSTAR-2 — and a corrected covered area of 13.1% signals heavy primary-study
  overlap, so the apparent weight of 11 reviews overstates the independent evidence.
  The GRADE-informed certainty is accordingly modest. For practice, the shift away
  from formocresol toward bioceramics is well-supported; the fine-grained choice between
  specific CSMs is not yet evidence-driven.'
