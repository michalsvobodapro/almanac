summaryDeep: 'AI orthodontics research has exploded — 1,917 articles identified, 110
  meeting inclusion criteria — but this scoping review reveals a field that is producing
  models faster than it is validating them. Diagnosis and treatment planning applications
  (automated cephalometrics, malocclusion classification, tooth segmentation) represent
  the bulk of published work, with deep learning architectures increasingly dominant
  after 2018. Practice management and education applications are emerging but thin.
  The review''s most important contribution is its honest audit of validation quality:
  most studies test on internal datasets, few report external validation, and prospective
  clinical trials are nearly absent. This matters enormously for a student who will
  encounter AI-assisted cephalometric software in clinic within years of graduation
  — the accuracy numbers in marketing materials are almost always internal-validation
  figures. The authors call for interdisciplinary collaboration between clinicians,
  data scientists, and regulators, which is the right prescription. Until prospective
  clinical evidence accumulates, AI tools in orthodontics are decision-support aids,
  not autonomous systems.'
