summaryDeep: Extranodal extension of nodal metastasis is one of the most powerful
  negative prognostic indicators in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), yet its molecular
  drivers have been poorly characterized. This study combines transcriptome profiling,
  tissue microarray histopathology, and functional cell assays to implicate metastasis-associated
  fibroblasts (MAFs) — specifically those activated via Yes-associated protein (YAP)
  signaling — as architects of the desmoplastic microenvironment that enables ENE.
  ENE-positive MAFs isolated from lymph nodes showed superior proliferative and migratory
  capacity and drove significantly more ECM remodeling (collagen deposition, matrix
  metalloproteinase activity) than ENE-negative MAFs. Co-culture with HSC-3 tumor
  cells amplified these effects, suggesting a reciprocal tumor-stroma feedback loop.
  YAP knockdown experiments reduced MAF activation and ECM remodeling, positioning
  YAP as a druggable node. For clinical practice, this is basic science — it doesn't
  change staging or surgical margins today. But it provides a mechanistic rationale
  for why some N+ OSCC patients progress rapidly despite seemingly adequate nodal
  clearance, and it points toward stromal-targeted adjuvant strategies.
