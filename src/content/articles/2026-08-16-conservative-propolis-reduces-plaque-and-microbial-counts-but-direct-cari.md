summaryDeep: This PROSPERO-registered review screened 1,919 records and included 45
  RCTs assessing propolis dentifrices, mouthwashes, and tablets. The plaque-reduction
  effect versus negative control was large and statistically robust (SMD –1.11, 95%
  CI –1.52 to –0.70), and microbial count reductions were similarly significant. Against
  chlorhexidine, propolis performed comparably on plaque. However, the authors applied
  GRADE throughout and found that direct caries indices — the outcome that actually
  matters for prevention — were assessed in too few trials and with too much heterogeneity
  for pooling. The biological plausibility is solid (flavonoids, phenolic acids, antimicrobial
  activity), and patient acceptance is high. But the leap from plaque reduction to
  caries prevention requires RCTs with hard endpoints, and none exist at scale yet.
  Clinicians can reasonably mention propolis as an adjunct for patients seeking natural
  alternatives, but should not position it as a proven caries-preventive agent.
