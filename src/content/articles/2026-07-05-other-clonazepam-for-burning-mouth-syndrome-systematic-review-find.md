summaryDeep: 'Burning mouth syndrome affects predominantly postmenopausal women and
  remains one of oral medicine''s most frustrating management challenges. Clonazepam
  — a benzodiazepine with GABA-A agonist activity — is widely used off-label, but
  the evidence base has never been rigorously synthesised using modern methods. This
  systematic review applies the SWiM (Synthesis Without Meta-analysis) framework,
  appropriate given the heterogeneity of included studies. The review confirms that
  clonazepam (topical and systemic) produces clinically meaningful VAS pain reductions
  in a subset of BMS patients, but response is strongly modulated by psychological
  comorbidity profile — patients with high anxiety or depression scores respond differently
  from those without. Dosing protocols varied widely across studies, and no study
  used a standardised BMS diagnostic framework consistently. The clinical bottom line:
  clonazepam is a reasonable second-line option for BMS, particularly in patients
  with anxiety comorbidity, but the evidence does not yet support a specific dosing
  protocol or patient-selection algorithm. Refer complex BMS cases to oral medicine
  specialists.'
