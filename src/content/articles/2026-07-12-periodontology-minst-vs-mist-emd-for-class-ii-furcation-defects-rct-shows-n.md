summaryDeep: 'This RCT directly compared minimally invasive non-surgical therapy (MINST)
  with flapless subgingival EMD application against the established minimally invasive
  surgical technique (MIST) plus EMD in patients with Class II mandibular furcation
  defects. Both arms achieved comparable probing depth reduction, clinical attachment
  level gain, and radiographic bone fill — meeting the pre-specified non-inferiority
  margin. Where MINST pulled ahead was on patient-centred outcomes: less postoperative
  pain, faster recovery, and higher satisfaction scores. The findings are clinically
  significant because MIST requires surgical expertise, longer chair time, and carries
  greater morbidity. If non-inferiority holds in larger multicentre replication, MINST
  + EMD could become the first-line approach for Class II furcations, reserving surgery
  for cases that fail to respond. The study is well-designed and high-grade evidence;
  the main caveat is that furcation defect morphology varies considerably, and the
  non-inferiority margin should be scrutinised before wholesale protocol change.'
