summaryDeep: 'The nasopalatine canal (NPC) frequently complicates implant placement
  in the anterior maxilla, particularly after post-extraction ridge resorption. This
  scoping review mapped 29 clinical studies describing either NPC obliteration (83
  patients, 92 implants) or NPC repositioning (26 patients, 57 implants). Implant
  survival was 100% in the repositioning group and 95.6% in the obliteration group
  — a difference that favours repositioning on survival alone. However, neurosensory
  disturbance of the palatal mucosa occurred in 34.6% of repositioning cases versus
  9.6% of obliteration cases, and persistent altered sensation was documented exclusively
  in the repositioning group. This is a clinically important asymmetry: a 100% implant
  survival rate is less meaningful if one-third of patients have lasting sensory changes.
  The evidence base is characterised by small samples, inconsistent outcome reporting,
  and variable follow-up — all acknowledged limitations of a scoping review. For students,
  the practical message is that NPC obliteration with a graft material is the more
  conservative and neurologically safer approach when the canal interferes with implant
  placement.'
