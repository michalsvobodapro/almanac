summary: Transferring saliva from periodontitis patients into germ-free mice reproduces
  altered IgA-related immune features, providing causal evidence that the dysbiotic
  microbiome — not just host genetics — drives immune dysregulation. This positions
  salivary microbiota as an active pathogenic agent, not a bystander.
summaryDeep: Germ-free animal models are the cleanest way to isolate the contribution
  of microbiota from host factors, and this study exploits that design to test whether
  periodontitis-associated saliva can reconstitute disease-relevant immune phenotypes.
  Mice colonised with periodontitis donor microbiota showed altered IgA coating of
  bacteria and disrupted mucosal immune signalling compared to controls receiving
  healthy donor saliva. IgA is the primary mucosal antibody that shapes which bacteria
  colonise oral and gut surfaces, so its dysregulation has downstream consequences
  beyond the periodontium. The findings strengthen the case that periodontitis is
  a communicable dysbiosis in a meaningful immunological sense — relevant for patient
  counselling on household transmission risk. Mechanistically, it suggests that restoring
  IgA-mediated microbial selection could be a therapeutic target alongside scaling
  and root planing. For students, this is a masterclass in using germ-free models
  to move from association to causation in microbiome research.
