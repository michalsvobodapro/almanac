summary: An in vitro study of 44 maxillary premolars compared fracture resistance
  across four restoration types after endodontic treatment and MOD cavity preparation.
  CAD/CAM lithium disilicate endocrowns showed the highest fracture resistance, while
  the novel self-adhesive bulk-fill composite (Stela) performed comparably to short-fiber-reinforced
  composite — both falling short of the ceramic option.
summaryDeep: 'Endodontically treated teeth with MOD cavities are notoriously fracture-prone,
  and material selection is a daily clinical decision. This study tested four approaches:
  a self-adhesive bulk-fill resin (Stela, SDI), a short-fiber-reinforced composite
  (EverX Flow + nanohybrid cap), a conventional nanohybrid alone (Filtek Z250 XT),
  and CAD/CAM lithium disilicate endocrowns (IPS e.max). With n=11 per group, statistical
  power is limited, and in vitro fracture testing does not replicate the fatigue loading
  of clinical function. Nevertheless, the endocrown''s superiority aligns with existing
  literature and supports its use when sufficient coronal tooth structure allows.
  The Stela bulk-fill''s performance is notable as a faster chairside alternative
  when indirect restoration is not feasible, though long-term clinical data are absent.'
