excerpt: 'Aim: Gutta-percha is the most commonly used biocompatible obturation material
  in endodontics for the three-dimensional and hermetic sealing of the root canal
  system. However, despite the dimensions defined in the ISO 6877 standard, dimensional
  discrepancies between gutta-percha cones and endodontic files have been reported.
  The aim of this study was to comparatively evaluate the dimensional variability
  of ISO #30/.06 gutta-percha cones from four different manufacturers using an endodontic
  gauge. Methodology: A total of 240 ISO #30/.06 gutta-percha cones (n = 60 per brand)
  from four manufacturers (ProTaper Ultimate, MetaBiomed, Diadent, and EndoArt) were
  evaluated. Cone diameters were measured at the D0, D5, D10, and D15 levels using
  a Woodpecker R1 Plus endodontic gauge. Based on the ISO 6877 nominal dimensions,
  measurements were categorized as undersized, within the nominal range, or oversized.
  Statistical analysis was performed using Pearson''s chi-square and Fisher''s exact
  tests with Bonferroni correction (p &lt; 0.05). Results: Dimensional discrepancies
  from the nominal ISO values were observed across all measurement levels, with statistically
  significant differences among the brands (p &lt; 0.05). ProTaper Ultimate and MetaBiomed
  showed the highest compatibility with nominal dimensions, whereas EndoArt exhibited
  a consistently high proportion of undersized cones (57.1–67.2%) across all regions.

summaryDeep: 'Dimensional accuracy of gutta-percha cones is a prerequisite for the
  tug-back technique and reliable apical seal. This in-vitro study measured 60 cones
  per brand (ProTaper Ultimate, MetaBiomed, Diadent, EndoArt) at D0, D5, D10, and
  D15 using a calibrated Woodpecker R1 Plus gauge. Chi-square and Fisher''s exact
  tests with Bonferroni correction confirmed significant between-brand differences
  at every level. EndoArt''s consistent undersizing (57–67%) is the headline finding
  — an undersized master cone will not achieve tug-back regardless of technique, compromising
  the apical seal. Diadent showed a different failure mode: oversizing at D15, which
  risks canal transportation during placement. The study is limited to a single cone
  size (#30/.06) and four brands, so generalisability is partial. But the practical
  message is immediate: verify tug-back clinically and consider gauge-checking a sample
  from each new batch, especially with less-established brands.'
