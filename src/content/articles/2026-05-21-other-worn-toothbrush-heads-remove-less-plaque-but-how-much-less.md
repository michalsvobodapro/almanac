summary: A controlled study quantifies the drop in artificial plaque removal efficacy
  as toothbrush bristles wear, providing an evidence base for the standard three-month
  replacement recommendation. Worn brushes showed measurable but not catastrophic
  efficacy loss — the clinical nuance is in the degree of wear, not a binary new-vs-old
  comparison. Relevant for patient counselling and paediatric dentistry.
summaryDeep: Toothbrush replacement guidelines are ubiquitous but surprisingly under-evidenced.
  This European Journal of Oral Sciences study uses a standardised artificial plaque
  model to measure removal efficacy across a range of bristle wear states, from new
  to heavily frayed. The results confirm that worn brushes underperform, but the relationship
  is graded rather than a cliff-edge — moderately worn brushes still remove substantial
  plaque, while severely worn ones show clinically meaningful deficits. The study
  also examines whether bristle splaying is a reliable visual proxy for reduced efficacy
  (it is, but only at more advanced wear stages). For clinical practice, this supports
  personalised replacement advice — patients with aggressive brushing technique may
  need more frequent changes — rather than a blanket three-month rule applied uniformly.
