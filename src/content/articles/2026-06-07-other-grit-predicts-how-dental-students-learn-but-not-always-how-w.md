summaryDeep: 'This study mapped the relationship between Grit — the combination of
  perseverance and sustained passion toward long-term goals — academic achievement,
  and learning approaches in a dental student cohort. The headline result is a dissociation:
  Grit tracks strongly with deep, meaning-oriented learning strategies, but its correlation
  with GPA or exam scores is modest at best. That gap matters. It suggests that high-Grit
  students are building durable clinical reasoning frameworks even when surface metrics
  don''t reflect it yet — and that low-Grit students may be gaming assessments without
  internalising knowledge. For dental educators, the implication is pointed: if you
  only optimise for grades, you''re blind to a predictor of long-term professional
  resilience. Screening or fostering Grit — through mentorship structures, deliberate
  practice, and goal-setting curricula — could be a higher-yield intervention than
  another MCQ revision session. For students themselves, the takeaway is that the
  slog of preclinical years is doing more for you than your transcript shows.'
