  soft-tissue response and outcomes in dental implantology. This ex vivo study evaluated

summaryDeep: 'Mucosal thickness at implant sites predicts soft-tissue stability and
  aesthetic outcomes, but current measurement methods each carry trade-offs: transgingival
  probing is invasive, CBCT delivers radiation and overestimates thickness, and conventional
  ultrasound is operator-dependent. This ex vivo study (18 porcine hemimandibles,
  guided implant placement, five standardised measurement points) tested a 17 MHz
  ultrasound system with a custom probe holder against all three reference methods,
  adding an AI deep-learning segmentation layer. Expert-annotated US showed no significant
  difference from probing (p=0.328) and moderate ICC agreement (0.58). The AI model
  reached ICC 0.67 against expert annotation — a promising result for a first-generation
  automated system. CBCT was the outlier, yielding significantly higher MT values
  than probing (p=0.035) and poor ICC against US (0.14). The study is ex vivo and
  limited to porcine tissue, so clinical translation requires in vivo validation,
  but the AI-ultrasound combination is a credible path toward non-invasive, radiation-free
  mucosal assessment in implant follow-up.'
