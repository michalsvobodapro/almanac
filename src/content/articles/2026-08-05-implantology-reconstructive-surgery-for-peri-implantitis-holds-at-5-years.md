summaryDeep: Twenty-eight patients (32 implants) with peri-implantitis — probing depth
  ≥5 mm with bleeding/suppuration and radiographic bone loss ≥3 mm — underwent reconstructive
  surgery using deproteinized bovine bone mineral combined with biphasic calcium sulfate,
  followed for a mean of 61.4 months. Implant survival was 100% at 5 years, and both
  probing depth and clinical attachment level improved significantly from baseline
  to 3- and 5-year follow-up (p < 0.001). Radiographic defect fill was also significant
  (p ≤ 0.002). However, treatment success — defined as absence of disease recurrence
  — was achieved in only 75% of implants, with 25% showing recurrence. The study is
  retrospective, single-center, and lacks a control arm, so it cannot establish superiority
  over other protocols. What it does confirm is that non-contained defects are reconstructable
  with meaningful long-term bone fill, but that ongoing supportive peri-implant therapy
  is non-negotiable to prevent recurrence in a quarter of cases.
