  it is of interest to evaluate the relationship between early childhood caries (ECC)

summaryDeep: 'Early childhood caries (ECC) is the most prevalent chronic disease in
  young children, but its downstream orthodontic consequences are often siloed from
  the restorative conversation. This cross-sectional study systematically assessed
  the relationship between ECC severity and developing malocclusion patterns, finding
  that severe ECC correlates significantly with increased overjet, posterior crossbite,
  and anterior open bite. Premature loss of primary teeth disrupts arch length, alters
  tongue posture, and shifts eruption timing — mechanisms the study contextualises
  alongside behavioral and dietary contributors. The cross-sectional design cannot
  establish causation, and the sample characteristics limit generalisability. But
  the clinical message is integrative: a pediatric patient presenting with rampant
  caries should trigger an orthodontic risk conversation, not just a restorative treatment
  plan. For students in Prague, where pediatric dental access varies by socioeconomic
  background, this framing matters.'
