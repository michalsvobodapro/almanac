summaryDeep: 'Hereditary angioedema is rare — prevalence around 1:50,000 — but dental
  procedures are a well-documented trigger for potentially life-threatening laryngeal
  oedema. The pathophysiology is bradykinin-mediated, not IgE-mediated, which means
  the standard anaphylaxis protocol (adrenaline, antihistamines, corticosteroids)
  is largely ineffective. This case series presents four patients managed with pre-procedural
  prophylaxis and on-site availability of specific HAE treatments. The clinical value
  is not in the sample size — four cases is not evidence — but in the framework: identify
  HAE patients in the medical history, liaise with their immunologist before any procedure,
  have a written emergency plan, and never assume swelling is anaphylaxis without
  ruling out HAE. For students in Prague, where rare disease protocols are increasingly
  formalised under EU orphan disease frameworks, this is the kind of case that will
  appear in OSCEs and, eventually, in practice.'
