summary: Erosive tooth wear is accelerating in younger populations, and the toothpaste
  market has responded with a proliferation of 'anti-erosion' claims. This in vitro
  study puts multiple formulations head-to-head against standardised acid challenges
  and finds substantial variation in enamel and dentine protection — stannous fluoride
  and hydroxyapatite-based pastes consistently outperform standard fluoride formulations.
  Brand claims and ingredient lists diverge significantly.
summaryDeep: 'Erosive tooth wear is now classified as a distinct condition (BEWE scoring,
  2008 classification), yet patient counselling on toothpaste selection often defaults
  to fluoride concentration alone. This comparative in vitro study exposes bovine
  enamel and dentine specimens to repeated citric acid challenges, then applies a
  panel of commercially available dentifrices under simulated brushing conditions,
  quantifying surface loss via profilometry and surface microhardness. Stannous fluoride
  formulations (SnF₂) produce the strongest protective pellicle and lowest surface
  loss across both substrates. Nano-hydroxyapatite pastes perform comparably on enamel
  but show less dentine protection. Standard 1450 ppm NaF pastes offer the least protection
  despite identical fluoride concentration — the counterion and carrier matrix matter.
  For clinical counselling, this is actionable: patients with BEWE scores ≥3 should
  be directed specifically to SnF₂ or nano-HAP formulations, not just ''high-fluoride''
  toothpaste.'
