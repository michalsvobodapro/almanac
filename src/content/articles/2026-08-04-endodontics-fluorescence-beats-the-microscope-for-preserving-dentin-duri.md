summary: An in-vitro micro-CT study of 35 extracted teeth finds that 405 nm fluorescence-assisted
  magnification (ReVeal) outperforms both the dental operating microscope and loupes
  in reducing dentin loss and residual cement during fiber post removal. The post–cement–dentin
  interface, invisible under white light, fluoresces distinctly, enabling more conservative
  ultrasonic preparation.
summaryDeep: 'Fiber post retrieval during retreatment is one of the most dentin-destructive
  steps in endodontics, largely because the post, cement, and dentin share similar
  colour and translucency under conventional illumination. This study tested four
  visual-guidance conditions — naked eye, ×2.5 loupes, ×6.9 operating microscope,
  and ×2.5 loupes with integrated 405 nm fluorescence (ReVeal) — against pre- and
  post-operative micro-CT datasets measuring volumetric dentin loss, residual cement,
  and remaining dentin thickness at 1–6 mm from the post tip. The fluorescence group
  produced the least dentin loss and the lowest residual cement volume, outperforming
  even the higher-magnification microscope. The mechanism is straightforward: resin
  cements fluoresce under violet light, making the interface visible that is otherwise
  cryptic. The study is in vitro and operator-blinded conditions may not reflect clinical
  reality, but the effect size is large enough to be clinically meaningful. Fluorescence-capable
  loupes are increasingly affordable and this evidence supports their adoption for
  retreatment cases.'
