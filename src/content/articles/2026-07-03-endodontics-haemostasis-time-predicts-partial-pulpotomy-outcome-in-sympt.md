summaryDeep: 'Partial pulpotomy in teeth with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP)
  challenges the long-held dogma that vital pulp therapy is contraindicated once symptoms
  cross the irreversible threshold. This single-centre, one-arm clinical trial tracked
  the time required to achieve haemostasis after pulp amputation and correlated it
  with clinical and radiographic outcomes. Cases where bleeding was controlled quickly
  showed significantly higher success rates, while prolonged haemostasis — a proxy
  for deeper pulp inflammation — predicted failure. The study provides a practical
  intraoperative decision point: if bleeding does not arrest within a defined window,
  conversion to full root canal treatment is warranted. The one-arm design limits
  causal inference, and the excerpt does not specify the haemostasis time threshold,
  but the directional finding aligns with emerging evidence that SIP is a spectrum
  rather than a binary state. For students, this reinforces the value of attempting
  pulp preservation first in appropriate cases while having a clear abort criterion.'
