summaryDeep: 'This BMC Oral Health systematic review searched six databases through
  October 2025 and identified 19 eligible studies evaluating AI for orthodontic diagnosis
  and treatment planning. Most used supervised learning for binary extraction decisions
  or skeletal class assignment; a minority tackled multi-step planning. Performance
  was generally comparable to expert clinicians, with some models achieving accuracy
  above 85% on specific tasks. However, ROBINS-I risk-of-bias assessment revealed
  substantial methodological heterogeneity — different datasets, outcome definitions,
  and validation strategies — precluding pooled estimates. No study demonstrated prospective
  clinical integration or reported patient outcomes. The review highlights a recurring
  gap: models trained on single-institution data rarely generalize, and ''black box''
  architectures limit clinical trust. For a student entering a field where AI tools
  are already being marketed, this is a useful calibration: the technology is promising
  but not yet practice-ready as a standalone decision-maker.'
