summary: An in vitro study of 248 specimens across two permanent crown resins tests
  four surface-treatment protocols — mechanical polishing, glaze after post-curing,
  glaze before post-curing, and combined pre/post glaze — for color stability after
  thermocycling and staining. Applying Optiglaze before post-curing outperforms conventional

summaryDeep: As 3D-printed permanent resin crowns move from novelty to routine, the
  question of how to finish them for long-term esthetic stability becomes clinically
  urgent. This study fabricated 248 specimens from two commercial permanent crown
  resins and subjected them to four surface protocols, then measured color change
  (ΔE₀₀, CIEDE2000) after 10,000 thermocycles and 30-day immersion in artificial saliva,
  coffee, and red wine. The novel finding is that applying Optiglaze before post-curing
  — rather than after — produces superior color stability, likely because the glaze
  layer becomes integrated into the polymer network during final curing rather than
  sitting as a surface coating. FE-SEM cross-sections support this interpretation.
  All groups exceeded the perceptibility threshold (PT₀₀ = 0.81) after thermocycling,
  but the pre-cure glaze group stayed closest to the acceptability threshold (AT₀₀
  = 1.81). This is an in vitro study with one glaze product, so clinical translation
  requires caution — but the workflow implication is clear enough to pilot in practice.
