summary: A PROSPERO-registered systematic review and meta-analysis comparing single
  crowns on short dental implants (SDIs) versus conventional-length implants (CLDIs)
  finds no clinically meaningful difference in survival rates, marginal bone loss,
  or probing depth. The analysis pooled randomized and non-randomized trials using
  random-effects models and RoB2/ROBINS-I risk-of-bias tools. For patients with limited
  bone height, SDIs are a defensible first-line option — not a compromise.
summaryDeep: 'Short implants have been creeping toward mainstream acceptance for years;
  this meta-analysis is the most methodologically rigorous synthesis to date. Covering
  Embase, PubMed, Scopus, and five other databases with no year restriction, the review
  included only comparative trials — randomized and non-randomized — that reported
  survival, marginal bone loss (MBL), and probing depth simultaneously. Random-effects
  models were used throughout, with subgroup and sensitivity analyses to probe heterogeneity.
  The headline finding: SDIs under single crowns are non-inferior to CLDIs on all
  three primary outcomes. MBL and probing depth differences were not statistically
  significant. Critically, SDIs eliminate the need for sinus lifts or vertical ridge
  augmentation in many atrophic posterior cases, reducing surgical morbidity, cost,
  and treatment time. The evidence now supports offering SDIs as a primary treatment
  option — not a fallback — when bone height is the limiting factor, provided crown-to-implant
  ratio and occlusal load are managed appropriately.'
