summaryDeep: 'As additive manufacturing displaces milling for full-arch implant frameworks,
  understanding which fabrication variables govern dimensional accuracy is clinically
  urgent — marginal fit directly affects peri-implant bone health. This in vitro study
  (n = 10 per condition) used metrology software and a digital triple-scan protocol
  to assess trueness and marginal gap across six DLP conditions plus a subtractive
  control. The key finding: removing the support bar improved trueness, counterintuitively,
  because support structures introduce distortion during removal. The choice between
  xenon-flash and LED polymerization units mattered less and interacted with geometry.
  Subtractive manufacturing still outperformed all additive conditions on marginal
  fit, but the gap is narrowing. For students entering a world where in-office 3D
  printing is routine, this study is a reminder that workflow parameters — not just
  material choice — determine whether a printed framework is clinically acceptable.'
