summaryDeep: 'Working length determination is one of the most debated steps in root
  canal treatment, with electronic apex locators increasingly preferred over radiography
  on grounds of accuracy and radiation reduction. This RCT adds a patient-centred
  outcome — postoperative pain — to that debate. One hundred and twenty-five patients
  with asymptomatic irreversible pulpitis in single-rooted, single-canal teeth were
  randomized across five groups; a single clinician performed all treatment under
  standardized conditions. Pain was assessed by VAS at four time points and analgesic
  intake was recorded. No statistically significant differences emerged at any time
  point, and no patient-level variables (age, sex, tooth location) modified the effect.
  The study is limited to asymptomatic irreversible pulpitis in single-canal teeth,
  so extrapolation to necrotic or multi-canal cases requires caution. The practical
  message is reassuring: if your clinic uses periapical radiography as a backup when
  the apex locator signal is unreliable, you are not imposing additional postoperative
  pain on your patient.'
