summaryDeep: Point-of-care GCF biomarker testing (notably aMMP-8) is increasingly
  marketed for periodontal risk assessment, so this large clinical microbiological
  study (n=515) adds important nuance. Both IL-8 and aMMP-8 were elevated in advanced
  periodontitis, confirming their association with disease severity. However, when
  bacterial loads of six key periodontal pathogens were correlated with biomarker
  concentrations, only IL-8 showed a specific link to P. gingivalis — the keystone
  pathogen most associated with disease progression. aMMP-8 did not discriminate by
  pathogen species. Critically, smoking confounded both biomarkers, meaning clinicians
  using GCF tests in smokers need to interpret results with caution. For a student
  learning periodontal diagnostics, this is a useful reminder that biomarker elevation
  signals inflammation load, not necessarily a specific pathogen, and that smoking
  status must always be factored in.
