summaryDeep: 'Early childhood caries remains the most prevalent chronic disease in
  preschool children globally, and identifying modifiable risk factors early is the
  lever for prevention. This study mines the KUNO-Kids Health Study — an ongoing German
  birth cohort — using repeated cross-sectional questionnaire data at ages 2, 3, 4,
  and 5. Parental factors including education, migration background, feeding practices,
  and oral hygiene habits were analysed as predictors of parent-reported caries. The
  repeated cross-sectional design is a pragmatic strength (it captures the same population
  at multiple time points) but also a limitation: it cannot establish causality, and
  parent-reported caries is a softer endpoint than clinical examination. Still, the
  pattern of associations is consistent with the broader ECC literature and reinforces
  that socioeconomic and behavioural determinants cluster early. For a student, the
  clinical takeaway is about anticipatory guidance: the risk profile is identifiable
  at the first well-child dental visit, well before lesions appear.'
