summaryDeep: 'This systematic review set out to quantify whether residual periodontal
  pockets after active therapy increase peri-implantitis risk — a question with direct
  implications for treatment sequencing. Only four cohort studies met inclusion criteria
  from 3,870 screened records, with follow-up ranging from 1 to 23 years. Despite
  substantial heterogeneity in how both residual pockets and peri-implantitis were
  defined across studies, all four consistently reported that deeper residual pockets
  (≥5 or ≥6 mm) and higher proportions of residual diseased sites correlated with
  greater peri-implantitis occurrence. One study additionally flagged inflammatory
  conditions on adjacent teeth as an independent risk indicator. The inability to
  pool data statistically is a limitation, but the unanimous directional signal across
  four independent cohorts — spanning more than two decades of follow-up — is hard
  to dismiss. The practical implication is straightforward: periodontal stability,
  not just completion of active therapy, should be the threshold for implant placement.'
