  natural ingredients used in toothpastes and gels in improving gingival health. Materials
  and methods A comprehensive search strategy using different databases was conducted
  in accordance with PRISMA guidelines to identify randomized clinical trials (RCTs)
  on the use of natural ingredients for improving gingival health. Included studies
  focussed on outcomes measuring gingival health parameters, such as gingival index
  (GI), or bleeding on probing (BoP). Out of 249 identified studies, 10 RCTs met the
  inclusion criteria. Formulations included natural ingredients from plant extracts
  such as Aloe vera or Pudilan, and hydroxyapatite (as tooth-like calcium phosphate).
  Results The meta-analysis demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in
  gingival health when natural ingredients were used compared to control toothpastes
  and gels (p=0.0051). These findings suggest that natural ingredients may offer comparable
  or superior efficacy to conventional oral care products in terms of gingival health.
  Notably, Aloe vera and hydroxyapatite consistently demonstrated clinical benefits.
  Conclusions Natural ingredients used in oral care products represent a promising

summaryDeep: 'Natural oral care products occupy a large and growing market share,
  but evidence for their gingival benefits has been scattered across small trials.
  This systematic review screened 249 studies and retained 10 RCTs evaluating plant
  extracts (Aloe vera, Pudilan) and hydroxyapatite in toothpastes and gels, with gingival
  index and bleeding on probing as primary outcomes. The pooled meta-analysis reached
  statistical significance (p = 0.0051), with Aloe vera and hydroxyapatite showing
  the most consistent benefit across studies. Hydroxyapatite is particularly interesting
  because it addresses both mineralisation and biofilm disruption — mechanisms relevant
  to gingivitis. Limitations include the small number of included trials, heterogeneous
  formulations, and short follow-up periods typical of oral hygiene RCTs. Still, this
  is the strongest aggregated signal yet that these ingredients are not merely marketing:
  they offer a plausible, evidence-backed adjunct or alternative to conventional formulations
  for patients seeking natural products.'
