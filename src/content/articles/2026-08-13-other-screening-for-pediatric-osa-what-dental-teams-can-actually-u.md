summaryDeep: 'Paediatric OSA affects a significant proportion of children and is associated
  with behavioural problems, growth disruption, and cardiovascular risk — yet it is
  frequently missed until school age or later. This review assesses tools including
  the Pediatric Sleep Questionnaire (PSQ), STOP-BANG adaptations, and clinical signs
  (tonsillar hypertrophy, retrognathia, mouth breathing, bruxism) that dentists are
  positioned to observe. The PSQ emerges as the best-validated short questionnaire
  for dental settings. The review is honest that polysomnography remains the diagnostic
  gold standard and that dental screening is a referral trigger, not a diagnosis.
  For a student, the practical takeaway is structural: add OSA risk questions to your
  paediatric history form and know the referral pathway to paediatric sleep medicine.
  The intersection with orthodontic treatment planning (mandibular advancement, palatal
  expansion) makes this directly relevant to clinical decision-making.'
