summaryDeep: 'Condensing osteitis — a focal sclerosing osteomyelitis presenting as
  a periapical radiopacity — is encountered regularly in dental practice but has been
  poorly characterized epidemiologically. Prevalence estimates in the literature range
  from negligible to surprisingly common, largely because studies differ in radiographic
  modality, diagnostic criteria, and population. This systematic review and meta-analysis
  pools available radiographic prevalence data to produce more reliable estimates.
  For a student, the clinical relevance is in differential diagnosis: condensing osteitis
  must be distinguished from other periapical radiopacities (hypercementosis, idiopathic
  osteosclerosis, periapical cemental dysplasia), and its association with a non-vital
  or chronically irritated pulp means it often signals a need for endodontic intervention.
  A clearer prevalence figure also helps calibrate how often to expect this finding
  on routine radiographs. The meta-analysis design is appropriate for this question,
  though heterogeneity across included studies is likely to be a limiting factor.'
