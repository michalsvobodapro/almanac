summaryDeep: 'Calcium channel blockers — amlodipine, nifedipine, verapamil — are among
  the most commonly prescribed cardiovascular drugs globally, and drug-induced gingival
  overgrowth is a well-documented adverse effect that impairs oral hygiene, drives
  periodontal inflammation, and can affect medication adherence. This cross-sectional
  survey of 139 Turkish cardiologists reveals a knowledge and practice gap that is
  almost certainly not unique to Turkey. Nearly a third reported no knowledge of DIGO
  pathogenesis at all. Only 22.3% counselled patients proactively; 61.2% waited for
  complications. Multivariable analysis identified predictors of better preventive
  behaviour, though the survey design limits causal inference. For dental students,
  the clinical implication is immediate: when a patient presents on a CCB, ask about
  gingival changes proactively and consider liaising with their cardiologist about
  drug substitution (e.g., switching to a non-dihydropyridine or an ACE inhibitor)
  if overgrowth is significant. The medical team is unlikely to raise it first.'
