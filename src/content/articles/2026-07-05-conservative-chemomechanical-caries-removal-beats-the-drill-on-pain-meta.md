summary: A PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis of 17 RCTs (1,306 children) confirms
  that chemomechanical caries removal in primary molars delivers significantly less
  pain than conventional drilling, with comparable caries removal efficacy. Child
  behaviour and satisfaction also favour CMCR. The evidence base is now large enough
  to support routine use in paediatric practice, particularly for anxious children.
summaryDeep: Chemomechanical caries removal — using agents like Carisolv or Papacárie
  to selectively dissolve infected dentine — has long been positioned as a gentler
  alternative to rotary instrumentation in children, but the evidence had been fragmented.
  This meta-analysis synthesises 17 RCTs covering 1,306 paediatric patients with carious
  primary molars, using GRADE assessment and Cochrane RoB 2.0. The pooled standardised
  mean difference for pain intensity strongly favours CMCR, and child behaviour scores
  and satisfaction ratings follow the same direction. Caries removal efficacy was
  comparable between groups, meaning CMCR is not sacrificing thoroughness for comfort.
  Operative time was longer with CMCR — a real-world consideration in a busy clinic
  — but for anxious or needle-phobic children, the trade-off is clearly worthwhile.
  GRADE confidence was rated moderate to high across primary outcomes. This is the
  kind of meta-analysis that should move CMCR from 'nice option' to 'default consideration'
  in paediatric caries management.
