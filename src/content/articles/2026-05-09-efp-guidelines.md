summaryDeep: |
  The S3-level update is the first major revision of the EFP's stage IV guideline since 2020. The downgrade isn't a rejection of regenerative surgery — it's a calibration. Real-world outcomes from registries in Germany, Italy, and the Nordics have shown wider variance than the trial data suggested, particularly for furcation defects beyond grade II.

  The new "may consider in selected cases" framing aligns with stronger evidence for non-surgical therapy outcomes in moderate stage IV disease, and clearer criteria for when surgical intervention is warranted (deep intrabony defects with residual pockets >6 mm after thorough non-surgical therapy, in compliant patients with controlled risk factors). Regenerative materials remain unchanged — the change is about patient selection, not technique.
excerptFull: |
  EFP S3-level Clinical Practice Guideline — Stage IV Periodontitis: 2026 Update.

  Background: The 2020 EFP guideline on stage IV periodontitis recommended regenerative surgery should be considered for intrabony defects ≥3 mm following non-surgical therapy. Subsequent registry data and pragmatic trials have suggested narrower indication windows than the recommendation implied.

  Method: GRADE-based S3 update following EFP methodology. Twelve panel members reviewed evidence from 2019–2025, including new RCT data, three large European registries (n>14,000 patients), and patient-reported outcome data.

  Key changes from 2020: (1) Regenerative surgery moved from "should consider" to "may consider in selected cases" for intrabony defects following thorough non-surgical therapy; (2) New explicit criteria for surgical candidacy: residual PD >6 mm at 3 months post-non-surgical, compliance with maintenance, controlled systemic risk factors; (3) Strengthened recommendation for individualized stepwise non-surgical re-instrumentation before surgical decision.

  Conclusion: The update reflects a more conservative threshold for regenerative surgery in stage IV disease, prioritizing patient selection and full non-surgical exhaustion before considering surgical intervention.
