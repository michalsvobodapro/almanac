summary: A retrospective review of 3,592 emergency appointments across 1,657 patients
  at the University of Oslo orthodontic clinic (2015–2022) found annual emergency
  numbers doubled over the period, with July consistently the quietest month. Operator

summaryDeep: Emergency appointments are a significant burden on postgraduate orthodontic
  clinics, yet their epidemiology is poorly characterised. This seven-year retrospective
  at Oslo screened 7,206 records and identified 3,592 qualifying emergencies across
  1,657 patients, with a mean of 2.2 emergencies per patient. The doubling of annual
  emergency numbers from 313 (2015) to 638 (2022) likely reflects clinic growth and
  case complexity rather than a true worsening of care quality, but the authors flag
  operator experience and training stage as key modifiers. The seasonal trough in
  July is predictable (reduced clinical activity) but statistically confirmed. Gender
  and age distributions were stable year-to-year. The most common emergency categories
  were active-treatment complications. For postgraduate programmes, this dataset is
  a useful benchmark — and a reminder that structured emergency protocols and supervision
  intensity matter more than patient selection for managing this workload.
