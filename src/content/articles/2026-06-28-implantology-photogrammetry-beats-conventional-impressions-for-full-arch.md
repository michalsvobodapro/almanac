summaryDeep: 'Full-arch implant impressions are among the most technically demanding
  procedures in prosthodontics, and cumulative error across multiple implants makes
  conventional techniques increasingly unreliable as implant number rises. This clinical
  cohort (10 maxillary, 10 mandibular arches; 4–8 implants each) compared a photogrammetric
  workflow — scan bodies captured with a dedicated photogrammetric camera, then merged
  with intraoral scan data — against the gold-standard two-step open-tray splinted
  PVS impression. Photogrammetry demonstrated better trueness; precision was assessed
  by repeat scanning in half the participants and found to be consistent. The study
  is small and non-randomized, which limits generalizability, but it aligns with a
  growing body of evidence favoring photogrammetry for spans beyond four implants.
  For students: if your clinic has photogrammetric capability, the evidence now supports
  preferring it over conventional impressions for full-arch cases — not just tolerating
  it as a novelty.'
