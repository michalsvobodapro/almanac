summary: A prospective calibration-validation study in 34 subjects shows that high-frequency
  intraoral ultrasound (40 MHz), calibrated with category-specific supracrestal tissue
  attachment values, can estimate probing depth within ±1 mm clinical acceptance limits.
  The technology offers a non-invasive, examiner-independent alternative to conventional
  probing — particularly relevant for monitoring periodontitis without repeated tissue
  trauma.
summaryDeep: 'Conventional periodontal probing is the gold standard for pocket depth
  measurement but is operator-dependent, uncomfortable, and causes micro-trauma that
  may confound longitudinal monitoring. This study used 40 MHz intraoral ultrasound
  — high enough resolution to distinguish the gingival margin, CEJ, and alveolar bone
  crest — across 955 annotated tooth-surface observations in healthy subjects and
  those with Stage II–IV periodontitis. The key methodological contribution is category-specific
  calibration: rather than applying a single STA correction factor, the authors derived
  separate values for healthy versus diseased sites, improving agreement with clinical
  probing depth. Bland-Altman analysis within the ±1 mm clinical acceptance limit
  is the right benchmark, and the validation set (234 observations, 7 subjects) is
  small but independent. Inter- and intra-rater reliability for ultrasound landmark
  annotation was high. This is the third ultrasound periodontal study in recent months,
  suggesting the technology is approaching clinical readiness — though a larger multicentre
  validation is needed before it displaces the probe.'
