excerpt: Background The aim of this study was to evaluate the technical quality of
  root canal fillings performed by undergraduate preclinical students and to interpret
  the findings according to tooth group and canal configuration. Methods This retrospective
  observational study included 1,050 root canal fillings of extracted teeth performed
  by 120 third-year dental students between 2019 and 2021. Canal preparation was performed
  using a standardized manual step-back instrumentation protocol, and obturation was
  performed using cold lateral condensation with gutta-percha cones and resin-based
  sealer. Standardized digital periapical radiographs were used to assess treatment
  quality based on length, homogeneity, and taper criteria. A scoring system (0, 1,
  or 2) was used for statistical analysis, and inter-examiner agreement was assessed

summaryDeep: 'Preclinical endodontic training is a high-stakes phase: the habits formed
  on extracted teeth translate directly to early clinical performance. This single-institution
  retrospective study assessed 1,050 root canal fillings across 120 third-year students
  using standardised digital periapical radiographs and a three-criterion scoring
  system (length, homogeneity, taper). Overall adequacy of 65.9% is broadly consistent
  with published benchmarks from other European dental schools, but the specialty-specific
  breakdown is more instructive: mandibular premolars achieved 82.6% adequacy while
  mandibular molars fell to 44.8%. The gap is driven by canal complexity — curved,
  narrow, and accessory canals in multi-rooted teeth exceed the technical ceiling
  of manual step-back instrumentation used in this curriculum. For dental educators,
  the implication is clear: more supervised molar practice, and potentially earlier
  introduction of rotary instrumentation, would target the most impactful deficit.
  For students, this is a realistic calibration of where the difficulty actually lies.'
