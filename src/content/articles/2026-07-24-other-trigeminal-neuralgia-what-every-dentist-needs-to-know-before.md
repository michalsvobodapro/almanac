summaryDeep: 'Trigeminal neuralgia presents with lancinating, unilateral facial pain
  that mimics toothache closely enough to trigger inappropriate dental treatment in
  a significant proportion of patients. This narrative review, drawing on PubMed literature
  from 2000–2025, synthesises current understanding of TN pathophysiology (predominantly
  neurovascular compression of CN V), diagnostic challenges, and the treatment ladder.
  Carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine remain first-line; surgical options including microvascular
  decompression offer durable relief in drug-refractory cases. The review explicitly
  targets dental practitioners as gatekeepers, noting that iterative clinical assessment
  over time — rather than a single appointment — is often necessary to distinguish
  TN from odontogenic pain. For a Prague dental student, this is essential differential
  diagnosis knowledge: the pattern of electric-shock pain triggered by light touch
  (trigger zones), absence of spontaneous dull ache, and normal pulp testing should
  raise immediate suspicion. Referring promptly to neurology rather than proceeding
  to endodontics or extraction is the correct response.'
