summaryDeep: 'Benjamin Tighe''s commentary makes a straightforward structural argument:
  hygienists and therapists see patients more frequently than GDPs, have extended
  one-to-one contact time, and are already assessing soft tissue and salivary markers
  that correlate with systemic disease. The piece calls for formalised screening protocols
  — blood pressure, HbA1c point-of-care testing, BMI — to be embedded in hygiene appointments.
  The systemic-link evidence underpinning the argument (perio–diabetes, perio–CVD)
  is well-established; what''s missing is the implementation research showing that
  hygienist-led screening actually changes patient outcomes at scale. As a news piece
  rather than a primary study, it carries no new data. Its value is as a policy-framing
  document at a moment when scope-of-practice debates are live across European dental
  regulators. For a Prague dental student, the parallel question is how the Czech
  dental team model compares — and whether ČSK has any appetite for similar scope
  expansion.'
