summaryDeep: Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis (SIP) lowers the pH around inflamed
  nerve fibres, impairing sodium-channel blockade and making standard IANB unreliable.
  This RCT assigned SIP patients to either the trypsin–bromelain–rutoside combination
  or placebo before root canal treatment and measured pulpal anaesthesia success (no
  or mild pain on access) plus postoperative pain scores. The premedication group
  achieved significantly higher anaesthetic success, likely because the proteolytic
  and anti-inflammatory agents attenuate the local inflammatory milieu that blunts
  local anaesthetics. Postoperative pain was also lower in the treatment arm. The
  trial is well-designed and addresses a genuine unmet need — most current rescue
  strategies (supplemental blocks, intraosseous injection) are technique-sensitive
  and add time. A systemic premedication taken before the appointment is operationally
  attractive. Limitations include the usual single-centre constraints; replication
  in larger multicentre cohorts is needed before this becomes a first-line protocol.
