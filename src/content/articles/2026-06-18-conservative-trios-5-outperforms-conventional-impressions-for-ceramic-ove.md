summaryDeep: 'Forty-eight sound maxillary first premolars were divided by preparation
  design (thin conventional vs. MODFL) and impression method (PVS, TRIOS 5, Medit
  i900), with marginal adaptation assessed by SEM after thermocycling (5,000 cycles).
  TRIOS 5 consistently achieved the lowest mean marginal gap across both designs,
  with statistically significant advantages over PVS and Medit i900 particularly in
  the more demanding MODFL geometry. All restorations were luted with Multilink Speed
  self-adhesive resin cement, controlling for the cementation variable. The in-vitro
  setting limits direct clinical translation — patient movement, saliva, and operator
  variability are absent — but the thermocycling protocol adds some aging realism.
  For students learning digital workflows, the key message is that scanner choice
  matters: not all intraoral scanners are equivalent, and preparation complexity amplifies
  the differences. The Medit i900, despite being a newer mid-range device, did not
  match TRIOS 5 accuracy here.'
