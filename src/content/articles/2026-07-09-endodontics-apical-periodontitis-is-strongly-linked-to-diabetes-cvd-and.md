  AP, chronic diseases, and smoking. A systematic literature search was conducted
  across major electronic databases from inception to February 12, 2026, to identify
  relevant meta-analyses. Eligible studies included meta-analyses assessing the association

summary: This umbrella review synthesizes 15 meta-analyses and finds robust associations
  between apical periodontitis (AP) and diabetes (OR 2.43), cardiovascular disease
  (OR 1.98), inflammatory bowel disease (OR 1.65), and smoking (OR 2.45). Methodological

  local infection.
summaryDeep: 'Apical periodontitis has long been suspected of systemic reach, but
  evidence was scattered across individual meta-analyses of variable quality. This
  umbrella review corrects that by pooling 15 meta-analyses through AMSTAR 2 quality
  appraisal and GRADE certainty ratings, covering studies up to February 2026. The
  headline numbers are striking: diabetes carries an OR of 2.43, cardiovascular disease
  an OR of 1.98 (and a separate RR of 1.32 for incidence), IBD an OR of 1.65, and
  smoking an OR of 2.45 — all statistically robust. The biological plausibility is
  well-established (bacteremia, systemic inflammatory mediators), but this review
  is the first to aggregate the aggregate evidence at scale. Clinically, the implication
  is that leaving periapical pathosis untreated in a diabetic or cardiac patient is
  no longer a defensible default. The authors appropriately flag residual confounding
  and causality limitations, but the direction and consistency of effect across conditions
  is hard to dismiss.'
