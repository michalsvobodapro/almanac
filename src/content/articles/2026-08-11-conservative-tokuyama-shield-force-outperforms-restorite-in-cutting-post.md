summaryDeep: 'Postoperative sensitivity after resin composite placement is a routine
  chairside complaint driven by incomplete dentinal tubule sealing, polymerization
  shrinkage, and microleakage. This RCT allocated 30 patients to control (no desensitizer),
  Restorite Desensitizer (Prime Dent), or Tokuyama Shield Force before adhesive placement.
  Both active agents reduced sensitivity compared to control, consistent with the
  hydrodynamic theory rationale for tubule occlusion. Tokuyama Shield Force — a fluoride-releasing,
  MDP-containing one-step agent — outperformed Restorite across the measured timepoints.
  The n=10 per group is the study''s main weakness: effect sizes may shift in a larger
  trial, and the follow-up duration is not specified in the excerpt. Nevertheless,
  for a student choosing between desensitizing agents before composite placement,
  this RCT provides the most direct comparative evidence currently available. The
  finding aligns with Shield Force''s known mechanism of forming a hybrid layer that
  physically occludes tubules rather than relying solely on precipitation.'
