summaryDeep: 'Mandibular molar distalization is an increasingly popular non-extraction
  strategy, but its feasibility depends critically on the available retromolar space
  — a dimension that varies substantially with skeletal pattern, vertical facial type,
  and third-molar status. This systematic review synthesised 14 CBCT/CT studies from
  seven countries (mean patient age 25.8 years) and found a consistent hierarchy:
  Class III > Class I > Class II for retromolar space. Normodivergent individuals
  had more space than hyperdivergent or hypodivergent patients. Importantly, the review
  found that third-molar angulation and its contact relationship with the second molar
  root — not simply whether a third molar is present — determines the effective distalization
  ceiling. Sex differences were inconsistent across studies. The clinical implication
  is that CBCT-based retromolar space assessment should be part of pre-treatment planning
  for any mandibular distalization case, and Class II hyperdivergent patients should
  be counselled that space may be a limiting factor.'
