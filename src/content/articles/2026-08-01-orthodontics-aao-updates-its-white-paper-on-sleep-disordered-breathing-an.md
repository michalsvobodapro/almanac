summaryDeep: 'Sleep-disordered breathing sits at an uncomfortable intersection for
  orthodontists: they are well-positioned to identify skeletal risk factors and manage
  dentofacial appliances, yet diagnosis and primary management of OSA belongs to sleep
  medicine physicians. The 2024 AAO task force update to the 2019 white paper addresses
  this tension directly, clarifying that orthodontists should screen, refer, and collaborate
  — not diagnose or treat OSA independently. The Chinese interpretation published
  here contextualizes the white paper for a non-US clinical environment, noting differences
  in referral pathways and appliance availability. For European and Czech students,
  the relevance is the underlying framework: orthodontists have a defined, evidence-supported
  role in SDB management that goes beyond simply avoiding treatment that worsens airway
  dimensions. Mandibular advancement, arch expansion, and orthognathic planning all
  intersect with airway outcomes. This guideline update is the clearest statement
  yet of where orthodontic responsibility begins and ends in the SDB pathway.'
