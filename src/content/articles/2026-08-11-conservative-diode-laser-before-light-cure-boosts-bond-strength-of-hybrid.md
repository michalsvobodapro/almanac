summary: An in-vitro study on 20 molars found that 940 nm diode laser irradiation
  applied after adhesive placement but before light polymerisation significantly improved
  micro-tensile bond strength of hybrid ceramic inlays — when using a two-step self-etch
  adhesive. The benefit did not extend to the one-step universal adhesive, pointing
  to an adhesive-specific interaction.
summaryDeep: Hybrid ceramics (resin-ceramic CAD/CAM blocks) are increasingly popular
  for chairside inlays, but bond durability to dentin remains a concern, especially
  after aging. This study tested whether a 940 nm diode laser applied post-adhesive
  and pre-polymerisation could enhance micro-tensile bond strength (µTBS), comparing
  Clearfil SE Bond 2 (two-step self-etch) and Clearfil S3 Bond Universal (one-step).
  After accelerated aging in 10% NaOCl, laser irradiation significantly increased
  µTBS only in the two-step group. The one-step universal adhesive showed no laser
  benefit — likely because its simplified chemistry and solvent content interact differently
  with laser energy. The n=5 per subgroup is very small, limiting statistical confidence,
  and the NaOCl aging model is aggressive and non-standard. Still, for practitioners
  already using two-step self-etch systems with hybrid ceramic inlays, the finding
  suggests a potentially useful adjunct. Replication in larger studies with ISO-standard
  aging is needed before changing protocol.
