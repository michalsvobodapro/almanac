excerpt: 'Introduction Accurate estimation of legal adulthood in undocumented individuals
  remains a major challenge in forensic and medico-legal contexts. This study evaluates
  mandibular condylar cortication and third molar apical closure assessed by cone
  beam computed tomography (CBCT) as potential indicators of age of majority. A retrospective
  observational pilot study was conducted on 142 individuals aged 13-25 years from
  six geographic regions (Spain, Uruguay, Peru, Mexico, Venezuela and Colombia). Two
  radiological variables were analyzed: the presence of cortical bone in the mandibular
  condyle and apical closure of the mandibular third molars. Associations with chronological
  age were explored descriptively, considering sex and geographic origin. Results
  Mandibular condylar cortication was identified exclusively in individuals aged 18
  years or older. It showed 89.2% sensitivity, 100% specificity, 100% positive predictive
  value, and 89.5% negative predictive value for identifying legal adulthood. Complete
  third molar apical closure showed lower sensitivity (64.7%) and specificity of 96.6%.
  Both markers were significantly associated with legal adulthood (Fisher''s exact
  test, p Conclusion Within the limitations of this pilot study, mandibular condylar
  cortication assessed by CBCT appears to be a promising indicator of legal adulthood,
  potentially offering higher specificity than third molar apical closure. Further
  large-scale studies are required to validate these findings.'

summaryDeep: 'Forensic age estimation in undocumented individuals is a medico-legal
  challenge with real human rights stakes, and third molar development has long been
  the go-to radiographic marker despite its well-known limitations. This pilot study
  proposes mandibular condylar cortication on CBCT as a complementary or superior
  indicator. In 142 individuals from Spain, Uruguay, Peru, Mexico, Venezuela, and
  Colombia, cortical bone in the mandibular condyle was never observed in those under
  18 — giving 100% positive predictive value for adulthood. Sensitivity of 89.2% means
  some adults will be missed, but the specificity advantage over third molar closure
  (100% vs 96.6%) is clinically significant in a legal context where false positives
  carry serious consequences. Limitations are substantial: pilot study, retrospective,
  geographic diversity without stratified analysis, and CBCT radiation exposure is
  not trivial for a screening purpose. Validation in larger, ethnically diverse cohorts
  is essential before forensic adoption.'
