summaryDeep: 'Restoring root canal-treated molars is one of the most consequential
  and contested decisions in daily practice. This cross-sectional vignette survey
  adapted a validated UK instrument for a Jordanian cohort of 364 dentists (roughly
  half GDPs, half specialists). Three clinical scenarios varied remaining wall count
  and crack presence: crowns dominated in scenarios with one remaining wall or a crack,
  onlays when three walls remained. Zirconia was the preferred indirect material regardless
  of scenario. Critically, remaining walls, ferrule, marginal ridge loss, and tooth
  position were the dominant decision drivers — but periodontal status and lateral
  guidance were rarely considered, suggesting a blind spot in how clinicians integrate
  occlusal risk into restorative planning. No significant differences emerged between
  GDPs and specialists, which is either reassuring (consensus exists) or concerning
  (specialists aren''t adding value). The survey design cannot establish causality
  or outcomes, but it maps the decision landscape usefully for educators and curriculum
  designers.'
