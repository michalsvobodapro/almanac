summaryDeep: This NMA pooled RCTs comparing autologous blood-derived products against
  open flap debridement in intrabony periodontal defects, assessing outcomes at 6,
  9, and 12 months using SUCRA ranking probabilities and CINeMA certainty grading.
  PRF-based protocols consistently ranked highest across all three clinical endpoints,
  with the 9-month window showing the most pronounced separation from OFD. Combinations
  of PRF with alendronate or rosuvastatin achieved the highest SUCRA scores and may
  offer additive regenerative benefit — but these adjuncts are not standard-of-care
  drugs in periodontics, and the certainty of evidence for those specific combinations
  is variable. Crucially, statistically significant superiority was not uniformly
  demonstrated at 12 months, meaning the long-term durability advantage of PRF over
  conventional surgery remains an open question. For a student or clinician choosing
  between regenerative options, PRF alone is the most defensible first-line blood
  derivative; adjunctive drug combinations are hypothesis-generating, not yet practice-changing.
