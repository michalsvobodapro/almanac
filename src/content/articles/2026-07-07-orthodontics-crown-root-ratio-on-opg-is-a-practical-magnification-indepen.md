summaryDeep: 'External apical root resorption is a well-known orthodontic complication
  that is routinely underreported because quantification is cumbersome. This retrospective
  study used WebCeph software to measure crown and root lengths on pre- and post-treatment
  digital OPGs in 75 non-extraction fixed appliance patients aged 15–30 years. Post-treatment
  crown-root ratios were significantly higher than pre-treatment for all teeth in
  both sexes, confirming EARR occurred universally in this cohort. The magnification-independence
  of the ratio is its key practical advantage over absolute length measurements, which
  require calibration and are sensitive to projection geometry. Limitations include
  retrospective design, OPG-only imaging (no periapical or CBCT confirmation), and
  a relatively short treatment duration (12–18 months). For a student, the takeaway
  is methodological: when you need to document or discuss EARR with a patient or supervisor,
  crown-root ratio from a standard OPG is a defensible, accessible metric.'
