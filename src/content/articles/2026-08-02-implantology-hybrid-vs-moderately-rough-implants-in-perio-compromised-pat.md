excerpt: Intro To compare clinical conditions and the implant marginal bone level
  after 1 year following placement of hybrid and moderate rough implants in the mandible
  of periodontally compromised patients using standardized radiographs. Methods In
  a multicenter study (three centers), thirty-nine periodontally compromised patients
  were randomly treated in a split-mouth study with both hybrid and moderate rough
  implants in the mandible at the premolar and molar site. In each patient, two tissue-level
  implants were inserted, and a healing cap was placed. After 3 months, the implants
  were restored with a FPD. The primary outcome consisted of radiographic assessments
  performed at 1-year follow-up. Results Thirty-six patients completed the study.
  Both types of implants showed a survival rate of 94.4% ± 3.8% (95% CI 87.0%-100%);
  all lost implants failed to achieve osseointegration. There is no significant difference
  found in the change of the bone level over time within the first year between the
  two groups (p = 0.25 for mesial pocket bone, p = 0.99 for distal pocket bone, p
  = 0.98 for mesial proximal bone and p = 0.68 for distal proximal bone). Conclusion
  After one year, no statistically significant radiographic and clinical differences
  were observed between hybrid and moderately rough implants. However, peri-implant
  diseases often manifest after a longer period. This underscores the importance of
  extended follow-up. A subsequent evaluation is planned at the three-year mark.

summaryDeep: Surface topography is one of the most marketed variables in implantology,
  yet head-to-head RCT data in high-risk populations are scarce. This multicenter
  split-mouth trial placed one hybrid and one moderately rough tissue-level implant
  in the mandible of each of 39 periodontally compromised patients, restoring them
  with fixed partial dentures after 3 months. At 1 year, both surface types showed
  94.4% survival — all failures were early osseointegration losses — and mesial and
  distal bone level changes were statistically indistinguishable across all four measurement
  points (p ranging 0.25–0.99). The split-mouth design elegantly controls for patient-level
  confounders like systemic disease and oral hygiene compliance. The honest limitation
  is that peri-implantitis, the outcome that surface roughness is theorised to influence
  most, typically emerges beyond year one. The planned 3-year evaluation is the one
  to watch; for now, surface choice in this population can be driven by cost and availability
  rather than evidence of superiority.
