summary: Aging and frailty alter bone quality, occlusal force patterns, and maintenance
  capacity in ways that standard implant protocols don't account for. This Periodontology
  2000 review maps the prosthetic and biomechanical adaptations — from cantilever
  limits to implant number — that evidence supports for older cohorts.
summaryDeep: 'As populations age, implant candidates increasingly present with sarcopenia,
  polypharmacy, reduced manual dexterity, and compromised bone density — none of which
  are adequately addressed by protocols developed in younger, healthier trial populations.
  This review synthesises evidence on how frailty indices should inform treatment
  planning: fewer cantilevers, preference for splinted over individual crowns, lower
  occlusal table width, and simplified hygiene architectures. Bone metabolism changes
  under common geriatric medications (bisphosphonates, corticosteroids, anticoagulants)
  are addressed with specific prosthetic workarounds. The authors also flag that implant
  survival data in frail patients is still sparse, meaning clinical judgment must
  bridge the evidence gap. For a Prague student heading into a practice that will
  see an increasingly elderly Czech population, this review is a practical framework
  for a patient group you''ll encounter far more than the textbooks suggest. The biomechanical
  reasoning is transferable to any compromised-host scenario.'
