summaryDeep: Reciprocating NiTi instrumentation has become a mainstream approach in
  root canal preparation, but the market is fragmented across systems with meaningfully
  different taper designs, heat treatments, and cross-sectional geometries. This study
  tested 441 new instruments across seven systems — one reduced-taper reciprocating
  design versus six commercially available alternatives — measuring geometric characteristics,
  metallurgical properties (phase transformation temperatures, surface hardness),
  cyclic fatigue resistance, and torsional strength. The reduced-taper instrument
  demonstrated favourable flexibility and cyclic fatigue resistance, particularly
  in simulated curved canals, without sacrificing torsional performance. For students
  learning to select instrumentation systems, the study reinforces that taper is a
  primary determinant of both canal transportation risk and file fracture susceptibility
  — and that reduced-taper designs merit serious consideration in curved posterior
  canals rather than being dismissed as underpreparing.
