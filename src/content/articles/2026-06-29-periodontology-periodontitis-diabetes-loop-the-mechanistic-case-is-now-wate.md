  drives periodontal destruction, while periodontal inflammation impairs glycaemic

summaryDeep: 'This review synthesises the mechanistic underpinnings of the periodontitis–diabetes
  bidirectional relationship, one of the most replicated oral-systemic associations
  in the literature. Hyperglycaemia promotes advanced glycation end-product (AGE)
  accumulation in periodontal tissues, amplifies pro-inflammatory cytokine cascades
  (IL-1β, TNF-α), and selects for a dysbiotic subgingival microbiome — all of which
  accelerate attachment loss. In the reverse direction, the chronic systemic inflammatory
  burden from periodontitis elevates HbA1c and blunts insulin signalling. The review
  maps each pathway with molecular specificity, providing a framework for understanding
  why periodontal intervention trials show modest but consistent HbA1c reductions.
  For the Prague dental student, the practical implication is straightforward: every
  patient with type 2 diabetes on your chair is a periodontal risk patient, and every
  severe periodontitis case warrants a glucose screening conversation with their GP.'
