  characterized by blister formation, skin fragility, and mucocutaneous blisters and
  erosions following minimal trauma. Oral manifestations and dental involvement of
  EB vary in frequency and severity depending on the subtype. Dental management of
  children with EB is complex due to the high risk of iatrogenic soft-tissue injury
  and requires strict adherence to evidence-based preventive and minimally invasive
  protocols. Published clinical practice guidelines recommend a trauma-free approach,
  reinforced preventive strategies, careful behavioral management, and modifications
  of dental instruments and procedures to minimize friction and pressure on oral tissues.

summaryDeep: Dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (DEB) is a rare genodermatosis where
  minor friction causes mucocutaneous blistering — making routine dental procedures
  genuinely hazardous. This case describes a 6-year-old girl managed under local anaesthesia
  alone (2% lidocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine), with instrument modifications to
  minimise oral tissue trauma, panoramic imaging adapted for limited mouth opening,
  and staged restorative treatment prioritising the least invasive approach. The accompanying
  evidence review synthesises published clinical practice guidelines, which consistently
  recommend trauma-free technique, fluoride-intensive prevention, and avoidance of
  rubber dam clamps or retraction cord in standard form. The case report format limits
  generalisability, but EB is rare enough that detailed clinical implementation reports
  are genuinely valuable for practitioners who encounter these patients unprepared.
  For dental students, this is a useful template for adapting standard protocols to
  medically complex paediatric patients — the principles (minimise friction, maximise
  prevention, stage treatment) transfer broadly.
