summaryDeep: External root resorption (ERR) adjacent to impacted third molars is underdiagnosed
  on periapical and panoramic radiographs because the lesions are typically on the
  distal surface of the second molar, hidden by the impacted crown. This meta-analysis
  pooled 26 CBCT studies (24 rated low risk of bias) and found a 31% pooled prevalence
  of ERR — far higher than clinically apparent rates. The key risk factors are impaction
  angulation (mesioangular, horizontal, or transverse) and direct contact between
  the impacted crown and the apical third of the second molar root. Vertical impactions
  and contact limited to the coronal or middle root third carry substantially lower
  risk. For clinical decision-making, these data support CBCT assessment when panoramic
  imaging shows a mesioangular or horizontal impaction in close proximity to the second
  molar, and they strengthen the case for earlier prophylactic extraction in such
  configurations before resorption becomes advanced.
