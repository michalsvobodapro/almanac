summaryDeep: 'The 2018 EFP/AAP classification introduced a staging and grading framework
  intended to reflect disease severity and complexity, but its microbiological correlates
  have been incompletely characterised outside Western populations. This Thai cohort
  study used 16S rRNA sequencing of subgingival plaque from 100 participants spanning
  the full disease spectrum — gingivitis, and Stages I through IV periodontitis —
  and found that microbial diversity and community composition shifted progressively
  with stage. Predicted functional profiles, including virulence-associated metabolic
  pathways, escalated in parallel. The cross-sectional design limits causal inference,
  but the consistency across stages supports the biological validity of the classification.
  For students learning to apply the 2018 classification clinically, this study provides
  the microbiological backbone: when you assign a stage, you are implicitly describing
  a microbial ecosystem, not just a pocket depth.'
