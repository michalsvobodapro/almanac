summaryDeep: 'Precision periodontology promises to tailor diagnosis and treatment
  to individual molecular and genetic profiles, but this narrative review — prioritising
  meta-analyses, systematic reviews, and guideline documents from 2010–2025 — finds
  the gap between research and chairside reality remains wide. Subgingival microbiome
  profiling has defined but narrow clinical indications; single-SNP genotyping (including
  IL-1 polymorphisms) has not demonstrated clinical utility commensurate with cost.
  Among host-response biomarkers, aMMP-8 point-of-care testing is the most extensively
  studied and may add value in selected monitoring and peri-implant scenarios, but
  evidence is insufficient for routine implementation. CBCT is justified when 3D data
  directly influence surgical decisions — not as a screening tool. The review proposes
  a conceptual decision-support framework linking diagnostic outputs to therapeutic
  actions, which is useful for structuring how you think about these tools even before
  they reach routine practice. For students, the key message is critical appraisal:
  precision tools are not yet a replacement for thorough clinical examination and
  BPE/full periodontal charting.'
