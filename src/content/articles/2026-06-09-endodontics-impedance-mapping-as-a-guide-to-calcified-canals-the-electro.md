summary: Calcified root canal systems defeat both visual and radiographic navigation.
  This paper lays out the biological and electrochemical rationale for using impedance
  patterns to localise pulp chambers and canal orifices — and introduces a conceptual
  model for reading impedance signatures at the dentine–gutta-percha interface in
  retreatment cases.
summaryDeep: 'When calcification obliterates the pulp chamber, clinicians are essentially
  drilling blind, relying on tactile feedback and cone-beam geometry that may itself
  be ambiguous. This paper argues that electrochemical impedance spectroscopy can
  discriminate between calcified dentine, residual pulp tissue, and gutta-percha based
  on their distinct electrical properties — providing a real-time signal that conventional
  apex locators do not offer. The biological foundations section is the strongest
  part: it maps the ionic conductance differences between vital pulp remnants and
  mineralised tissue, explaining why impedance contrast should exist even in heavily
  calcified systems. The conceptual application to retreatment — detecting the dentine–GP
  interface coronally — is novel and clinically provocative, though it remains a theoretical
  framework without prospective clinical data yet. Read this alongside the DeepSDF
  paper: together they represent two complementary strategies (predictive geometry
  vs. real-time electrochemical sensing) converging on the same unsolved problem.'
