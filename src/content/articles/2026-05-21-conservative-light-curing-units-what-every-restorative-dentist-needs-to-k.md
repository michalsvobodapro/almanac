summaryDeep: 'Light-curing units are among the most used — and most misunderstood
  — devices in restorative dentistry. This BDJ Open review systematically works through
  the variables that determine whether a composite actually reaches adequate conversion:
  irradiance (mW/cm²), spectral match to photoinitiator absorption peaks (camphorquinone
  vs. alternative initiators in bulk-fill and clear composites), tip diameter, and
  the often-ignored effect of curing distance. The authors evaluate newer optical
  functions — ramped intensity, pulse-delay, and multi-peak LED arrays — against the
  clinical evidence rather than manufacturer claims. Key take-away: many in-practice
  LCUs underperform their rated output due to tip contamination and age-related LED
  degradation, and periodic radiometric verification is not optional. For a student
  rotating through restorative clinics, this is the single reference to bookmark before
  touching a curing light.'
