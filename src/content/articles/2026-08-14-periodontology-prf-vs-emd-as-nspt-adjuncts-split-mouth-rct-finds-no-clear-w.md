summaryDeep: Platelet-rich fibrin and enamel matrix derivative are both widely used
  regenerative adjuncts to scaling and root planing, but direct comparative evidence
  has been scarce. This split-mouth RCT addresses that gap by allocating interproximal
  sites within the same patient to either PRF (prepared by horizontal centrifugation)
  or EMD (preceded by EDTA conditioning), measuring PD, CAL, gingival margin position,
  and BOP at baseline and six months. Both biomaterials produced statistically significant
  improvements across all measured parameters, and the between-group differences were
  not significant — meaning neither material demonstrated superiority. The split-mouth
  design elegantly controls for patient-level confounders, but n=13 completers is
  underpowered for detecting anything but large effect sizes. For a student or clinician
  choosing between these adjuncts, the practical implication is that PRF — cheaper,
  autologous, and requiring no cold-chain logistics — may be the pragmatic default
  when the evidence doesn't favour EMD's higher cost. Longer follow-up and larger
  trials are needed before this becomes a guideline-level statement.
