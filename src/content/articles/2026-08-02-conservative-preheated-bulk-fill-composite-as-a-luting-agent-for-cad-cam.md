  their effects on marginal integrity and mechanical performance of inlays remain

summaryDeep: 'Conventional dual-cure resin cements dominate CAD/CAM inlay cementation,
  but preheating resin composites to 60–68 °C reduces viscosity and may improve marginal
  adaptation without the added cost of dedicated luting systems. This study divided
  40 extracted mandibular molars into four groups: dual-cure cement, preheated regular
  composite, preheated bulk-fill composite, and intact controls. Standardised Class
  II MOD inlay preparations were restored with resin nano-ceramic CAD/CAM blocks,
  thermocycled, and subjected to chewing simulation. Marginal gap formation was quantified
  by micro-CT — a more sensitive method than conventional SEM — and fracture strength
  was measured on a universal testing machine. Both preheated groups performed comparably
  to the dual-cure cement on marginal integrity and fracture resistance. The study
  is limited by its in-vitro design (no pulpal pressure, no salivary contamination)
  and single composite brand, so clinical translation requires caution. Nevertheless,
  for practices already stocking bulk-fill composites, preheating before inlay cementation
  is a low-barrier protocol change worth piloting.'
