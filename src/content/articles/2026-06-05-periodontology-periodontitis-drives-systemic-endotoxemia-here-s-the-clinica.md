summaryDeep: 'The endotoxemia hypothesis — that LPS from gram-negative periodontal
  pathogens translocates through inflamed sulcular epithelium into circulation — has
  long been invoked to explain links between periodontitis and cardiovascular disease,
  diabetes, and adverse pregnancy outcomes, but direct clinical measurement has been
  sparse. This study measured LPS activity in both subgingival plaque samples and
  matched serum samples, giving a paired microbiological-and-systemic picture rarely
  seen in perio research. The findings confirm that higher subgingival LPS burden
  correlates with detectable serum endotoxemia, lending biological plausibility to
  the systemic complication narrative. Clinically, this matters because it shifts
  the conversation from ''perio affects systemic health somehow'' to a quantifiable,
  mechanism-driven argument you can use when counselling patients with cardiovascular
  or metabolic comorbidities. It also raises the stakes for thorough debridement:
  reducing the subgingival reservoir is not just about probing depths, it''s about
  lowering a systemic inflammatory load. Expect this kind of paired biomarker design
  to become the methodological standard for perio-systemic studies going forward.'
