summaryDeep: 'Panoramic radiograph interpretation is a core clinical skill that is
  notoriously undertaught in formal curricula. This study gave 60 third-year students
  access to a self-paced mobile app covering 61 anatomical landmarks, 20 intraosseous
  pathologies, and 26 dental material appearances across two 30-minute sessions. Pre-
  and post-training scores were compared using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test; the
  mean score rose from 65.92 to 80.33, and normalized learning gain (Hake g) was calculated
  to quantify improvement magnitude. The absence of a randomized control group means
  we cannot rule out test-retest effects or motivated-student bias. Still, the magnitude
  of gain in under an hour of self-directed learning is striking and suggests that
  targeted digital tools can efficiently address specific competency gaps. For a student
  in Prague, this is immediately actionable: the app is mobile-compatible and the
  learning content maps directly to what is examined in Czech dental curricula.'
