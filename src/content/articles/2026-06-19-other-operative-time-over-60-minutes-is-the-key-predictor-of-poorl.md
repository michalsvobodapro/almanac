summaryDeep: 'This prospective observational study of 307 mandibular third molar surgeries
  collected perioperative variables and pain intensity at rest and during movement
  over seven days, using multilevel mixed-effects modelling and Kaplan-Meier analysis
  for time to pain resolution. PPP incidence was 15.3% — higher than many clinicians
  assume. The only statistically independent predictor in multivariable analysis was
  operative time >60 minutes (aOR 3.95, 95% CI 1.29–12.03), which is a surgically
  modifiable variable. Peak pain occurred at 6 hours post-operatively, with 20% reduction
  by 12 hours. Tender, throbbing, and aching descriptors dominated the first 24 hours.
  For a student entering oral surgery rotations, the message is direct: surgical efficiency
  matters not just for theatre throughput but for patient welfare. Preoperative difficulty
  assessment (angulation, depth, root morphology) should inform whether a case is
  within your current skill level or requires referral to a more experienced operator.'
