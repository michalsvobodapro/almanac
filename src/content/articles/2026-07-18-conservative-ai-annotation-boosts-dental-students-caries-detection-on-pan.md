excerpt: Dental caries remains one of the most prevalent oral diseases globally. The
  integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into dental radiographic interpretation,
  particularly for caries detection, has expanded rapidly. AI-assisted caries annotation
  may help dental students identify carious lesions on panoramic radiographs-a commonly
  used diagnostic tool for evaluating teeth and surrounding structures-thereby improving
  diagnostic accuracy, efficiency, and confidence. This study aimed to assess the
  effect of AI-assisted caries annotation on dental students' diagnostic performance,
  confidence, and time efficiency in detecting caries on panoramic radiographs. Fifty
  panoramic radiographs with multistage carious lesions, verified by bite-wing radiographs
  as the gold standard, were randomly selected. Caries were annotated using recently
  developed AI-assisted software. Forty fourth-year dental students participated after
  calibration with ten sets of unannotated and AI-annotated radiographs. In Session
  1, participants identified carious lesions on 40 unannotated panoramic radiographs.

summaryDeep: 'Using 50 panoramic radiographs with bite-wing-verified carious lesions
  as ground truth, researchers had 40 fourth-year students assess the same 40 films
  twice — first unaided, then one month later with AI annotation. The within-subject
  design controls for learning effects but cannot fully separate AI benefit from simple
  re-reading. Despite that caveat, sensitivity and balanced accuracy improved meaningfully
  with AI support, and students reported higher diagnostic confidence and shorter
  reading times. The finding matters for a Prague dental student specifically: AI-assisted
  radiograph reading is already entering clinical workflows, and understanding both
  its benefits and its failure modes (false positives, lesion depth misclassification)
  is becoming a core competency. The authors rightly note that AI should augment,
  not replace, clinical judgment — a point echoed by a companion methodological review
  published the same day on AI caries prognostic models.'
