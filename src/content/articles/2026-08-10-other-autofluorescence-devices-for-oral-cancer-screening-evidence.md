  present persistent diagnostic challenges, with late detection driving poor prognosis

  and OralID have been developed to complement conventional oral examination (COE),

  This scoping review maps the available evidence on GOCCLES and OralID, characterising
  study populations, clinical contexts, and reported diagnostic outcomes. Methods

summary: A PRISMA-ScR scoping review of 13 studies (577 participants) evaluating GOCCLES
  and OralID for oral cancer and potentially malignant disorder screening finds highly
  variable diagnostic performance across populations and clinical contexts. Neither

  conventional oral examination. The evidence base is small, heterogeneous, and largely
  observational.
summaryDeep: 'Oral squamous cell carcinoma carries a poor prognosis largely because
  of late detection, and autofluorescence devices have been marketed as a chairside
  adjunct to improve early identification of oral potentially malignant disorders
  and OSCC. This scoping review searched five databases from 1996 to June 2026, identifying
  13 eligible studies (6 on GOCCLES, 7 on OralID) across 577 participants in seven
  countries. Study designs ranged from clinical trials to case reports, and populations
  were heterogeneous — mixed OPMD/OSCC cohorts, OPMD-only, OSCC-only, and other groups.
  Reported diagnostic performance varied substantially, and no formal critical appraisal
  was undertaken per JBI scoping methodology. The review maps the evidence landscape
  rather than synthesising pooled estimates, which is appropriate given the heterogeneity
  but limits actionable conclusions. For a student in Prague: these devices are present
  in some practices and marketed aggressively, but the evidence does not yet support
  recommending them over a thorough conventional oral mucosal examination. The priority
  remains knowing what to look for, not which device to use.'
