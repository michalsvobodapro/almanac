summaryDeep: 'This retrospective study compared clear aligner mandibular advancement
  (MA, n=33) with the Activator (n=37) in growing patients with skeletal Class II
  malocclusion, using both 2D lateral cephalograms and 3D facial photographs (3dMD
  system) for outcome assessment. Point-to-point 3D superimposition across seven soft
  tissue regions showed that all regions except the upper lip increased significantly
  from pre- to post-treatment in both groups. MA produced 2.57° greater correction
  of soft tissue convexity and 0.97 mm more chin advancement (sl-VL) than the Activator.
  Dentally, MA achieved significantly greater upper incisor retraction (U1-SN: 5.57°
  vs 3.51°) and overjet reduction (3.49 mm vs 2.25 mm). The retrospective design and
  lack of randomisation are the main limitations — group allocation was not random,
  and baseline differences may not be fully controlled. Nevertheless, the 3D facial
  imaging methodology is a genuine methodological advance over purely cephalometric
  comparisons. For students, the takeaway is that MA aligners are not simply a cosmetic
  alternative to functional appliances — they produce measurable skeletal and soft
  tissue changes in growing patients, with some advantages over the Activator.'
