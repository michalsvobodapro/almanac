summaryDeep: 'Combined endodontic-periodontal lesions are notoriously difficult to
  debride completely, and this single-center retrospective controlled study is the
  largest to date testing water laser as an adjunct. Of 634 teeth followed for 12
  ± 3 months, 295 received laser-assisted treatment and 339 conventional therapy;
  propensity score overlap weighting balanced baseline differences. The laser group
  showed statistically significant improvements across all pre-specified outcomes
  — probing depth, clinical attachment level, BOP, radiographic healing, and acute
  exacerbation rate — with an adverse-event profile meeting noninferiority. The subgroup
  with baseline PD ≥6 mm drove the largest gains, which makes clinical sense: deeper
  pockets are precisely where conventional instruments struggle. Limitations include
  retrospective design, single center, and a relatively short follow-up. Still, the
  effect sizes are clinically meaningful and the sample size is unusually large for
  this niche. Clinicians managing true endo-perio lesions with deep pocketing have
  a reasonable evidence base to consider laser adjunction.'
