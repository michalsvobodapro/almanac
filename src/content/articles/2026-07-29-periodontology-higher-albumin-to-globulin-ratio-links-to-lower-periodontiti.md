summary: 'A cross-sectional analysis of 10,094 NHANES participants shows an L-shaped
  association between albumin-to-globulin ratio (AGR) and periodontitis: above an
  AGR of 1.79, each unit increase cuts periodontitis odds by 63%. A Mendelian randomisation

summaryDeep: AGR is a cheap, routinely available inflammatory and nutritional index
  derived from standard serum protein electrophoresis. The NHANES cross-sectional
  data are compelling in scale and adjustment depth, with restricted cubic splines
  revealing a nonlinear threshold effect rather than a simple linear dose-response.
  The effect was strongest in adults over 60. But the Mendelian randomisation — which
  uses genetic variants as instruments to approximate a natural experiment — failed
  to substantiate a causal pathway, suggesting that AGR is a marker of systemic health
  rather than a driver of periodontal disease. For the clinician, this means AGR is
  not a screening tool for periodontitis risk, and improving nutrition will not predictably
  reduce periodontal inflammation without addressing local factors. The finding does,
  however, add to the growing literature linking systemic inflammatory burden to periodontal
  status.
