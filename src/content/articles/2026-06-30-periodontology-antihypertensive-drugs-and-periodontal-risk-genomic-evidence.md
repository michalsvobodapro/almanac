summaryDeep: 'Antihypertensives are among the most widely prescribed drug classes
  globally, yet their specific effects on periodontal health have been difficult to
  disentangle from the underlying cardiovascular disease and its shared risk factors.
  This study applies Mendelian randomisation — using genetic variants as instruments
  for drug target activity — to estimate the causal oral health impact of different
  antihypertensive classes. The genomic design substantially reduces confounding and
  reverse causation compared to standard observational studies. Results indicate that
  not all antihypertensive classes carry the same periodontal risk profile; some appear
  protective, others potentially harmful. The practical implication for dental students
  is real: when reviewing a new patient''s medication list, the specific antihypertensive
  class matters, not just the presence of hypertension. Gingival overgrowth with calcium
  channel blockers is already textbook, but this study hints at subtler, systemic
  periodontal effects worth tracking in long-term patients. Larger replication studies
  are needed before class-specific counselling changes.'
