summary: In an in-vitro trial of 76 endodontically treated mandibular premolars, the
  sandwich technique (glass-ionomer base + composite) outperformed both Nayyar core
  composite and semi-direct composite onlay for fracture resistance. The semi-direct
  onlay — increasingly popular for its cuspal coverage — actually performed worst,
  significantly below even the Nayyar core. Lab data, but the margins are clinically
  meaningful.
summaryDeep: 'Restoring endodontically treated posterior teeth is one of the most
  consequential decisions in restorative dentistry, and mandibular premolars are particularly
  fracture-prone due to their narrow buccolingual dimension. This study loaded 76
  extracted premolars to fracture after standardised RCT and one of three restorative
  protocols: sandwich technique (GIC base + composite), Nayyar core composite, or
  semi-direct composite onlay. Mean fracture resistance was 7.50 kN for sandwich,
  6.10 kN for Nayyar, and 4.94 kN for semi-direct onlay — the latter significantly
  lower than both the sandwich group and intact controls. The semi-direct onlay''s
  inferior performance is counterintuitive given its cuspal-coverage rationale, and
  the authors attribute it to the adhesive interface and polymerisation dynamics outside
  the mouth. Limitations are the usual in-vitro caveats: no fatigue cycling, no periodontal
  ligament simulation. Still, for a student choosing a restorative approach after
  RCT on a premolar with moderate coronal loss, the sandwich technique has the strongest
  bench data here.'
