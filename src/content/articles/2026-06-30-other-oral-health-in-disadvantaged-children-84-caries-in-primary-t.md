summaryDeep: 'This WHO-methodology cross-sectional survey examined 343 children aged
  6–16 in a socioeconomically disadvantaged district of Central Anatolia, recording
  DMFT/dmft indices, gingival bleeding, and mucosal lesions. The primary caries prevalence
  of 84.2% is striking; the permanent dentition figure of 37.6% signals that the disease
  burden is not self-limiting as children age. More than half had gingival bleeding,
  indicating widespread gingivitis. Urgency stratification showed nearly a quarter
  of children needed immediate treatment and 9% required emergency intervention —
  a significant unmet need. Age, gender, school type, and nationality were all significantly
  associated with oral health status. The study is descriptive and single-region,
  limiting generalisability, but the WHO methodology enables cross-country comparison.
  For a Prague student, the relevance is both clinical empathy and public health literacy:
  these patterns are not unique to Türkiye, and understanding the social determinants
  of oral disease is increasingly part of dental education.'
