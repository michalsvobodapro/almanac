summaryDeep: 'Vertical alveolar ridge augmentation remains one of the most technically
  demanding and unpredictable procedures in implant dentistry, with outcomes heavily
  dependent on surgeon experience and membrane adaptation. This case series introduces
  a workflow where CBCT and intraoral scans are merged to simulate the ideal implant
  position, and membrane-cutting guides are 3D-printed to shape dense PTFE membranes
  to the planned augmentation volume. At 9-month CBCT reassessment across 15 sites,
  mean vertical bone height increased from 15.70 mm to 19.96 mm, and volumetric gain
  (755 mm³) matched planned volume (757 mm³) with no statistically significant difference
  and a strong Spearman correlation. The case series design (n=15, no control group)
  limits causal inference, and 9-month follow-up does not capture long-term implant
  outcomes. But the proof-of-concept is compelling: digital planning can translate
  to surgical precision in a notoriously variable procedure. This topicThread — digital
  workflows for GBR — is one to follow.'
