summaryDeep: 'The study fills a real gap: virtually all prior research on mineral
  metabolism and implant failure focused on postmenopausal women or patients with
  manifest osteoporosis. This cohort recruited young and middle-aged adults presenting
  for implant rehabilitation and ran a full metabolic workup — vitamin D3, osteocalcin,
  PTH, calcium-phosphorus ratios, and routine biochemistry — alongside standard clinical
  and imaging assessment. Patients with confirmed D3 deficiency showed altered bone
  remodeling markers and a postoperative course marked by more inflammatory peri-implant
  events and compromised osseointegration features. The clinical implication is direct:
  pre-implant screening for vitamin D3 status in patients under 50 is not currently
  standard practice in most clinics, but this evidence suggests it should be. Correction
  of deficiency before surgery is low-cost and low-risk; ignoring it in a younger
  patient who expects decades of implant function is not. The moderate evidence grade
  reflects the dissertation-cohort design — an RCT on supplementation timing would
  be the logical next step.'
