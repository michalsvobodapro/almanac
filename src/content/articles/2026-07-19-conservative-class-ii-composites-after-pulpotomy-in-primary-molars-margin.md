summary: A 24-month prospective within-patient controlled study of 80 primary molar
  restorations finds that pulpotomised teeth show significantly worse marginal integrity
  at both 12 and 24 months compared to non-pulpotomised controls. Despite this, rates

summaryDeep: 'This within-patient design is methodologically clean: each child contributed
  one pulpotomised and one non-pulpotomised primary molar, eliminating inter-patient
  confounding. NeoMTA was used for pulpotomies; restorations were placed under rubber
  dam with a fifth-generation adhesive and resin composite. At 24 months (32 children,
  64 teeth), marginal integrity was significantly lower in pulpotomised teeth (p =
  0.011), and longitudinal analysis showed progressive deterioration over time. Yet
  no significant differences emerged for marginal discoloration, surface texture,
  wear, postoperative sensitivity, or recurrent caries. The disconnect suggests that
  marginal gaps in pulpotomised primary teeth may not translate to clinical failure
  within a 24-month window — possibly because primary teeth are exfoliated before
  the gap becomes pathological, or because the NeoMTA-composite interface behaves
  differently than expected. For pediatric practice, the message is nuanced: marginal
  integrity scores will look worse on audit, but the restorations are not failing
  clinically. Longer follow-up is needed to determine whether the gap eventually drives
  caries.'
