summaryDeep: Using ESE radiographic criteria, two blinded examiners classified 31
  extracted permanent teeth as deep (n=16) or extremely deep (n=15) caries. Demineralised
  histological sections stained with H&E and Taylor's modified Brown and Brenn stain
  were evaluated for bacterial penetration and pulpal inflammation. Bacteria reached
  the pulp in 13/16 deep and 13/15 extremely deep lesions — an overall rate of 83.9%
  — with most showing moderate or extensive inflammation. Fisher's exact test found
  no significant association between radiographic category and bacterial penetration
  (p = 1.000). The study is extraction-enriched by design, meaning these teeth were
  already destined for removal and likely represent a more severe end of the spectrum;
  this limits direct extrapolation to teeth managed in situ. Nevertheless, the findings
  challenge the implicit assumption in selective and stepwise caries removal protocols
  that a radiographically intact pulp floor equals a bacteriologically intact pulp.
  Clinicians should counsel patients that deep caries management preserves tooth structure
  but does not guarantee pulp sterility, and long-term pulp vitality monitoring remains
  essential.
