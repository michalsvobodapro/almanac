  issue due to high prescription rates of inducing pharmaceuticals. Therefore, the
  main focus of this study was to determine what experiences dentists in Germany have
  had with DIGO and which active ingredients cause this ADR in their patients. The
  conducted online survey was sent to all 17 German dental associations asking them
  to forward the survey to their members. Five associations complied with the request.
  Out of 25,562 dentists who were potentially eligible, 118 questionnaires were evaluated,
  resulting in a response rate of 0.46%. Furthermore, the 24 questions were designed
  to shed light on the frequency with which this adverse drug reaction (ADR) occurs,
  as well as patient factors, treatment strategies and potential risks. Amlodipine,

summaryDeep: 'DIGO is underdiagnosed partly because dentists don''t always connect
  a puffy gingival margin to a patient''s antihypertensive or immunosuppressant. This
  survey asked German dentists directly and found amlodipine — now the dominant calcium
  channel blocker in cardiovascular prescribing — topping the list ahead of the historically
  cited nifedipine, with ciclosporin third. No gender predilection was found, contrasting
  with some earlier literature. Treatment success was reported by 69% of respondents,
  with the hierarchy oral hygiene → drug substitution → surgery reflecting current
  consensus. The critical caveat: 118 responses from a potential pool of 25,562 dentists
  (0.46%) means severe self-selection bias, and prevalence estimates from this data
  are unreliable. The value here is confirmatory rather than generative — it reinforces
  that any patient on amlodipine presenting with gingival enlargement warrants a DIGO
  workup and liaison with their prescribing physician. Relevant for Czech practice
  given amlodipine''s ubiquity in Czech cardiovascular prescribing.'
