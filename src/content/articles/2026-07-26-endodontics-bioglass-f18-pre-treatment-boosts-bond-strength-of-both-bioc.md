summary: Dentin pre-treatment with Bioglass F18 suspension (5% concentration) significantly
  increased push-out and tensile bond strength for both Bio-C Sealer and AH Plus compared
  to controls. The effect was concentration-dependent, and SEM confirmed hydroxyapatite-like
  deposition on the canal wall. This is in vitro, but the mechanism is sound and the
  effect sizes are clinically interesting.
summaryDeep: Sealer-dentin adhesion is a persistent weak point in root canal obturation,
  and bioactive glasses have attracted interest as canal pre-treatment agents because
  they promote mineral deposition on dentin. This study treated extracted human root
  canals with Bioglass F18 at 2.5%, 5%, and 10% concentrations, then evaluated surface
  morphology by SEM/EDX and bond strength by push-out and tensile testing using two
  clinically dominant sealers — the bioceramic Bio-C Sealer and the epoxy resin AH
  Plus. All F18 concentrations outperformed controls, with 5% and 10% showing comparable
  and superior effects. Micro-FTIR analysis after PBS immersion confirmed the material-dentin
  interface was chemically altered. The finding that F18 benefits both a bioceramic
  and an epoxy sealer is notable — it suggests the mechanism is dentin-surface modification
  rather than sealer-specific chemistry. Translation to clinical protocols requires
  in vivo and long-term leakage data, but this is a well-designed in vitro study with
  a plausible mechanistic story.
