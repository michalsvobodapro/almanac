summary: A retrospective cohort tracks tooth survival after treatment of combined
  endodontic-periodontal lesions, quantifying the prognostic weight of lesion classification,
  probing depth, and bone loss pattern. True combined lesions fare significantly worse
  than primary endo or primary perio lesions treated in isolation. The data give clinicians
  a defensible framework for extraction vs. retention decisions.
summaryDeep: 'Endodontic-periodontal lesions sit at one of dentistry''s most contested
  intersections: the prognosis depends on correctly identifying which pathology is
  primary, yet that distinction is often made under clinical uncertainty. This retrospective
  study follows a cohort of teeth with confirmed endo-perio involvement through treatment
  and into maintenance, reporting survival rates stratified by lesion type (primary
  endodontic, primary periodontal, true combined). True combined lesions showed substantially
  lower survival, with deep residual pocketing and furcation involvement emerging
  as the strongest negative predictors. Importantly, the study also identifies cases
  where aggressive retention attempts prolonged treatment without improving outcomes
  — a finding with direct implications for treatment planning conversations. For students,
  this is the kind of long-term outcome data that textbook classifications rarely
  provide: it translates the Endo-Perio classification into actual tooth-loss risk.'
