  mouthwash with alcohol (CHXalc+) on plaque and parameters of gingivitis. Side effects

  databases were searched up to January 2025 to identify eligible studies. Studies
  evaluating the effect of CHXalc- compared to CHXalc+ were included. A descriptive
  analysis and, when feasible, a meta-analysis were performed. The risk of bias was
  assessed, and the body of evidence was evaluated using the GRADE approach. After
  screening, 17 papers met the inclusion criteria and were included. A descriptive

summaryDeep: 'This review searched MEDLINE-PubMed and Cochrane-CENTRAL through January
  2025, identifying 17 eligible papers comparing CHXalc− versus CHXalc+. In brushing
  studies — the clinically relevant scenario for most patients — no significant differences
  emerged in plaque index, bleeding on probing, or gingival index scores. In non-brushing
  studies (a more experimental model), CHXalc+ showed a statistically significant
  advantage in plaque reduction (SMD = 0.49–0.82). Tooth surface discoloration, a
  common patient complaint with CHX, did not differ significantly between formulations.
  Taste preference was evenly split across comparisons. The GRADE approach was applied
  to evaluate evidence quality. The practical implication is clear: for patients who
  cannot tolerate alcohol-containing mouthwash — pregnant women, those with alcohol
  sensitivity, or those on relevant medications — the alcohol-free formulation is
  a clinically sound substitute without meaningful efficacy compromise in real-world
  brushing conditions.'
