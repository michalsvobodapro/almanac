summaryDeep: 'Gingival enlargement is one of those conditions that gets a paragraph
  in the textbook and then surprises you in clinic. This systematic review, following
  full PRISMA methodology with separate PECOTS and PICOTS frameworks, is the most
  rigorous synthesis to date of both its risk indicators and its management evidence.
  The meta-analysis confirms that drug class (particularly calcium-channel blockers,
  phenytoin, and cyclosporins), poor plaque control, and gingival inflammation are
  the dominant modifiable risk factors — and that their interaction is multiplicative,
  not additive. On the treatment side, non-surgical periodontal therapy reduces enlargement
  substantially in drug-induced cases when the offending drug can be substituted or
  withdrawn, but surgical gingivectomy remains necessary for fibrotic overgrowth.
  For a Prague dental student, the practical takeaway is immediate: medication reconciliation
  at every new-patient exam is not optional, and early referral for drug substitution
  should precede any surgical planning. This paper belongs in your perio folder.'
