summaryDeep: Bonding to caries-affected dentin (CAD) remains a weak link in selective
  caries removal protocols. This study used a validated Streptococcus mutans biofilm
  model to generate CAD in 48 third molars, then tested six pretreatment conditions
  — including P11-4 at two concentrations, DMP-1, and their combination — before applying
  an etch-and-rinse adhesive and resin composite. High-concentration P11-4 (1 mg/mL)
  was the clear winner among CAD groups at 33.1 MPa, reducing premature cohesive failures
  and shifting failure mode toward mixed. DMP-1 alone performed no better than untreated
  CAD. The combination of DMP-1 + P11-4 underperformed the high-dose peptide alone,
  suggesting no synergy at these concentrations. Critically, even the best CAD result
  was 42% below sound dentin (57.2 MPa), and the 24-hour storage window doesn't capture
  long-term durability. This is a well-designed lab study, but clinical translation
  requires aging data and in-vivo confirmation before P11-4 pretreatment becomes a
  chairside protocol.
