summaryDeep: Twenty-four patients with confirmed irreducible temporomandibular disc
  displacement (DDwoR) were randomised to hypertonic dextrose (HD) plus stabilisation
  appliance or concentrated growth factors (CGF) plus stabilisation appliance. VAS
  pain and assisted maximum mouth opening were assessed at 1 week, 1 month, and 6
  months; synovial fluid MPO was sampled at baseline and 6 months. Both interventions
  produced significant and sustained improvements in pain and function, with MPO levels
  falling substantially — suggesting genuine anti-inflammatory activity rather than
  placebo. No significant between-group differences were detected, meaning neither
  treatment is clearly superior. The trial is small (n=24) and the lack of a sham-injection
  control limits interpretation. Still, for a condition where surgical options carry
  real morbidity, evidence that regenerative injections can produce 6-month functional
  gains is clinically relevant. Dextrose prolotherapy is cheaper and more accessible
  than CGF, which requires centrifugation equipment.
