  healthcare and central to clinical practice, including orthodontics. Despite the
  widespread emphasis on safety in healthcare delivery, knowledge specific to patient
  safety in orthodontics remains limited. Aims (1) To analyse the reasons why patients
  file claims for compensation related to orthodontic care, (2) to summarise the events
  that lead to patient harm, and (3) to describe the types of harms associated with
  the provision of orthodontic care. Materials & methods Data from the Danish Dental
  Compensation Association (DDCA), a national compensation scheme established by law,
  were retrospectively extracted from the electronic database and included all patient-reported
  claims between June 2017 and August 2024 related to orthodontic care. Results A
  total of 644 patient-reported orthodontic-related claims were reviewed. Of these
  claims, 196 (30.4%) were recognized as patient harm and led to compensation. Orthodontic
  treatment was the most frequently reported source of harm (51.5%, n = 101), followed

summaryDeep: 'The Danish Dental Compensation Association database provided a rare
  national-level view of orthodontic patient safety over seven years. Of 644 claims,
  196 were recognised as genuine harm and compensated. Orthodontic treatment itself
  was the most common source (51.5%), but delayed diagnosis — missing pathology during
  treatment — was the second largest category at 24.5%, and retention-related complications
  added another 9.2%. Root resorption leading to tooth loss was the single most common
  specific harm. These findings are directly relevant to clinical training: the data
  suggest that monitoring protocols for root resorption (periodic radiographic assessment
  during prolonged treatment) and thorough pre-treatment diagnostic workup are not
  just best practice but medico-legally essential. The Danish compensation system
  is statutory and comprehensive, making this dataset more complete than voluntary
  reporting systems used elsewhere in Europe.'
