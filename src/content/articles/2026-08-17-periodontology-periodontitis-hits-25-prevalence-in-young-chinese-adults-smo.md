excerpt: Periodontitis is a chronic multifactorial inflammatory disease that damages
  periodontal tissues and is associated with systemic diseases. It shows a global
  younger onset trend, with rising incidence in Chinese young adults, but studies
  mostly focus on those ≥35 years, lacking systematic exploration of 18 to 35-year-olds.
  From December 2024 to May 2025, 2888 adults aged 18 to 35 years were recruited from
  Tongji Hospital. Periodontitis was diagnosed per the 2018 Classification. Questionnaires
  collected demographic, physical and mental health, oral hygiene and lifestyle data.
  Saliva samples underwent 16S rRNA sequencing and untargeted metabolomics. Statistical
  analyses included independent samples t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, chi-square test
  and binary logistic regression. Periodontitis prevalence was 25.00%. Logistic regression
  identified marital status, suboptimal health, adverse childhood experiences, smoking
  and alcohol drinking were correlated with periodontitis, while longer per-brushing
  duration was negatively correlated with periodontitis. Salivary microbiota community
  richness and composition differed significantly between groups, with higher Fusobacteriota
  in periodontitis patients. Untargeted metabolomics identified 267 differential metabolites

summaryDeep: Recruited between December 2024 and May 2025, 2,888 young adults (18–35
  years) underwent full periodontal examination, questionnaire assessment of lifestyle
  and psychosocial factors, 16S rRNA salivary microbiome sequencing, and untargeted
  metabolomics. Periodontitis prevalence of 25% in this age group is striking and
  aligns with the global 'younger onset' trend. Logistic regression identified marital
  status, suboptimal health, adverse childhood experiences (ACEs), smoking, and alcohol
  as positive correlates; longer per-brushing duration was protective. The ACE finding
  is novel in a periodontal context and suggests psychosocial stress pathways warrant
  investigation alongside conventional microbial and behavioural risk factors. Salivary
  microbiota differed significantly between groups, with Fusobacteriota enriched in
  periodontitis; metabolomics identified 267 differential metabolites, predominantly
  lipids and amino acids, with nucleotide metabolism pathways enriched. The cross-sectional
  design prevents causal inference, and the hospital-recruited sample may not represent
  the general population. Still, the scale and multi-omics depth make this one of
  the more comprehensive young-adult periodontitis datasets published.
