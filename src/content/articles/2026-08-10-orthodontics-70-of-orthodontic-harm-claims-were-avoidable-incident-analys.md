  Aim (1) To conduct an incident analysis, (2) to measure avoidability, (3) to investigate

  harm incidents. Materials and methods Data were retrospectively collected from consecutively

summaryDeep: 'Patient safety in orthodontics has received far less systematic attention
  than in medicine or even other dental specialties. This study retrospectively analysed
  all 125 approved-for-compensation claims filed with the Danish Dental Compensation
  Association between September 2019 and August 2024, applying the Eindhoven incident
  analysis framework and a large language model for qualitative interpretation of
  contributory factor interplay. Human-related factors dominated — particularly failures
  in monitoring, verification, and clinical knowledge — followed by organisational
  factors including management priorities, protocols, and workplace culture. Crucially,
  70.1% of harms (n=82) were rated as avoidable. The use of AI-assisted analysis to
  identify complex factor interactions is methodologically novel and adds depth beyond
  simple frequency counts. Limitations include the Danish-specific compensation system
  and potential selection bias toward more severe or clear-cut cases. For a student,
  the takeaway is structural: avoidable harm in orthodontics is primarily a systems
  and knowledge problem, not a technical one — which means checklists, supervision
  protocols, and honest self-assessment matter as much as clinical skill.'
