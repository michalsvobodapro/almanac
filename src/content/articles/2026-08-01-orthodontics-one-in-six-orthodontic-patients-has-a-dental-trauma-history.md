summaryDeep: 'Orthodontic treatment spans the peak years for traumatic dental injuries
  (ages 7–18), yet the literature on how trauma intersects with orthodontic planning
  is thin. This large retrospective cohort from Western Norway fills part of that
  gap. Of 1,529 patients, 252 (16.5%) had documented traumatic injuries — predominantly
  mild (85%), involving ≤2 teeth (79.8%), typically maxillary central incisors, and
  most often occurring at school (40%). Males were significantly more affected (OR
  1.81, 95% CI 1.38–2.39). The most alarming finding is not the prevalence but the
  documentation quality: only 9% of patients with pre-treatment trauma had adequate
  information in their referral records. Trauma history directly affects orthodontic
  planning — root resorption risk, pulp vitality, timing of tooth movement — making
  this documentation failure clinically consequential. For students entering clinical
  rotations, the lesson is to actively screen for trauma history at the orthodontic
  intake appointment, not rely on referral letters.'
