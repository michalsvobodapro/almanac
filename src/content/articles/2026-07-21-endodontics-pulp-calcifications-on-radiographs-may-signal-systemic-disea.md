summaryDeep: 'The review pooled studies examining imaging-detected pulp calcification
  (PC) against a range of systemic conditions, applying meta-analytic methods where
  data permitted. Cardiovascular disease — particularly coronary artery disease and
  hypertension — showed the strongest and most consistent associations. Metabolic
  and renal conditions also featured. The biological rationale centres on shared mineralisation
  pathways, oxidative stress, and chronic low-grade inflammation. Methodological heterogeneity
  across included studies is a real limitation: PC was defined and detected differently
  across cohorts, and most evidence is cross-sectional. Causality is not established.
  Nevertheless, for a clinician reviewing a panoramic or periapical radiograph, noting
  extensive pulp calcifications in a young patient and flagging it in the medical
  history conversation is low-cost and potentially high-yield. This is also relevant
  for dental students learning radiographic interpretation — PC should not be dismissed
  as a normal variant without context.'
