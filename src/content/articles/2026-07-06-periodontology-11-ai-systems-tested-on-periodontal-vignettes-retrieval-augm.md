summaryDeep: 'This is the most rigorous AI benchmarking study in periodontology to
  date: 11 systems, standardized clinical vignettes, blinded expert raters. General-purpose
  LLMs (think GPT-4-class models) were consistently outperformed by retrieval-augmented
  generation (RAG) platforms that ground answers in curated periodontal literature.
  The study design mirrors how these tools would actually be used chairside — a clinician
  types a case description and expects actionable guidance. The performance gap matters
  because dental students and junior clinicians are already using general-purpose
  chatbots for clinical decision support, often without knowing their limitations.
  RAG systems are not yet widely deployed in Czech or EU clinical settings, but the
  evidence now exists to justify their development. The study also surfaces a calibration
  problem: LLMs tend to sound confident even when wrong, which is the most dangerous
  failure mode in a clinical context.'
