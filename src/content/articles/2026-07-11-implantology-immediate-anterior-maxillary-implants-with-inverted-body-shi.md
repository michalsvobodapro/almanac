excerptFull: The aim of this article was to evaluate clinical outcomes of single immediately
  placed and restored dental implants using a co-axial inverted body-shift design
  with a synthetic graft material in the anterior maxilla. A prospective case series
  was conducted on patients receiving single immediate implants in the anterior maxilla.
  Sixteen-month clinical and radiographic assessments included biological and technical
  complications, gingival margin stability, papilla preservation, and aesthetic outcomes
  using the modified Pink Esthetic Score (modPES) and White Esthetic Score (WES).
  Post-operative cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) measured labial plate thickness,
  and serial intra-oral scans quantified soft-tissue changes. Fifteen patients were
  evaluated at 16 months. Mean modPES was 7.5 and mean WES was 7. CBCT showed a mean
  labial plate thickness of 1.5 mm (0.6–3.1 mm). Buccal crest position was coronal
  to the abutment in 11/15 cases (mean +1.1 mm). Soft-tissue changes showed mean mid-facial
  recession of -0.5 mm, mesial papilla loss of -0.5 mm, and distal papilla loss of
  -0.3 mm. To conclude, immediate placement and loading using this implant design,
  combined with the dual-zone approach and synthetic grafting, resulted in minimal
  soft-tissue change, maintained papillae, and yielded high aesthetic scores, supporting
  it as a predictable option for anterior single-tooth replacement.

summaryDeep: 'Immediate implant placement and provisionalization in the anterior maxilla
  remains one of the most technically demanding procedures in implantology, with soft
  tissue stability being the primary concern. This prospective case series evaluated
  a co-axial inverted body-shift implant design combined with a dual-zone approach
  and synthetic grafting in 15 patients over 16 months. Serial intraoral scans quantified
  soft tissue changes: mean mid-facial recession was −0.5 mm, mesial papilla loss
  −0.5 mm, and distal papilla loss −0.3 mm — all within clinically acceptable thresholds.
  CBCT confirmed a mean labial plate thickness of 1.5 mm, and buccal crest position
  was coronal to the abutment in 11 of 15 cases. Aesthetic outcomes (modPES 7.5/10,
  WES 7/10) are modest but functional. The obvious limitation is the small sample
  (n=15) and single-arm design — there is no comparator group and no randomisation.
  The 16-month follow-up is also short for implant research. The results are hypothesis-generating
  rather than practice-changing, but the implant design concept and the dual-zone
  grafting protocol are worth understanding as a student.'
