excerpt: This prospective registration specifies a systematic review of histopathological
  diagnoses and related etiologic findings in human persistent apical radiolucencies
  associated with root-filled permanent teeth and sampled during apical surgery, biopsy,
  or extraction. Planned outcomes include granuloma, radicular cyst, abscess, fibrous
  scar, intra- and extraradicular infection, foreign material or foreign-body reaction,
  and non-endodontic diagnoses. Random-effects meta-analysis of proportions will be
  performed only for sufficiently compatible defined-denominator cohorts. All quantitative
  estimates will be interpreted as applying to lesions selected for tissue sampling,
  not to all persistent post-treatment radiolucencies.

summaryDeep: The decision to retreat a root-filled tooth versus proceed to apical
  surgery hinges partly on what the clinician expects to find at the apex — and the
  histopathological reality of persistent radiolucencies is poorly characterised in
  the aggregate. This PROSPERO-registered protocol specifies a systematic review with
  random-effects meta-analysis of proportions for histopathological diagnoses (granuloma,
  radicular cyst, abscess, fibrous scar, intra- and extraradicular infection, foreign
  material, non-endodontic diagnoses) in surgically sampled human lesions. Crucially,
  the authors explicitly limit inference to lesions selected for tissue sampling,
  not all post-treatment radiolucencies — a methodological honesty that is rarer than
  it should be. For endodontic students, this maps directly onto the ESE guidelines
  on management of apical periodontitis. The protocol itself is the deliverable today;
  the completed review will be the practice-relevant output. Flagging it now means
  you will recognise it when it lands.
