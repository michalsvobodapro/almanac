summaryDeep: This in-vitro three-body wear study tested three widely used CAD/CAM
  materials — PMMA, nano-ceramic composite, and PEEK — across 72 disc specimens (n=24/group)
  in a chewing simulator. The 120,000-cycle protocol with pumice slurry represents
  approximately one year of clinical function. Nano-ceramic composite (Grandio Blocs,
  VOCO) won on volume loss by a wide margin, losing 0.08 mm³ vs. 0.23 mm³ for PEEK
  and 0.55 mm³ for PMMA. Surface roughness increased significantly in all groups post-simulation,
  with a significant material-by-wear interaction. Vickers hardness showed no significant
  interaction between material and wear. PMMA's poor wear performance confirms its
  role as a provisional rather than definitive material. PEEK's intermediate performance
  is notable given its growing use in implant-supported frameworks. The study is in-vitro
  and uses a single antagonist material and slurry, so clinical wear rates will differ
  — but the ranking is consistent with prior literature and clinically actionable.
