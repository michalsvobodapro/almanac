summary: A mechanistic study maps how Porphyromonas gingivalis translocates from the
  oral cavity to cardiac atrial tissue, proposing a direct microbial contribution
  to atrial fibrosis and atrial fibrillation. The evidence is preclinical, but the
  oral–atrial axis concept adds mechanistic specificity to the longstanding perio–cardiovascular
  association. This is the second P. gingivalis systemic-pathway paper in this digest
  cycle.
summaryDeep: The association between periodontitis and cardiovascular disease is epidemiologically
  robust but mechanistically underspecified. This study narrows the gap by tracing
  P. gingivalis translocation along what the authors term the oral–atrial axis, demonstrating
  that the bacterium reaches atrial tissue and contributes to fibrotic remodelling
  — a plausible substrate for atrial fibrillation. The mechanistic model is built
  on a translocation study design, which is inherently limited in causal inference
  compared to interventional designs. Notably, a companion paper in this digest (JCPE,
  trained immunity/colitis) shows P. gingivalis driving systemic inflammation via
  a completely different pathway (glycolysis–SIRT1 epigenetic reprogramming), reinforcing
  that this pathogen operates through multiple systemic routes simultaneously. Neither
  paper alone changes clinical perio management, but together they strengthen the
  biological rationale for aggressive periodontal treatment in patients with cardiac
  comorbidities.
