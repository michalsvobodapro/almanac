summary: Odontoblast polarization — the prerequisite for tubular dentine formation
  — is governed by mechanobiological cues that have remained largely uncharacterised.
  A combined wet-lab and computational model now maps the biomechanical signals that
  orient odontoblasts, filling a critical gap for dentine regeneration strategies.
summaryDeep: Regenerative dentistry assumes that scaffold geometry and mechanical
  environment can guide odontoblast behaviour, but the molecular mechanobiology underpinning
  polarization has been a black box. This wet-and-dry research model pairs in vitro
  experiments with computational biomechanics to identify the physical cues — substrate
  stiffness, fluid shear, and cytoskeletal tension — that determine how odontoblasts
  orient and extend their processes into dentinal tubules. Understanding these signals
  matters because scaffold designers have been working largely by trial and error;
  knowing which mechanical parameters drive polarization allows rational engineering
  of biomaterials that recapitulate the native dentine matrix environment. The findings
  also have implications for direct pulp capping and vital pulp therapy, where the
  quality of the tertiary dentine bridge depends on how well the remaining odontoblasts
  polarize in response to the capping material's mechanical properties.
