excerpt: Objectives To compare fascia lata allograft (FLA) and subepithelial connective
  tissue graft (SCTG) for peri-implant mucosal augmentation performed simultaneously
  with implant placement. Materials and methods This prospective, randomized, examiner-blinded
  pilot clinical trial enrolled 16 patients requiring single-tooth implant placement
  with a thin peri-implant soft-tissue phenotype. Participants were randomly allocated
  (1:1) to SCTG or FLA. The primary outcome was peri-implant mucosal thickness (PMT)
  measured by using a customized stent-guided transmucosal method at standardized
  buccal reference points. Secondary outcomes included keratinized mucosal width (KMW),
  postoperative pain (VAS), and modified wound healing index (MWHI). Follow-up was
  24 weeks. Results Both groups demonstrated significant improvements in PMT and KMW
  over time. Between-group differences were generally non-significant, except localized
  PMT differences at the intermediate reference level, favoring SCTG at later visits.
  FLA showed lower postoperative pain at selected time points. Healing outcomes were
  comparable. Conclusion Both SCTG and FLA were effective for peri-implant mucosal
  augmentation. FLA may represent a viable alternative that avoids donor-site morbidity.
  Larger long-term trials are required. Clinical relevance FLA can be considered a
  viable alternative for enhancing peri-implant mucosal thickness around simultaneously
  placed dental implants. Trial registration ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04679922, r

summaryDeep: Patients with thin peri-implant phenotype requiring single-tooth implant
  placement were randomized 1:1 to SCTG or FLA augmentation performed simultaneously
  with implant surgery. Primary outcome was peri-implant mucosal thickness (PMT) measured
  via stent-guided transmucosal probing at standardized buccal points; secondary outcomes
  included keratinized mucosal width, VAS pain, and wound healing index. Both groups
  showed statistically significant PMT and KMW improvements over 24 weeks. Between-group
  differences were non-significant at most time points and reference levels, with
  one exception favoring SCTG at an intermediate buccal level at later visits. FLA
  patients reported lower pain at selected postoperative time points. The pilot size
  (n=16) limits definitive conclusions, but the data support FLA as a viable alternative
  that eliminates palatal donor-site morbidity — a meaningful patient-centered advantage.
  Larger, longer trials are needed.
