summaryDeep: 'The editorial raises a structural integrity question that the dental
  academic community has been slow to confront: when reviewers use large language
  models to draft or substantially shape their critiques, the independence and expertise
  that peer review is supposed to guarantee become fictional. Unlike AI-assisted authorship
  — which at least has nascent disclosure norms — reviewer AI use is almost entirely
  ungoverned, invisible to editors, and undetectable post-publication. The piece doesn''t
  argue that AI assistance is inherently wrong; it argues that the absence of transparency
  is the problem, and that journals need explicit reviewer AI policies before the
  practice becomes entrenched. For a dentistry student in Prague, this has immediate
  relevance: the evidence hierarchy you''re being taught to trust — systematic reviews,
  RCTs, meta-analyses — rests on peer review quality. If that quality is silently
  degrading, critical appraisal skills matter even more than your curriculum suggests.
  Watch for journal policy updates from major publishers; this debate is about to
  accelerate.'
