summary: Stage IV periodontitis with pathologic tooth migration is one of the hardest
  clinical scenarios to manage, and this Periodontology 2000 review lays out the evidence
  for combining regenerative surgery with orthodontic therapy. The sequencing logic
  — stabilise the periodontium first, then move teeth — is clarified with outcome
  data. For students seeing these cases in clinic, this is the framework to internalise.
summaryDeep: 'Stage IV periodontitis is defined partly by its complexity: tooth mobility,
  drifting, flaring, and the psychological burden of a visibly collapsing dentition.
  This review in Periodontology 2000 tackles the combined regenerative-orthodontic
  approach head-on, detailing when and how periodontal regeneration (guided tissue
  regeneration, bone grafting) should precede orthodontic loading. The core argument
  is that orthodontic forces applied to a regenerated periodontium can be not only
  safe but therapeutically beneficial — improving bone architecture and root support
  — provided the inflammatory disease is fully controlled first. The review synthesises
  case series and cohort data to define patient selection criteria, timing windows
  between surgical and orthodontic phases, and retention considerations specific to
  the post-perio patient. For a dental student, this is the clearest available map
  of a treatment pathway that will appear in complex cases throughout a career. The
  clinical takeaway: don''t extract a migrated tooth in a Stage IV patient before
  evaluating whether a regenerative-ortho sequence could save it.'
