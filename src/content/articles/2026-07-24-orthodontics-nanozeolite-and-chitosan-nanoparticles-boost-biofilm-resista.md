summaryDeep: 3D-printed clear aligners are rapidly displacing thermoformed ones in
  many practices, but their susceptibility to bacterial biofilm is a documented clinical
  concern. This in-vitro study fabricated 432 specimens across six groups, systematically
  varying nanozeolite and chitosan nanoparticle concentrations, and evaluated biofilm
  resistance (crystal violet assay against S. mutans), degree of conversion, flexural
  strength, and Vickers microhardness — before and after 14-day aging. Both nanomodifiers
  reduced biofilm at clinically relevant timepoints. Critically, mechanical properties
  were preserved, addressing the concern that antimicrobial modification might weaken
  the material. The hybrid group (0.25% NZ + 0.25% Chs NPs) offered the best balance.
  This is bench science, and the path to clinical translation requires cytotoxicity
  testing and in-vivo validation, but the material science foundation is solid. For
  orthodontics students, it's a preview of where aligner material development is heading.
