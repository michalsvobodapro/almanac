excerptFull: The age-related decline in the pro-angiogenic capacity of mature dental
  pulp stem cells (DPSCs) severely limits pulp regeneration. We identify impaired
  glycolytic metabolism, driven by reduced glucose transporter type 1 (GLUT1) and
  hexokinase 2 (HK2) expression, as the key mechanism, as its inhibition diminished
  endothelial tube formation. To reverse this, we developed an aminolyzed highly branched
  poly(β-amino ester) (HBPA) as a vector for GLUT1/HK2 mRNA co-delivery, achieving
  &gt;90% transfection efficiency with excellent biocompatibility. In vitro, conditioned
  medium from reprogrammed mature DPSCs resulted in a 2.0-fold increase in capillary
  length and a 2.3-fold increase in branch points, restoring angiogenic potential
  to levels equivalent to those of immature DPSCs. This efficacy translated robustly
  in vivo, where a tooth root slice model showed reprogrammed cells generated tissue
  with a vessel density of 10.2 vessels per mm2, 2.5-fold higher than that of untreated
  controls. Crucially, this level of vascularization was statistically indistinguishable
  from that achieved by the benchmark immature DPSCs. Our study demonstrates that
  HBPA-mediated metabolic reprogramming effectively rejuvenates mature DPSCs by restoring
  the “Metabolic-ECM-Angiogenesis Axis”, offering a translatable strategy for predictable,
  vascularized pulp regeneration.

  is that their DPSCs have diminished pro-angiogenic capacity — without adequate vascularisation,
  regenerated tissue cannot survive. This study identifies impaired glycolytic metabolism
  (reduced GLUT1 and HK2 expression) as the mechanistic culprit and delivers a targeted
  fix: co-delivery of GLUT1 and HK2 mRNA using an aminolyzed highly branched poly(β-amino
  ester) vector achieving >90% transfection efficiency with good biocompatibility.
  In vitro, conditioned medium from reprogrammed cells produced a 2.0-fold increase
  in capillary length and 2.3-fold increase in branch points. In vivo, the tooth root
  slice model showed vessel density statistically equivalent to the immature DPSC
  benchmark. This is lab-stage work — no human trials, no regulatory pathway yet —
  but the mRNA delivery strategy is conceptually aligned with the broader mRNA therapeutics
  wave post-COVID, and the translational logic is sound. For students, it''s a preview
  of what regenerative endodontics may look like in a decade.'
