summary: An RCT with 2.8-year CBCT follow-up (n=59) found no statistically significant
  difference in healing between single-visit and two-visit root canal treatment for
  necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis. Loose healing rates were nearly identical

summaryDeep: Fifty-nine patients with necrotic teeth and apical periodontitis were
  randomised to single-visit treatment or two-visit treatment with calcium hydroxide
  dressing for 10–14 days. At a mean follow-up of 2.8 years, CBCT-based strict healing
  was 25% (single-visit) versus 50% (two-visit) — a numerically meaningful difference
  that did not reach statistical significance (RR 2.0, 95% CI 0.8–4.8, P=0.121), likely
  due to the small sample. Loose healing (healed + healing) was virtually identical
  at 90% versus 86.4%. Lesion volumes decreased in both groups, with a non-significant
  10.3 mm³ advantage for two-visit. The trial is underpowered to rule out a clinically
  relevant benefit of calcium hydroxide dressing, and the strict healing gap (25 percentage
  points) warrants attention even without statistical significance. For now, single-visit
  remains defensible, but larger trials are needed before definitively abandoning
  the two-visit protocol in large lesions.
