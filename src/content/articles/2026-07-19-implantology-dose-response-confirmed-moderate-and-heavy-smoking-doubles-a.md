summary: A cross-sectional study of 1,021 implant patients finds a clear dose-response
  relationship between cigarette consumption and peri-implantitis. Moderate smokers
  (10–19 cigs/day) carry twice the adjusted odds (AOR 2.01), heavy smokers (≥20/day)
  carry more than three times the odds (AOR 3.57) compared to non-smokers. Light smokers
  and former smokers showed no statistically significant elevation — a clinically
  important nuance.
summaryDeep: 'This is the clearest dose-response signal yet linking cigarette intensity
  to peri-implantitis. Among 1,021 patients with at least one functional implant,
  peri-implantitis was identified in 14.7% overall. After adjusting for age, sex,
  periodontitis stage, diabetes, and hypertension, moderate smokers (10–19 cigarettes/day)
  had an AOR of 2.01 (95% CI 1.13–3.59) and heavy smokers (≥20/day) an AOR of 3.57
  (95% CI 2.00–6.38). Crucially, light smokers (1–9/day) and former smokers did not
  reach statistical significance — suggesting that cessation and reduction below a
  threshold may genuinely attenuate risk rather than merely shift it. The cross-sectional
  design cannot establish causality, and self-reported cigarette counts introduce
  measurement error, but the gradient is robust across adjustment models. For implant
  planning conversations, this study gives practitioners concrete, quantified language:
  cutting to fewer than 10 cigarettes daily may meaningfully lower biological risk,
  and cessation appears to normalize it.'
