summaryDeep: Self-cured bulk-fill composites are clinically attractive for deep cavities
  and difficult access situations where light penetration is unreliable, but their
  polymerization behavior has been poorly characterized. This in vitro study tested
  STELA Automix under four bonding protocols against two light-cured bulk-fills using
  bonded-disk shrinkage strain, Bioman II shrinkage stress, and Martens hardness over
  7 days. All STELA configurations showed higher final shrinkage strain than light-cured
  controls (p <0.05), which is a concern for marginal integrity. But shrinkage stress
  — the clinically more relevant parameter for cuspal deflection and adhesive failure
  — ranged from 1.34 to 2.39 MPa, with the STA+AU (Adhese Universal without light
  cure) protocol producing the lowest values. The dedicated STELA Primer advanced
  shrinkage onset and widened the interval before peak shrinkage rate, suggesting
  a more gradual stress buildup that could be favorable for bond integrity. Hardness
  at 7 days exceeded SDR, indicating adequate final conversion. The study is purely
  in vitro; clinical performance data are needed before STELA can be recommended for
  routine use.
