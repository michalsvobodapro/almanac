summaryDeep: 'Dental pulp sits in a low-compliance bony chamber where neurogenic inflammation
  — driven by neuropeptides like Substance P (SP) and calcitonin gene-related peptide
  (CGRP) — can rapidly escalate intrapulpal pressure, compromise microvascular flow,
  and produce irreversible pulpitis. This review positions Neuropeptide Y as the system''s
  endogenous counterregulator: NPY inhibits nociceptive transmission, modulates vascular
  tone, and suppresses the release of pro-inflammatory neuropeptides. The balance
  between NPY and SP/CGRP may determine whether pulpal inflammation resolves (reversible
  pulpitis) or progresses (irreversible pulpitis and necrosis). Clinically, this framework
  suggests that the threshold for irreversibility is not purely structural but neurochemical
  — and potentially modifiable. For vital pulp therapy and direct pulp capping, understanding
  NPY signalling could inform the development of adjunctive pharmacological agents
  that tip the balance toward resolution. For students, this is foundational pulp
  biology that explains why two teeth with apparently similar carious exposure can
  have dramatically different clinical trajectories.'
