summaryDeep: 'This Periodontology 2000-style narrative review synthesizes nearly 100
  studies on sex-based and hormonal differences in periodontal and peri-implant disease.
  The headline finding is consistent with epidemiology: males have higher prevalence
  and severity of periodontitis, with greater CAL and probing depths. But the review
  surfaces important nuance — adolescent studies frequently show no sex difference,
  and hormonal states (menopause, pregnancy, androgen therapy) modify both disease
  expression and biomarker profiles in ways that cut across simple male/female binaries.
  For peri-implantitis, the evidence is thinner and more heterogeneous. The animal
  literature, while mechanistically informative, doesn''t map cleanly onto clinical
  populations. The practical upshot: sex-disaggregated risk assessment is warranted,
  but clinicians should be asking about hormonal status — particularly in perimenopausal
  women and patients on hormone therapy — rather than relying on sex alone as a proxy.'
