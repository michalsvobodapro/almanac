summaryDeep: 'This in vitro study fabricated 16 twelve-unit zirconia implant-supported
  fixed complete prostheses (n=8 per workflow) on a standardised All-on-4 master model,
  comparing direct IOS at multi-unit abutment level against indirect digitisation
  of conventional stone casts. Scan trueness was assessed by 3D analysis software;
  marginal fit was evaluated by digital gap measurement and stereomicroscopy, including
  the one-screw test. IOS showed significantly higher scan body distance deviations
  overall (p≤0.001), with the effect concentrated at posterior abutments (positions
  13 and 16), likely reflecting cumulative error over longer spans. Marginal gap values
  did not differ significantly between workflows at most abutment positions. Spearman
  correlation confirmed moderate agreement between digital and stereomicroscopic gap
  measurements. The study is limited by its in vitro design and single master model.
  For students entering a clinic where full-arch digital workflows are being adopted,
  the message is nuanced: IOS is not yet equivalent to indirect workflows for full-arch
  cases, and the one-screw test remains a critical clinical verification step.'
