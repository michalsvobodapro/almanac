summaryDeep: This RCT randomised 50 adults with gingivitis undergoing either clear
  aligner therapy (CAT, n=25) or fixed orthodontic therapy (OT, n=25) to mechanical
  debridement alone or debridement plus aPDT (0.01% methylene blue, 660-nm diode laser).
  At one month, CAT patients showed significant reductions in plaque index, gingival
  index, and probing depth from baseline — but these gains were not maintained at
  three months, and aPDT added nothing over debridement alone at either timepoint.
  Fixed appliance patients showed no significant improvement at either follow-up,
  irrespective of treatment modality. Linear regression identified duration of fixed
  OT as a significant positive predictor of worse periodontal parameters. The study
  is small (n=50 total, split four ways) and the three-month follow-up is short, but
  the null result for aPDT is consistent with mixed findings in the broader aPDT literature.
  For students, the clinical message is that aPDT is not a substitute for adequate
  mechanical debridement and oral hygiene instruction — and that fixed appliance patients
  need more intensive periodontal monitoring throughout treatment.
