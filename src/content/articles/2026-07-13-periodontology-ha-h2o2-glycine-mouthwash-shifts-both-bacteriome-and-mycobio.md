summaryDeep: 'Periodontal research has long focused on bacteria, but the oral mycobiome
  is increasingly recognized as a co-driver of dysbiosis. This split-mouth RCT is
  one of the first to track both kingdoms simultaneously after a short adjunctive
  intervention. Seven days of twice-daily BMG0703A rinsing produced a bacteriome shift
  — Rothia nearly doubled (11.9% → 22.7%), anaerobes receded — and a mycobiome ''normalization'':
  Candida dropped by more than half and fungal Shannon diversity in pockets returned
  to healthy-site levels. Inter-kingdom correlation analysis suggested the expanding
  commensals actively suppress opportunistic fungi. The caveat is glaring: n=13, 7-day
  follow-up, no clinical attachment level data reported in the excerpt. This is a
  pilot generating hypotheses, not changing protocols. But the methodological approach
  — simultaneous 16S and ITS2 sequencing in a controlled design — is a template worth
  watching.'
