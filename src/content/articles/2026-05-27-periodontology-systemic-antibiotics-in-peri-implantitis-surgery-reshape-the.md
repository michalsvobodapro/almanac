summary: Adding systemic antibiotics to surgical peri-implantitis treatment doesn't
  just fight infection locally — it leaves a measurable fingerprint on the salivary

  questions every implant surgeon should sit with.
summaryDeep: 'Peri-implantitis surgery is already a contested protocol space, and
  the role of systemic antibiotics remains one of its most debated adjuncts. This
  JCPE EarlyView study goes beyond clinical attachment and bleeding scores to characterise
  what antibiotic co-administration actually does to the salivary microbial community.
  The results show significant compositional shifts in the salivary microbiome following
  antibiotic use — changes that extend well beyond the implant site and persist after
  the treatment window closes. That matters clinically because the oral microbiome
  is not a passive bystander: dysbiosis at the salivary level can influence systemic
  inflammatory burden and set the stage for recurrence. For a Prague student watching
  EU antimicrobial stewardship tighten, this is the kind of evidence that will eventually
  feed into guideline revisions. The take-home is not ''never use antibiotics'' but
  rather that the decision needs to be weighed against a broader biological cost than
  previously modelled.'
