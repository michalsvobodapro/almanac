summaryDeep: 'South Korea''s expansion of national dental coverage under long-term
  care insurance is a natural experiment in what happens when you extend benefits
  to a vulnerable older population. This analysis of the NHIS-Senior 2.0 database
  matched 170,407 LTCI recipients to 486,788 non-recipients and tracked dental utilization
  across seven treatment types from 2015 to 2019. The headline finding is sobering:
  despite coverage expansion, a persistent 17-percentage-point utilization gap remained.
  Both groups shifted toward more complex prosthetic treatments — metal-based complete
  dentures and implants replaced resin dentures — but LTCI recipients accessed implants
  at nearly half the rate of non-recipients. Most concerning is the silent signal:
  endodontic treatment rates fell among LTCI recipients while extraction rates held
  steady, suggesting teeth are being lost that could have been saved. For a Czech
  student, the Korean data are a useful comparator as EU member states debate dental
  coverage models: coverage on paper does not automatically translate to access in
  practice, especially for institutionalized or mobility-limited patients.'
