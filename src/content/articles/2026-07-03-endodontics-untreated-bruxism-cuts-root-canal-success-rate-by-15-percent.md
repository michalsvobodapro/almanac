summaryDeep: 'This STROBE-compliant retrospective cohort from CEU San Pablo University
  analysed one index tooth per patient across 551 cases treated between 2006 and 2009,
  with a minimum one-year follow-up. Bruxism was diagnosed clinically by a single
  examiner; outcomes were classified using European Society of Endodontology criteria.
  In systemically healthy patients, success fell from 93.3% (no bruxism) to 78.3%
  (untreated bruxism), a 15-percentage-point difference that survived Benjamini-Hochberg
  correction for multiple comparisons. Multilevel interactions showed the penalty
  was steeper in older patients and in teeth with pre-existing periapical lesions.
  The mechanism is plausible: chronic occlusal overload sustains mechanical irritation
  of the periodontium and may impair periapical healing even after adequate canal
  disinfection. The study is limited by its retrospective design and single-examiner
  bruxism diagnosis, but the effect size is large enough that waiting for an RCT before
  acting is hard to justify. Clinically, this means documenting parafunctional status
  before every RCT and counselling patients — or referring for occlusal splint therapy
  — before or alongside endodontic treatment.'
