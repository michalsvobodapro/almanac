summary: Researchers developed and psychometrically validated the MIH-TH scale — the
  first condition-specific, child-reported outcome measure for molar incisor hypomineralisation-related

summaryDeep: MIH affects an estimated 14% of children globally, and tooth hypersensitivity
  is its most functionally disabling feature — yet until now, no validated tool existed
  that was developed with children's own language and experience. This three-stage
  development study used semi-structured interviews (n=17), cognitive interviews and
  Delphi consensus (n=29), then Rasch analysis and psychometric evaluation (n=103).
  Children described cold as the dominant trigger, noted the 'invisible' nature of
  their pain, and used vocabulary that existing generic OHRQoL instruments failed
  to capture. The final scale shows strong internal consistency (ω=0.87), acceptable
  test-retest reliability (ICC=0.74), and strong convergent validity (r=0.78 with
  global question). For clinical practice, this is primarily a research and audit
  tool — but it signals that MIH hypersensitivity is a distinct, measurable patient
  experience that deserves targeted management conversations, not just a note in the
  chart.
