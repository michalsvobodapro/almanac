summary: A formal critique of the evidentiary framework underpinning the joint EFCD-ESE-ORCA
  guideline on deep caries management — the document that shapes stepwise and selective
  removal protocols across Europe. The commentary questions how the evidence was graded
  and what that means for the strength of the recommendations you are taught to follow.
summaryDeep: 'The EFCD-ESE-ORCA guideline on deep caries management is the closest
  thing European dentistry has to a consensus protocol for when to go selective, when
  to go stepwise, and when to consider pulp capping. This commentary dissects the
  evidentiary framework behind those recommendations, raising concerns about how studies
  were selected, how evidence quality was graded, and whether the resulting recommendation
  strength is justified by the underlying data. For a student in Prague, this matters
  directly: ČSK-aligned teaching leans heavily on these European guidelines, and if
  the grading methodology is flawed, the clinical confidence you are asked to place
  in specific protocols may be overstated. The piece does not argue that the clinical
  recommendations are wrong — it argues that the certainty attached to them may be
  inflated. That is a subtler but more important point, and it is the kind of methodological
  literacy that separates a clinician who uses guidelines from one who understands
  them.'
