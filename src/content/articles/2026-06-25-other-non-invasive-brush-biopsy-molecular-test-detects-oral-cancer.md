summaryDeep: 'This prospective diagnostic case-control study validated qMIDS V3 —
  a four-gene qPCR panel applied to paired brush biopsies from lesion and contralateral
  normal mucosa — in 545 patients (443 OSCC, 63 oral leukoplakia, 39 oral lichen planus).
  Each patient provided two brush samples, enabling within-patient normalization.
  The algorithm generated a malignancy index that achieved AUC 0.975 with 95.7% sensitivity
  and 95.1% specificity for OSCC versus low-risk OPMD. False-positive and false-negative
  rates were both under 5%. The test is non-invasive, rapid, and designed for chairside
  or outpatient use without pathology lab infrastructure. For a Prague dental student,
  this is directly relevant: oral potentially malignant disorders are routinely encountered
  in general practice, and the current standard — wait-and-watch or refer for scalpel
  biopsy — is both slow and patient-aversive. A validated brush-biopsy triage tool
  at this accuracy level could reshape referral pathways across EU primary dental
  care.'
