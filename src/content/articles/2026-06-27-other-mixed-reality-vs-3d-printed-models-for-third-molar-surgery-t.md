summaryDeep: 'Students completed training on both modalities in a single session,
  with order randomized. The MR simulator showed a significant within-modality reduction
  in bone removal volume between Period 1 and Period 2 (β = −33.29 mm³, p = 0.0016),
  while the plastic model did not — suggesting the MR system is more sensitive to
  skill acquisition within a session. The higher lingual perforation rate in MR is
  a safety signal worth noting: it may reflect haptic feedback differences or the
  simulator''s geometry, and it means MR-trained students may need explicit attention
  to lingual plate protection before transitioning to real patients. The study is
  small (n = 20) and the crossover design means both groups eventually trained on
  both modalities, limiting between-group conclusions. For dental schools evaluating
  simulation investment, this suggests MR and physical models are complementary rather
  than substitutable — each exposes different error patterns.'
