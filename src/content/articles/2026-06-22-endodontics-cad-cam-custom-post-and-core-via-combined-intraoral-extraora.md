summaryDeep: Restoring endodontically treated teeth with custom post-and-cores has
  resisted full digitization because intraoral scanners struggle with the depth and
  darkness of post spaces. This technique article solves that by combining an extraoral
  scan of a direct resin post-space impression with an intraoral scan of the arch,
  merging the datasets in CAD software to design and mill a custom post-and-core.
  The direct-indirect hybrid approach preserves the dimensional accuracy of a physical
  impression for the critical apical geometry while capturing the coronal context
  digitally. The result is a milled post-and-core — typically zirconia or milled metal
  — with a passive fit that is difficult to achieve with conventional casting. Evidence
  level is descriptive (no RCT, no comparative cohort), so this is a workflow adoption
  decision rather than an evidence-based protocol change. Still, for a student in
  a digitally equipped clinic, understanding this workflow is increasingly relevant
  as CAD/CAM post-and-core fabrication enters mainstream prosthodontic practice.
