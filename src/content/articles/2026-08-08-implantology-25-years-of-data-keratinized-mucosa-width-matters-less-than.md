summaryDeep: 'This cohort followed partially edentulous patients rehabilitated with
  implants over 25 years, all enrolled in structured supportive peri-implant care
  with adequate oral hygiene. Keratinized mucosa width (KMW) and mucosal thickness
  (MT) were assessed against peri-implant clinical parameters and patient-reported
  brushing discomfort across the full follow-up period. The 25-year horizon is exceptional
  — most implant cohorts top out at 10 years — and the moderate evidence grade reflects
  inherent cohort limitations rather than poor design. The data challenge the binary
  ''≥2 mm KMW is safe'' heuristic: mucosal thickness emerges as an independent variable,
  and brushing discomfort is a real patient-reported burden in sites with thin or
  narrow keratinized tissue. For students planning implant cases or soft-tissue augmentation
  decisions, this study reframes the pre-implant periodontal assessment: phenotype
  matters as much as width.'
