summaryDeep: 'Edentulism affects roughly 30% of adults aged 60 and over, and conventional
  complete dentures frequently fail to deliver adequate retention or masticatory performance.
  This systematic review pooled 21 RCTs comparing implant-retained overdentures to
  conventional dentures, using random-effects meta-analyses and structured qualitative
  subgroup analysis. IODs showed superior OHRQoL outcomes, especially on oral function
  subscales; denture satisfaction and masticatory performance required narrative synthesis
  due to methodological heterogeneity across trials. The I² >90% is a red flag for
  interpreting pooled effect sizes — variation in implant number (two vs. four), attachment
  systems, and patient populations drives much of it. Nevertheless, no individual
  RCT favoured conventional dentures on OHRQoL, making the directional conclusion
  robust. For a student in Prague, the practical implication is clear: when treating
  edentulous older patients, IODs should be the default recommendation where cost
  and systemic status permit, and the conversation about implant rehabilitation should
  happen early.'
