summary: A systematic review with meta-analysis confirms that periodontitis — and
  especially edentulism — is associated with both the presence and development of
  IBD, including Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. The oral–gut axis is no longer
  a hypothesis; it's a documented epidemiological signal. Periodontal patients with
  GI symptoms deserve prompt co-management, not watchful waiting.
summaryDeep: 'This meta-analysis pooled data across multiple study designs to test
  whether periodontitis and tooth loss independently predict inflammatory bowel disease.
  The signal is consistent: periodontitis elevates IBD risk, and edentulism — a proxy
  for severe, longstanding periodontal destruction — shows an even stronger association.
  The proposed mechanism runs through the oral microbiome: dysbiotic oral bacteria
  are swallowed continuously, seeding gut inflammation and disrupting the intestinal
  epithelial barrier. Crohn''s disease and ulcerative colitis both appear in the association,
  though effect sizes differ. For the clinician, this means that a patient presenting
  with active periodontitis and unexplained abdominal symptoms or weight loss should
  trigger a referral conversation, not just a recall appointment. The evidence doesn''t
  yet establish causality, but the consistency across study designs is enough to change
  how you take a medical history. This is the kind of systemic-link paper that tends
  to move into clinical guidelines within a few years.'
