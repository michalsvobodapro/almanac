summaryDeep: 'Periapical lesions scheduled for endodontic surgery vary enormously
  in three-dimensional extent, proximity to anatomical structures, and cortical plate
  involvement — none of which 2D periapical radiographs reliably capture. This protocol
  paper from the International Endodontic Journal lays out a structured methodology
  for developing a CBCT-derived 3D classification system, covering lesion dimensions,
  bone morphology, and spatial relationships to adjacent structures. The goal is a
  clinically usable taxonomy that can guide surgical access design, predict difficulty,
  and allow meaningful cross-study comparisons. The paper is a protocol, not a completed
  classification — validation cohorts are the next step — but the framework is methodologically
  sound and addresses a genuine gap. For students rotating through oral surgery or
  endodontics, understanding that lesion geometry in three dimensions drives surgical
  decision-making (flap design, osteotomy size, root-end preparation depth) is foundational.
  Watch this space: if the classification gets validated, it could become a guideline-level
  tool.'
