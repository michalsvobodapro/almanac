summaryDeep: 'Four systems (LOCATOR standard, LOCATOR extended-range, LOCATOR R-Tx,
  and Novaloc) were tested across three retention levels using strain gauges during
  simulated 3 mm posterior upward displacement until insert dislodgement. The Novaloc
  finding is the most clinically relevant: despite its reputation for low-profile
  design and patient-friendly insertion, it transferred more strain to peri-implant
  bone than the LOCATOR variants in this loading scenario. The mesial site concentration
  suggests a lever-arm effect specific to posterior displacement. Retention level
  (light/medium/heavy) did not significantly modulate strain — meaning clinicians
  cannot compensate for a high-strain attachment by simply choosing a lighter insert.
  Limitations are the usual in-vitro caveats: rigid phantom, single displacement direction,
  no mucosal simulation. Still, for a student learning overdenture prosthetics, this
  is a useful reminder that attachment geometry — not just retention force — drives
  bone loading.'
