summary: A phenomenological study of Dutch RA patients finds that oral health is consistently
  deprioritised around the time of diagnosis and through the early disease course,
  despite the well-established bidirectional link between RA and periodontitis. Patients
  and rheumatologists alike are missing a critical intervention window.
summaryDeep: Rheumatoid arthritis and periodontitis share pathophysiological pathways
  — chronic inflammation, cytokine dysregulation, and immune activation — and treating
  one can measurably improve the other. Yet this qualitative study of Dutch RA patients
  reveals that oral health barely registers as a concern at diagnosis or in the early
  disease course. Patients are focused on joint symptoms, medication side effects,
  and functional limitations; oral health is an afterthought. Rheumatologists rarely
  prompt dental referral. The result is a missed window when periodontal intervention
  could potentially reduce systemic inflammatory burden and improve RA outcomes. For
  dental students, the practical implication is that patients with RA who do present
  to the dental chair may have neglected their oral health for years — expect more
  advanced periodontal disease and be prepared to explain the bidirectional relationship
  clearly. Interdisciplinary communication between dental and rheumatology teams remains
  underdeveloped.
