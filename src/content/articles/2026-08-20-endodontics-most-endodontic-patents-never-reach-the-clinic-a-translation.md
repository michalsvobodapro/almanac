summaryDeep: 'The endodontic market generates a steady stream of patented innovations
  — new file systems, irrigants, obturation materials, imaging tools — but the fraction
  that actually reach clinical use is unknown. This retrospective cohort analysis
  of endodontic patents characterises the translational pathway: grant rates, the
  time dynamics of the grant process, jurisdictional distribution, and the attrition
  between patent grant and commercial clinical availability. Published in the International
  Endodontic Journal, it provides a rare quantitative look at how innovation actually
  moves (or fails to move) from intellectual property to chairside practice. For a
  student, this reframes how to evaluate new product launches: a patent is an early-stage
  signal, not a clinical endorsement, and the majority of patented concepts will never
  be validated in clinical trials. It also raises a structural question about whether
  the endodontic field''s innovation pipeline is efficiently translating into patient
  benefit.'
