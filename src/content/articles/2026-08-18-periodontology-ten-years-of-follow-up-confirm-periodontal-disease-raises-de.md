summaryDeep: The Hisayama Study — a long-running Japanese community cohort — followed
  1,396 dentate adults free of dementia at baseline for 10 years, reassessing periodontal
  status at the 5-year mark. Poorer periodontal status was independently associated
  with higher incidence of all-cause dementia and Alzheimer's disease after adjustment
  for established confounders; associations with vascular dementia were also examined.
  The 10-year design and repeated periodontal measurement are methodological strengths
  that push this beyond the usual cross-sectional noise in the perio-systemic literature.
  While causality cannot be established from an observational cohort, the dose-response
  pattern and long follow-up substantially strengthen the biological plausibility
  of the periodontal-neuroinflammation pathway. For clinicians, this reinforces the
  case for aggressive periodontal management in older patients — not as a proven dementia
  prevention strategy, but as a modifiable risk factor worth addressing on its own
  terms and worth communicating to patients and GPs alike.
