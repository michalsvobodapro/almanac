summaryDeep: 'This review searched MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, and LILACS,
  ultimately including 25 studies (17 quantitative, 8 qualitative) from an initial
  2,073 records. Separate meta-analyses were run for lithium disilicate, alumina,
  and zirconia, comparing failures, technical failures, and repairable fracture complications
  between tooth- and implant-supported substrates. Heterogeneity was low across all
  analyses, and GRADEpro-assessed confidence in the cumulative evidence was rated
  favorably. The biomechanical concern — that implants transmit stress differently
  than the periodontal ligament, potentially increasing ceramic fracture risk — is
  not borne out at the clinical level. For students: this means you can apply the
  same ceramic material selection logic regardless of whether you''re crowning a tooth
  or an implant, simplifying treatment planning. The caveat is that most included
  studies have limited follow-up, so ultra-long-term data remain sparse.'
