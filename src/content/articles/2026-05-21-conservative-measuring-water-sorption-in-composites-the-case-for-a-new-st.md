summaryDeep: 'Water sorption is a key determinant of long-term composite performance:
  absorbed water plasticises the polymer matrix, degrades filler-matrix bonding, and
  contributes to marginal breakdown. The ISO 4049 method has been the benchmark for
  decades, but this European Journal of Oral Sciences study argues it systematically
  underestimates clinically relevant sorption by using specimen geometries and immersion
  conditions that don''t reflect intraoral reality. The authors propose and validate
  an alternative protocol, then apply both methods to a panel of contemporary composites
  — including bulk-fill and nanohybrid materials. The rankings shift meaningfully
  between methods, with some highly marketed materials performing worse under the
  new protocol. If the proposed method gains traction in regulatory or procurement
  frameworks, it could reshape how dental schools and practices evaluate material
  datasheets.'
