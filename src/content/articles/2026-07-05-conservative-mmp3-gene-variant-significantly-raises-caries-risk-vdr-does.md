summaryDeep: 'Genetic caries risk assessment has long been a research aspiration,
  and this study takes a focused look at two candidate polymorphisms — MMP3 rs679620
  (matrix metalloproteinase-3, involved in dentine matrix degradation) and VDR rs731236
  (vitamin D receptor, linked to enamel mineralisation). In 162 males divided into
  high-caries (DMFT ≥14) and low-caries (DMFT ≤5) groups, MMP3 genotype was significantly
  associated with caries experience (p<0.001) while VDR was not (p=0.862). The regression
  model''s 61.1% explanatory power is notable — most caries risk models explain far
  less — though the all-male, single-centre design limits generalisability. The practical
  implication is not immediate chairside genetic testing, but it reinforces that caries
  is genuinely polygenic and that MMP3 variants may eventually contribute to personalised
  prevention protocols. For a student: understand this as mechanistic evidence, not
  a clinical tool yet.'
