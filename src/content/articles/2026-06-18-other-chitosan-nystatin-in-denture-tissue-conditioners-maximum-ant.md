summaryDeep: 'Ninety-two samples were divided into six groups testing nystatin alone
  (3.2%), chitosan alone (5% or 7%), combined formulations, and an unmodified control.
  Antifungal efficacy was assessed by disc diffusion and hardness by durometer testing.
  The 7% chitosan + 3.2% nystatin combination (G6) showed the highest inhibition zones
  at both 1 and 7 days, suggesting sustained release of nystatin potentiated by chitosan''s
  intrinsic antifungal activity. Crucially, neither additive significantly compromised
  the resilience of the tissue conditioner — a common concern when modifying these
  materials. The in-vitro design is the main limitation: clinical performance in the
  oral environment, with salivary dilution and biofilm complexity, may differ substantially.
  This is the second chitosan study in today''s digest (alongside the pulpotomy RCT),
  reinforcing chitosan''s emergence as a versatile dental biomaterial. For prosthodontic
  students managing denture stomatitis, this offers a plausible path to antifungal
  tissue conditioners without sacrificing material properties.'
