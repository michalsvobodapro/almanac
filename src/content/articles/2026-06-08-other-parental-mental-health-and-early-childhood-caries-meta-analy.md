summaryDeep: 'Early childhood caries remains the most prevalent chronic disease in
  children globally, and identifying modifiable upstream determinants — including
  caregiver mental health — is a legitimate public health priority. The original meta-analysis
  attempted to quantify the association between parental mental health status and
  ECC risk. This commentary, however, raises methodological concerns that matter for
  how strongly the finding should be acted upon: issues around study heterogeneity,
  confounding by socioeconomic status, and the direction of causality are all plausible
  targets. For a student, the value here is less the specific finding and more the
  habit of reading systematic reviews critically — especially when they concern behavioural
  or social determinants where confounding is structurally difficult to eliminate.
  ECC prevention guidance should be evidence-graded, and this exchange is a reminder
  that a meta-analysis headline is not the end of the conversation.'
