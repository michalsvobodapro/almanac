excerpt: Background. Dental implantation is the gold standard for rehabilitation of
  patients with partial and complete tooth loss. The surface condition of implants
  is a key determinant of long-term survival — both at placement (in terms of roughness
  and biocompatibility) and at late stages when inflammatory complications arise.
  Laser technologies occupy a prominent place among methods of implant surface modification
  and decontamination. Objective. To analyze data on the use of different laser types
  for primary dental implant surface treatment and decontamination in peri-implantitis,
  and to assess the pattern of micro-relief damage at various laser parameters. Materials
  and methods. An in vitro experimental study was conducted using scanning electron
  microscopy (SEM) of dental implant surfaces (Straumann, Astra Tech, Osstem, Alpha
  Bio) following exposure to a diode laser «LSP-IRE-Polyus» (970 nm) and a Nd:YAG
  laser at power settings of 1 W and 2 W. Surface damage was graded on a 0–5-point
  scale. Results. The 970 nm diode laser at 1 W caused no damage to any of the studied
  implant surfaces (0 points), comparable to the pre-experimental intact surface.
  At 2 W, minor micro-relief disruptions (1–2 points) were detected only in Osstem
  and Alpha Bio implants. In contrast, the Nd:YAG laser at 1 W caused severe surface
  damage to all implants (2–5 points), including complete micro-relief disruption,
  delamination, and visible cracks. At 2 W, the damage was catastrophic. Conclusion.
