summaryDeep: 'Older adults bear a disproportionate burden of both periodontitis and
  chronic systemic disease, yet this scoping review of 24 systematic reviews (2010–2024,
  seven databases, PRISMA-ScR compliant) reveals that age-specific synthesis is almost
  entirely absent from the literature. Most included reviews pooled general adult
  data without stratifying by age, making it impossible to determine whether the periodontitis–systemic
  disease association is stronger, weaker, or differently mediated in patients over
  65. The one partial exception is cognitive impairment and dementia, where some age-focused
  analyses exist. Heterogeneity in disease definitions and inadequate confounder control
  compound the problem. For a Prague dental student treating an increasingly elderly
  Czech population, this review is a reminder that the confident-sounding associations
  taught in pathophysiology lectures rest on evidence that largely excludes the very
  patients most at risk. It also signals a clear research priority: longitudinal cohorts
  with age-stratified periodontal and systemic outcomes in adults over 65.'
