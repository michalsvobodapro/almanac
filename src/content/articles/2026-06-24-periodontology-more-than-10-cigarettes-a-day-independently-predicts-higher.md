summary: A cross-sectional study of 781 clinic patients confirms a dose-dependent
  relationship between daily cigarette consumption and periodontitis stage severity.
  Smoking more than 10 cigarettes/day remains an independent predictor of advanced
  disease after adjustment — and is associated with greater probing depths.
summaryDeep: This is confirmatory rather than groundbreaking, but the dataset (781
  patients, standardised 2017 AAP/EFP staging and grading) provides solid cross-sectional
  evidence for a dose-response relationship that is sometimes underemphasised in clinical
  conversations. The adjusted ordinal logistic regression model isolates smoking >10
  cigarettes/day as an independent predictor of higher periodontitis stage, with probing
  depth values also significantly associated (p = 0.012). Notably, the study excluded
  e-cigarettes and heated tobacco products — a limitation given their growing prevalence,
  but also a clean design choice. For a student, this reinforces the importance of
  detailed smoking history (quantity, not just yes/no) in periodontal risk assessment
  and staging. The data also support aggressive cessation counselling as part of periodontal
  management, not an afterthought.
