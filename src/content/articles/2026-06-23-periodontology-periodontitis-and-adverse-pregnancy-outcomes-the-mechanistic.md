summaryDeep: 'The association between periodontal disease and adverse pregnancy outcomes
  (APOs) has been debated for decades, often dismissed because RCTs of periodontal
  treatment during pregnancy have shown inconsistent birth-outcome benefits. This
  systematic review shifts the frame: rather than re-litigating association, it consolidates
  mechanistic evidence — prostaglandin E2 and TNF-α elevation, haematogenous spread
  of periodontal pathogens, placental inflammation, and T-regulatory cell suppression
  — that explains how gingivitis and periodontitis plausibly cause early pregnancy
  loss, preterm birth, preeclampsia, and low birth weight. The quality of included
  studies is rated high. Critically, the review identifies that gingivitis alone —
  not just established periodontitis — is mechanistically implicated, which broadens
  the clinical target population to essentially all pregnant patients. For a student
  on rotation in a maternity-adjacent setting, this paper provides the scientific
  rationale to prioritise periodontal assessment at the first prenatal dental visit
  and to treat rather than defer.'
