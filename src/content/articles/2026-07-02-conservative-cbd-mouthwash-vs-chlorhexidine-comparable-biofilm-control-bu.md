summaryDeep: Cannabidiol is attracting interest as an antimicrobial oral care ingredient,
  but in situ evidence has been thin. This crossover study had 14 participants wear
  intraoral appliances carrying bovine enamel fragments exposed to four CBD concentrations
  (0%, 0.01%, 0.05%, 0.1%) and 0.12% chlorhexidine over 7-day phases with washout
  periods. Biofilm CFU counts were similar between CHX and CBD 0.05–0.1%, which is
  the headline result. However, CHX outperformed all CBD concentrations on yeast suppression
  — relevant for patients at candidiasis risk. CBD 0.1% produced greater enamel surface
  roughness than CHX under sucrose exposure, a safety signal worth noting. Microhardness
  differences were not significant across treatments, and CHX caused the most color
  change (staining). The sample size is small and the model uses bovine enamel, so
  extrapolation to clinical populations is limited. CBD mouthwash is not ready to
  replace CHX, but the biofilm data justify a properly powered RCT.
