  finds that those with neurologist-confirmed migraine report significantly higher

summaryDeep: 'Central sensitization in migraine patients has long been suspected to
  amplify orofacial pain, but its specific effect on endodontic presentations had
  not been cleanly quantified. This prospective cohort does exactly that: 72 patients,
  neurologist-confirmed migraine classification, standardized quantitative sensory
  testing (MDT, MPT, MPS) at an extracephalic reference site, and VAS pain scores
  at multiple post-operative time points. The pre-operative pain gap is clinically
  meaningful — a 2.2-point VAS difference is above the minimum clinically important
  difference for acute pain. Elevated mechanical detection thresholds in the migraine
  group suggest altered somatosensory processing rather than simply higher pain reporting.
  Crucially, post-operative pain does not differ, meaning root canal treatment is
  equally effective regardless of migraine status. The practical implication: migraine
  patients presenting with pulpitis may be more distressed and require more thorough
  pre-operative counselling, but their prognosis after treatment is the same. Anaesthetic
  considerations (potential for anaesthetic failure due to central sensitization)
  are not addressed and warrant future study.'
