summaryDeep: 'The paper argues that the traditional biofilm-centric narrative of periodontal
  bone loss is incomplete: the tissue destruction is not merely collateral damage
  from immune activation but reflects a specific dysregulation of osteoclast-osteoblast
  coupling. P. gingivalis and other keystone pathogens tip the RANKL/OPG balance toward
  net resorption, and the inflammatory milieu sustains that imbalance even after biofilm
  reduction. This framing has direct relevance to adjunctive therapies — bisphosphonates,
  anti-RANKL biologics, and host-modulation agents — that target the uncoupling mechanism
  rather than the bacteria themselves. For students, this is the mechanistic bridge
  between the microbiology lectures and the pharmacology of bone-active drugs. The
  narrative review format limits quantitative conclusions, but the conceptual synthesis
  is timely given growing interest in systemic bone diseases intersecting with periodontitis.'
